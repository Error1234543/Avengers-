NEET Quick Revision: Chemistry in Everyday Life

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Chemistry in Everyday Life: The branch of chemistry that studies the chemical principles behind substances used in daily life, including medicines, food additives, cleaning agents, and cosmetics.
  • Drugs: Chemical substances that interact with biological systems to produce a physiological or psychological effect, primarily used for therapeutic purposes.
  • Pharmacology: The study of how drugs interact with living organisms to produce a change in function.
  • Chemotherapy: The use of chemical substances to treat diseases, particularly cancer, by targeting and killing abnormal cells.
  • Antacids: Substances that neutralize excess stomach acid to relieve indigestion, heartburn, or acid reflux.
  • Analgesics: Drugs that relieve pain without causing loss of consciousness.
  • Antibiotics: Substances produced by microorganisms that inhibit the growth of or destroy other microorganisms, primarily bacteria.
  • Antiseptics: Chemical agents that kill or inhibit the growth of microorganisms on living tissues to prevent infection.
  • Disinfectants: Chemical agents used to kill microorganisms on non-living surfaces to prevent infection.
  • Food Preservatives: Substances added to food to prevent spoilage caused by microbial growth or undesirable chemical changes.

Important facts

  • Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) is an analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory drug that inhibits COX enzymes. Overuse can cause gastric ulcers due to inhibition of protective prostaglandins.
  • Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is an analgesic and antipyretic but lacks anti-inflammatory action. Overdose can cause liver damage due to toxic metabolite (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine).
  • Morphine is a potent opioid analgesic used for severe pain but has high addiction potential. It acts on μ-opioid receptors in the central nervous system.
  • Penicillin, the first antibiotic, was discovered by Alexander Fleming. It inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by blocking transpeptidase enzymes.
  • Sulfa drugs (e.g., sulfanilamide) are bacteriostatic antibiotics that inhibit folic acid synthesis in bacteria by competing with PABA.
  • Dettol (chloroxylenol) is a common antiseptic used for wound cleaning and disinfection. It disrupts bacterial cell membranes.
  • Iodine (tincture or solution) is an antiseptic that oxidizes microbial proteins and nucleic acids, killing bacteria, viruses, and fungi.
  • Sodium benzoate is a food preservative that inhibits mold and yeast growth in acidic foods (pH < 4.5).
  • Aspartame is an artificial sweetener (200 times sweeter than sucrose) used in diet sodas. It is unstable at high temperatures and unsuitable for baking.
  • Saccharin is a non-nutritive sweetener (300-400 times sweeter than sucrose) with a bitter aftertaste. It is stable at high temperatures and used in baked goods.
  • Soaps form scum in hard water due to the formation of insoluble calcium and magnesium salts of fatty acids. Synthetic detergents do not form scum.
  • Anionic detergents (e.g., sodium lauryl sulfate) are commonly used in household cleaning products and shampoos due to their excellent foaming and cleaning properties.
  • Cationic detergents (e.g., cetyltrimethylammonium bromide) are used in fabric softeners and hair conditioners due to their ability to reduce static cling.
  • Non-ionic detergents (e.g., polyethoxylated alcohols) are used in low-foaming applications like dishwashing liquids and industrial cleaners.
  • BHA (butylated hydroxyanisole) and BHT (butylated hydroxytoluene) are synthetic antioxidants used to prevent rancidity in fats and oils.

Memory aids

  • To remember the classification of drugs: Use the mnemonic **AAA-TAAD** (Antacids, Analgesics, Antipyretics, Tranquilizers, Antibiotics, Antiseptics, Disinfectants).
  • For antibiotic mechanisms: **Penicillin Punch** (Penicillin inhibits Peptidoglycan synthesis in bacterial cell walls).
  • For antacids: **Sodium Bicarbonate Saves Stomachs** (Sodium bicarbonate neutralizes stomach acid).
  • For analgesics: **Aspirin Aches Away** (Aspirin is an analgesic and anti-inflammatory).
  • For food preservatives: **Benzoate Blocks Bacteria** (Sodium benzoate inhibits microbial growth).
  • For artificial sweeteners: **Aspartame is Artificial, Saccharin is Stronger** (Aspartame is 200x sweeter, saccharin is 300-400x sweeter than sucrose).
  • For cleansing agents: **Soap Scum, Detergent Doesn’t** (Soaps form scum in hard water; detergents do not).

Common traps

  • Confusing antiseptics with disinfectants: Antiseptics are used on living tissues (e.g., dettol, iodine), while disinfectants are used on non-living surfaces (e.g., phenol, bleach).
  • Assuming all analgesics are anti-inflammatory: Paracetamol is an analgesic and antipyretic but lacks anti-inflammatory action, unlike aspirin or ibuprofen.
