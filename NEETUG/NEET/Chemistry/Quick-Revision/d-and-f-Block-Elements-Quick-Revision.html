NEET Chemistry Quick Revision: d and f Block Elements

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • d-Block Elements: Transition elements located in groups 3 to 12 of the periodic table, characterized by the filling of the (n-1)d orbitals. They exhibit variable oxidation states, form colored compounds, and act as catalysts.
  • f-Block Elements: Inner transition elements consisting of lanthanoids (4f series) and actinoids (5f series), where the 4f and 5f orbitals are progressively filled. They show similar chemical properties within their respective series.
  • Transition Metal: An element whose atom has an incomplete d sub-shell, or which can give rise to cations with an incomplete d sub-shell. This definition excludes group 12 elements (Zn, Cd, Hg) in NEET context.
  • Lanthanoid Contraction: The gradual decrease in atomic and ionic radii of lanthanoid elements with increasing atomic number, caused by poor shielding of the 4f electrons, leading to increased effective nuclear charge.
  • Actinoid Contraction: Similar to lanthanoid contraction but observed in the actinoid series, resulting in a decrease in atomic and ionic radii across the series.

Important facts

  • Scandium (Sc) is the first d-block element but does not exhibit typical transition metal properties (e.g., no colored compounds, only +3 oxidation state).
  • Zinc (Zn), Cadmium (Cd), and Mercury (Hg) are not considered transition metals in NEET as their cations have completely filled d-orbitals (d¹⁰).
  • Lanthanoids are also called rare earth elements, though they are not particularly rare (except Promethium, which is radioactive and scarce).
  • Actinoids are all radioactive; Uranium (U) and Thorium (Th) are naturally occurring, while elements beyond Uranium are synthetic (transuranic elements).
  • The +3 oxidation state is most common for lanthanoids, but Cerium (Ce) shows +4 and Europium (Eu) shows +2 due to stability of empty, half-filled, or fully-filled f-orbitals.
  • Actinoids exhibit a wider range of oxidation states than lanthanoids due to smaller energy gap between 5f, 6d, and 7s orbitals (e.g., U: +3, +4, +5, +6).
  • Potassium Dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇) is a strong oxidizing agent in acidic medium (Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O).
  • Potassium Permanganate (KMnO₄) acts as an oxidizing agent in acidic (MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O), neutral (MnO₄⁻ + 2H₂O + 3e⁻ → MnO₂ + 4OH⁻), and alkaline media (MnO₄⁻ + e⁻ → MnO₄²⁻).
  • The stability of higher oxidation states increases down a group in d-block (e.g., Mo and W show +6, while Cr shows +6 but is less stable).
  • Lanthanoid ions are used as phosphors in color TV screens (e.g., Eu³⁺ for red, Tb³⁺ for green).

Common traps

  • Assuming all d-block elements are transition metals: Zn, Cd, and Hg are d-block but not transition metals as per NEET definition.
  • Confusing lanthanoids with actinoids: Remember lanthanoids are 4f series (Ce to Lu), actinoids are 5f series (Th to Lr).
  • Forgetting that Scandium (Sc) and Zinc (Zn) do not exhibit variable oxidation states or colored compounds, unlike other d-block elements.
  • Misidentifying the oxidation state of Mn in KMnO₄: It is +7, not +6 (common mistake due to similarity with Cr in K₂Cr₂O₇).
  • Assuming all transition metal ions are colored: d⁰ (e.g., Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺) and d¹⁰ (e.g., Zn²⁺, Cu⁺) ions are colorless due to absence of d-d transitions.
