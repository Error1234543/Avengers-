NEET Quick Revision: Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Amines: Derivatives of ammonia (NH₃) where one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. Classified as primary (1°), secondary (2°), or tertiary (3°) based on the number of carbon-containing groups attached to the nitrogen atom.
  • Nitro compounds: Organic compounds containing the nitro group (-NO₂) attached to a carbon atom. Classified as aliphatic or aromatic based on the carbon skeleton.
  • Cyanides (Nitriles): Organic compounds containing the cyano group (-C≡N) attached to an alkyl or aryl group. General formula: R-C≡N.
  • Isocyanides (Isonitriles): Organic compounds containing the isocyano group (-N≡C) attached to an alkyl or aryl group. General formula: R-N≡C.
  • Diazonium salts: Aromatic compounds containing the diazonium group (-N₂⁺ X⁻), where X⁻ is an anion (e.g., Cl⁻). General formula: Ar-N₂⁺ X⁻.
  • Amides: Derivatives of carboxylic acids where the -OH group is replaced by an -NH₂, -NHR, or -NR₂ group. Classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary based on the number of carbon-containing groups attached to the nitrogen atom.

Important facts

  • Primary amines give a positive carbylamine test (foul-smelling isocyanides), while secondary and tertiary amines do not.
  • Aromatic amines are weaker bases than aliphatic amines due to the resonance stabilization of the lone pair on nitrogen with the benzene ring.
  • Diazonium salts are stable only at low temperatures (0-5°C) and decompose upon heating.
  • The Sandmeyer reaction is used to introduce Cl, Br, or CN groups into an aromatic ring via diazonium salts.
  • Nitrobenzene undergoes electrophilic substitution predominantly at the meta position due to the electron-withdrawing nature of the nitro group.
  • Isocyanides are foul-smelling compounds and are used as a test for primary amines (carbylamine reaction).
  • Amides are neutral compounds, unlike amines, which are basic, due to the resonance stabilization of the lone pair on nitrogen.
  • The Hinsberg test is a qualitative test to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines based on their reactivity with benzene sulfonyl chloride.

Memory aids

  • For **basicity of amines**: Remember 'AAB' (Aliphatic > Ammonia > Aromatic) to recall the order of basicity.
  • For **Hofmann bromamide degradation**: 'Hofmann loses a carbon' — the reaction reduces the carbon chain by one carbon.
  • For **diazonium salt reactions**: 'Sandmeyer uses Cu, Gattermann uses Cu powder' to remember the reagents for halogen substitution.
  • For **carbylamine reaction**: 'Primary amines stink' — the foul smell of isocyanides indicates a positive test for primary amines.
  • For **Hinsberg test**: 'Primary soluble, secondary insoluble, tertiary no reaction' — the solubility of sulfonamides in alkali distinguishes the amines.

Common traps

  • Confusing the classification of amines: Remember that the classification (1°, 2°, 3°) is based on the number of carbon-containing groups attached to nitrogen, not the number of hydrogen atoms.
