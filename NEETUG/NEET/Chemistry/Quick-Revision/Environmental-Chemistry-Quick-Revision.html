NEET Chemistry Quick Revision: Environmental Chemistry

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Environmental Chemistry: The branch of chemistry that studies the chemical phenomena occurring in the environment, including the sources, reactions, transport, effects, and fates of chemical species in air, water, and soil.
  • Pollutant: A substance or energy introduced into the environment that has undesired effects or adversely affects the usefulness of a resource.
  • Primary Pollutant: A pollutant emitted directly from a source (e.g., CO, SO₂, NO₂, particulate matter).
  • Secondary Pollutant: A pollutant formed by the chemical reaction of primary pollutants in the environment (e.g., ozone, peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN), sulfuric acid).
  • Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD): The amount of dissolved oxygen required by aerobic biological organisms to break down organic material present in a given water sample at a certain temperature over a specific time period (usually 5 days at 20°C).
  • Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD): A measure of the amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize organic and inorganic substances in water.
  • Eutrophication: The process by which a body of water becomes overly enriched with minerals and nutrients, inducing excessive growth of algae and depleting oxygen levels.
  • Greenhouse Effect: The warming of Earth's surface and lower atmosphere due to the presence of greenhouse gases (e.g., CO₂, CH₄, N₂O) that trap infrared radiation.
  • Ozone Layer: A region of Earth's stratosphere that absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet (UV) radiation, primarily composed of ozone (O₃).
  • Acid Rain: Precipitation with a pH lower than 5.6, primarily caused by the dissolution of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) in water vapor.

Important facts

  • The primary source of CO₂ emissions is the combustion of fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas).
  • Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) is a major contributor to acid rain and respiratory diseases.
  • Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) are produced from vehicle emissions and industrial processes, contributing to smog and acid rain.
  • Particulate matter (PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀) penetrates deep into the lungs, causing cardiovascular and respiratory diseases.
  • The ozone layer is located in the stratosphere, approximately 10-50 km above Earth's surface.
  • Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) were widely used in refrigeration and aerosol propellants before being phased out under the Montreal Protocol.
  • Eutrophication is primarily caused by the runoff of nitrates and phosphates from agricultural fertilizers and sewage.
  • Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) is a critical parameter for assessing water quality; high BOD indicates severe organic pollution.
  • Heavy metals like mercury (Hg) and lead (Pb) are neurotoxic and can cause irreversible damage to the nervous system.
  • The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels, preferably to 1.5°C.

Memory aids

  • Remember the '3 Ps' of atmospheric pollution: **P**rimary pollutants (directly emitted), **P**hotochemical reactions (forming secondary pollutants), and **P**AN (peroxyacetyl nitrate, a secondary pollutant).
  • For greenhouse gases, think of 'CO₂-MAN': **CO₂** (carbon dioxide), **M**ethane (CH₄), **A**nd **N**itrous oxide (N₂O).
  • To recall the effects of acid rain, use the acronym 'SCALE': **S**oil nutrient depletion, **C**orrosion of buildings, **A**quatic life harm, **L**oss of forests, **E**cosystem disruption.
  • For ozone layer depletion, remember 'CFCs = Chlorine Frees Catalytically': Chlorine atoms from CFCs catalytically destroy ozone molecules.
  • BOD vs. COD: 'BOD is Biological, COD is Chemical' — BOD measures oxygen used by microbes, while COD measures oxygen used by chemical oxidation.

Common traps

  • Confusing primary and secondary pollutants: Primary pollutants are directly emitted (e.g., CO, SO₂), while secondary pollutants form in the atmosphere (e.g., O₃, PAN).
  • Assuming all ozone is harmful: Stratospheric ozone is protective, while tropospheric ozone is a harmful pollutant.
