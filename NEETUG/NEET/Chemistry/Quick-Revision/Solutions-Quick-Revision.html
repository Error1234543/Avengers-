NEET Chemistry Quick Revision: Solutions

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances, where the solute is uniformly distributed within the solvent.
  • **Solute**: The component present in smaller quantity, which dissolves in the solvent.
  • **Solvent**: The component present in larger quantity, which dissolves the solute. Water is the most common solvent (aqueous solutions).
  • **Solubility**: The maximum amount of solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature, usually expressed in grams of solute per 100 grams of solvent.
  • **Concentration**: A measure of the amount of solute present in a given quantity of solution or solvent. Common units include molarity (M), molality (m), mole fraction (χ), and mass percentage.

It is temperature-dependent because volume changes with temperature. Example: A 1 M NaCl solution contains 1 mole of NaCl in 1 liter of solution. Molarity is widely used in stoichiometric calculations for reactions in solution.'}

Unlike molarity, molality is temperature-independent because mass does not change with temperature. It is useful in colligative property calculations (e.g., freezing point depression, boiling point elevation). Example: A 1 m NaCl solution contains 1 mole of NaCl in 1 kg of water.'}
  • {'concept': 'Mole Fraction (χ)', 'content': "Mole fraction is the ratio of the number of moles of a component to the total number of moles of all components in the solution. Formula: χ_i = n_i / (n_1 + n_2 + ... + n_n). Mole fraction is dimensionless and is used in Raoult's law and gas mixture calculations. For a binary solution, χ_solute + χ_solvent = 1."}
  • {'concept': 'Mass Percentage and Parts Per Million (ppm)', 'content': 'Mass percentage is the mass of solute divided by the total mass of the solution, multiplied by 100. Formula: (mass of solute / mass of solution) × 100. Parts per million (ppm) is used for very dilute solutions: ppm = (mass of solute / mass of solution) × 10^6. Example: A 5% (w/w) NaCl solution means 5 grams of NaCl in 100 grams of solution.'}
  • {'concept': 'Solubility and Factors Affecting It', 'content': "Solubility depends on: (1) **Nature of solute and solvent**: Polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents (e.g., NaCl in water), non-polar solutes dissolve in non-polar solvents (e.g., iodine in carbon tetrachloride). (2) **Temperature**: For most solids, solubility increases with temperature (e.g., sugar in water). For gases, solubility decreases with increasing temperature (e.g., oxygen in water). (3) **Pressure**: Affects only the solubility of gases (Henry's Law). Higher pressure increases gas solubility in liquids."}
  • {'concept': "Henry's Law", 'content': "Henry's Law states that the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid. Formula: S = k_H × P, where S is solubility, k_H is Henry's law constant (dependent on temperature and gas-solvent pair), and P is partial pressure. Example: Carbonated drinks are bottled under high CO₂ pressure to increase its solubility in water. When the bottle is opened, pressure decreases, and CO₂ escapes (effervescence)."}
  • {'concept': "Raoult's Law", 'content': "Raoult's Law states that the partial vapor pressure of a solvent in a solution is equal to the mole fraction of the solvent multiplied by the vapor pressure of the pure solvent. Formula: P_solution = χ_solvent × P°_solvent. For a non-volatile solute, the vapor pressure of the solution is always less than that of the pure solvent. This law is the basis for colligative properties like vapor pressure lowering."}
  • {'concept': 'Colligative Properties', 'content': "Colligative properties depend only on the number of solute particles in a solution, not on their identity. Key colligative properties for NEET: (1) **Vapor pressure lowering**: Due to Raoult's Law. (2) **Boiling point elevation (ΔT_b)**: ΔT_b = i × K_b × m, where i is van't Hoff factor, K_b is ebullioscopic constant, and m is molality. (3) **Freezing point depression (ΔT_f)**: ΔT_f = i × K_f × m, where K_f is cryoscopic constant. (4) **Osmotic pressure (π)**: π = i × C × R × T, where C is molarity, R is gas constant, and T is temperature in Kelvin. These properties are used to determine molecular weights of solutes."}
  • {'concept': "van't Hoff Factor (i)", 'content': "The van't Hoff factor accounts for the number of particles a solute dissociates into in solution. For non-electrolytes (e.g., glucose), i = 1. For electrolytes, i depends on the degree of dissociation: (1) NaCl (strong electrolyte) → Na⁺ + Cl⁻, i = 2 (theoretical). (2) CaCl₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻, i = 3 (theoretical). In practice, i is less than theoretical due to ion pairing. The van't Hoff factor is used to correct colligative property calculations for electrolytes."}
  • {'concept': 'Ideal vs. Non-Ideal Solutions', 'content': "An **ideal solution** obeys Raoult's Law over the entire range of concentrations. Characteristics: (1) ΔH_mixing = 0 (no heat absorbed or released), (2) ΔV_mixing = 0 (no volume change). Example: Benzene and toluene. A **non-ideal solution** deviates from Raoult's Law. Deviations can be: (1) **Positive deviation**: Vapor pressure is higher than predicted (e.g., ethanol and water). (2) **Negative deviation**: Vapor pressure is lower than predicted (e.g., acetone and chloroform). Non-ideal behavior arises due to differences in intermolecular forces between solute-solute, solvent-solvent, and solute-solvent interactions."}

Important facts

  • The **common ion effect** reduces the solubility of a salt when a common ion is already present in the solution (e.g., adding NaCl to a saturated AgCl solution decreases AgCl solubility).
  • For **gases in liquids**, solubility decreases with increasing temperature (exothermic dissolution) and increases with increasing pressure (Henry's Law).
  • The **ebullioscopic constant (K_b)** and **cryoscopic constant (K_f)** are characteristic of the solvent, not the solute. For water, K_b = 0.512 °C kg/mol and K_f = 1.86 °C kg/mol.
  • Osmotic pressure is a **colligative property** and is directly proportional to the molarity of the solution. It is the most sensitive colligative property for determining molecular weights of large molecules like proteins.
  • A **semi-permeable membrane** allows only solvent molecules to pass through, not solute particles. This is the basis for osmosis and osmotic pressure.
  • For **electrolytes**, the observed colligative effect is always less than the theoretical value due to ion pairing, which reduces the effective number of particles in solution.
  • The **relative lowering of vapor pressure** is equal to the mole fraction of the solute in a solution containing a non-volatile solute: (P° - P) / P° = χ_solute.
  • In **azeotropic mixtures**, the liquid and vapor phases have the same composition, and the mixture boils at a constant temperature. Example: 95.6% ethanol and 4.4% water (minimum boiling azeotrope).

Memory aids

  • **Molarity vs. Molality**: Molarity is 'Moles per Liter' (M), Molality is 'Moles per Kilogram' (m). Remember: 'M' for 'Molarity' and 'Mass' for 'Molality'.
  • **Henry's Law**: 'Henry's k_H is High for Hardly soluble gases' (higher k_H means lower solubility at a given pressure).
  • **Raoult's Law**: 'Raoult's Law is for the solvent's vapor pressure' (P_solution = χ_solvent × P°_solvent).
