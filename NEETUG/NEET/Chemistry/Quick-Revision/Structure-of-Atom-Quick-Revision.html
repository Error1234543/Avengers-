Definitions

  • Atom: The smallest indivisible unit of matter that retains the chemical properties of an element. Composed of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • Proton (p⁺): Positively charged subatomic particle found in the nucleus with a relative mass of 1 and charge +1.
  • Neutron (n⁰): Neutral subatomic particle found in the nucleus with a relative mass of 1 and no charge.
  • Electron (e⁻): Negatively charged subatomic particle orbiting the nucleus with negligible mass (~1/1836 of a proton) and charge -1.
  • Atomic Number (Z): Number of protons in the nucleus, defining the element's identity and position in the periodic table.
  • Mass Number (A): Sum of protons and neutrons in the nucleus (A = Z + N, where N = number of neutrons).
  • Isotopes: Atoms of the same element with identical atomic numbers but different mass numbers due to varying neutron counts.
  • Isobars: Atoms of different elements with the same mass number but different atomic numbers.
  • Isotones: Atoms of different elements with the same number of neutrons but different atomic numbers.
  • Electronic Configuration: Distribution of electrons in atomic orbitals following the Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, and Hund’s rule.
  • Quantum Numbers: Set of four numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ) that describe the energy, shape, orientation, and spin of an electron in an atom.
  • Orbital: Region in space around the nucleus where the probability of finding an electron is maximum. Defined by quantum numbers (s, p, d, f).
  • Ground State: The lowest energy state of an atom where electrons occupy the innermost available orbitals.
  • Excited State: Higher energy state of an atom where one or more electrons occupy orbitals farther from the nucleus than in the ground state.

Important facts

  • The radius of an atom is approximately 10⁻¹⁰ m (1 Å), while the radius of the nucleus is about 10⁻¹⁵ m (1 fm). The nucleus is thus ~10⁵ times smaller than the atom.
  • The mass of an electron is 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg, while the mass of a proton is 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (approximately 1836 times heavier).
  • The charge of an electron is -1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, and the charge of a proton is +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (elementary charge, e).
  • Isotopes of hydrogen: Protium (¹H, 0 neutrons), Deuterium (²H, 1 neutron), Tritium (³H, 2 neutrons).
  • The maximum number of electrons in a shell is given by 2n², where n is the principal quantum number (e.g., n = 1 → 2 electrons, n = 2 → 8 electrons, n = 3 → 18 electrons).
  • The maximum number of electrons in a subshell is given by 2(2l + 1) (e.g., s subshell → 2 electrons, p subshell → 6 electrons, d subshell → 10 electrons, f subshell → 14 electrons).
  • The energy of an orbital increases with the value of (n + l). If two orbitals have the same (n + l) value, the orbital with the lower n value has lower energy (e.g., 3d > 4s).
  • The shape of orbitals: s orbital → spherical, p orbital → dumbbell-shaped, d orbital → cloverleaf-shaped (except dₓ², which is doughnut-shaped).
  • The number of radial nodes in an orbital is given by (n - l - 1), and the number of angular nodes is given by l.
  • The photoelectric effect (Einstein) demonstrated the particle nature of light (photons) and provided evidence for the quantization of energy (E = hν).
  • The Balmer series in the hydrogen spectrum corresponds to transitions where n₁ = 2 (visible region), while the Lyman series corresponds to n₁ = 1 (ultraviolet region).
