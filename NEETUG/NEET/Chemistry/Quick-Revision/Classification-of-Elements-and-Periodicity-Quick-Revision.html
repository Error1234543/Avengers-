Key concepts

  • {'concept': 'Modern Periodic Law', 'content': 'The properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers. This law, proposed by Henry Moseley, corrected Mendeleev’s periodic law, which was based on atomic masses. The modern periodic table arranges elements in order of increasing atomic number, leading to the correct placement of isotopes and resolution of anomalies like tellurium and iodine.'}
  • {'concept': 'Electronic Configuration and Position in Periodic Table', 'content': 'The position of an element in the periodic table is determined by its electronic configuration. The period number corresponds to the principal quantum number (n) of the outermost shell, while the group number is related to the number of valence electrons. For example, sodium (Na) has the configuration [Ne] 3s¹, placing it in Period 3 and Group 1.'}
  • {'concept': 'Atomic Radius', 'content': 'The distance from the nucleus to the outermost electron shell. It decreases across a period due to increasing nuclear charge pulling electrons closer, and increases down a group as new electron shells are added. Exceptions include noble gases, which have larger atomic radii than halogens due to van der Waals radii measurements.'}
  • {'concept': 'Ionization Enthalpy (IE)', 'content': 'The energy required to remove an electron from a neutral gaseous atom. It increases across a period due to increasing nuclear charge and decreasing atomic radius, and decreases down a group as outer electrons are farther from the nucleus. Exceptions include Group 13 (e.g., B < Be) and Group 16 (e.g., O < N) due to half-filled or fully-filled subshell stability.'}
  • {'concept': 'Electron Gain Enthalpy (EGE)', 'content': 'The energy change when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom. It becomes more negative (exothermic) across a period as atoms gain electrons more readily, and less negative down a group. Noble gases have positive EGE due to stable electronic configurations. Exceptions include Group 2 (positive EGE) and Group 15 (less negative than Group 14).'}
  • {'concept': 'Electronegativity', 'content': 'The ability of an atom to attract shared electrons in a covalent bond. It increases across a period and decreases down a group. Fluorine is the most electronegative element (Pauling scale value of 4.0). Electronegativity differences determine bond polarity (e.g., ionic vs. covalent bonds).'}
  • {'concept': 'Metallic and Non-Metallic Character', 'content': 'Metallic character (tendency to lose electrons) decreases across a period and increases down a group. Transition metals (d-block) show variable oxidation states and form colored compounds.'}

Important facts

  • The periodic table has 7 periods and 18 groups, with the f-block elements (lanthanides and actinides) placed separately at the bottom.
  • Hydrogen is unique and does not fit neatly into any group; it is placed in Group 1 due to its 1s¹ configuration but also resembles halogens in some properties.
  • Helium is placed in Group 18 (noble gases) despite its 1s² configuration because it is chemically inert, unlike Group 2 elements.
  • The d-block elements (Groups 3-12) are called transition metals and exhibit properties like variable oxidation states, formation of colored compounds, and catalytic activity.
  • Lanthanides (atomic numbers 58-71) and actinides (atomic numbers 90-103) are part of the f-block and are characterized by the filling of 4f and 5f subshells, respectively.
  • The second period (Li to Ne) is unique because it contains elements with the smallest atomic sizes and highest ionization enthalpies in their respective groups.
  • Diagonal relationships exist between certain elements (e.g., Li-Mg, Be-Al, B-Si), where elements in the second period resemble elements in the third period of the next group due to similar charge-to-size ratios.
  • Noble gases (Group 18) are chemically inert due to their stable ns²np⁶ electronic configuration (except He, which has 1s²).

  • {'trap': 'Assuming All Noble Gases Have ns²np⁶ Configuration', 'content': 'Helium (He) has a 1s² configuration, not ns²np⁶. It is placed in Group 18 due to its inertness, not its electronic configuration.'}
