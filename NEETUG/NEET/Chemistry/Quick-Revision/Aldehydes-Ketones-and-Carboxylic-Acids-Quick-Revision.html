NEET Quick Revision: Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Aldehydes: Organic compounds containing the carbonyl group (C=O) bonded to at least one hydrogen atom, with the general formula R-CHO. The carbonyl carbon is terminal.
  • Ketones: Organic compounds containing the carbonyl group (C=O) bonded to two carbon atoms, with the general formula R-COR'. The carbonyl carbon is non-terminal.
  • Carboxylic Acids: Organic compounds containing the carboxyl group (-COOH), which consists of a carbonyl group (C=O) and a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to the same carbon atom. General formula: R-COOH.
  • Nucleophilic Addition: A reaction where a nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, leading to the formation of a tetrahedral intermediate. Common in aldehydes and ketones.
  • Tollens' Test: A chemical test used to distinguish aldehydes from ketones. Aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate) to metallic silver, forming a silver mirror.
  • Fehling's Test: A chemical test to differentiate aldehydes from ketones. Aldehydes reduce Fehling's solution (copper(II) ions in alkaline medium) to a red precipitate of copper(I) oxide.
  • Carboxylation: The process of introducing a carboxyl group (-COOH) into an organic compound, often via the reaction of carbon dioxide with a Grignard reagent.
  • Decarboxylation: The removal of a carboxyl group from a carboxylic acid, resulting in the release of carbon dioxide (CO₂). Commonly occurs in β-keto acids and malonic acid derivatives.
  • Esterification: The reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol to form an ester and water, typically catalyzed by an acid (e.g., H₂SO₄).

Important facts

  • Formaldehyde (methanal) is the simplest aldehyde and is used as a preservative and disinfectant. It exists as a gas at room temperature but is commonly used as a 40% aqueous solution (formalin).
  • Acetone (propanone) is the simplest ketone and is widely used as a solvent. It is miscible with water and has a characteristic sweet odor.
  • Formic acid (methanoic acid) is the simplest carboxylic acid and is unique because it contains both an aldehyde and a carboxyl group. It is found in ant venom.
  • Acetic acid (ethanoic acid) is a weak acid and the primary component of vinegar (5-8% solution). Glacial acetic acid is the pure, anhydrous form.
  • Benzoic acid is a common food preservative (E210) and is used in the manufacture of plastics, dyes, and insect repellents.
  • The carboxyl group (-COOH) is highly polar and can form hydrogen bonds, leading to higher boiling points compared to aldehydes and ketones of similar molecular weight.
  • Carboxylic acids with 1-4 carbon atoms are miscible with water due to hydrogen bonding, but solubility decreases with increasing chain length.
  • The pKa of carboxylic acids typically ranges from 4 to 5, making them stronger acids than alcohols (pKa ~16) but weaker than mineral acids (e.g., HCl, pKa ~-7).
  • Aldehydes and ketones with α-hydrogens can undergo aldol condensation in the presence of a base, forming β-hydroxy aldehydes or ketones.
  • Carboxylic acids react with bases (e.g., NaOH) to form water-soluble salts, which are used in soaps (e.g., sodium stearate).

Memory aids

  • Aldehydes end with '-al' (e.g., ethanal). Think of 'Aldehyde' as 'Al' (alcohol) + 'de' (dehydrogenated).
  • Ketones end with '-one' (e.g., propanone). Remember: 'Ketone' has an 'O' in the middle, just like the carbonyl group is in the middle of the chain.
  • Carboxylic acids end with '-oic acid' (e.g., ethanoic acid). Think of 'COOH' as 'C' (carbon) + 'OOH' (like 'oh oh' for the two oxygens).
