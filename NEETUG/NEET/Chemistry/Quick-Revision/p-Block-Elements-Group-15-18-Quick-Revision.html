NEET Chemistry Quick Revision: p-Block Elements (Group 15-18)

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • p-Block Elements: Elements in Groups 15 to 18 of the periodic table where the last electron enters the p-orbital of the outermost shell. These groups include nonmetals, metalloids, and metals with diverse chemical properties.
  • Inert Pair Effect: The tendency of the two electrons in the outermost s-orbital to remain unshared in compounds of post-transition metals, leading to lower oxidation states (e.g., Pb²⁺ instead of Pb⁴⁺).
  • Allotropy: The existence of an element in two or more different forms in the same physical state, differing in atomic arrangement or molecular structure (e.g., O₂ and O₃ for oxygen, white/red/black phosphorus).
  • Interhalogen Compounds: Compounds formed between two different halogen elements (e.g., ClF, BrF₃, ICl). These are highly reactive and often used as fluorinating agents.
  • Pseudohalogens: Polyatomic analogues of halogens that exhibit similar chemical behavior (e.g., CN⁻, SCN⁻). They form salts, acids, and inter-pseudohalogen compounds.

Important facts

  • Nitrogen Fixation: Conversion of atmospheric N₂ to ammonia (NH₃) by bacteria (e.g., Rhizobium) or industrial processes (Haber process). Critical for plant growth.
  • Phosphorus Allotropes: White phosphorus (P₄, highly reactive, toxic) and red phosphorus (polymeric, stable, used in matches). Black phosphorus is thermodynamically stable but less common.
  • Sulfur Allotropes: Rhombic sulfur (α-sulfur, stable at room temperature) and monoclinic sulfur (β-sulfur, stable above 95.5°C). Both consist of S₈ rings.
  • Contact Process: Industrial production of sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) using V₂O₅ catalyst: SO₂ + O₂ → SO₃ (exothermic), followed by SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum), then H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄.
  • Ostwald Process: Industrial production of nitric acid (HNO₃) from ammonia: 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (Pt-Rh catalyst), followed by 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂, then 3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO.
  • Bleaching Action of Chlorine: Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HClO (hypochlorous acid), which decomposes to release nascent oxygen (O), a strong oxidizing agent.
  • Xenon Compounds: XeF₂ (linear), XeF₄ (square planar), XeF₆ (distorted octahedral). XeO₃ is explosive and forms xenates (XeO₄²⁻) and perxenates (XeO₆⁴⁻).
  • Noble Gas Compounds: First synthesized by Neil Bartlett in 1962 (XePtF₆). Xenon compounds are used in lasers and as oxidizing agents.
