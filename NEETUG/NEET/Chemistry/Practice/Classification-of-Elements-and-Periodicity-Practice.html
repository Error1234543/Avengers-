NEET Chemistry Practice - Classification of Elements and Periodicity (Part 1)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Classification of Elements and Periodicity in Chemistry, designed for NEET difficulty. Each question has four options (A-D), with a single correct answer. Read carefully and select the best response. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following statements correctly explains the trend of atomic radii in the modern periodic table?

A. Atomic radii increase down a group due to the addition of new electron shells and decrease across a period due to increased nuclear charge.
B. Atomic radii decrease down a group due to increased shielding effect and increase across a period due to reduced nuclear charge.
C. Atomic radii remain constant down a group but decrease across a period due to the addition of electrons in the same shell.
D. Atomic radii increase both down a group and across a period due to the cumulative effect of electron addition.
Answer: A

Atomic radii increase down a group because each successive element has an additional electron shell, increasing the distance of the outermost electrons from the nucleus. Across a period, atomic radii decrease due to increased effective nuclear charge, which pulls the outer electrons closer to the nucleus.

2. Which of the following isoelectronic species has the smallest ionic radius?

A. O²⁻
B. F⁻
C. Na⁺
D. Mg²⁺
Answer: D

All the given species are isoelectronic (10 electrons). The ionic radius decreases as the nuclear charge increases because the greater positive charge pulls the electrons closer to the nucleus. Mg²⁺ has the highest nuclear charge (12) among the options, resulting in the smallest ionic radius.

3. The correct order of increasing first ionization enthalpy for the elements Li, Be, B, and C is:

A. Li < B < Be < C
B. Li < Be < B < C
C. Be < Li < B < C
D. B < Li < Be < C
Answer: A

First ionization enthalpy generally increases across a period. However, Be has a higher ionization enthalpy than B because Be has a fully filled 2s subshell, which is more stable. The correct order is Li (lowest) < B < Be < C (highest).

4. Which of the following elements exhibits the most metallic character?

A. Aluminum (Al)
B. Silicon (Si)
C. Phosphorus (P)
D. Sulfur (S)
Answer: A

Metallic character decreases across a period from left to right. Aluminum (Al) is the leftmost element among the options and thus exhibits the most metallic character due to its tendency to lose electrons easily.

5. The element with the electronic configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵ belongs to which group and period in the periodic table?

A. Group 17, Period 3
B. Group 15, Period 3
C. Group 17, Period 2
D. Group 7, Period 3
Answer: A

The electronic configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵ corresponds to chlorine (Cl). The outermost shell (n=3) indicates Period 3, and the presence of 7 valence electrons (3s² 3p⁵) places it in Group 17 (halogens).

6. Which of the following statements about electronegativity is correct?

A. Electronegativity increases down a group and decreases across a period.
B. Electronegativity decreases down a group and increases across a period.
C. Electronegativity remains constant across a period but decreases down a group.
D. Electronegativity increases both down a group and across a period.
Answer: B

Electronegativity decreases down a group due to increased atomic size and shielding effect, which reduces the attraction for bonding electrons. Across a period, electronegativity increases due to increased nuclear charge and smaller atomic size.

7. The correct order of increasing acidic strength for the oxides of the third-period elements is:

A. Na₂O < MgO < Al₂O₃ < SiO₂ < P₄O₁₀ < SO₃ < Cl₂O₇
B. Cl₂O₇ < SO₃ < P₄O₁₀ < SiO₂ < Al₂O₃ < MgO < Na₂O
C. Na₂O < MgO < SiO₂ < Al₂O₃ < P₄O₁₀ < SO₃ < Cl₂O₇
D. Al₂O₃ < Na₂O < MgO < SiO₂ < P₄O₁₀ < SO₃ < Cl₂O₇
Answer: A

Acidic strength of oxides increases across a period from left to right. Basic oxides (Na₂O, MgO) are on the left, amphoteric oxides (Al₂O₃) are in the middle, and acidic oxides (SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₃, Cl₂O₇) are on the right. The correct order of increasing acidic strength is as given in option A.

8. The element with the lowest melting point in Group 1 (alkali metals) is:

A. Lithium (Li)
B. Sodium (Na)
C. Potassium (K)
D. Cesium (Cs)
Answer: D

Melting points of alkali metals decrease down the group due to weakening metallic bonds as atomic size increases. Cesium (Cs) has the largest atomic size among the given options and thus the lowest melting point.

9. Which of the following elements is classified as a metalloid?

A. Aluminum (Al)
B. Silicon (Si)
C. Phosphorus (P)
D. Sulfur (S)
Answer: B

Metalloids exhibit properties intermediate between metals and non-metals. Silicon (Si) is a classic example of a metalloid, located along the staircase line in the periodic table that separates metals from non-metals.

10. Which of the following elements has the highest electron affinity?

A. Fluorine
B. Chlorine
C. Bromine
D. Iodine
Answer: B

Chlorine has the highest electron affinity among the halogens due to its optimal balance between nuclear charge and atomic size. Fluorine, despite its high electronegativity, has a lower electron affinity because of electron-electron repulsion in its compact 2p orbital.

11. Which of the following statements about the modern periodic table is INCORRECT?

A. Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number.
B. The table consists of 18 groups and 7 periods.
C. Lanthanides and actinides are placed in the same period.
D. Group 1 elements are called alkali metals.
Answer: C

Lanthanides and actinides are placed in separate rows below the main periodic table and belong to different periods (6th and 7th, respectively). They are not placed in the same period.

12. The element with the electronic configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴ belongs to which block and group?

A. s-block, Group 2
B. p-block, Group 16
C. d-block, Group 6
D. p-block, Group 14
Answer: B

The electronic configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴ corresponds to sulfur (S), which belongs to the p-block and Group 16 (the oxygen family).

13. Which of the following trends is observed down Group 17 (halogens) in the periodic table?

A. Increasing electronegativity
B. Increasing atomic radius
C. Decreasing electron affinity
D. Decreasing melting point
Answer: B

Down Group 17, atomic radius increases due to the addition of new electron shells. Electronegativity and electron affinity decrease, while melting and boiling points generally increase.

14. An element X has the following properties: high ionization enthalpy, low electron affinity, and forms basic oxides. It is most likely a:

A. Noble gas
B. Alkali metal
C. Alkaline earth metal
D. Transition metal
Answer: C

Alkaline earth metals (Group 2) have relatively high ionization enthalpies, low electron affinities, and form basic oxides. Noble gases have very high ionization enthalpies and negligible electron affinity, while alkali metals have low ionization enthalpies.

15. Which of the following elements is a metalloid?

A. Aluminum
B. Silicon
C. Phosphorus
D. Sulfur
Answer: B

Silicon is a metalloid, exhibiting properties intermediate between metals and nonmetals. Aluminum is a metal, while phosphorus and sulfur are nonmetals.

16. The element with the highest metallic character in Period 3 is:

A. Sodium
B. Magnesium
C. Aluminum
D. Silicon
Answer: A

Metallic character decreases across a period from left to right. Sodium (Na), being the first element in Period 3, has the highest metallic character.

17. The correct order of increasing electronegativity for the elements O, F, S, Cl is:

A. S < Cl < O < F
B. Cl < S < O < F
C. S < O < Cl < F
D. O < F < S < Cl
Answer: A

Electronegativity increases across a period and decreases down a group. Fluorine (F) is the most electronegative element, followed by oxygen (O), chlorine (Cl), and sulfur (S).
