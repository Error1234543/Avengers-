NEET Chemistry Practice: p-Block Elements (Group 15-18) - Part 1

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on Group 15-18 p-Block Elements for NEET. Each question has four options, and only one is correct. Select the best answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following elements exhibits the highest electronegativity in Group 15?

A. Nitrogen
B. Phosphorus
C. Arsenic
D. Bismuth
Answer: A

Nitrogen has the highest electronegativity in Group 15 due to its small atomic size and high effective nuclear charge, which strongly attracts bonding electrons. Electronegativity decreases down the group as atomic size increases.

2. The correct statement about the structure of P₄ (white phosphorus) is:

A. It has a tetrahedral structure with 6 P-P bonds.
B. It has a square planar structure with 4 P-P bonds.
C. It has a linear structure with 2 P-P bonds.
D. It has a trigonal planar structure with 3 P-P bonds.
Answer: A

White phosphorus (P₄) has a tetrahedral structure where each phosphorus atom is bonded to three other phosphorus atoms, resulting in 6 P-P bonds. The bond angle is 60°, making it highly strained and reactive.

3. Which of the following is NOT a property of ozone (O₃)?

A. It is a powerful oxidizing agent.
B. It is diamagnetic in nature.
C. It absorbs ultraviolet radiation in the stratosphere.
D. It is a linear molecule.
Answer: D

Ozone (O₃) is a bent molecule with a bond angle of approximately 117°. It is paramagnetic due to the presence of unpaired electrons in its resonance structures, though it is often described as diamagnetic in its ground state. It is a strong oxidizing agent and absorbs UV radiation.

4. The correct order of thermal stability of Group 16 hydrides is:

A. H₂O > H₂S > H₂Se > H₂Te
B. H₂Te > H₂Se > H₂S > H₂O
C. H₂S > H₂O > H₂Se > H₂Te
D. H₂O > H₂Te > H₂Se > H₂S
Answer: A

Thermal stability of Group 16 hydrides decreases down the group due to the weakening of the E-H bond (where E = O, S, Se, Te). H₂O is the most stable, followed by H₂S, H₂Se, and H₂Te.

5. Which of the following noble gases is NOT found in the atmosphere?

A. Helium
B. Neon
C. Radon
D. Argon
Answer: C

Radon is a radioactive noble gas produced by the decay of radium and uranium. It is not present in significant amounts in the atmosphere. Helium, Neon, and Argon are all naturally occurring noble gases in the Earth's atmosphere.

6. The correct statement about the reaction of phosphorus with chlorine is:

A. Phosphorus forms PCl₃ only.
B. Phosphorus forms PCl₅ only.
C. Phosphorus forms both PCl₃ and PCl₅ depending on the amount of chlorine.
D. Phosphorus does not react with chlorine.
Answer: C

Phosphorus reacts with chlorine to form PCl₃ (phosphorus trichloride) when chlorine is limited and PCl₅ (phosphorus pentachloride) when chlorine is in excess. Both are important compounds in phosphorus chemistry.

7. Which of the following interhalogen compounds is T-shaped?

A. ClF₃
B. BrF₅
C. IF₇
D. ICl
Answer: A

ClF₃ (Chlorine trifluoride) has a T-shaped molecular geometry due to 3 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs of electrons around the central chlorine atom, following VSEPR theory. BrF₅ is square pyramidal, IF₇ is pentagonal bipyramidal, and ICl is linear.

8. Which of the following statements about xenon compounds is INCORRECT?

A. XeF₂ is linear in shape.
B. XeF₄ is square planar in shape.
C. XeF₆ is octahedral in shape.
D. XeO₃ is trigonal pyramidal in shape.
Answer: C

XeF₆ (Xenon hexafluoride) has a distorted octahedral structure due to the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the xenon atom. The other statements are correct: XeF₂ is linear, XeF₄ is square planar, and XeO₃ is trigonal pyramidal.

9. The correct statement about the allotropy of sulfur is:

A. Rhombic sulfur is stable below 95.5°C and monoclinic sulfur is stable above this temperature.
B. Monoclinic sulfur is stable below 95.5°C and rhombic sulfur is stable above this temperature.
C. Plastic sulfur is the most stable allotrope at room temperature.
D. Sulfur does not exhibit allotropy.
Answer: A

Sulfur exhibits allotropy with rhombic sulfur (α-sulfur) stable below 95.5°C and monoclinic sulfur (β-sulfur) stable above this temperature. Plastic sulfur is metastable and forms when molten sulfur is rapidly cooled.

10. Nitrogen forms a variety of oxides. Which of the following oxides of nitrogen is paramagnetic?

A. N₂O
B. NO
C. N₂O₃
D. N₂O₅
Answer: B

Nitric oxide (NO) is paramagnetic because it has an unpaired electron in its molecular orbital configuration. The other oxides listed (N₂O, N₂O₃, N₂O₅) are diamagnetic as they do not have unpaired electrons.

11. Which of the following is the correct order of bond angles in the hydrides of Group 16 elements?

A. H₂O > H₂S > H₂Se > H₂Te
B. H₂Te > H₂Se > H₂S > H₂O
C. H₂O > H₂Te > H₂Se > H₂S
D. H₂S > H₂O > H₂Se > H₂Te
Answer: A

The bond angle in the hydrides of Group 16 elements decreases down the group due to decreasing electronegativity and increasing size of the central atom. Thus, the order is H₂O (104.5°) > H₂S (92.1°) > H₂Se (91°) > H₂Te (89.5°).

12. In the contact process for the manufacture of sulfuric acid, which of the following catalysts is used?

A. Iron
B. Platinum
C. Vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅)
D. Nickel
Answer: C

Vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅) is the catalyst used in the contact process for the oxidation of sulfur dioxide (SO₂) to sulfur trioxide (SO₃), which is a key step in the manufacture of sulfuric acid.

13. Which of the following noble gases is used in radiotherapy for cancer treatment?

A. Helium
B. Neon
C. Argon
D. Radon
Answer: D

Radon is a radioactive noble gas and is used in radiotherapy for cancer treatment due to its ability to emit alpha particles, which can target cancer cells.

14. Which of the following is the correct explanation for the inertness of noble gases?

A. Noble gases have a complete octet and high ionization enthalpy, making them chemically inert.
B. Noble gases have very low electron affinity, which prevents them from forming compounds.
C. Noble gases exist as monoatomic gases due to strong intermolecular forces.
D. Noble gases have the smallest atomic radii in their respective periods.
Answer: A

Noble gases are chemically inert because they have a complete octet of electrons in their valence shell, resulting in high ionization enthalpy and very low reactivity. While they do have low electron affinity, the primary reason for their inertness is their stable electronic configuration.

15. Which of the following reactions correctly represents the preparation of phosphine (PH₃)?

A. Ca₃P₂ + 6H₂O → 3Ca(OH)₂ + 2PH₃
B. P₄ + 3NaOH + 3H₂O → 3NaH₂PO₂ + PH₃
C. 2P + 3H₂ → 2PH₃
D. PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl
Answer: A

Phosphine (PH₃) is prepared by the hydrolysis of metal phosphides such as calcium phosphide (Ca₃P₂) with water: Ca₃P₂ + 6H₂O → 3Ca(OH)₂ + 2PH₃. The other reactions do not produce PH₃ as the primary product.

16. Which of the following is the correct order of bond dissociation enthalpy for the halogens?

A. F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂
B. Cl₂ > Br₂ > F₂ > I₂
C. I₂ > Br₂ > Cl₂ > F₂
D. Br₂ > Cl₂ > F₂ > I₂
Answer: B

The bond dissociation enthalpy of halogens decreases down the group due to increasing atomic size and weaker bond strength. However, F₂ has a lower bond dissociation enthalpy than Cl₂ due to repulsion between lone pairs of electrons on the small fluorine atoms. Thus, the correct order is Cl₂ > Br₂ > F₂ > I₂.

17. Which of the following statements about the oxides of nitrogen is incorrect?

A. Nitrous oxide (N₂O) is a linear molecule and is used as an anesthetic.
B. Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) is a brown gas and exists in equilibrium with its dimer N₂O₄.
C. Dinitrogen pentoxide (N₂O₅) is the anhydride of nitric acid (HNO₃).
D. Nitric oxide (NO) is a diamagnetic molecule and is highly soluble in water.
Answer: D

Nitric oxide (NO) is a paramagnetic molecule due to the presence of an unpaired electron, not diamagnetic. It is also relatively insoluble in water. The other statements about the oxides of nitrogen are correct.
