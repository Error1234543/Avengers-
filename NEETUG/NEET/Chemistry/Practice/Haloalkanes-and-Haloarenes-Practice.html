1. Which of the following statements correctly describes the bond polarity in a carbon-halogen bond in haloalkanes?

A. The carbon-halogen bond is non-polar because the electronegativity difference is negligible.
B. The carbon-halogen bond is polar, with the halogen atom carrying a partial negative charge.
C. The carbon-halogen bond is polar, with the carbon atom carrying a partial negative charge.
D. The carbon-halogen bond polarity decreases from fluorine to iodine due to increasing bond length.
Answer: B

In haloalkanes, the carbon-halogen bond is polar due to the higher electronegativity of the halogen atom compared to carbon. This results in the halogen atom carrying a partial negative charge (δ⁻) and the carbon atom carrying a partial positive charge (δ⁺). The polarity decreases from fluorine to iodine because the electronegativity difference between carbon and the halogen decreases down the group, despite the increasing bond length.

2. The reaction of 2-bromobutane with alcoholic KOH primarily yields:

A. Butan-1-ol
B. Butan-2-ol
C. But-1-ene
D. But-2-ene
Answer: D

When 2-bromobutane is treated with alcoholic KOH, it undergoes an elimination reaction (dehydrohalogenation) to form alkenes. The major product is but-2-ene due to the formation of the more stable alkene (Saytzeff's rule), where the double bond is formed between the carbon atoms that originally held the bromine and a hydrogen atom from the adjacent carbon.

3. Which of the following haloalkanes will react fastest in an Sₙ2 reaction?

A. 1-Chlorobutane
B. 2-Chlorobutane
C. 2-Chloro-2-methylpropane
D. 1-Chloro-2,2-dimethylpropane
Answer: A

Sₙ2 reactions are favored by primary haloalkanes because the backside attack by the nucleophile is less sterically hindered. Among the options, 1-chlorobutane is a primary haloalkane, making it the fastest to react in an Sₙ2 reaction. The other options are secondary or tertiary haloalkanes, which are less reactive in Sₙ2 due to steric hindrance.

4. The correct order of reactivity of the following halides towards Sₙ1 reaction is: (I) CH₃CH₂Br, (II) (CH₃)₃CBr, (III) CH₃CH(Br)CH₃

A. I < II < III
B. II < III < I
C. III < I < II
D. II < I < III
Answer: C

Sₙ1 reactions proceed via the formation of a carbocation intermediate. The stability of the carbocation determines the reactivity: tertiary > secondary > primary. Thus, (II) (CH₃)₃CBr (tertiary) is the most reactive, followed by (III) CH₃CH(Br)CH₃ (secondary), and (I) CH₃CH₂Br (primary) is the least reactive.

5. Which of the following is the major product when chlorobenzene is treated with chlorine in the presence of FeCl₃?

A. o-Dichlorobenzene
B. p-Dichlorobenzene
C. m-Dichlorobenzene
D. A mixture of o- and p-dichlorobenzene
Answer: D

Chlorination of chlorobenzene in the presence of FeCl₃ (a Lewis acid catalyst) is an electrophilic aromatic substitution reaction. The chlorine substituent is ortho/para directing, so the major products are a mixture of o-dichlorobenzene and p-dichlorobenzene. Meta substitution does not occur due to the directing effect of the chlorine atom.

6. The reaction of bromoethane with aqueous NaOH follows which mechanism?

A. Sₙ1 only
B. Sₙ2 only
C. Both Sₙ1 and Sₙ2
D. E2 only
Answer: B

Bromoethane (a primary haloalkane) reacts with aqueous NaOH via an Sₙ2 mechanism. In Sₙ2, the nucleophile (OH⁻) attacks the carbon bearing the bromine from the backside, leading to a concerted substitution reaction. Sₙ1 is unlikely for primary haloalkanes due to the instability of primary carbocations, and E2 requires a strong base in an alcoholic medium.

7. Which of the following compounds will exhibit optical isomerism?

A. 1,1-Dibromopropane
B. 2-Bromobutane
C. 1-Bromo-2-methylpropane
D. Bromobenzene
Answer: B

Optical isomerism occurs in compounds that have a chiral center (a carbon atom bonded to four different groups). 2-Bromobutane has a chiral carbon (the second carbon atom bonded to H, Br, CH₃, and C₂H₅), making it optically active. The other options either lack a chiral center or have symmetry.

8. The correct IUPAC name for the compound CH₃-CH₂-CH(Cl)-CH₂-CH₃ is:

A. 2-Chloropentane
B. 3-Chloropentane
C. 1-Chloro-2-ethylpropane
D. 2-Chloro-3-methylbutane
Answer: A

The given compound is CH₃-CH₂-CH(Cl)-CH₂-CH₃. The longest carbon chain is 5 carbons (pentane), and the chlorine atom is attached to the second carbon. Thus, the correct IUPAC name is 2-chloropentane.

9. Which of the following reagents is used to convert bromoethane to ethene?

A. Aqueous NaOH
B. Alcoholic KOH
C. Zn/HCl
D. Na in dry ether
Answer: B

Bromoethane can be converted to ethene via an elimination reaction (dehydrohalogenation). Alcoholic KOH is a strong base that promotes elimination (E2 mechanism) to form ethene. Aqueous NaOH would lead to substitution (ethanol), Zn/HCl is used for reduction, and Na in dry ether is used for Wurtz reaction (coupling).

10. The Fittig reaction involves the coupling of:

A. Two alkyl halides in the presence of Na in dry ether
B. An alkyl halide and an aryl halide in the presence of Na in dry ether
C. Two aryl halides in the presence of Na in dry ether
D. An alkyl halide with benzene in the presence of AlCl₃
Answer: C

The Fittig reaction is a coupling reaction where two aryl halides react in the presence of sodium metal in dry ether to form a biaryl compound. The Wurtz reaction involves two alkyl halides, and the Friedel-Crafts reaction involves an alkyl halide with benzene in the presence of AlCl₃.

11. Which of the following statements about the boiling points of haloalkanes is correct?

A. Boiling points decrease with increasing molecular mass due to decreased polarity.
B. Boiling points increase with increasing molecular mass and surface area.
C. Boiling points are independent of the halogen atom present.
D. Boiling points are higher for branched haloalkanes compared to their straight-chain isomers.
Answer: B

The boiling points of haloalkanes increase with increasing molecular mass and surface area due to stronger van der Waals forces. For a given alkyl group, the boiling point increases with the size of the halogen (I > Br > Cl > F). Branched haloalkanes have lower boiling points than their straight-chain isomers due to reduced surface area and weaker van der Waals forces.

12. The reaction of chlorobenzene with chloral in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄ yields:

A. DDT
B. BHC
C. Gammexane
D. Chloropicrin
Answer: A

The reaction of chlorobenzene with chloral (trichloroacetaldehyde) in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄ yields DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane), an insecticide. This is an example of an electrophilic aromatic substitution reaction where chloral acts as the electrophile.

13. Which of the following is the correct order of dipole moments for the given haloalkanes: (I) CH₃Cl, (II) CH₃Br, (III) CH₃I?

A. I > II > III
B. III > II > I
C. II > I > III
D. I = II = III
Answer: A

The dipole moment of a haloalkane depends on the electronegativity difference between carbon and the halogen, as well as the bond length. Although bond length increases from Cl to I, the electronegativity difference decreases more significantly, leading to a decrease in dipole moment. Thus, the order is CH₃Cl > CH₃Br > CH₃I.

14. Which of the following reagents is most suitable for the conversion of 2-bromobutane to but-2-ene under E2 elimination conditions?

A. Alcoholic KOH
B. Aqueous KOH
C. Zn/HCl
D. LiAlH4
Answer: A

E2 elimination requires a strong base in an alcoholic solvent to favor elimination over substitution. Alcoholic KOH provides the necessary basic conditions (OH⁻) in ethanol, promoting the formation of the alkene (but-2-ene) by removing HBr. Aqueous KOH favors substitution (forming an alcohol), while Zn/HCl and LiAlH4 are reducing agents, not bases for elimination.

15. The reaction of chlorobenzene with sodium hydroxide at 300°C and high pressure yields:

A. Phenol
B. Benzyl alcohol
C. Anisole
D. Benzene
Answer: A

Chlorobenzene undergoes nucleophilic aromatic substitution with NaOH at high temperature (300°C) and pressure to form phenol. This is a Dow process, where the hydroxide ion replaces the chlorine atom. Benzyl alcohol, anisole, and benzene are not formed under these conditions.

16. The correct order of reactivity of the following halides towards SN2 displacement is: (I) CH3Cl, (II) (CH3)2CHCl, (III) (CH3)3CCl, (IV) C6H5Cl

A. I > II > III > IV
B. IV > III > II > I
C. I > IV > II > III
D. III > II > I > IV
Answer: A

SN2 reactivity decreases with steric hindrance: methyl (I) > primary > secondary (II) > tertiary (III). Aryl halides like C6H5Cl (IV) are unreactive in SN2 due to the sp² hybridized carbon and resonance stabilization. Thus, the order is I > II > III > IV.

17. The major product formed when 2-bromopentane is treated with ethanolic silver nitrate is:

A. Pent-1-ene
B. Pent-2-ene
C. A mixture of pent-1-ene and pent-2-ene with pent-2-ene as the major product
D. Pentan-2-ol
Answer: C

Ethanolic silver nitrate promotes E2 elimination, favoring the more stable alkene (Zaitsev's rule). Pent-2-ene (more substituted) is the major product, with pent-1-ene as the minor product. Pentan-2-ol (substitution) is not formed under these conditions.

18. The correct order of boiling points for the following compounds is: (I) CH3CH2Cl, (II) CH3CH2Br, (III) CH3CH2I, (IV) CH3CH3

A. IV < I < II < III
B. I < II < III < IV
C. IV < III < II < I
D. III < II < I < IV
Answer: A

Boiling points increase with molecular weight and polarity. Ethane (IV) has the lowest boiling point (nonpolar), followed by chloroethane (I), bromoethane (II), and iodoethane (III), which has the highest due to its larger size and greater van der Waals forces.

19. The major product formed in the reaction of 1-chlorobutane with sodium iodide in acetone is:

A. 1-Iodobutane via SN2 mechanism
B. But-1-ene via E2 mechanism
C. Butan-1-ol via SN1 mechanism
D. 2-Iodobutane via SN1 mechanism
Answer: A

Sodium iodide in acetone (Finkelstein reaction) promotes SN2 substitution of chloride by iodide, forming 1-iodobutane. E2 elimination (option B) is not favored here, and SN1 (options C/D) is unlikely for a primary halide like 1-chlorobutane.
