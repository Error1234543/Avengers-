NEET Chemistry Practice - States of Matter (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic 'States of Matter' for NEET. Each question has four options, and only one is correct. Select the best answer for each. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. At what temperature will the average kinetic energy of gas molecules be exactly half of its value at 27°C?

A. -123°C
B. 0°C
C. 13.5°C
D. 150°C
Answer: A

The average kinetic energy of gas molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature (Kelvin scale). Given temperature T₁ = 27°C = 300 K. Let T₂ be the temperature at which the kinetic energy is half. Then, (KE)₂ = ½ (KE)₁ ⇒ T₂ = ½ T₁ = ½ × 300 K = 150 K. Converting 150 K to Celsius: 150 - 273 = -123°C.

2. Which of the following conditions favors the liquefaction of a gas?

A. High temperature and low pressure.
B. Low temperature and high pressure.
C. High temperature and high pressure.
D. Low temperature and low pressure.
Answer: B

Liquefaction of a gas occurs when the gas molecules are brought close enough for intermolecular forces to dominate. This is favored by low temperatures (which reduce the kinetic energy of molecules) and high pressures (which compress the molecules into a smaller volume). Thus, low temperature and high pressure favor liquefaction.

3. At constant temperature, the pressure of a gas is doubled. What happens to its volume according to Boyle's Law?

A. Volume is halved
B. Volume is doubled
C. Volume remains unchanged
D. Volume becomes four times
Answer: A

Boyle's Law states that for a given mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure of the gas is inversely proportional to its volume (P ∝ 1/V). If pressure is doubled, volume must be halved to maintain the relationship.

4. The root mean square speed (u_rms) of gas molecules is directly proportional to:

A. Square root of absolute temperature (√T)
B. Absolute temperature (T)
C. Inverse of absolute temperature (1/T)
D. Square of absolute temperature (T²)
Answer: A

The root mean square speed (u_rms) of gas molecules is given by the equation u_rms = √(3RT/M), where R is the gas constant, T is the absolute temperature, and M is the molar mass. Thus, u_rms is directly proportional to the square root of absolute temperature (√T).

5. A gas at 2 atm pressure occupies 10 liters. If the pressure is increased to 5 atm at constant temperature, what will be the new volume?

A. 2 liters
B. 4 liters
C. 5 liters
D. 25 liters
Answer: B

Using Boyle's Law (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂), V₂ = (P₁V₁) / P₂ = (2 atm × 10 L) / 5 atm = 4 liters.

6. A mixture of gases contains 2 moles of oxygen (O₂), 3 moles of nitrogen (N₂), and 5 moles of carbon dioxide (CO₂). If the total pressure of the mixture is 10 atm, what is the partial pressure of nitrogen (N₂)?

A. 2 atm
B. 3 atm
C. 5 atm
D. 6 atm
Answer: B

The partial pressure of a gas in a mixture is given by P_gas = (moles of gas / total moles) × total pressure. Total moles = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 moles. Partial pressure of N₂ = (3 / 10) × 10 atm = 3 atm.
