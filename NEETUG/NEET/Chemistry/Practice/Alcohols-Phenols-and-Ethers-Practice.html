NEET Chemistry Practice: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers (Part 1 of 25)

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. The compound obtained by the reaction of phenol with chloroform in the presence of sodium hydroxide is:

A. Salicylaldehyde
B. Benzaldehyde
C. Salicylic acid
D. Chlorobenzene
Answer: A

This is the Reimer-Tiemann reaction, where phenol reacts with chloroform in alkaline medium to form salicylaldehyde as the major product.

2. Which of the following statements about the acidity of phenols is correct?

A. Phenol is more acidic than ethanol due to the stability of the phenoxide ion by resonance.
B. Phenol is less acidic than ethanol because the phenoxide ion is unstable.
C. Phenol and ethanol have similar acidity as both contain hydroxyl groups.
D. Phenol does not exhibit acidic properties.
Answer: A

Phenol is more acidic than ethanol because the phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance, delocalizing the negative charge over the benzene ring, whereas the ethoxide ion lacks such stabilization.

3. Ethers are generally less reactive than alcohols and alkenes because:

A. They lack a hydroxyl group.
B. The C-O-C bond is highly polar and strong.
C. They do not have a π-bond or active hydrogen.
D. They are insoluble in water.
Answer: C

Ethers are relatively inert because they lack a π-bond (unlike alkenes) and do not have an active hydrogen (unlike alcohols), making them less prone to nucleophilic or electrophilic attacks.

4. The oxidation of a secondary alcohol with acidified potassium dichromate yields:

A. A carboxylic acid
B. An aldehyde
C. A ketone
D. An alkene
Answer: C

Secondary alcohols are oxidized to ketones by acidified potassium dichromate. Primary alcohols yield aldehydes or carboxylic acids, while tertiary alcohols do not undergo oxidation under these conditions.

5. The reaction of tert-butyl methyl ether with HI results in the formation of:

A. tert-Butyl alcohol and methyl iodide
B. tert-Butyl iodide and methanol
C. Isobutylene and methanol
D. tert-Butyl alcohol and methane
Answer: B

In the cleavage of ethers by HI, the bond breaks to form the more stable carbocation. tert-Butyl methyl ether yields tert-butyl iodide (from the tertiary carbocation) and methanol.

6. Which of the following is used as an antiseptic and is also known as carbolic acid?

A. Ethanol
B. Phenol
C. Methanol
D. Glycerol
Answer: B

Phenol, also known as carbolic acid, is widely used as an antiseptic and disinfectant due to its ability to denature proteins and disrupt cell membranes.

7. Which of the following compounds will give a positive iodoform test?

A. Ethanol
B. Methanol
C. Propan-2-ol
D. Both A and C
Answer: D

The iodoform test is positive for compounds containing the CH₃CH(OH)- group or ethanol. Ethanol and propan-2-ol (isopropyl alcohol) both give a positive iodoform test, yielding a yellow precipitate of iodoform (CHI₃).

8. The compound formed by the dehydration of ethanol at 170°C in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄ is:

A. Ethene
B. Diethyl ether
C. Ethanal
D. Ethanoic acid
Answer: A

At 170°C, ethanol undergoes intramolecular dehydration to form ethene. At 140°C, intermolecular dehydration occurs, yielding diethyl ether.

9. Which of the following statements about phenols is incorrect?

A. Phenols are more acidic than alcohols due to resonance stabilization of the phenoxide ion.
B. Phenols undergo electrophilic aromatic substitution more readily than benzene.
C. Phenols do not react with sodium bicarbonate to evolve CO₂.
D. Phenols are less soluble in water than alcohols due to their larger hydrophobic ring.
Answer: D

Phenols are more soluble in water than alcohols of comparable molecular weight due to their ability to form stronger hydrogen bonds with water. The other statements are correct.

10. The compound that gives a violet coloration with neutral FeCl₃ solution is:

A. Ethanol
B. Phenol
C. Diethyl ether
D. Benzyl alcohol
Answer: B

Phenol reacts with neutral FeCl₃ solution to form a violet-colored complex due to the presence of the phenolic hydroxyl group, which is a characteristic test for phenols.

11. In the reaction of phenol with bromine water, the product formed is:

A. 2-Bromophenol
B. 3-Bromophenol
C. 2,4,6-Tribromophenol
D. Benzyl bromide
Answer: C

Phenol undergoes bromination with bromine water to form 2,4,6-tribromophenol, a white precipitate, due to the high activating effect of the hydroxyl group on the benzene ring.

12. Which of the following methods is used for the preparation of diethyl ether?

A. Dehydration of ethanol at 140°C in the presence of H₂SO₄
B. Reaction of ethyl bromide with sodium ethoxide
C. Oxidation of ethanol with KMnO₄
D. Reduction of acetaldehyde with LiAlH₄
Answer: A

Diethyl ether is prepared by the dehydration of ethanol at 140°C in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄. This is an intermolecular dehydration reaction that forms ethers.

13. Which of the following statements about the Williamson ether synthesis is INCORRECT?

A. It involves the reaction of an alkoxide ion with an alkyl halide.
B. It is an SN2 reaction.
C. Tertiary alkyl halides are preferred for better yields.
D. It can be used to prepare both symmetrical and unsymmetrical ethers.
Answer: C

Tertiary alkyl halides are not preferred in Williamson ether synthesis because they undergo elimination (E2) reactions instead of substitution (SN2), leading to poor yields of ethers.

14. The product formed when phenol is treated with chloroform and aqueous NaOH is:

A. Salicylaldehyde
B. Benzaldehyde
C. Benzoic acid
D. o-Chlorophenol
Answer: A

The reaction of phenol with chloroform and aqueous NaOH is known as the Reimer-Tiemann reaction, which introduces an aldehyde group (-CHO) ortho to the hydroxyl group, forming salicylaldehyde.

15. Which of the following is the major product when phenol is treated with dilute HNO₃ at low temperature?

A. o-Nitrophenol
B. p-Nitrophenol
C. 2,4-Dinitrophenol
D. m-Nitrophenol
Answer: A

When phenol is treated with dilute HNO₃ at low temperature, the major product is o-nitrophenol due to the ortho-directing effect of the hydroxyl group. However, p-nitrophenol is also formed as a minor product.

16. Which of the following statements correctly explains why phenols are more acidic than alcohols?

A. The phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance, whereas the alkoxide ion is not.
B. Phenols have a higher molecular weight, which increases their acidity.
C. Alcohols form stronger hydrogen bonds, making them less likely to donate protons.
D. The carbon-oxygen bond in phenols is weaker than in alcohols, facilitating proton release.
Answer: A

Phenols are more acidic than alcohols because the phenoxide ion (the conjugate base of phenol) is stabilized by resonance. The negative charge on the oxygen atom is delocalized over the benzene ring, which increases the stability of the phenoxide ion. In contrast, the alkoxide ion (the conjugate base of an alcohol) does not have such resonance stabilization, making it less stable and thus alcohols less acidic. The other options are incorrect: molecular weight does not significantly affect acidity (B), hydrogen bonding does not directly explain acidity differences (C), and the carbon-oxygen bond strength is not the primary factor (D).

17. In the reaction sequence below, identify the product **Z**: Ethanol → (PBr₃) **X** → (Mg, dry ether) **Y** → (Ethylene oxide, H₃O⁺) **Z**

A. 1-Butanol
B. 2-Butanol
C. 1-Propanol
D. 2-Propanol
Answer: A

The reaction sequence involves the following steps: 1. Ethanol reacts with PBr₃ to form bromoethane (**X**). 2. Bromoethane reacts with magnesium in dry ether to form ethylmagnesium bromide (**Y**), a Grignard reagent. 3. The Grignard reagent reacts with ethylene oxide, followed by hydrolysis with H₃O⁺, to form **1-Butanol (Z)**. This is a classic example of the Grignard reaction with ethylene oxide, which extends the carbon chain by two carbons and results in a primary alcohol.
