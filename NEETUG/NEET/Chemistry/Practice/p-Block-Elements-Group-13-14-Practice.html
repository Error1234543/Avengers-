NEET Chemistry Practice: p-Block Elements (Group 13 & 14) - Part 1

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on p-Block Elements (Group 13 & 14) for NEET. Each question has four options, only one of which is correct. Select the correct answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following elements of Group 13 exhibits the most stable +1 oxidation state due to the inert pair effect?

A. Boron (B)
B. Aluminium (Al)
C. Gallium (Ga)
D. Thallium (Tl)
Answer: D

Thallium (Tl) exhibits the most stable +1 oxidation state in Group 13 due to the inert pair effect, where the s-electrons in the outermost shell (6s²) are reluctant to participate in bonding. This effect becomes more pronounced down the group, making Tl⁺ more stable than Tl³⁺.

2. The compound formed by boron with nitrogen (BN) is isoelectronic with which of the following?

A. Carbon (C)
B. Carbon monoxide (CO)
C. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
D. Graphite
Answer: D

Boron nitride (BN) is isoelectronic with graphite because both have the same number of valence electrons (12) and similar layered structures. BN exists in hexagonal and cubic forms, analogous to graphite and diamond, respectively.

3. Which of the following statements about aluminium chloride (AlCl₃) is correct?

A. It exists as a dimer (Al₂Cl₆) in the solid state due to coordinate bonding.
B. It is a strong Lewis base due to the presence of lone pairs on chlorine.
C. It dissociates completely into Al³⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in aqueous solution.
D. It forms a covalent network solid similar to silicon dioxide (SiO₂).
Answer: A

Aluminium chloride (AlCl₃) exists as a dimer (Al₂Cl₆) in the solid and vapor states due to coordinate bonding between chlorine atoms and aluminium. This dimerization satisfies the electron deficiency of aluminium by forming two coordinate bonds.

4. The correct order of acidic strength of the oxides of Group 13 elements is:

A. B₂O₃ > Al₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > In₂O₃ > Tl₂O₃
B. Tl₂O₃ > In₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > Al₂O₃ > B₂O₃
C. Al₂O₃ > B₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > In₂O₃ > Tl₂O₃
D. B₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > Al₂O₃ > In₂O₃ > Tl₂O₃
Answer: A

The acidic strength of the oxides of Group 13 elements decreases down the group due to the increasing metallic character. Boron oxide (B₂O₃) is the most acidic, while thallium oxide (Tl₂O₃) is the least acidic (or more basic). The order is B₂O₃ > Al₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > In₂O₃ > Tl₂O₃.

5. Which of the following allotropes of carbon is thermodynamically the most stable under standard conditions?

A. Diamond
B. Graphite
C. Fullerene (C₆₀)
D. Carbon nanotubes
Answer: B

Graphite is the thermodynamically most stable allotrope of carbon under standard conditions. It has a layered structure with delocalized π-electrons, making it more stable than diamond, fullerenes, or carbon nanotubes.

6. The hybridization of carbon in carborundum (silicon carbide, SiC) is:

A. sp
B. sp²
C. sp³
D. dsp²
Answer: C

In carborundum (SiC), carbon is sp³ hybridized. Silicon carbide has a diamond-like structure where each carbon atom is tetrahedrally bonded to four silicon atoms (and vice versa), resulting in sp³ hybridization.

7. The compound used as a reducing agent in the extraction of lead from its ore is:

A. Carbon monoxide (CO)
B. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
C. Silicon dioxide (SiO₂)
D. Boron carbide (B₄C)
Answer: A

Carbon monoxide (CO) is used as a reducing agent in the extraction of lead from its ore (e.g., galena, PbS). The reaction is: PbO + CO → Pb + CO₂. CO is a strong reducing agent at high temperatures.

8. The correct statement about the reaction of aluminium with sodium hydroxide is:

A. Aluminium dissolves to form Al³⁺ ions and hydrogen gas.
B. Aluminium dissolves to form [Al(OH)₄]⁻ and hydrogen gas.
C. Aluminium does not react with sodium hydroxide.
D. Aluminium forms a passive layer of Al₂O₃ and stops reacting.
Answer: B

Aluminium reacts with sodium hydroxide to form sodium aluminate ([Al(OH)₄]⁻) and hydrogen gas. The reaction is: 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + 3H₂. This reaction demonstrates the amphoteric nature of aluminium.

9. Aluminum chloride exists as a dimer (Al₂Cl₆) in the vapor phase. Which of the following best explains this behavior?

A. Aluminum achieves a stable octet configuration by forming coordinate bonds with chlorine atoms.
B. Aluminum chloride is highly ionic and forms a lattice structure in the vapor phase.
C. The dimerization is driven by the formation of hydrogen bonds between chlorine atoms.
D. Aluminum chloride undergoes redox reactions to form the dimer.
Answer: A

Aluminum chloride forms a dimer (Al₂Cl₆) in the vapor phase because aluminum, with only six electrons in its valence shell, achieves a stable octet by forming coordinate bonds with chlorine atoms from another AlCl₃ molecule.

10. Which of the following reactions is used for the extraction of boron?

A. Reduction of B₂O₃ with magnesium.
B. Electrolysis of molten borax.
C. Oxidation of boric acid with chlorine.
D. Thermal decomposition of boron trichloride.
Answer: A

Boron is extracted by reducing boron oxide (B₂O₃) with magnesium. This reaction produces amorphous boron, which can be further purified. The other methods listed are not standard for boron extraction.

11. The compound which is used as a flux in metallurgy and is also a component of borosilicate glass is:

A. Borax
B. Boric acid
C. Aluminum oxide
D. Silicon carbide
Answer: A

Borax (Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O) is used as a flux in metallurgy to remove oxides and other impurities. It is also a key component in the manufacture of borosilicate glass, which is heat-resistant and durable.

12. The element that does NOT exhibit catenation to a significant extent is:

A. Carbon
B. Silicon
C. Germanium
D. Lead
Answer: D

Catenation is the ability of an element to form bonds with itself, leading to the formation of chains or rings. Carbon exhibits the highest degree of catenation, followed by silicon and germanium. Lead, being the heaviest element in Group 14, shows negligible catenation due to weak Pb-Pb bonds.

13. In the Hall-Héroult process for the extraction of aluminum, cryolite is added to:

A. Increase the melting point of alumina.
B. Decrease the melting point of alumina and improve conductivity.
C. Act as a reducing agent for alumina.
D. Prevent the formation of aluminum carbide.
Answer: B

In the Hall-Héroult process, cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) is added to alumina (Al₂O₃) to lower its melting point from ~2000°C to ~950°C, making the electrolysis process more energy-efficient. It also improves the electrical conductivity of the melt.

14. Which of the following statements correctly explains the trend in the stability of +2 oxidation state down Group 14?

A. The stability of the +2 oxidation state increases down the group due to the inert pair effect, where the s-electrons become more reluctant to participate in bonding.
B. The stability of the +2 oxidation state decreases down the group because the atomic size increases, making it harder to lose electrons.
C. The +2 oxidation state is equally stable across all Group 14 elements due to similar electronic configurations.
D. The stability of the +2 oxidation state increases down the group because the electronegativity of the elements increases, favoring the loss of fewer electrons.
Answer: A

The stability of the +2 oxidation state in Group 14 elements increases as we move down the group due to the **inert pair effect**. This effect describes the reluctance of the two electrons in the outermost s-orbital (ns²) to participate in bonding. As the atomic size increases down the group, the s-electrons are held more tightly by the nucleus, making them less available for bonding. This results in the +2 oxidation state becoming more stable relative to the +4 oxidation state. For example, lead (Pb) predominantly exhibits the +2 oxidation state, while carbon (C) and silicon (Si) favor the +4 state. The other options are incorrect because they either misrepresent the trend or the underlying reason.
