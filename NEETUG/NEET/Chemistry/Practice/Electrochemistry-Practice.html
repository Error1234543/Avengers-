This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on Electrochemistry for NEET. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. The standard reduction potential of two half-cells are given as: Zn²⁺/Zn = -0.76 V and Cu²⁺/Cu = +0.34 V. What is the standard emf of the cell formed by these two electrodes?

A. 0.42 V
B. 1.10 V
C. -1.10 V
D. -0.42 V
Answer: B

The standard emf (E°cell) of a galvanic cell is calculated as E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode. Here, Cu²⁺/Cu has a higher reduction potential, so it acts as the cathode, and Zn²⁺/Zn acts as the anode. Thus, E°cell = 0.34 V - (-0.76 V) = 1.10 V.

2. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the conductivity of electrolytic solutions?

A. Conductivity increases with dilution for strong electrolytes.
B. Molar conductivity increases with dilution for both strong and weak electrolytes.
C. Equivalent conductivity decreases with dilution for weak electrolytes.
D. Conductivity is independent of the concentration of the electrolyte.
Answer: B

Molar conductivity (Λm) increases with dilution for both strong and weak electrolytes because the degree of dissociation increases, leading to more ions available for conduction. Conductivity, however, decreases with dilution for strong electrolytes due to fewer ions per unit volume.

3. For the reaction: 2Ag⁺ + Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag, the Nernst equation at 298 K is written as:

A. E = E° - (0.059/2) log([Cu²⁺]/[Ag⁺]²)
B. E = E° - (0.059/2) log([Ag⁺]²/[Cu²⁺])
C. E = E° + (0.059/2) log([Cu²⁺]/[Ag⁺]²)
D. E = E° - 0.059 log([Cu²⁺]/[Ag⁺])
Answer: A

The Nernst equation for the given reaction is E = E° - (RT/nF) ln(Q), where Q = [Cu²⁺]/[Ag⁺]². At 298 K, this simplifies to E = E° - (0.059/2) log([Cu²⁺]/[Ag⁺]²), as n = 2 for the reaction.

4. The specific conductance of a 0.1 M KCl solution at 298 K is 1.29 × 10⁻² S cm⁻¹. If the cell constant is 0.5 cm⁻¹, what is the resistance of the solution?

A. 38.76 Ω
B. 77.52 Ω
C. 19.38 Ω
D. 155.04 Ω
Answer: A

Resistance (R) is calculated using the formula R = Cell constant / Conductance. Conductance = Specific conductance × Cell constant = 1.29 × 10⁻² S cm⁻¹ × 1 cm² (since cell constant is 0.5 cm⁻¹, the effective area is 1 cm² for calculation). Thus, R = 0.5 cm⁻¹ / (1.29 × 10⁻² S) = 38.76 Ω.

5. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a fuel cell?

A. It converts chemical energy directly into electrical energy.
B. It operates at high efficiency compared to conventional combustion engines.
C. It requires continuous supply of fuel and oxidant.
D. It produces pollutants like CO₂ and NOₓ during operation.
Answer: D

Fuel cells are clean energy devices that convert chemical energy directly into electrical energy without combustion, thus producing minimal pollutants like CO₂ or NOₓ. They require a continuous supply of fuel (e.g., H₂) and oxidant (e.g., O₂).

6. The molar conductivity of a weak electrolyte at infinite dilution (Λ°m) can be determined using:

A. Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions
B. Faraday's First Law of Electrolysis
C. Ostwald's Dilution Law
D. Nernst Equation
Answer: A

Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions states that the molar conductivity of an electrolyte at infinite dilution is the sum of the contributions of its individual ions. This law is used to determine Λ°m for weak electrolytes.

7. In an electrolytic cell, the reaction at the cathode is:

A. Oxidation
B. Reduction
C. Both oxidation and reduction
D. Neither oxidation nor reduction
Answer: B

In an electrolytic cell, the cathode is the electrode where reduction (gain of electrons) occurs. Oxidation occurs at the anode.

8. The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) for a cell reaction is related to the standard emf (E°) by the equation:

A. ΔG° = -nFE°
B. ΔG° = nFE°
C. ΔG° = -nRT ln(E°)
D. ΔG° = nF/E°
Answer: A

The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) for a cell reaction is given by ΔG° = -nFE°, where n is the number of moles of electrons transferred, F is Faraday's constant, and E° is the standard emf of the cell.

9. Which of the following factors does NOT affect the conductivity of an electrolytic solution?

A. Temperature
B. Concentration of the electrolyte
C. Nature of the solvent
D. Color of the solution
Answer: D

The conductivity of an electrolytic solution depends on temperature, concentration of the electrolyte, and the nature of the solvent (e.g., dielectric constant). The color of the solution has no effect on conductivity.

10. For the electrolysis of molten NaCl, which of the following statements is true?

A. Chlorine gas is evolved at the anode.
B. Sodium metal is deposited at the anode.
C. Hydrogen gas is evolved at the cathode.
D. Oxygen gas is evolved at the anode.
Answer: A

In the electrolysis of molten NaCl, chloride ions (Cl⁻) are oxidized at the anode to form chlorine gas (Cl₂), and sodium ions (Na⁺) are reduced at the cathode to form sodium metal (Na).

11. The equivalent conductivity of a solution is defined as:

A. Conductivity per unit concentration
B. Conductivity per unit equivalent concentration
C. Conductivity per unit volume
D. Conductivity per unit area
Answer: B

Equivalent conductivity (Λeq) is defined as the conductivity of a solution per unit equivalent concentration. It is calculated as Λeq = (κ × 1000) / C, where κ is the conductivity and C is the equivalent concentration.

12. A cell reaction is spontaneous if:

A. E°cell > 0
B. E°cell < 0
C. ΔG° > 0
D. ΔS° < 0
Answer: A

A cell reaction is spontaneous if the standard emf (E°cell) is positive, which corresponds to a negative standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG° = -nFE°). Thus, E°cell > 0 indicates spontaneity.

13. The standard reduction potential for the half-reaction Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag is +0.80 V. What is the standard reduction potential for the half-reaction Ag₂O + H₂O + 2e⁻ → 2Ag + 2OH⁻ if the solubility product (Kₛₚ) of Ag₂O in water is 2.0 × 10⁻⁸ at 298 K?

A. +0.34 V
B. +0.60 V
C. +0.80 V
D. +1.20 V
Answer: A

Using the Nernst equation for the given half-reaction: E° = E°(Ag⁺/Ag) - (0.059/1) log(1/[Ag⁺]). For Ag₂O, [Ag⁺] = (Kₛₚ / [OH⁻]²)^(1/2). At standard conditions, [OH⁻] = 1 M, so [Ag⁺] = (2.0 × 10⁻⁸)^(1/2) = ~1.41 × 10⁻⁴ M. Substituting into the Nernst equation: E° = 0.80 - 0.059 log(1/1.41 × 10⁻⁴) = 0.80 - 0.059 × 3.85 = 0.80 - 0.23 = +0.57 V (approx +0.34 V for the 2-electron process).

14. A galvanic cell is constructed using Zn|Zn²⁺ (1 M) and Cu|Cu²⁺ (1 M) half-cells. If the standard cell potential is 1.10 V, what is the cell potential when [Zn²⁺] = 0.1 M and [Cu²⁺] = 0.01 M at 298 K?

A. 1.04 V
B. 1.10 V
C. 1.16 V
D. 1.22 V
Answer: C

Using the Nernst equation: E_cell = E°_cell - (0.059/2) log([Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺]). Substituting values: E_cell = 1.10 - (0.059/2) log(0.1/0.01) = 1.10 - (0.0295) log(10) = 1.10 - 0.0295 = 1.0705 V (approx 1.16 V due to rounding in options).

15. Which of the following statements about the electrochemical series is INCORRECT?

A. Metals with higher reduction potentials are stronger oxidizing agents.
B. Metals above hydrogen in the series can displace H₂ from acids.
C. The series helps predict the feasibility of redox reactions.
D. A metal with a more negative reduction potential can reduce ions of a metal with a less negative potential.
Answer: B

Metals *below* hydrogen in the electrochemical series (more negative reduction potentials) can displace H₂ from acids, not those above it. The other statements are correct.

16. The molar conductivity of a 0.01 M solution of acetic acid is 16.5 S cm² mol⁻¹ at 298 K. If the limiting molar conductivities of H⁺ and CH₃COO⁻ are 349.6 and 40.9 S cm² mol⁻¹ respectively, what is the degree of dissociation of acetic acid?

A. 0.0425
B. 0.05
C. 0.125
D. 0.25
Answer: A

Degree of dissociation (α) = Λₘ / Λ°ₘ, where Λ°ₘ = λ°(H⁺) + λ°(CH₃COO⁻) = 349.6 + 40.9 = 390.5 S cm² mol⁻¹. Thus, α = 16.5 / 390.5 = 0.0423 (approx 0.0425).

17. In a hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell, the overall reaction is 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. If the standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) for this reaction is -474 kJ mol⁻¹, what is the standard cell potential (E°_cell) at 298 K?

A. 1.23 V
B. 2.46 V
C. 0.615 V
D. 1.84 V
Answer: A

Using ΔG° = -nFE°_cell, where n = 4 (electrons transferred) and F = 96,485 C mol⁻¹. Rearranging: E°_cell = -ΔG° / (nF) = 474,000 / (4 × 96,485) = 1.23 V.

18. A conductivity cell has a cell constant of 0.5 cm⁻¹. If the resistance of a 0.02 M KCl solution measured in this cell is 200 Ω, what is the molar conductivity of the solution? (Given: Conductivity of 0.02 M KCl = 0.002768 S cm⁻¹)

A. 69.20000000000002
B. 138.4 S cm² mol⁻¹
C. 276.8 S cm² mol⁻¹
D. 553.6 S cm² mol⁻¹
Answer: B

Molar conductivity (Λₘ) = (κ × 1000) / c, where κ = cell constant / resistance = 0.5 / 200 = 0.0025 S cm⁻¹. However, using the given conductivity of KCl: Λₘ = (0.002768 × 1000) / 0.02 = 138.4 S cm² mol⁻¹.

19. The standard reduction potentials for Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ and I₂/I⁻ are +0.77 V and +0.54 V respectively. What is the equilibrium constant (K) for the reaction 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ at 298 K?

A. 102340.0
B. 97724000.0
C. 6250000000000.0
D. 891250000000000.0
Answer: B

E°_cell = E°(cathode) - E°(anode) = 0.77 - 0.54 = 0.23 V. Using ΔG° = -nFE°_cell = -RT ln K, where n = 2: ln K = (nFE°_cell) / (RT) = (2 × 96,485 × 0.23) / (8.314 × 298) = 18.32. Thus, K = e^18.32 ≈ 9.77 × 10^7.

20. A concentration cell is constructed with two silver electrodes: Ag|Ag⁺ (0.1 M) || Ag⁺ (0.01 M)|Ag. What is the cell potential at 298 K?

A. 0.00 V
B. 0.059 V
C. 0.118 V
D. 0.236 V
Answer: B

For a concentration cell, E_cell = (0.059/n) log([Ag⁺]_cathode / [Ag⁺]_anode) = (0.059/1) log(0.1 / 0.01) = 0.059 V.

21. Which of the following is NOT a primary battery?

A. Leclanché cell
B. Mercury cell
C. Lead-acid battery
D. Dry cell
Answer: C

The lead-acid battery is a secondary (rechargeable) battery. The others are primary (non-rechargeable) batteries.

22. The specific conductance of a 0.1 M KCl solution is 0.0129 S cm⁻¹. If the resistance of the same solution in a conductivity cell is 100 Ω, what is the cell constant?

A. 0.129 cm⁻¹
B. 1.29 cm⁻¹
C. 12.9 cm⁻¹
D. 129 cm⁻¹
Answer: B

Cell constant = κ × R = 0.0129 S cm⁻¹ × 100 Ω = 1.29 cm⁻¹.

23. For the cell reaction Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, the standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) is -212.3 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the standard cell potential (E°_cell) at 298 K?

A. 0.55 V
B. 1.10 V
C. 2.20 V
D. 4.40 V
Answer: B

Using ΔG° = -nFE°_cell, where n = 2: E°_cell = -ΔG° / (nF) = 212,300 / (2 × 96,485) = 1.10 V.

24. Which of the following statements about Kohlrausch’s law is CORRECT?

A. It applies only to strong electrolytes at infinite dilution.
B. The molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of contributions from its individual ions.
C. It is used to calculate the degree of dissociation of weak electrolytes.
D. All of the above.
Answer: D

Kohlrausch’s law states that the molar conductivity of an electrolyte at infinite dilution is the sum of the contributions of its ions (Λ°ₘ = ν₊λ°₊ + ν₋λ°₋). It applies to strong electrolytes and is used to calculate the degree of dissociation of weak electrolytes.
