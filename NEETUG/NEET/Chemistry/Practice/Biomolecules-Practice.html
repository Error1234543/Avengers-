NEET Chemistry: Biomolecules - Practice Set (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) focused on Biomolecules, aligned with the NEET exam pattern. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. Which of the following biomolecules is a polymer of α-D-glucose linked by α(1→4) glycosidic bonds with α(1→6) branch points?

A. Cellulose
B. Glycogen
C. Amylose
D. Sucrose
Answer: B

Glycogen is the storage polysaccharide in animals and is a polymer of α-D-glucose with α(1→4) glycosidic bonds and frequent α(1→6) branch points. Cellulose has β(1→4) linkages, amylose is unbranched, and sucrose is a disaccharide.

2. The peptide bond in proteins is formed between which two functional groups?

A. Carboxyl group of one amino acid and amino group of another
B. Amino group of one amino acid and R-group of another
C. Carboxyl group of one amino acid and hydroxyl group of another
D. Amino group of one amino acid and phosphate group of another
Answer: A

A peptide bond is formed via a condensation reaction between the carboxyl group (-COOH) of one amino acid and the amino group (-NH₂) of another, releasing a molecule of water.

3. Which of the following statements about DNA and RNA is correct?

A. DNA contains uracil, while RNA contains thymine
B. DNA is typically single-stranded, while RNA is double-stranded
C. DNA contains deoxyribose sugar, while RNA contains ribose sugar
D. DNA is involved in protein synthesis, while RNA stores genetic information
Answer: C

DNA contains deoxyribose sugar and thymine, while RNA contains ribose sugar and uracil. DNA is double-stranded and stores genetic information, whereas RNA is typically single-stranded and participates in protein synthesis.

4. The secondary structure of a protein is primarily stabilized by which type of interaction?

A. Hydrophobic interactions
B. Disulfide bridges
C. Hydrogen bonds
D. Ionic bonds
Answer: C

The secondary structure of proteins (e.g., α-helix and β-pleated sheet) is stabilized by hydrogen bonds between the amino hydrogen and carboxyl oxygen atoms of the peptide backbone.

5. Which of the following amino acids is classified as basic due to the presence of an additional amino group in its side chain?

A. Alanine
B. Lysine
C. Glutamic acid
D. Valine
Answer: B

Lysine is a basic amino acid because its side chain contains an additional amino group (-NH₂), which can accept protons. Alanine and valine are neutral, while glutamic acid is acidic.

6. In the double helix structure of DNA, the two strands are held together by:

A. Covalent bonds between sugar-phosphate backbones
B. Hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases
C. Ionic bonds between phosphate groups
D. Hydrophobic interactions between deoxyribose sugars
Answer: B

The two strands of DNA are held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases: adenine pairs with thymine (A-T) via two hydrogen bonds, and guanine pairs with cytosine (G-C) via three hydrogen bonds.

7. Which of the following vitamins is fat-soluble and acts as an antioxidant?

A. Vitamin C
B. Vitamin B₁₂
C. Vitamin E
D. Vitamin K
Answer: C

Vitamin E is a fat-soluble vitamin that functions as an antioxidant, protecting cell membranes from oxidative damage. Vitamin C is water-soluble, while Vitamin K is involved in blood clotting.

8. The process by which a protein loses its native structure and function due to external stress is called:

A. Denaturation
B. Hydrolysis
C. Polymerization
D. Saponification
Answer: A

Denaturation refers to the loss of a protein's native structure (and thus function) due to external stresses such as heat, pH changes, or chemical agents. Hydrolysis involves breaking peptide bonds, while polymerization and saponification are unrelated processes.

9. Which of the following is a nucleotide found in RNA but not in DNA?

A. Adenosine monophosphate (AMP)
B. Guanosine monophosphate (GMP)
C. Uridine monophosphate (UMP)
D. Cytidine monophosphate (CMP)
Answer: C

Uridine monophosphate (UMP) contains uracil, which is a nitrogenous base found in RNA but not in DNA. DNA contains thymine instead of uracil.

10. The quaternary structure of a protein involves:

A. The sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain
B. The folding of a polypeptide chain into α-helices or β-sheets
C. The three-dimensional arrangement of a single polypeptide chain
D. The association of two or more polypeptide chains into a multi-subunit complex
Answer: D

The quaternary structure of a protein refers to the association of two or more polypeptide chains (subunits) into a functional multi-subunit complex, such as hemoglobin, which consists of four subunits.

11. A nucleotide is composed of:

A. A nitrogenous base and a phosphate group
B. A nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group
C. A pentose sugar and a phosphate group
D. A nitrogenous base and a pentose sugar
Answer: B

A **nucleotide** consists of three components: (1) a **nitrogenous base** (purine or pyrimidine), (2) a **pentose sugar** (ribose in RNA, deoxyribose in DNA), and (3) a **phosphate group**. A nucleoside lacks the phosphate group (D).

12. Which of the following disaccharides yields glucose and fructose upon hydrolysis?

A. Maltose
B. Lactose
C. Sucrose
D. Cellobiose
Answer: C

**Sucrose** is a non-reducing disaccharide composed of **glucose and fructose** linked by an α(1→2) glycosidic bond. Maltose (A) yields two glucose molecules, lactose (B) yields glucose and galactose, and cellobiose (D) is a glucose dimer from cellulose.

13. Which of the following statements about cellulose is TRUE?

A. Cellulose is a branched polymer of β-D-glucose.
B. Humans can digest cellulose due to the presence of cellulase.
C. Cellulose is the primary structural component of plant cell walls.
D. Cellulose is soluble in water.
Answer: C

**Cellulose** is a **linear** (not branched) polymer of **β-D-glucose** linked by β(1→4) glycosidic bonds and is the primary structural component of plant cell walls. Humans lack cellulase (B) and cannot digest cellulose, and it is insoluble in water (D).

14. In the structure of DNA, the two polynucleotide chains are:

A. Parallel and held together by ionic bonds
B. Antiparallel and held together by hydrogen bonds
C. Parallel and held together by hydrogen bonds
D. Antiparallel and held together by covalent bonds
Answer: B

The two polynucleotide chains in DNA are **antiparallel** (5’→3’ and 3’→5’) and held together by **hydrogen bonds** between complementary nitrogenous bases (A-T and C-G). Ionic bonds (A/C) and covalent bonds (D) are not involved in base pairing.

15. Which of the following statements correctly describes the structural difference between starch and cellulose, and its biological significance?

A. Starch is a linear polymer of α-D-glucose, while cellulose is a branched polymer of β-D-glucose. This makes starch digestible by humans, while cellulose is not.
B. Starch consists of both amylose (linear) and amylopectin (branched) polymers of α-D-glucose, while cellulose is a linear polymer of β-D-glucose. The β-glycosidic linkages in cellulose make it indigestible by humans.
C. Cellulose is a polymer of α-D-glucose with β-glycosidic linkages, while starch is a polymer of β-D-glucose with α-glycosidic linkages. This structural difference makes cellulose a storage polysaccharide and starch a structural polysaccharide.
D. Starch and cellulose are both polymers of α-D-glucose, but starch has β-glycosidic linkages, making it indigestible, while cellulose has α-glycosidic linkages, making it digestible.
Answer: B

Starch is composed of two types of polymers: amylose (linear chain of α-D-glucose units linked by α-1,4-glycosidic bonds) and amylopectin (branched chain with α-1,4 and α-1,6-glycosidic bonds). Cellulose, on the other hand, is a linear polymer of β-D-glucose units linked by β-1,4-glycosidic bonds. Humans lack the enzyme cellulase required to break β-glycosidic linkages, making cellulose indigestible, while the α-glycosidic linkages in starch can be hydrolyzed by amylase enzymes. This structural difference is biologically significant as it determines the digestibility and functional roles of these polysaccharides.
