NEET Chemistry Practice - Thermodynamics (Part 1 of 2)

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on Thermodynamics for NEET. Each question has four options, and only one is correct. Select the most appropriate answer. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following statements correctly defines a state function in thermodynamics?

A. A property that depends only on the initial and final states of the system, not on the path taken.
B. A property that depends on the path taken to reach the final state from the initial state.
C. A property that is always constant for a given process.
D. A property that measures the heat transferred during a process.
Answer: A

A state function is a property of a system that depends only on the current state of the system, not on the path taken to reach that state. Examples include internal energy (U), enthalpy (H), and entropy (S).

2. For an isothermal expansion of an ideal gas against a constant external pressure, which of the following is true?

A. ΔU = 0 and q = -w
B. ΔU = q and w = 0
C. ΔU = w and q = 0
D. ΔU = 0 and q = w
Answer: A

In an isothermal process, the temperature remains constant, so the internal energy change (ΔU) for an ideal gas is zero. By the first law of thermodynamics, ΔU = q + w. Since ΔU = 0, it follows that q = -w.

3. The enthalpy change (ΔH) for a reaction is -200 kJ/mol. If the reaction is carried out at constant pressure, which of the following is correct?

A. 200 kJ of heat is absorbed by the system.
B. 200 kJ of heat is released by the system.
C. The internal energy change (ΔU) is also -200 kJ/mol.
D. The work done by the system is 200 kJ/mol.
Answer: B

Enthalpy change (ΔH) is the heat transferred at constant pressure. A negative ΔH indicates an exothermic reaction, meaning heat is released by the system. ΔU may differ from ΔH if work is done (ΔH = ΔU + PΔV).

4. Calculate the work done when 2 moles of an ideal gas expand isothermally from 10 L to 20 L against a constant external pressure of 1 atm at 300 K.

A. -1013 J
B. -2026 J
C. -101.3 J
D. -506.5 J
Answer: A

Work done in expansion against constant external pressure is given by w = -P_ext ΔV. Here, ΔV = 20 L - 10 L = 10 L = 0.01 m³, P_ext = 1 atm = 101325 Pa. Thus, w = -101325 Pa × 0.01 m³ = -1013.25 J ≈ -1013 J.

5. Which of the following is true for the standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f) of a compound?

A. It is the enthalpy change when one mole of the compound is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states.
B. It is always negative for stable compounds.
C. It is zero for all elements in their standard states.
D. Both A and C are correct.
Answer: D

The standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f) is the enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states. By definition, ΔH°f is zero for elements in their standard states. It can be positive or negative depending on the compound.

6. Which of the following is an example of an extensive property?

A. Temperature
B. Pressure
C. Volume
D. Density
Answer: C

Extensive properties depend on the amount of substance in the system. Volume is an extensive property, while temperature, pressure, and density are intensive properties (independent of the amount of substance).

7. The first law of thermodynamics is best represented by which of the following equations?

A. ΔU = q + w
B. ΔU = q - w
C. ΔH = ΔU + PΔV
D. ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
Answer: A

The first law of thermodynamics states that the change in internal energy (ΔU) of a system is equal to the heat added to the system (q) plus the work done on the system (w). The sign convention may vary, but the standard form is ΔU = q + w.

8. For an adiabatic process involving an ideal gas, which of the following is true?

A. q = 0 and ΔU = w
B. q = w and ΔU = 0
C. q = 0 and ΔU = -w
D. ΔU = q and w = 0
Answer: A

In an adiabatic process, no heat is exchanged with the surroundings (q = 0). By the first law of thermodynamics, ΔU = q + w, so ΔU = w. The work done on or by the system changes its internal energy.
