1. Which of the following statements correctly describes a crystalline solid?

A. It has a long-range ordered arrangement of particles and a sharp melting point.
B. It has a short-range ordered arrangement of particles and a broad melting range.
C. It lacks any ordered arrangement of particles and melts over a range of temperatures.
D. It consists of particles arranged in a random manner but has a sharp melting point.
Answer: A

Crystalline solids are characterized by a long-range ordered arrangement of particles (atoms, ions, or molecules) in a three-dimensional lattice. This ordered structure results in a sharp melting point, as all particles require the same amount of energy to transition from solid to liquid.

2. In a face-centered cubic (FCC) unit cell, the number of atoms per unit cell is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 4
D. 6
Answer: C

In a face-centered cubic (FCC) unit cell, there are 8 corner atoms (each shared by 8 unit cells, contributing 1 atom) and 6 face-centered atoms (each shared by 2 unit cells, contributing 3 atoms). Total atoms per unit cell = 1 + 3 = 4.

3. A compound crystallizes in a cubic close-packed (CCP) structure. If the edge length of the unit cell is 400 pm, what is the radius of the atom?

A. 100 pm
B. 141.4 pm
C. 200 pm
D. 282.8 pm
Answer: B

In a cubic close-packed (CCP) or face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, the relationship between the edge length 'a' and the atomic radius 'r' is given by: a = 2√2 r. Rearranging, r = a / (2√2) = 400 pm / (2√2) ≈ 141.4 pm.

4. Which type of defect is introduced in a crystal when an ion occupies an interstitial site?

A. Frenkel defect
B. Schottky defect
C. Vacancy defect
D. Impurity defect
Answer: A

A Frenkel defect occurs when an ion leaves its regular lattice site and occupies an interstitial site within the crystal. This creates a vacancy at the original site and an interstitial defect at the new site.

5. The density of a unit cell is given by the formula: ρ = (Z × M) / (a³ × Nₐ). What does 'Z' represent in this formula?

A. Number of atoms per unit cell
B. Atomic mass of the element
C. Avogadro's number
D. Edge length of the unit cell
Answer: A

In the density formula for a unit cell, 'Z' represents the number of atoms (or formula units) per unit cell. 'M' is the molar mass, 'a' is the edge length, and 'Nₐ' is Avogadro's number.

6. A metal crystallizes in a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure with an edge length of 300 pm. What is the distance between two nearest neighbor atoms?

A. 259.8 pm
B. 300 pm
C. 346.4 pm
D. 519.6 pm
Answer: A

In a BCC structure, the nearest neighbor distance is the distance between the central atom and a corner atom. This distance is given by (√3/2) × a, where 'a' is the edge length. Thus, distance = (√3/2) × 300 pm ≈ 259.8 pm.

7. In a crystal lattice, if the number of tetrahedral voids is 'T' and the number of octahedral voids is 'O', what is the relationship between T and O for a close-packed structure?

A. T = O
B. T = 2O
C. O = 2T
D. T = 3O
Answer: B

In a close-packed structure (either hexagonal close-packed or cubic close-packed), the number of tetrahedral voids (T) is twice the number of octahedral voids (O). Thus, T = 2O.

8. Which of the following defects does NOT alter the density of a crystal?

A. Frenkel defect
B. Schottky defect
C. Interstitial defect
D. Vacancy defect
Answer: A

Frenkel defect is a combination of a vacancy and an interstitial defect where an ion moves from its lattice site to an interstitial site. Since no ions are removed from the crystal, the density remains unchanged. Schottky defect involves removal of equal numbers of cations and anions, reducing density. Interstitial and vacancy defects alter the mass or volume, thus affecting density.

9. Which of the following is an example of a p-type semiconductor?

A. Silicon doped with phosphorus
B. Silicon doped with boron
C. Germanium doped with arsenic
D. Germanium doped with antimony
Answer: B

A p-type semiconductor is formed when a trivalent impurity (like boron) is doped into a tetravalent semiconductor (like silicon). This creates electron deficiencies or 'holes,' which act as positive charge carriers.

10. The ratio of the number of vacancies to the number of lattice sites in a crystal at a given temperature is proportional to:

A. T
B. T^2
C. e^(-E/kT)
D. e^(E/kT)
Answer: C

The number of vacancies in a crystal at thermal equilibrium is given by the Boltzmann distribution: n = N e^(-E/kT), where E is the energy required to create a vacancy, k is Boltzmann's constant, and T is the absolute temperature. Thus, the ratio n/N is proportional to e^(-E/kT).

11. In a crystal, if the distance between two adjacent parallel planes (d) is 200 pm and the angle of diffraction (θ) is 30°, what is the wavelength of the X-ray used (in pm)? Use Bragg's equation: nλ = 2d sinθ.

A. 100 pm
B. 200 pm
C. 300 pm
D. 400 pm
Answer: B

Using Bragg's equation for first-order diffraction (n = 1): λ = 2d sinθ. Substituting d = 200 pm and θ = 30° (sin 30° = 0.5), λ = 2 × 200 × 0.5 = 200 pm.

12. A metal crystallizes in a hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure. What is the coordination number of each atom in this structure?

A. 6
B. 8
C. 10
D. 12
Answer: D

In a hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure, each atom is in contact with 12 neighboring atoms: 6 in the same plane, 3 in the plane above, and 3 in the plane below. Thus, the coordination number is 12.

13. The density of a crystal is 2.16 g/cm³, and the edge length of its cubic unit cell is 400 pm. If the crystal has 4 atoms per unit cell, what is the molar mass of the substance (in g/mol)? Use Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

A. 27 g/mol
B. 54 g/mol
C. 108 g/mol
D. 216 g/mol
Answer: B

Density (ρ) = (Z × M) / (a³ × Nₐ), where Z = number of atoms per unit cell, M = molar mass, a = edge length, Nₐ = Avogadro's number. Rearranging: M = (ρ × a³ × Nₐ) / Z. Substituting ρ = 2.16 g/cm³, a = 400 pm = 4 × 10⁻⁸ cm, Z = 4, Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³: M = (2.16 × (4 × 10⁻⁸)³ × 6.022 × 10²³) / 4 ≈ 54 g/mol.

14. A compound crystallizes in a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice with a unit cell edge length of 400 pm. If the density of the compound is 8.92 g/cm³, what is the molar mass of the compound? (Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

A. 58.5 g/mol
B. 63.5 g/mol
C. 108 g/mol
D. 184 g/mol
Answer: B

For an FCC lattice, the number of atoms per unit cell (Z) is 4. The formula for density (ρ) is: ρ = (Z × M) / (a³ × Nₐ), where M is the molar mass, a is the edge length, and Nₐ is Avogadro's number. Rearranging to solve for M: M = (ρ × a³ × Nₐ) / Z. Convert a to cm: 400 pm = 4 × 10⁻⁸ cm. Substitute values: M = (8.92 g/cm³ × (4 × 10⁻⁸ cm)³ × 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) / 4 = 63.5 g/mol. This matches the molar mass of copper, a common FCC metal.

15. A crystal of NaCl has a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice. If the edge length of the unit cell is 564 pm, what is the distance between the nearest Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in the crystal?

A. 282 pm
B. 399 pm
C. 244 pm
D. 488 pm
Answer: A

In a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice of NaCl, the Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions are arranged such that each Na⁺ ion is surrounded by 6 Cl⁻ ions and vice versa. The nearest distance between Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions is half the length of the unit cell's body diagonal for the FCC structure. However, in NaCl, the nearest distance is actually half the edge length of the unit cell because the ions are located at the edges and face centers. Thus, the distance = (edge length)/2 = 564 pm / 2 = 282 pm.
