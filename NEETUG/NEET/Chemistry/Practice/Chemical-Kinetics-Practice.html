This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Chemical Kinetics for NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. For a first-order reaction, the half-life period is independent of:

A. Initial concentration of the reactant
B. Rate constant
C. Temperature
D. Activation energy
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, the half-life (t₁/₂) is given by t₁/₂ = 0.693/k, where k is the rate constant. The half-life is independent of the initial concentration of the reactant but depends on the rate constant, temperature (which affects k), and activation energy (which influences k).

2. The rate constant of a reaction is 2.5 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 25°C and 5.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 35°C. What is the activation energy (Eₐ) of the reaction? (R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)

A. 52.9 kJ mol⁻¹
B. 25.6 kJ mol⁻¹
C. 75.3 kJ mol⁻¹
D. 105.8 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A

Using the Arrhenius equation: ln(k₂/k₁) = (Eₐ/R) × (1/T₁ - 1/T₂). Here, k₁ = 2.5 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, k₂ = 5.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, T₁ = 298 K, T₂ = 308 K, and R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Solving for Eₐ: Eₐ = [ln(5.0/2.5) × 8.314 × 298 × 308] / (308 - 298) = 52,900 J mol⁻¹ or 52.9 kJ mol⁻¹.

3. In a reaction, the rate expression is given as rate = k[A]²[B]. If the concentration of A is doubled and that of B is halved, the new rate will be:

A. Same as the original rate
B. Twice the original rate
C. Four times the original rate
D. Half the original rate
Answer: B

The original rate = k[A]²[B]. New concentrations: [A]′ = 2[A], [B]′ = 0.5[B]. New rate = k(2[A])²(0.5[B]) = k × 4[A]² × 0.5[B] = 2k[A]²[B] = 2 × original rate.

4. Which of the following statements is correct for a zero-order reaction?

A. The rate of reaction is proportional to the concentration of the reactant.
B. The half-life of the reaction is independent of the initial concentration.
C. The rate constant has the units of mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹.
D. The concentration of the reactant decreases exponentially with time.
Answer: C

For a zero-order reaction, the rate = k (constant), so the rate constant (k) has units of mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹. The rate is independent of reactant concentration (A is incorrect). The half-life depends on initial concentration (B is incorrect), and the concentration decreases linearly with time (D is incorrect).

5. The rate of a reaction increases by 100 times when the temperature is increased from 25°C to 35°C. What is the activation energy of the reaction? (R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)

A. 52.9 kJ mol⁻¹
B. 107.2 kJ mol⁻¹
C. 25.6 kJ mol⁻¹
D. 75.3 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: B

Using the Arrhenius equation: ln(k₂/k₁) = (Eₐ/R) × (1/T₁ - 1/T₂). Here, k₂/k₁ = 100, T₁ = 298 K, T₂ = 308 K. Solving for Eₐ: Eₐ = [ln(100) × 8.314 × 298 × 308] / (308 - 298) = 107,200 J mol⁻¹ or 107.2 kJ mol⁻¹.

6. For a reaction, the plot of ln(k) vs. 1/T is a straight line with a slope of -5000 K. What is the activation energy of the reaction? (R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)

A. 41.57 kJ mol⁻¹
B. 50.00 kJ mol⁻¹
C. 25.00 kJ mol⁻¹
D. 100.0 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A

The slope of the ln(k) vs. 1/T plot is -Eₐ/R. Given slope = -5000 K, so Eₐ = -slope × R = 5000 × 8.314 = 41,570 J mol⁻¹ or 41.57 kJ mol⁻¹.

7. In the reaction 2A + B → C, the rate law is found to be rate = k[A][B]². What is the order of the reaction with respect to A?

A. 0
B. 1
C. 2
D. 3
Answer: B

The order of the reaction with respect to a reactant is the exponent to which its concentration is raised in the rate law. Here, the rate = k[A]¹[B]², so the order with respect to A is 1.

8. A first-order reaction has a rate constant of 0.0693 min⁻¹. What is the half-life of the reaction?

A. 5 minutes
B. 10 minutes
C. 15 minutes
D. 20 minutes
Answer: B

For a first-order reaction, t₁/₂ = 0.693/k. Here, k = 0.0693 min⁻¹, so t₁/₂ = 0.693 / 0.0693 = 10 minutes.

9. The rate constant of a reaction is 3.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹ at 300 K. If the activation energy is 50 kJ mol⁻¹, what is the rate constant at 310 K? (R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)

A. 4.5 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
B. 6.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
C. 7.5 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
D. 9.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹
Answer: B

Using the Arrhenius equation: ln(k₂/k₁) = (Eₐ/R) × (1/T₁ - 1/T₂). Here, k₁ = 3.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹, Eₐ = 50,000 J mol⁻¹, T₁ = 300 K, T₂ = 310 K. Solving for k₂: ln(k₂/3.0 × 10⁻²) = (50,000/8.314) × (1/300 - 1/310) = 0.645. Thus, k₂ = 3.0 × 10⁻² × e⁰·⁶⁴⁵ ≈ 6.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹.

10. For the reaction A → B, the rate law is rate = k[A]⁰. Which of the following graphs represents the correct variation of concentration of A with time?

A. A straight line with a negative slope
B. A straight line with a positive slope
C. An exponential decay curve
D. A hyperbolic curve
Answer: A

For a zero-order reaction (rate = k[A]⁰ = k), the concentration of A decreases linearly with time, represented by a straight line with a negative slope.

11. The half-life of a first-order reaction is 10 minutes. What fraction of the reactant remains after 30 minutes?

A. 1/2
B. 1/4
C. 1/8
D. 1/16
Answer: C

For a first-order reaction, the fraction remaining after n half-lives is (1/2)ⁿ. Here, 30 minutes = 3 half-lives (t₁/₂ = 10 minutes). So, fraction remaining = (1/2)³ = 1/8.

12. Which of the following factors does NOT affect the rate of a chemical reaction?

A. Concentration of reactants
B. Temperature
C. Presence of a catalyst
D. Molecularity of the reaction
Answer: D

The rate of a reaction depends on the concentration of reactants, temperature, and the presence of a catalyst. Molecularity is a theoretical concept referring to the number of molecules involved in an elementary step and does not directly affect the rate of the reaction.

13. The unit of rate constant for a zero-order reaction is:

A. mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
B. s⁻¹
C. L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
D. mol² L⁻² s⁻¹
Answer: A

For a zero-order reaction, rate = k, where the rate is expressed in mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹. Thus, the unit of the rate constant k is mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹.

14. A reaction is 50% complete in 2 hours and 75% complete in 4 hours. The order of the reaction is:

A. Zero order
B. First order
C. Second order
D. Third order
Answer: B

For a first-order reaction, the half-life is constant. Here, 50% completion in 2 hours and 75% completion (which is two half-lives) in 4 hours confirms the reaction is first-order.

15. The activation energy of a reaction can be determined from the slope of the plot of:

A. ln k vs. T
B. ln k vs. 1/T
C. k vs. T
D. k vs. 1/T
Answer: B

The Arrhenius equation is ln k = ln A - (Eₐ/R)(1/T). A plot of ln k vs. 1/T gives a straight line with a slope of -Eₐ/R, from which activation energy (Eₐ) can be determined.

16. For a reaction, the rate constant at 300 K is 2 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ and at 310 K is 4 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. The activation energy of the reaction is: (R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

A. 53.6 kJ/mol
B. 26.8 kJ/mol
C. 107.2 kJ/mol
D. 13.4 kJ/mol
Answer: A

Using the Arrhenius equation: ln(k₂/k₁) = (Eₐ/R) × (1/T₁ - 1/T₂). Substituting the values: ln(4 × 10⁻³ / 2 × 10⁻³) = (Eₐ/8.314) × (1/300 - 1/310). Solving gives Eₐ ≈ 53.6 kJ/mol.

17. In the reaction 2A + B → C, the rate law is rate = k[A][B]². If the concentration of A is doubled and that of B is kept constant, the rate will:

A. Remain unchanged
B. Double
C. Quadruple
D. Increase eightfold
Answer: B

The rate law is rate = k[A][B]². Doubling [A] while keeping [B] constant changes the rate to k[2A][B]² = 2k[A][B]², which is double the original rate.

18. The rate of a reaction increases by a factor of 2.25 when the temperature is raised from 298 K to 308 K. The activation energy of the reaction is: (R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

A. 50 kJ/mol
B. 75 kJ/mol
C. 100 kJ/mol
D. 125 kJ/mol
Answer: B

Using the Arrhenius equation: ln(2.25) = (Eₐ/8.314) × (1/298 - 1/308). Solving gives Eₐ ≈ 75 kJ/mol.

19. In a first-order reaction, the concentration of the reactant decreases from 0.8 M to 0.4 M in 15 minutes. The rate constant of the reaction is:

A. 0.0462 min⁻¹
B. 0.0231 min⁻¹
C. 0.0924 min⁻¹
D. 0.0115 min⁻¹
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, t₁/₂ = 0.693/k. Here, t₁/₂ = 15 minutes. Thus, k = 0.693/15 ≈ 0.0462 min⁻¹.

20. The rate constant of a reaction is 3.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹ at 300 K. If the activation energy is 50 kJ/mol, what is the temperature at which the rate constant becomes 6.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹? (R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

A. 305 K
B. 310 K
C. 315 K
D. 320 K
Answer: B

Using the Arrhenius equation: ln(6.0 × 10⁻² / 3.0 × 10⁻²) = (50,000/8.314) × (1/300 - 1/T). Solving gives T ≈ 310 K.

21. For a first-order reaction, the half-life period is 30 minutes. What percentage of the reactant will remain after 90 minutes?

A. 12.5%
B. 25%
C. 6.25%
D. 50%
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, the half-life (t₁/₂) is constant and independent of the initial concentration. After each half-life, the remaining reactant is halved. Given t₁/₂ = 30 minutes, after 90 minutes (which is 3 half-lives), the remaining reactant is calculated as: (1/2)³ = 1/8 = 12.5% of the original amount. Thus, 12.5% of the reactant remains after 90 minutes.

22. For a first-order reaction, the time required for 99% completion is twice the time required for 90% completion. If the rate constant of the reaction is 6.93 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, what is the time required for 90% completion of the reaction?

A. 333.33 seconds
B. 666.67 seconds
C. 166.67 seconds
D. 500.00 seconds
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, the time required for a certain percentage of completion is given by the formula: t = (2.303/k) log([A]₀/[A]). For 90% completion, [A] = 0.1[A]₀, so t₉₀ = (2.303/k) log(1/0.1) = (2.303/k) log(10) = 2.303/k. For 99% completion, [A] = 0.01[A]₀, so t₉₉ = (2.303/k) log(1/0.01) = (2.303/k) log(100) = 4.606/k. Given that t₉₉ = 2 × t₉₀, this confirms the first-order kinetics. Substituting k = 6.93 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ into t₉₀ = 2.303/k, we get t₉₀ = 2.303 / (6.93 × 10⁻³) = 333.33 seconds.
