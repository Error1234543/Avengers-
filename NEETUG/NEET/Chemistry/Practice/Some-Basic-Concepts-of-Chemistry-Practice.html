NEET Chemistry Practice - Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry (Part 1 of 2)

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions covering key sub-topics in 'Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry' for NEET. Each question has four options (A-D), with only one correct answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following statements correctly defines the term 'mole' in chemistry?

A. The mass of one atom of an element expressed in grams
B. The amount of substance containing as many entities as there are atoms in exactly 12 grams of carbon-12
C. The volume occupied by one gram of any gas at STP
D. The number of atoms present in 1 gram of any element
Answer: B

The mole is defined as the amount of substance that contains as many elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions, etc.) as there are atoms in exactly 12 grams of carbon-12. This number is Avogadro's constant (6.022 × 10²³).

2. A compound contains 40% carbon, 6.67% hydrogen, and 53.33% oxygen by mass. What is the empirical formula of the compound?

A. CH₂O
B. C₂H₄O₂
C. C₃H₆O₃
D. CH₄O
Answer: A

Assume 100 g of the compound: 40 g C, 6.67 g H, 53.33 g O. Moles: C = 40/12 = 3.33, H = 6.67/1 = 6.67, O = 53.33/16 = 3.33. Divide by smallest (3.33): C = 1, H = 2, O = 1. Empirical formula = CH₂O.

3. What is the mass percentage of oxygen in calcium carbonate (CaCO₃)? (Atomic masses: Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)

A. 12%
B. 16%
C. 48%
D. 32%
Answer: C

Molar mass of CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + (3 × 16) = 100 g/mol. Mass of oxygen = 48 g. Mass percentage = (48/100) × 100 = 48%.

4. A gas occupies 2.24 L at STP. How many molecules of the gas are present? (Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³)

A. 6.022 × 10²²
B. 6.022 × 10²¹
C. 6.022 × 10²³
D. 1.204 × 10²³
Answer: A

At STP, 22.4 L of gas contains 6.022 × 10²³ molecules. For 2.24 L: (6.022 × 10²³ × 2.24) / 22.4 = 6.022 × 10²² molecules.

5. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a limiting reagent?

A. It is completely consumed in the reaction
B. It determines the amount of product formed
C. It is present in excess in the reaction mixture
D. It limits the yield of the reaction
Answer: C

A limiting reagent is the reactant that is completely consumed first in a reaction, thereby limiting the amount of product formed. It is not present in excess; excess reagents remain unreacted.

6. What volume of 0.5 M H₂SO₄ is required to neutralize 25 mL of 0.2 M NaOH solution?

A. 5 mL
B. 10 mL
C. 2.5 mL
D. 25 mL
Answer: A

The reaction is H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. Moles of NaOH = 0.2 × 0.025 = 0.005. Moles of H₂SO₄ required = 0.005 / 2 = 0.0025. Volume of H₂SO₄ = 0.0025 / 0.5 = 0.005 L = 5 mL.

7. The percentage composition of a compound is 26.57% potassium, 35.36% chromium, and 38.07% oxygen. What is the empirical formula of the compound?

A. KCrO₄
B. K₂Cr₂O₇
C. K₂CrO₄
D. KCr₂O₇
Answer: B

Assume 100 g: K = 26.57 g, Cr = 35.36 g, O = 38.07 g. Moles: K = 26.57/39.1 ≈ 0.68, Cr = 35.36/52 ≈ 0.68, O = 38.07/16 ≈ 2.38. Divide by smallest (0.68): K = 1, Cr = 1, O ≈ 3.5. Multiply by 2 to get whole numbers: K₂Cr₂O₇.

8. A solution is prepared by dissolving 18 g of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) in 100 g of water. What is the mole fraction of glucose in the solution? (Molar mass of glucose = 180 g/mol)

A. 0.018
B. 0.01
C. 0.091
D. 0.1
Answer: A

Moles of glucose = 18 / 180 = 0.1. Moles of water = 100 / 18 ≈ 5.56. Mole fraction of glucose = 0.1 / (0.1 + 5.56) ≈ 0.018.

9. What is the molarity of a solution containing 5.85 g of NaCl dissolved in 500 mL of solution? (Molar mass of NaCl = 58.5 g/mol)

A. 0.1 M
B. 0.2 M
C. 0.05 M
D. 0.4 M
Answer: B

Moles of NaCl = 5.85 / 58.5 = 0.1. Volume of solution = 0.5 L. Molarity = 0.1 / 0.5 = 0.2 M.

10. Which of the following is an example of a homogeneous mixture?

A. Sand and water
B. Oil and water
C. Sugar dissolved in water
D. Iron filings and sulfur powder
Answer: C

A homogeneous mixture has a uniform composition throughout. Sugar dissolved in water forms a single phase, making it homogeneous. The other options are heterogeneous mixtures.

11. The mass of one atom of an element is 6.64 × 10⁻²³ g. The atomic mass of the element is:

A. 12 u
B. 40 u
C. 60 u
D. 100 u
Answer: B

The atomic mass unit (u) is defined as 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom, which is approximately 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g. The mass of one atom of the element is 6.64 × 10⁻²³ g. Thus, atomic mass = (6.64 × 10⁻²³) / (1.66 × 10⁻²⁴) ≈ 40 u.

12. The percentage composition of oxygen in CaCO₃ is: (Atomic masses: Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16)

A. 12%
B. 16%
C. 48%
D. 60%
Answer: C

Molar mass of CaCO₃ = 40 (Ca) + 12 (C) + 3 × 16 (O) = 100 g/mol. Mass of oxygen in CaCO₃ = 3 × 16 = 48 g. Percentage of oxygen = (48 / 100) × 100 = 48%.

13. The number of molecules present in 22.4 L of a gas at STP is:

A. 6.022 × 10²³
B. 12.044 × 10²³
C. 3.011 × 10²³
D. 22.4 × 10²³
Answer: A

At standard temperature and pressure (STP), 1 mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L. The number of molecules in 1 mole of a gas is given by Avogadro's number, which is 6.022 × 10²³ molecules.

14. A compound has an empirical formula of CH₂ and a molecular mass of 56 g/mol. Its molecular formula is:

A. CH₂
B. C₂H₄
C. C₄H₈
D. C₃H₆
Answer: C

The empirical formula mass of CH₂ is 12 (C) + 2 × 1 (H) = 14 g/mol. The molecular mass is 56 g/mol. The ratio of molecular mass to empirical formula mass is 56 / 14 = 4. Thus, the molecular formula is (CH₂)₄ = C₄H₈.

15. Which of the following is NOT a postulate of Dalton's atomic theory?

A. Atoms of the same element are identical in mass and properties.
B. Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.
C. Atoms are divisible into smaller particles like protons, neutrons, and electrons.
D. Chemical reactions involve rearrangement of atoms.
Answer: C

Dalton's atomic theory states that atoms are indivisible particles. The discovery of subatomic particles (protons, neutrons, electrons) later disproved this postulate. The other options are correct postulates of Dalton's theory.

16. The mass of 112 cm³ of a gas at STP is 0.14 g. The molar mass of the gas is:

A. 14 g/mol
B. 28 g/mol
C. 44 g/mol
D. 56 g/mol
Answer: B

At STP, 22.4 L (22400 cm³) of a gas contains 1 mole of the gas. Thus, 112 cm³ of the gas contains (112 / 22400) = 0.005 moles. Molar mass = mass / moles = 0.14 g / 0.005 mol = 28 g/mol.
