This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on d and f Block Elements, aligned with the NEET exam pattern. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. Which of the following statements correctly describes the trend in atomic radii of transition elements across a period?

A. Atomic radii decrease continuously from left to right due to increasing nuclear charge.
B. Atomic radii remain nearly constant due to the shielding effect of inner d-electrons.
C. Atomic radii increase continuously due to the addition of electrons in the d-orbitals.
D. Atomic radii first decrease, then increase toward the end of the series due to electron-electron repulsion.
Answer: B

The atomic radii of transition elements remain nearly constant across a period because the increase in nuclear charge is counterbalanced by the shielding effect of the added electrons in the inner d-orbitals. This results in minimal variation in atomic size.

2. The actinoid contraction is greater than the lanthanoid contraction because:

A. 5f electrons are more effectively shielded than 4f electrons.
B. 5f orbitals have a poorer shielding effect than 4f orbitals.
C. Actinoids have larger atomic radii than lanthanoids.
D. Actinoids exhibit higher oxidation states than lanthanoids.
Answer: B

The actinoid contraction is greater because 5f electrons have a poorer shielding effect compared to 4f electrons. This results in a stronger attraction between the nucleus and outer electrons, leading to a more pronounced contraction.

3. Which of the following statements about potassium dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇) is correct?

A. It is a strong oxidizing agent in acidic medium.
B. It is used as a primary standard in redox titrations.
C. It exhibits a green color in solution.
D. It is insoluble in water.
Answer: A

Potassium dichromate (K₂Cr₂O₇) is a strong oxidizing agent in acidic medium, where it is reduced to Cr³⁺ (green). It is soluble in water and is not typically used as a primary standard due to its hygroscopic nature.

4. The correct order of stability of +2 oxidation states for the first row transition metals is:

A. Mn²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > Co²⁺ > Ni²⁺
B. Fe²⁺ > Co²⁺ > Ni²⁺ > Mn²⁺
C. Ni²⁺ > Co²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > Mn²⁺
D. Co²⁺ > Ni²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > Mn²⁺
Answer: C

The stability of +2 oxidation states increases across the first row of transition metals due to the increasing nuclear charge and the stability of the d⁸ configuration (Ni²⁺). The order is Ni²⁺ > Co²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > Mn²⁺.

5. Which of the following actinoids is not naturally occurring?

A. Thorium
B. Uranium
C. Plutonium
D. Actinium
Answer: C

Plutonium is not naturally occurring and is primarily produced artificially in nuclear reactors. Thorium, uranium, and actinium are found naturally in the Earth's crust.

6. The complex [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is:

A. Paramagnetic and octahedral
B. Diamagnetic and octahedral
C. Paramagnetic and tetrahedral
D. Diamagnetic and square planar
Answer: B

The complex [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ has Co³⁺ in a d⁶ low-spin configuration with no unpaired electrons, making it diamagnetic. It adopts an octahedral geometry due to the strong field ligands (NH₃).

7. Which of the following elements exhibits the highest melting point among the transition metals?

A. Iron
B. Tungsten
C. Copper
D. Nickel
Answer: B

Tungsten (W) has the highest melting point among all transition metals, approximately 3422°C, due to strong metallic bonding involving its 5d electrons.

8. Which of the following statements about the actinide series is correct?

A. All actinides are radioactive.
B. Actinides exhibit only +3 oxidation states.
C. Actinides have completely filled 5f orbitals.
D. Actinides are less reactive than lanthanoids.
Answer: A

All actinides are radioactive due to their unstable nuclei. They exhibit multiple oxidation states, do not have completely filled 5f orbitals, and are generally more reactive than lanthanoids.

9. Which of the following statements correctly explains the lanthanide contraction?

A. The gradual decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of lanthanides with increasing atomic number due to poor shielding of 4f electrons.
B. The increase in the atomic radii of lanthanides due to the addition of electrons in the 5d orbital.
C. The constant atomic radii of lanthanides because the 4f electrons effectively shield the outer electrons.
D. The expansion of ionic radii in lanthanides due to the strong repulsion between 4f electrons.
Answer: A

Lanthanide contraction refers to the gradual decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of the lanthanide elements as the atomic number increases. This occurs because the 4f electrons shield the outer electrons poorly, leading to an increased effective nuclear charge that pulls the outer electrons closer to the nucleus. This contraction has significant consequences, such as the similarity in size between second and third-row transition metals (e.g., Zr and Hf).

10. A coordination compound has the formula [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂. Which of the following is true regarding its magnetic properties and geometry?

A. It is paramagnetic and has an octahedral geometry.
B. It is diamagnetic and has a tetrahedral geometry.
C. It is diamagnetic and has an octahedral geometry.
D. It is paramagnetic and has a square planar geometry.
Answer: C

The complex [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ contains Co³⁺, which has a d⁶ electronic configuration. In the presence of strong-field ligands like NH₃, the complex is low-spin and diamagnetic (no unpaired electrons). The coordination number is 6, leading to an octahedral geometry. Thus, the correct statement is that it is diamagnetic and octahedral.

11. The correct order of stability for the following oxidation states of manganese (Mn) in aqueous solution is:

A. Mn²⁺ > Mn³⁺ > Mn⁴⁺ > Mn⁷⁺
B. Mn⁷⁺ > Mn⁴⁺ > Mn³⁺ > Mn²⁺
C. Mn²⁺ > Mn⁴⁺ > Mn³⁺ > Mn⁷⁺
D. Mn⁴⁺ > Mn²⁺ > Mn³⁺ > Mn⁷⁺
Answer: A

In aqueous solution, the stability of manganese oxidation states follows the order Mn²⁺ > Mn³⁺ > Mn⁴⁺ > Mn⁷⁺. Mn²⁺ is the most stable due to its half-filled d⁵ configuration, which provides extra stability. Higher oxidation states like Mn⁷⁺ (e.g., in MnO₄⁻) are strong oxidizing agents and less stable in aqueous environments.
