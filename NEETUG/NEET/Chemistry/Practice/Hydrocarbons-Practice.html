NEET Chemistry Practice: Hydrocarbons (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on Hydrocarbons, aligned with the NEET exam pattern. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following hydrocarbons is classified as an alkyne?

A. C2H4
B. C3H6
C. C4H6
D. C5H12
Answer: C

Alkynes have the general formula CnH2n-2 and contain a carbon-carbon triple bond. C4H6 fits this formula (n=4, 2n-2=6), making it an alkyne. C2H4 is an alkene (CnH2n), C3H6 can be an alkene or cycloalkane, and C5H12 is an alkane (CnH2n+2).

2. Which reagent is used to distinguish between ethene and ethyne?

A. Bromine water
B. Ammoniacal silver nitrate
C. Alkaline KMnO4
D. Concentrated H2SO4
Answer: B

Ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollens' reagent for alkynes) reacts with ethyne to form a white precipitate of silver acetylide, while ethene does not react. Bromine water and alkaline KMnO4 react with both, and concentrated H2SO4 does not distinguish them clearly.

3. The process of converting alkenes into alkanes is called:

A. Hydrogenation
B. Halogenation
C. Hydration
D. Polymerization
Answer: A

Hydrogenation is the addition of hydrogen (H2) to alkenes in the presence of a catalyst (e.g., Ni, Pd, or Pt) to form alkanes. Halogenation involves adding halogens, hydration adds water, and polymerization forms polymers.

4. The product formed when propyne is treated with excess hydrogen in the presence of a palladium catalyst is:

A. Propene
B. Propane
C. 1-Propanol
D. 2-Propanol
Answer: B

Propyne (an alkyne) undergoes complete hydrogenation in the presence of excess hydrogen and a palladium catalyst to form propane. Partial hydrogenation would yield propene, but excess hydrogen ensures full saturation.

5. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic reaction of alkanes?

A. Combustion
B. Substitution
C. Addition
D. Cracking
Answer: C

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons and do not undergo addition reactions, which are characteristic of unsaturated hydrocarbons like alkenes and alkynes. They undergo combustion, substitution (e.g., halogenation), and cracking (thermal decomposition).

6. The compound formed by the reaction of acetylene with water in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 is:

A. Ethanol
B. Acetaldehyde
C. Ethylene glycol
D. Acetic acid
Answer: B

Acetylene (ethyne) reacts with water in the presence of HgSO4 and H2SO4 (Kucherov reaction) to form acetaldehyde (ethanal). This is a hydration reaction specific to alkynes.

7. Which of the following hydrocarbons will exhibit cis-trans isomerism?

A. 2-Methylpropene
B. But-2-ene
C. Propene
D. 2,3-Dimethylbut-2-ene
Answer: B

Cis-trans isomerism occurs in alkenes where each double-bonded carbon atom is attached to two different groups. But-2-ene (CH3-CH=CH-CH3) satisfies this condition, as each carbon in the double bond has one hydrogen and one methyl group. The other options either lack a double bond with distinct groups or have identical groups on one carbon, preventing isomerism.

8. The IUPAC name of the compound CH3-CH2-C≡C-CH(CH3)2 is:

A. 5-Methylhex-2-yne
B. 2-Methylhex-4-yne
C. 5-Methylhex-3-yne
D. 2-Methylhex-3-yne
Answer: C

The longest carbon chain containing the triple bond is 6 carbons long. The triple bond is given the lowest possible number (starting from the end nearest to it), placing it at carbon 3. The methyl group is attached to carbon 5, leading to the IUPAC name 5-methylhex-3-yne.

9. Which of the following compounds will give a white precipitate with ammoniacal silver nitrate?

A. Ethene
B. Ethyne
C. Benzene
D. Cyclohexane
Answer: B

Ethyne (acetylene), being a terminal alkyne, reacts with ammoniacal silver nitrate to form a white precipitate of silver acetylide. Ethene, benzene, and cyclohexane do not react with this reagent.

10. Which of the following is the correct structure of 1,3-butadiene?

A. CH2=CH-CH=CH2
B. CH3-CH=CH-CH3
C. CH2=C=CH-CH3
D. CH≡C-CH2-CH3
Answer: A

1,3-Butadiene is a conjugated diene with the structure CH2=CH-CH=CH2, where the double bonds are separated by a single bond. This arrangement allows for resonance stabilization. The other options represent different compounds (e.g., but-2-ene, a cumulated diene, or but-1-yne).

11. Which of the following statements correctly explains the difference between the boiling points of *n*-pentane and neopentane?

A. *n*-Pentane has a higher boiling point due to stronger London dispersion forces resulting from its linear structure and larger surface area.
B. Neopentane has a higher boiling point because its spherical shape allows for closer packing in the liquid state, increasing intermolecular forces.
C. *n*-Pentane and neopentane have identical boiling points as they are structural isomers with the same molecular formula and molecular weight.
D. Neopentane has a higher boiling point due to the presence of stronger hydrogen bonding between its branched alkyl groups.
Answer: A

The boiling point of hydrocarbons is primarily determined by the strength of London dispersion forces, which increase with molecular surface area and contact. *n*-Pentane, being a linear molecule, has a larger surface area and greater contact with neighboring molecules compared to neopentane, which has a compact, spherical structure. This results in stronger intermolecular forces and a higher boiling point for *n*-pentane. Neopentane's branched structure reduces surface contact, leading to weaker London dispersion forces and a lower boiling point. Hydrogen bonding is not present in alkanes, so option D is incorrect. Option B incorrectly attributes closer packing to higher boiling points, which is not the dominant factor here.

12. In the presence of ultraviolet light, methane reacts with chlorine to form chloromethane and hydrogen chloride. Which of the following mechanisms best describes this reaction?

A. Electrophilic addition
B. Nucleophilic substitution
C. Free radical substitution
D. Electrophilic substitution
Answer: C

The reaction between methane and chlorine under ultraviolet light proceeds via a free radical substitution mechanism. Ultraviolet light initiates the homolytic fission of chlorine molecules to form chlorine free radicals, which then abstract a hydrogen atom from methane to form a methyl free radical. This methyl free radical reacts with another chlorine molecule to form chloromethane and a chlorine free radical, propagating the chain reaction. This mechanism is characteristic of alkanes and is neither electrophilic nor nucleophilic in nature.

13. An unknown hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass and has a molar mass of 56 g/mol. On ozonolysis, it yields only one product, which is a ketone. Which of the following is the correct structure of the hydrocarbon?

A. CH3-CH=CH-CH3
B. CH3-CH2-CH=CH2
C. (CH3)2C=C(CH3)2
D. CH3-CH2-C≡CH
Answer: C

Given the percentage of carbon (85.7%) and molar mass (56 g/mol), the empirical formula of the hydrocarbon is calculated as C4H8. The molecular formula is also C4H8, indicating an alkene. Ozonolysis yielding only one product suggests the alkene is symmetrical and the product is a ketone, which rules out options A, B, and D. Option C, (CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 (2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene), is a symmetrical alkene that produces acetone (a ketone) as the sole product upon ozonolysis, confirming it as the correct structure.
