NEET Chemistry Practice: Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids (Part 1 of 25)

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids as per the NEET Chemistry syllabus. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Choose the correct option for each question.

1. The product formed when propanal is treated with methylmagnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis is:

A. 2-Butanol
B. 1-Propanol
C. 2-Methyl-2-propanol
D. 2-Pentanol
Answer: A

Propanal (an aldehyde) reacts with methylmagnesium bromide (a Grignard reagent) to form a secondary alcohol, 2-butanol, after hydrolysis. The nucleophilic addition of the Grignard reagent to the carbonyl group followed by hydrolysis yields the alcohol.

2. Which of the following carboxylic acids does NOT undergo decarboxylation on heating?

A. Malonic acid
B. Acetic acid
C. Oxalic acid
D. β-Keto acids
Answer: B

Acetic acid does not undergo decarboxylation on heating because it lacks the structural requirement (e.g., a β-keto group or a malonic acid structure) for easy loss of CO₂. Malonic acid, oxalic acid, and β-keto acids readily decarboxylate upon heating.

3. The compound formed when ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid is:

A. Ethyl ethanoate
B. Ethyl methanoate
C. Diethyl ether
D. Ethanoic anhydride
Answer: A

The reaction between ethanoic acid and ethanol in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid is an esterification reaction, producing ethyl ethanoate (an ester) and water. This is a classic example of Fischer esterification.

4. The reaction of benzaldehyde with concentrated sodium hydroxide solution results in:

A. Cannizzaro reaction producing benzyl alcohol and benzoic acid
B. Aldol condensation producing cinnamaldehyde
C. Formation of benzophenone
D. No reaction
Answer: A

Benzaldehyde undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction in the presence of concentrated sodium hydroxide. This disproportionation reaction produces benzyl alcohol (a reduction product) and benzoic acid (an oxidation product). Aldehydes without α-hydrogens undergo this reaction.

5. The product formed when propanoic acid is heated with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) is:

A. Ethane
B. Propane
C. Methane
D. Ethene
Answer: A

When a carboxylic acid is heated with soda lime (a mixture of NaOH and CaO), it undergoes decarboxylation to form an alkane with one less carbon atom. Propanoic acid (3 carbons) decarboxylates to form ethane (2 carbons).

6. Which carboxylic acid derivative is the most reactive towards nucleophilic acyl substitution?

A. Ester
B. Acid anhydride
C. Acid chloride
D. Amide
Answer: C

Acid chlorides are the most reactive carboxylic acid derivatives due to the highly electronegative chlorine atom, which creates a strong partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon, making it highly susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

7. Which reagent is used to convert a carboxylic acid directly into an ester without the formation of an acid chloride intermediate?

A. SOCl₂ followed by alcohol
B. Alcohol in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄
C. P₂O₅ followed by alcohol
D. Diazomethane (CH₂N₂)
Answer: D

Diazomethane is a mild reagent that converts carboxylic acids directly into methyl esters without the need for acid chloride formation. The other options either require an intermediate or are not direct methods.

8. Which of the following compounds will not undergo decarboxylation on heating?

A. Malonic acid
B. Acetoacetic acid
C. Benzoic acid
D. Oxalic acid
Answer: C

Benzoic acid is a stable aromatic carboxylic acid and does not undergo decarboxylation on heating. Malonic acid, acetoacetic acid, and oxalic acid are β-keto acids or 1,1-dicarboxylic acids, which readily decarboxylate upon heating.

9. The correct sequence of steps in the mechanism of nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl compound is:

A. Nucleophilic attack → Protonation → Formation of tetrahedral intermediate
B. Protonation → Nucleophilic attack → Formation of tetrahedral intermediate
C. Formation of tetrahedral intermediate → Nucleophilic attack → Protonation
D. Nucleophilic attack → Formation of tetrahedral intermediate → Protonation
Answer: D

The mechanism of nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl compound involves: (1) Nucleophilic attack on the carbonyl carbon, (2) Formation of a tetrahedral intermediate, and (3) Protonation of the intermediate to yield the final product.

10. In the reaction sequence below, compound X is treated with aqueous NaOH followed by acidification to yield a product Y. Which of the following correctly identifies Y when X is acetaldehyde?

A. Ethanol
B. 3-Hydroxybutanal
C. Butanoic acid
D. Acetone
Answer: B

Acetaldehyde (X) undergoes aldol condensation in the presence of aqueous NaOH. Two molecules of acetaldehyde combine to form 3-hydroxybutanal (aldol product). Upon acidification, the aldol product remains stable and does not dehydrate under these conditions. Thus, the product Y is 3-hydroxybutanal. This reaction is a classic example of nucleophilic addition in aldehydes lacking α-hydrogen in one reactant, but acetaldehyde has α-hydrogens, enabling self-condensation.
