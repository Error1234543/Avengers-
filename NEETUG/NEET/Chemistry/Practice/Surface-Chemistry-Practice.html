1. The extent of adsorption of a gas on a solid depends on which of the following factors?

A. Temperature only
B. Pressure only
C. Nature of the gas and the solid, temperature, and pressure
D. Color of the gas and the solid
Answer: C

The extent of adsorption depends on the nature of the gas (e.g., ease of liquefaction) and the solid (e.g., surface area and porosity), as well as temperature and pressure. Higher pressure and lower temperature generally favor adsorption, while color is irrelevant.

2. The process of removing an adsorbed substance from the surface of a solid is called:

A. Sorption
B. Desorption
C. Absorption
D. Chemisorption
Answer: B

Desorption is the process of removing an adsorbed substance from the surface of a solid. It is the reverse of adsorption and can be achieved by increasing temperature or decreasing pressure.

3. In the context of adsorption, what is the role of activated charcoal?

A. It acts as a catalyst for chemical reactions.
B. It is used as an adsorbent due to its high surface area.
C. It increases the temperature of the system to enhance adsorption.
D. It is used to desorb gases from the surface of solids.
Answer: B

Activated charcoal is widely used as an adsorbent due to its highly porous structure and large surface area, which allows it to adsorb a wide range of gases and solutes from solutions.

4. Which of the following is an application of adsorption in everyday life?

A. The use of silica gel in packaging to control humidity
B. The use of chlorine in water purification
C. The use of sulfuric acid in lead-acid batteries
D. The use of sodium hydroxide in soap making
Answer: A

Silica gel is commonly used in packaging to control humidity by adsorbing moisture from the air. This is an example of the practical application of adsorption in everyday life.

5. The plot of log(x/m) versus log(P) for the Freundlich adsorption isotherm is:

A. A straight line with a slope of 1/n and intercept of log(k)
B. A parabola opening upwards
C. A hyperbola
D. A straight line with a slope of n and intercept of k
Answer: A

The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by x/m = kP^(1/n). Taking the logarithm of both sides, log(x/m) = log(k) + (1/n)log(P). This equation represents a straight line with a slope of 1/n and an intercept of log(k) when log(x/m) is plotted against log(P).

6. Which of the following statements about enzymes as biological catalysts is correct?

A. Enzymes increase the activation energy of biochemical reactions.
B. Enzymes are non-specific and can catalyze any reaction.
C. Enzymes are proteins that catalyze biochemical reactions with high specificity.
D. Enzymes are consumed during the reaction and must be continuously synthesized.
Answer: C

Enzymes are biological catalysts, typically proteins, that speed up biochemical reactions by lowering the activation energy. They are highly specific to their substrates and are not consumed during the reaction, allowing them to be reused.

7. Which of the following is an example of a positive catalyst in a catalytic reaction?

A. Lead in the hydrogenation of alkenes.
B. Iron in the Haber process for ammonia synthesis.
C. Glycerol in the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.
D. Quartz in the oxidation of sulfur dioxide.
Answer: B

Iron acts as a positive catalyst in the Haber process, speeding up the synthesis of ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen. Lead poisons catalysts, glycerol is a negative catalyst for hydrogen peroxide decomposition, and quartz is inert in the oxidation of sulfur dioxide.

8. The process of desorption in adsorption isotherms can be best described as:

A. The removal of the adsorbate from the surface of the adsorbent.
B. The formation of a chemical bond between the adsorbate and adsorbent.
C. The increase in the concentration of the adsorbate on the surface.
D. The diffusion of the adsorbate into the bulk of the adsorbent.
Answer: A

Desorption is the reverse of adsorption, where the adsorbate molecules leave the surface of the adsorbent. It is an endothermic process and occurs when conditions like temperature or pressure change.

9. Which type of adsorption isotherm is characterized by a plateau indicating the formation of a monolayer?

A. Freundlich adsorption isotherm.
B. Langmuir adsorption isotherm.
C. BET adsorption isotherm.
D. Temkin adsorption isotherm.
Answer: B

The Langmuir adsorption isotherm assumes monolayer adsorption and reaches a plateau when the surface is fully covered by a unimolecular layer of the adsorbate. The BET isotherm accounts for multilayer adsorption.

10. In the context of colloids, what is the role of an emulsifying agent?

A. It increases the surface tension between two immiscible liquids.
B. It stabilizes the emulsion by forming a protective layer around the dispersed droplets.
C. It causes the coagulation of the colloidal particles.
D. It converts a lyophilic colloid into a lyophobic colloid.
Answer: B

An emulsifying agent stabilizes an emulsion by reducing the interfacial tension between two immiscible liquids and forming a protective layer around the dispersed droplets, preventing them from coalescing.

11. The Hardy-Schulze rule states that the coagulating power of an electrolyte for a colloidal solution depends on:

A. The concentration of the electrolyte only.
B. The valency of the active ion carrying a charge opposite to that of the colloidal particles.
C. The size of the electrolyte molecules.
D. The temperature of the colloidal solution.
Answer: B

According to the Hardy-Schulze rule, the coagulating power of an electrolyte is primarily determined by the valency of the active ion (counter ion) that carries a charge opposite to that of the colloidal particles. Higher valency ions are more effective in causing coagulation.

12. Which of the following is NOT a method for the preparation of colloids?

A. Mechanical dispersion using a colloid mill.
B. Peptization of a precipitate with an electrolyte.
C. Coagulation of a lyophilic sol by adding an electrolyte.
D. Condensation methods like reduction or hydrolysis.
Answer: C

Coagulation is a method of destroying a colloidal solution, not preparing it. Methods for preparing colloids include mechanical dispersion (e.g., colloid mill), peptization, and condensation methods (e.g., reduction, hydrolysis).

13. The Tyndall effect is observed when:

A. Light is absorbed by the colloidal particles.
B. Light is scattered by the colloidal particles.
C. Light passes through the colloidal solution without any interaction.
D. The colloidal particles settle down due to gravity.
Answer: B

The Tyndall effect occurs when light is scattered by the particles in a colloidal solution. This scattering makes the path of the light visible, which is not observed in true solutions due to the smaller size of solute particles.

14. Which of the following statements about lyophilic and lyophobic colloids is correct?

A. Lyophilic colloids are irreversible, while lyophobic colloids are reversible.
B. Lyophilic colloids are easily coagulated by adding electrolytes, unlike lyophobic colloids.
C. Lyophilic colloids are more stable and can be re-dispersed after coagulation, while lyophobic colloids cannot.
D. Lyophobic colloids have a higher viscosity compared to lyophilic colloids.
Answer: C

Lyophilic colloids are more stable and can be re-dispersed after coagulation because they have a strong affinity for the dispersion medium. Lyophobic colloids, on the other hand, are less stable and cannot be easily re-dispersed once coagulated.

15. A catalyst alters the rate of a chemical reaction by:

A. Shifting the equilibrium position toward the products.
B. Providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy.
C. Increasing the concentration of reactants at the surface.
D. Changing the standard Gibbs free energy (ΔG°) of the reaction.
Answer: B

A catalyst speeds up a reaction by providing an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy, allowing more molecules to overcome the energy barrier. It does not alter the equilibrium position (Option A), change reactant concentrations (Option C), or affect the standard Gibbs free energy (ΔG°) of the reaction (Option D).

16. The phenomenon of adsorption is preferred over absorption in which of the following scenarios?

A. When a gas is uniformly distributed throughout the bulk of a solid.
B. When a substance is concentrated only at the surface of another substance rather than throughout its bulk.
C. When a liquid is dissolved in another liquid to form a homogeneous solution.
D. When a solid is dissolved in a liquid to form a colloidal solution.
Answer: B

Adsorption is the process where atoms, ions, or molecules (adsorbate) adhere to the surface of a material (adsorbent), leading to a higher concentration at the surface rather than uniform distribution throughout the bulk. This distinguishes it from absorption, where the substance is uniformly distributed throughout the bulk. Option B correctly describes adsorption, while the other options describe absorption or other processes like dissolution or colloid formation.
