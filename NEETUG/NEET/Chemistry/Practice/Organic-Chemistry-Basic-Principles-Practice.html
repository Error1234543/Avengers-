NEET Chemistry Practice - Organic Chemistry: Basic Principles (Part 1)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) focused on the fundamental principles of Organic Chemistry as per the NEET syllabus. Each question has four options, and only one is correct. Choose the most appropriate answer. Negative marking applies as per NEET guidelines (+4 for correct, -1 for incorrect).

1. Which of the following functional groups is characterized by a carbon atom double-bonded to an oxygen atom and single-bonded to a hydroxyl group?

A. Aldehyde
B. Ketone
C. Carboxylic acid
D. Ester
Answer: C

A carboxylic acid functional group (-COOH) consists of a carbonyl group (C=O) and a hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to the same carbon atom. Aldehydes (-CHO) and ketones (C=O) lack the hydroxyl group, while esters (-COOR) have an alkoxy group instead of the hydroxyl group.

2. In the reaction: CH₃-CH=CH₂ + HBr → Product, the major product formed under normal conditions is:

A. CH₃-CH₂-CH₂Br
B. CH₃-CHBr-CH₃
C. CH₂Br-CH=CH₂
D. CH₃-CH₂-CH₃
Answer: B

This is an example of electrophilic addition following Markovnikov's rule, where the hydrogen atom (from HBr) adds to the carbon with the greater number of hydrogen atoms, and the bromide ion adds to the carbon with fewer hydrogen atoms. Thus, the major product is 2-Bromopropane (CH₃-CHBr-CH₃).

3. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of free radical reactions?

A. They are initiated by heat or light.
B. They proceed via a chain mechanism involving initiation, propagation, and termination steps.
C. They are highly stereospecific and favor the formation of a single stereoisomer.
D. They often result in the formation of racemic mixtures in chiral molecules.
Answer: C

Free radical reactions are not stereospecific; they typically produce racemic mixtures when chiral centers are involved due to the planar nature of free radicals. They are initiated by heat/light, proceed via a chain mechanism, and are not selective for stereoisomer formation.

4. The compound with the molecular formula C₄H₈ can represent all of the following EXCEPT:

A. An alkene
B. A cycloalkane
C. An alkyne
D. A combination of isomers
Answer: C

The molecular formula C₄H₈ corresponds to alkenes (e.g., butene) and cycloalkanes (e.g., cyclobutane). Alkynes have the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₋₂, so C₄H₈ cannot represent an alkyne (which would require C₄H₆).

5. In the homologous series of alkanes, the general formula is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. What is the molecular formula of the alkane containing 6 carbon atoms?

A. C₆H₁₂
B. C₆H₁₄
C. C₆H₁₀
D. C₆H₆
Answer: B

Using the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ for alkanes, substituting n = 6 gives C₆H₁₄. This is the molecular formula for hexane, the alkane with 6 carbon atoms.

6. Which of the following statements correctly describes the inductive effect?

A. It is a permanent effect caused by the complete transfer of electrons in a sigma bond.
B. It is a temporary effect observed only in conjugated systems.
C. It involves the polarization of a sigma bond due to the electronegativity difference between bonded atoms, diminishing with distance.
D. It is responsible for the stability of free radicals and carbocations only.
Answer: C

The inductive effect is a permanent electronic effect where the polarization of a sigma bond occurs due to the electronegativity difference between bonded atoms. This effect weakens as the distance from the substituent increases. It influences the distribution of electron density in molecules and affects properties like acidity and basicity.

7. Which of the following reagents is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones via the Tollens' test?

A. 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine
B. Ammoniacal silver nitrate
C. Fehling's solution
D. Bromine water
Answer: B

Tollens' test uses ammoniacal silver nitrate to distinguish aldehydes from ketones. Aldehydes reduce Ag+ to metallic silver (silver mirror), while ketones do not react. Fehling's solution is another test for aldehydes but is less commonly used for this distinction in NEET.

8. The correct order of acidity for the following compounds is: (I) CH3COOH (II) C6H5COOH (III) CH3CH2OH (IV) C6H5OH

A. III < IV < I < II
B. III < I < IV < II
C. IV < III < I < II
D. III < IV < II < I
Answer: A

The acidity order is: alcohols (III, CH3CH2OH) < phenols (IV, C6H5OH) < carboxylic acids (I, CH3COOH < II, C6H5COOH). Benzoic acid (II) is more acidic than acetic acid (I) due to the electron-withdrawing phenyl group.

9. Which of the following reactions involves the formation of a carbocation intermediate?

A. SN2 reaction
B. E2 elimination
C. Friedel-Crafts alkylation
D. Hydroboration-oxidation
Answer: C

Friedel-Crafts alkylation involves the formation of a carbocation intermediate when an alkyl halide reacts with a Lewis acid (e.g., AlCl3). SN2 is a concerted reaction, E2 is a one-step elimination, and hydroboration-oxidation proceeds via a syn addition mechanism.

10. Which of the following statements about hyperconjugation is correct?

A. It involves the interaction of sigma electrons with a pi system or an empty p-orbital.
B. It is only observed in alkenes and not in carbocations.
C. It decreases the stability of carbocations.
D. It requires the presence of a double bond adjacent to a single bond.
Answer: A

Hyperconjugation involves the delocalization of sigma electrons (usually C-H bonds) with an adjacent pi system or empty p-orbital, which stabilizes the molecule. It is commonly observed in carbocations and alkenes, increasing their stability.

11. In the IUPAC nomenclature of organic compounds, which of the following correctly represents the structure of 3-ethyl-2,4-dimethylpentane?

A. CH3-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH(CH3)-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH3
B. CH3-CH(CH3)-CH(C2H5)-CH(CH3)-CH3
C. CH3-CH2-CH(CH3)-C(CH3)2-CH2-CH3
D. CH3-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH(CH3)-CH(C2H5)-CH3
Answer: B

The IUPAC name 3-ethyl-2,4-dimethylpentane indicates a pentane (5-carbon) parent chain with ethyl and methyl substituents. The correct structure must have: (1) a 5-carbon main chain, (2) methyl groups on carbons 2 and 4, and (3) an ethyl group on carbon 3. Option B correctly places the ethyl group on carbon 3 and methyl groups on carbons 2 and 4, while maintaining the pentane backbone. Option A has 7 carbons, Option C has incorrect branching, and Option D has the ethyl group misplaced, making them invalid representations.
