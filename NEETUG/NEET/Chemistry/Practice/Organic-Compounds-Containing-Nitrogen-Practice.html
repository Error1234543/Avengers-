NEET Chemistry Practice - Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen (Part 1)

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen, aligned with the NEET syllabus and difficulty level. Each question has four options, a single correct answer, and a detailed explanation. Time yourself for 12 minutes (1 minute per question) to simulate exam conditions.

1. The reaction of aniline with bromine water at room temperature yields:

A. 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline as a white precipitate
B. Bromobenzene
C. p-Bromoaniline
D. N-Bromoaniline
Answer: A

Aniline is highly activating due to the lone pair on nitrogen, leading to electrophilic aromatic substitution at the ortho and para positions. With excess bromine water, 2,4,6-tribromoaniline forms as a white precipitate. This reaction is characteristic of aniline's reactivity.

2. In the Gabriel phthalimide synthesis, which of the following is used to liberate the primary amine from the phthalimide intermediate?

A. Hydrazine
B. Aqueous NaOH
C. Conc. H₂SO₄
D. Alcoholic KOH
Answer: A

In the Gabriel phthalimide synthesis, the phthalimide intermediate is hydrolyzed using hydrazine (NH₂NH₂) to liberate the primary amine. Aqueous NaOH is used in the initial step to form the potassium phthalimide salt, but hydrazine is required for the final liberation of the amine.

3. Which of the following amines will NOT give a positive carbylamine test?

A. Methylamine
B. Aniline
C. N-Methylaniline
D. Dimethylamine
Answer: D

The carbylamine test is positive for primary amines (both aliphatic and aromatic) when heated with chloroform and alcoholic KOH, producing isocyanides (carbylamines) with a foul odor. Secondary and tertiary amines do not give this test. Dimethylamine is a secondary amine and will not produce a positive result.

4. Which of the following reactions is used to convert nitrobenzene to aniline?

A. Reduction with Sn/HCl
B. Oxidation with KMnO₄
C. Nitration with conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄
D. Bromination with Br₂/FeBr₃
Answer: A

Nitrobenzene is reduced to aniline using reducing agents like Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl. This is a standard reduction reaction for nitro groups to amines. The other options involve oxidation, nitration, or bromination, which do not yield aniline.

5. In the preparation of sulfa drugs, the key intermediate is:

A. Sulfonamide
B. Nitrobenzene
C. Aniline
D. Benzene diazonium chloride
Answer: A

Sulfa drugs are derived from sulfonamides, which are prepared by the reaction of sulfonyl chlorides with amines. The key intermediate is the sulfonamide group, which imparts antibacterial properties to these drugs. Aniline and benzene diazonium chloride are precursors but not the direct intermediates for sulfa drugs.

6. Which of the following statements about the nitration of aniline is INCORRECT?

A. Aniline is highly activating and undergoes polysubstitution with nitrating mixture.
B. Aniline can be protected by acetylation before nitration to control the reaction.
C. Direct nitration of aniline yields m-nitroaniline as the major product.
D. The nitration of aniline is carried out at low temperatures to avoid oxidation.
Answer: C

Direct nitration of aniline does not yield m-nitroaniline as the major product. Instead, it leads to polysubstitution (e.g., 2,4,6-trinitroaniline) due to the high activating effect of the amino group. To obtain p-nitroaniline, aniline is first acetylated to protect the amino group, then nitrated, and finally hydrolyzed. The meta product is not favored in this reaction.

7. The reaction of ethylamine with nitrous acid at low temperatures produces:

A. Ethanol and nitrogen gas
B. Ethyl diazonium salt
C. N-Nitrosoethylamine
D. Ethyl isocyanide
Answer: A

Primary aliphatic amines like ethylamine react with nitrous acid (HNO₂) at low temperatures to form alcohols and liberate nitrogen gas. This is a characteristic reaction of primary amines. Ethyl diazonium salts are unstable and not formed under these conditions. N-Nitroso compounds are formed from secondary amines.

8. The reaction of benzamide with Br2 and NaOH (Hofmann bromamide reaction) yields:

A. Aniline
B. Benzylamine
C. Phenyl isocyanate
D. Benzoic acid
Answer: A

The Hofmann bromamide reaction involves the treatment of an amide with Br2 and NaOH, resulting in the loss of the carbonyl carbon and formation of a primary amine with one less carbon atom. Thus, benzamide yields aniline. This is a key method for descending the amine series.

9. The compound formed by the reaction of ethylamine with carbon disulfide followed by mercuric chloride is:

A. Ethyl isothiocyanate
B. Ethyl thiocyanate
C. Ethyl carbamate
D. Ethyl cyanide
Answer: A

Ethylamine reacts with carbon disulfide (CS2) to form a dithiocarbamate salt, which upon treatment with mercuric chloride (HgCl2) yields ethyl isothiocyanate. This is a characteristic reaction of primary amines and is used to prepare isothiocyanates.

10. Which of the following amines will react with nitrous acid to form a diazonium salt that is stable at low temperatures?

A. Methylamine
B. Aniline
C. Dimethylamine
D. Triethylamine
Answer: B

Only primary aromatic amines (e.g., aniline) react with nitrous acid at low temperatures (0-5°C) to form stable diazonium salts. Aliphatic primary amines (e.g., methylamine) form unstable diazonium salts that decompose to alcohols and nitrogen gas. Secondary and tertiary amines do not form diazonium salts.

11. The correct order of nucleophilicity among the following in a polar protic solvent is:

A. CH3O- > OH- > CH3COO- > C6H5O-
B. CH3COO- > CH3O- > OH- > C6H5O-
C. C6H5O- > CH3O- > OH- > CH3COO-
D. OH- > CH3O- > CH3COO- > C6H5O-
Answer: A

In polar protic solvents, nucleophilicity is influenced by solvation and basicity. Smaller, more localized anions (e.g., CH3O-) are less solvated and more nucleophilic than larger or resonance-stabilized anions (e.g., CH3COO- or C6H5O-). The order of nucleophilicity is generally: CH3O- > OH- > CH3COO- > C6H5O-.

12. In the reaction sequence below, compound **P** is treated with Sn/HCl, followed by NaNO₂/HCl at 0–5°C, and then heated with Cu₂Cl₂. The final product formed is:

A. Benzene
B. Chlorobenzene
C. Phenol
D. Nitrobenzene
Answer: B

The reaction sequence describes the conversion of an aromatic nitro compound (**P**, e.g., nitrobenzene) to chlorobenzene via the following steps: 1. **Reduction**: Sn/HCl reduces the nitro group (–NO₂) to an amino group (–NH₂), forming aniline. 2. **Diazotization**: NaNO₂/HCl at 0–5°C converts the amino group into a diazonium salt (C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻). 3. **Sandmeyer Reaction**: Heating the diazonium salt with Cu₂Cl₂ replaces the diazonium group with a chlorine atom, yielding chlorobenzene. This is a classic example of the Sandmeyer reaction, which is specific for introducing halogens (Cl, Br) into aromatic rings. The final product is **chlorobenzene (Option B)**.

13. A compound with the molecular formula C₃H₉N exhibits two distinct peaks in its ¹H NMR spectrum: a triplet (3H) and a quartet (6H). Which of the following structures corresponds to this data?

A. CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ (1-propanamine)
B. (CH₃)₂NCH₃ (N,N-dimethylmethanamine)
C. CH₃CH₂NHCH₃ (N-methylethanamine)
D. CH₃CH₂CH₂NHCH₃ (N-methyl-1-propanamine)
Answer: C

The ¹H NMR data (triplet 3H and quartet 6H) indicates an ethyl group (CH₃CH₂-) attached to a nitrogen with two equivalent methyl groups, which matches N-methylethanamine (CH₃CH₂NHCH₃). The triplet corresponds to the CH₃ of the ethyl group, and the quartet corresponds to the CH₂ of the ethyl group plus the N-CH₃ protons, which are equivalent in this structure. The other options would show different splitting patterns.

14. In the reaction of nitrobenzene with Sn/HCl, the intermediate formed before the final product is:

A. Nitrosobenzene
B. Phenylhydroxylamine
C. Azobenzene
D. Hydrazobenzene
Answer: B

The reduction of nitrobenzene with Sn/HCl proceeds through the formation of phenylhydroxylamine (C₆H₅NHOH) as an intermediate before further reduction to aniline. Nitrosobenzene is an earlier intermediate in some reduction pathways but not the immediate precursor in this reaction. Azobenzene and hydrazobenzene are formed under different conditions (e.g., alkaline reduction).
