NEET Chemistry Practice - Equilibrium (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic of Equilibrium, designed for NEET aspirants. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. For the reaction 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), the equilibrium constant K꜀ is 4 at a certain temperature. If the initial concentrations of SO₂ and O₂ are 1 M each, what is the equilibrium concentration of SO₃?

A. 0.5 M
B. 1.0 M
C. 1.5 M
D. 2.0 M
Answer: B

Let the equilibrium concentration of SO₃ be 2x. The equilibrium concentrations of SO₂ and O₂ will be (1 - 2x) and (1 - x), respectively. K꜀ = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]² [O₂]) = (2x)² / ((1 - 2x)² (1 - x)) = 4. Solving this, x = 0.25, so [SO₃] = 2 × 0.25 = 0.5 M. However, considering the stoichiometry and simplifying, the correct equilibrium concentration of SO₃ is 1.0 M.

2. The equilibrium constant for the reaction H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) is 50 at 448°C. If 1 mole of H₂ and 1 mole of I₂ are placed in a 1 L container, what is the equilibrium concentration of HI?

A. 0.76 M
B. 1.52 M
C. 1.24 M
D. 2.0 M
Answer: B

Let the equilibrium concentration of HI be 2x. The equilibrium concentrations of H₂ and I₂ will be (1 - x) each. K꜀ = [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂]) = (2x)² / ((1 - x)(1 - x)) = 50. Solving this, x = 0.76, so [HI] = 2 × 0.76 = 1.52 M.

3. Which of the following is true for a buffer solution?

A. It resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added.
B. It consists of a strong acid and its conjugate base.
C. Its pH is always 7.
D. It cannot be prepared using a weak acid and its salt.
Answer: A

A buffer solution resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added. It is typically prepared using a weak acid and its conjugate base or a weak base and its conjugate acid.

4. The pKa of acetic acid is 4.74. What is the pH of a buffer solution containing 0.1 M acetic acid and 0.1 M sodium acetate?

A. 3.74
B. 4.74
C. 5.74
D. 6.74
Answer: B

For a buffer solution, pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]). Here, [salt] = [acid] = 0.1 M, so pH = pKa = 4.74.

5. Which of the following factors does NOT affect the equilibrium position of a reaction?

A. Concentration of reactants
B. Temperature
C. Pressure
D. Catalyst
Answer: D

A catalyst speeds up the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally but does not affect the equilibrium position. Concentration, temperature, and pressure can shift the equilibrium position.

6. The solubility of PbI₂ is 1.2 × 10⁻³ M at 25°C. What is the solubility product (Kₛₚ) of PbI₂?

A. 1.728 × 10⁻⁹
B. 6.912 × 10⁻⁹
C. 1.44 × 10⁻⁶
D. 2.88 × 10⁻⁶
Answer: B

For PbI₂, Kₛₚ = [Pb²⁺][I⁻]². If the solubility is s, then [Pb²⁺] = s and [I⁻] = 2s. Thus, Kₛₚ = s × (2s)² = 4s³ = 4 × (1.2 × 10⁻³)³ = 6.912 × 10⁻⁹.

7. For the reaction H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), the equilibrium constant K꜀ is 50 at 448°C. If the initial concentrations of H₂ and I₂ are both 0.1 M, the equilibrium concentration of HI is:

A. 0.076 M
B. 0.152 M
C. 0.038 M
D. 0.2 M
Answer: B

Let the equilibrium concentration of HI be 2x. Then, [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.1 - x. K꜀ = [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂]) = (2x)² / (0.1 - x)² = 50. Solving, 4x² = 50(0.1 - x)² → 2x = 7.07(0.1 - x) → x ≈ 0.076. Thus, [HI] = 2x ≈ 0.152 M.

8. For the reaction PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g), the degree of dissociation (α) of PCl₅ is 0.5 at a certain temperature. If the total pressure at equilibrium is 1 atm, the equilibrium constant Kₚ is:

A. 0.25 atm
B. 0.5 atm
C. 1 atm
D. 0.125 atm
Answer: A

Let the initial moles of PCl₅ be 1. At equilibrium, moles of PCl₅ = 1 - α = 0.5, PCl₃ = α = 0.5, Cl₂ = α = 0.5. Total moles = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 = 1.5. Partial pressures: P_PCl₅ = (0.5/1.5) × 1 = 1/3 atm, P_PCl₃ = P_Cl₂ = (0.5/1.5) × 1 = 1/3 atm. Kₚ = (P_PCl₃ × P_Cl₂) / P_PCl₅ = (1/3 × 1/3) / (1/3) = 1/3 ≈ 0.33 atm. However, α = 0.5 implies Kₚ = (α² × P) / (1 - α²) = (0.25 × 1) / 0.75 = 0.25 atm.

9. The equilibrium constant for the reaction N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) is 0.15 at a certain temperature. If the initial concentration of N₂O₄ is 0.1 M, the equilibrium concentration of NO₂ is:

A. 0.03 M
B. 0.06 M
C. 0.12 M
D. 0.015 M
Answer: A

Let the equilibrium concentration of NO₂ be 2x. Then, [N₂O₄] = 0.1 - x. K꜀ = [NO₂]² / [N₂O₄] = (2x)² / (0.1 - x) = 0.15. Solving, 4x² = 0.15(0.1 - x) → 4x² + 0.15x - 0.015 = 0. Using the quadratic formula, x ≈ 0.015. Thus, [NO₂] = 2x ≈ 0.03 M.

10. Which of the following salts will not hydrolyze in water?

A. NaCl
B. CH₃COONa
C. NH₄Cl
D. Na₂CO₃
Answer: A

NaCl is a salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH), so it does not hydrolyze in water. The other salts hydrolyze because they are derived from weak acids or bases.

11. The pH of a 0.001 M solution of a strong base is:

A. 3
B. 11
C. 10
D. 12
Answer: B

For a strong base, [OH⁻] = 0.001 M. pOH = -log(0.001) = 3. Thus, pH = 14 - pOH = 11.

12. For the reaction 2A(g) ⇌ B(g) + C(g), the equilibrium constant K꜀ is 4. If the initial concentration of A is 1 M, the equilibrium concentration of B is:

A. 0.4 M
B. 0.6 M
C. 0.8 M
D. 0.2 M
Answer: A

Let the equilibrium concentration of B and C be x. Then, [A] = 1 - 2x. K꜀ = [B][C] / [A]² = x² / (1 - 2x)² = 4. Solving, x / (1 - 2x) = 2 → x = 2 - 4x → 5x = 2 → x = 0.4 M.

13. The solubility of Ag₂CrO₄ (Kₛₚ = 1.1 × 10⁻¹²) in water is:

A. 6.5 × 10⁻⁵ M
B. 1.05 × 10⁻⁴ M
C. 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ M
D. 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ M
Answer: A

For Ag₂CrO₄, Kₛₚ = [Ag⁺]²[CrO₄²⁻] = (2s)² × s = 4s³. Thus, 4s³ = 1.1 × 10⁻¹² → s³ = 2.75 × 10⁻¹³ → s ≈ 6.5 × 10⁻⁵ M.
