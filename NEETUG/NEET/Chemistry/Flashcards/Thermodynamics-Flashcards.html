NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the term 'thermodynamics' in the context of chemistry.

Thermodynamics is the branch of physical chemistry that studies the relationships between heat, work, temperature, and energy. It deals with how thermal energy is converted to and from other forms of energy and how it affects matter, particularly in chemical reactions and physical changes. The laws of thermodynamics govern these processes and help predict the spontaneity and equilibrium of reactions.

Card 2

State the **First Law of Thermodynamics** and provide its mathematical expression.

The First Law of Thermodynamics, also known as the Law of Energy Conservation, states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or converted from one form to another. Mathematically, it is expressed as: ΔU = q + w, where ΔU is the change in internal energy of the system, q is the heat added to the system, and w is the work done on the system. For a process at constant pressure, this can also be written as ΔH = q_p, where ΔH is the change in enthalpy.

Card 3

Explain the difference between **open**, **closed**, and **isolated** systems in thermodynamics.

**Open System**: Exchanges both matter and energy with its surroundings (e.g., a boiling pot of water without a lid). **Closed System**: Exchanges only energy (heat or work) with its surroundings, not matter (e.g., a sealed pressure cooker). **Isolated System**: Exchanges neither matter nor energy with its surroundings (e.g., a perfectly insulated thermos flask). Isolated systems are idealized and rarely achieved in practice.

Card 4

What is **enthalpy (H)**? How is it related to internal energy (U)?

Enthalpy (H) is a thermodynamic property defined as the sum of the internal energy (U) of a system and the product of its pressure (P) and volume (V): H = U + PV. It is a state function and is particularly useful for processes occurring at constant pressure, where the change in enthalpy (ΔH) equals the heat absorbed or released by the system (ΔH = q_p). Enthalpy simplifies the analysis of chemical reactions by accounting for work done by the system against constant external pressure.

Card 5

Define **standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f)** and explain its significance.

The standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f) is the change in enthalpy when one mole of a compound is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states (most stable form at 298 K and 1 bar pressure). By definition, ΔH°f for any element in its standard state is zero. ΔH°f values are used to calculate the standard enthalpy change of a reaction (ΔH°rxn) using Hess's Law: ΔH°rxn = Σ ΔH°f(products) - Σ ΔH°f(reactants).

Card 6

What is **Hess's Law**? Provide an example of its application.

Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change for a reaction is the same regardless of the pathway or number of steps taken to achieve the reaction. This is because enthalpy is a state function. Example: To find the enthalpy change for the formation of CO from C and O₂ (C + ½O₂ → CO), we can use the following steps: 1. C + O₂ → CO₂, ΔH = -393.5 kJ/mol 2. CO + ½O₂ → CO₂, ΔH = -283.0 kJ/mol Reversing the second reaction: CO₂ → CO + ½O₂, ΔH = +283.0 kJ/mol Adding the two reactions: C + ½O₂ → CO, ΔH = -393.5 + 283.0 = -110.5 kJ/mol.

Card 7

Explain the concept of **spontaneity** in thermodynamics. What role does Gibbs free energy (G) play?

Spontaneity refers to the tendency of a process to occur without the need for continuous external intervention. A spontaneous process may occur slowly or quickly but proceeds in a direction that increases the overall disorder of the universe. Gibbs free energy (G) is a thermodynamic potential that combines enthalpy (H) and entropy (S) to predict spontaneity: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS, where T is the temperature in Kelvin. For a process at constant temperature and pressure: - If ΔG < 0, the process is spontaneous. - If ΔG = 0, the process is at equilibrium. - If ΔG > 0, the process is non-spontaneous.

Card 8

Define **entropy (S)** and explain its significance in the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**.

Entropy (S) is a measure of the disorder or randomness of a system. The Second Law of Thermodynamics states that for any spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe (system + surroundings) always increases: ΔS_universe = ΔS_system + ΔS_surroundings > 0. Entropy is a state function, and its change (ΔS) for a reversible process is given by ΔS = q_rev / T, where q_rev is the heat transferred reversibly and T is the temperature. In chemical reactions, entropy changes are influenced by factors such as phase transitions, changes in the number of gas molecules, and molecular complexity.

Card 9

What is the **Third Law of Thermodynamics**? How does it relate to entropy?

The Third Law of Thermodynamics states that the entropy of a perfect crystalline substance approaches zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero (0 K). This is because a perfect crystal has only one possible microstate at 0 K, resulting in zero disorder. The law provides a reference point for calculating absolute entropies of substances, which are used to determine entropy changes in chemical reactions: ΔS°rxn = Σ S°(products) - Σ S°(reactants).

Card 10

Explain the difference between **exothermic** and **endothermic** processes. Provide examples.

**Exothermic Process**: Releases heat to the surroundings, resulting in a negative enthalpy change (ΔH < 0). Example: Combustion of methane (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, ΔH = -890 kJ/mol). **Endothermic Process**: Absorbs heat from the surroundings, resulting in a positive enthalpy change (ΔH > 0). Example: Dissolution of ammonium nitrate in water (NH₄NO₃ → NH₄⁺ + NO₃⁻, ΔH = +25.7 kJ/mol).

Card 11

What is **Gibbs free energy change (ΔG)** for a reaction at equilibrium?

At equilibrium, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) for a reaction is zero. This is because the system has reached a state where the rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal, and there is no net change in the concentrations of reactants and products. Mathematically, ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q, where Q is the reaction quotient. At equilibrium, Q = K (the equilibrium constant), and ΔG = 0, so ΔG° = -RT ln K.

Card 12

How does temperature affect the spontaneity of a reaction? Use the Gibbs free energy equation to explain.

The spontaneity of a reaction depends on the sign of ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. Temperature (T) plays a critical role in determining whether a reaction is spontaneous: 1. If ΔH < 0 and ΔS > 0: ΔG is always negative, so the reaction is spontaneous at all temperatures. 2. If ΔH > 0 and ΔS < 0: ΔG is always positive, so the reaction is non-spontaneous at all temperatures. 3. If ΔH < 0 and ΔS < 0: ΔG is negative at low temperatures (spontaneous) but becomes positive at high temperatures (non-spontaneous). 4. If ΔH > 0 and ΔS > 0: ΔG is positive at low temperatures (non-spontaneous) but becomes negative at high temperatures (spontaneous).

Card 13

What is the **standard Gibbs free energy of formation (ΔG°f)**? How is it used to calculate ΔG° for a reaction?

The standard Gibbs free energy of formation (ΔG°f) is the change in Gibbs free energy when one mole of a compound is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states. By definition, ΔG°f for any element in its standard state is zero. The standard Gibbs free energy change for a reaction (ΔG°rxn) is calculated using ΔG°f values: ΔG°rxn = Σ ΔG°f(products) - Σ ΔG°f(reactants). This value helps predict the spontaneity of the reaction under standard conditions.
