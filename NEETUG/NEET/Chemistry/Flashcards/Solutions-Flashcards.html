Card 1

Define a solution and classify it based on the physical states of solute and solvent.

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances, where the solute is uniformly dispersed in the solvent. Based on physical states: - **Solid in Liquid**: Sugar in water (e.g., syrup). - **Liquid in Liquid**: Ethanol in water (e.g., alcoholic beverages). - **Gas in Liquid**: Carbon dioxide in water (e.g., soda). - **Solid in Solid**: Alloys like brass (copper and zinc). - **Liquid in Solid**: Amalgams (e.g., mercury in silver). - **Gas in Solid**: Hydrogen in palladium. - **Gas in Gas**: Air (oxygen and nitrogen). Key: The solvent determines the phase of the solution.

Card 2

What is the difference between molarity and molality? Provide formulas and units.

**Molarity (M)**: Moles of solute per liter of solution. Formula: M = (moles of solute) / (volume of solution in liters). Units: mol/L. **Molality (m)**: Moles of solute per kilogram of solvent. Formula: m = (moles of solute) / (mass of solvent in kg). Units: mol/kg. Key difference: Molarity depends on temperature (volume changes), while molality is temperature-independent (mass remains constant).

Card 3

Explain Raoult’s Law for ideal solutions. What are its limitations?

**Raoult’s Law**: The partial vapor pressure of a component in a solution is proportional to its mole fraction in the solution. Formula: P_A = X_A * P_A°, where P_A = partial pressure, X_A = mole fraction, P_A° = vapor pressure of pure component. **Limitations**: - Applies only to **ideal solutions** (solute-solvent interactions ≈ solute-solute/solvent-solvent interactions). - Fails for **non-ideal solutions** (e.g., ethanol-water mixtures show positive/negative deviations due to hydrogen bonding). - Not valid for **volatile solutes** or **electrolytes** (dissociation affects mole fractions).

Card 4

Calculate the molarity of a solution containing 5.85 g of NaCl in 500 mL of solution. (Molar mass of NaCl = 58.5 g/mol)

Step 1: Calculate moles of NaCl. Moles = mass / molar mass = 5.85 g / 58.5 g/mol = 0.1 mol. Step 2: Convert volume to liters. 500 mL = 0.5 L. Step 3: Calculate molarity. M = moles / volume = 0.1 mol / 0.5 L = **0.2 M**.

Card 5

What is the van’t Hoff factor (i)? How does it affect colligative properties?

**Van’t Hoff factor (i)**: Ratio of the observed colligative property to the expected value for a non-electrolyte. Formula: i = (observed colligative property) / (expected value). **Effect on colligative properties**: - For **non-electrolytes** (e.g., glucose), i = 1 (no dissociation). - For **electrolytes** (e.g., NaCl), i > 1 (dissociation increases particle count). - For **association** (e.g., acetic acid dimers), i < 1. Example: NaCl dissociates into Na⁺ and Cl⁻, so i ≈ 2 for dilute solutions.

Card 6

Derive the formula for elevation in boiling point (ΔT_b) for a non-volatile solute.

**Derivation**: 1. Boiling point elevation is proportional to the molality (m) of the solution: ΔT_b ∝ m. 2. Introduce the ebullioscopic constant (K_b): ΔT_b = K_b * m. 3. For electrolytes, account for van’t Hoff factor (i): ΔT_b = i * K_b * m. **Formula**: ΔT_b = i * K_b * m. **Variables**: - ΔT_b = boiling point elevation (K or °C). - K_b = ebullioscopic constant (K·kg/mol). - m = molality (mol/kg). - i = van’t Hoff factor (dimensionless).

Card 7

A solution of 1.2 g urea (NH₂CONH₂) in 100 g water freezes at -0.372°C. Calculate the molar mass of urea. (K_f for water = 1.86 K·kg/mol)

Step 1: Use freezing point depression formula. ΔT_f = i * K_f * m. For urea (non-electrolyte), i = 1. ΔT_f = 0.372°C (since pure water freezes at 0°C). Step 2: Calculate molality (m). m = ΔT_f / (i * K_f) = 0.372 / 1.86 = 0.2 mol/kg. Step 3: Relate molality to moles of urea. m = moles of urea / mass of water (kg). 0.2 = moles / 0.1 kg → moles = 0.02 mol. Step 4: Calculate molar mass. Molar mass = mass / moles = 1.2 g / 0.02 mol = **60 g/mol**.

Card 8

What is osmotic pressure? Write the formula and explain its significance.

**Osmotic pressure (π)**: Pressure required to stop osmosis (flow of solvent through a semi-permeable membrane from dilute to concentrated solution). **Formula**: π = i * C * R * T. **Variables**: - π = osmotic pressure (atm or Pa). - i = van’t Hoff factor. - C = molar concentration (mol/L). - R = gas constant (0.0821 L·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹). - T = temperature (K). **Significance**: - Determines **cell turgor pressure** in biology. - Used to calculate **molar masses of polymers** (e.g., proteins). - Critical in **reverse osmosis** for water purification.

Card 9

Explain the concept of azeotropes with examples.

**Azeotropes**: Liquid mixtures that boil at a constant temperature and have the same composition in liquid and vapor phases. **Types**: 1. **Minimum boiling azeotrope**: Boils at a temperature lower than either pure component (e.g., ethanol-water, 95.6% ethanol, 4.4% water, bp = 78.2°C). 2. **Maximum boiling azeotrope**: Boils at a temperature higher than either pure component (e.g., nitric acid-water, 68% HNO₃, 32% water, bp = 120.5°C). **Key point**: Cannot be separated by simple distillation due to constant boiling point.

Card 10

How does the solubility of gases in liquids depend on temperature and pressure? State Henry’s Law.

**Henry’s Law**: The solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid. Formula: S = k_H * P, where S = solubility, k_H = Henry’s law constant, P = partial pressure. **Temperature effect**: Solubility of gases **decreases** with increasing temperature (exothermic process). **Pressure effect**: Solubility **increases** with increasing pressure (e.g., carbonated drinks). **Example**: Oxygen solubility in water decreases at higher temperatures, affecting aquatic life.

Card 11

What are colligative properties? List them and explain their dependence on solute concentration.

**Colligative properties**: Properties of solutions that depend on the **number of solute particles**, not their identity. **List**: 1. **Vapor pressure lowering** (Raoult’s Law). 2. **Boiling point elevation** (ΔT_b = i * K_b * m). 3. **Freezing point depression** (ΔT_f = i * K_f * m). 4. **Osmotic pressure** (π = i * C * R * T). **Dependence**: All colligative properties are **directly proportional** to the molality/molarity of the solute (adjusted by van’t Hoff factor for electrolytes). **Key**: Used to determine molar masses of unknown solutes.
