Card 1

Define environmental chemistry and state its primary focus areas.

Environmental chemistry is the scientific study of chemical and biochemical phenomena occurring in natural environments. Its primary focus areas include: 1. **Atmospheric Chemistry**: Study of chemical processes in the atmosphere, including pollution and climate change. 2. **Hydrosphere Chemistry**: Analysis of chemical composition and reactions in water bodies. 3. **Soil Chemistry**: Examination of chemical interactions in soil, including nutrient cycling and contamination. 4. **Pollution Control**: Identification, monitoring, and mitigation of pollutants in air, water, and soil. 5. **Green Chemistry**: Development of sustainable chemical processes to minimize environmental impact.

Card 2

What are the major layers of the Earth's atmosphere, and which layer contains the ozone layer?

The Earth's atmosphere is divided into five major layers: 1. **Troposphere** (0–12 km): Contains weather systems and most atmospheric mass. 2. **Stratosphere** (12–50 km): Contains the **ozone layer**, which absorbs harmful UV radiation. 3. **Mesosphere** (50–85 km): Where meteors burn up. 4. **Thermosphere** (85–600 km): Contains the ionosphere and auroras. 5. **Exosphere** (600+ km): Transition to outer space. The **ozone layer** is located in the **stratosphere**.

Explain the formation and depletion of ozone in the stratosphere. Include the role of CFCs.

**Formation of Ozone (O₃):** 1. UV radiation splits oxygen molecules (O₂) into oxygen atoms (O). 2. Oxygen atoms (O) react with O₂ to form ozone (O₃): O₂ + UV → 2O O + O₂ → O₃ **Depletion of Ozone:** 1. **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** release chlorine atoms (Cl) when exposed to UV radiation: CFCl₃ + UV → CFCl₂ + Cl 2. Chlorine atoms catalyze the breakdown of ozone: Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂ ClO + O → Cl + O₂ (regenerates Cl, allowing it to destroy more O₃). 3. One chlorine atom can destroy **thousands of ozone molecules**, leading to ozone layer depletion.

Card 4

What is the Montreal Protocol, and what was its significance in environmental chemistry?

The **Montreal Protocol** (1987) is an international treaty designed to phase out the production and consumption of **ozone-depleting substances (ODS)**, such as: - Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) - Halons - Carbon tetrachloride - Methyl chloroform **Significance:** 1. **Global Cooperation**: First treaty ratified by all UN member states. 2. **Ozone Layer Recovery**: Reduced ODS emissions, leading to gradual recovery of the ozone layer. 3. **Climate Benefits**: Many ODS are potent greenhouse gases; their phase-out also mitigated climate change. 4. **Scientific Validation**: Demonstrated that global environmental problems can be addressed through policy.

Card 5

Define **acid rain**, its primary causes, and its environmental effects.

**Definition**: Acid rain refers to precipitation (rain, snow, fog) with a pH below 5.6 due to dissolved acidic pollutants. **Primary Causes:** 1. **Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂)**: Released from burning fossil fuels (coal, oil) and volcanic eruptions. SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ (sulfurous acid) 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid) 2. **Nitrogen Oxides (NOₓ)**: Emitted from vehicle exhaust and power plants. 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ 2NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ (nitric acid) + HNO₂ (nitrous acid) **Environmental Effects:** 1. **Aquatic Ecosystems**: Lowers pH of lakes/streams, harming fish and aquatic life. 2. **Soil Degradation**: Leaches nutrients (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) and releases toxic metals (Al³⁺). 3. **Vegetation Damage**: Weakens plants by damaging leaves and inhibiting nutrient uptake. 4. **Infrastructure Corrosion**: Accelerates weathering of buildings, monuments, and metals.

Card 8

Define **greenhouse effect** and list the major greenhouse gases (GHGs). How do GHGs contribute to global warming?

**Greenhouse Effect**: A natural process where certain gases in Earth's atmosphere trap heat, keeping the planet warmer than it would be without an atmosphere. Human activities have intensified this effect, leading to **global warming**. **Major Greenhouse Gases (GHGs):** 1. **Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)**: Primary contributor (fossil fuels, deforestation). 2. **Methane (CH₄)**: 25–80x more potent than CO₂ (livestock, rice paddies, landfills). 3. **Nitrous Oxide (N₂O)**: 300x more potent than CO₂ (fertilizers, combustion). 4. **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**: Synthetic, also deplete ozone (now regulated). 5. **Water Vapor (H₂O)**: Most abundant GHG, but not directly influenced by human activity. **How GHGs Contribute to Global Warming:** 1. **Infrared Absorption**: GHGs absorb and re-emit infrared radiation (heat) emitted by Earth's surface. 2. **Radiative Forcing**: Increased GHG concentrations enhance the greenhouse effect, trapping more heat. 3. **Positive Feedback Loops**: Warming melts ice (reducing albedo), releases methane from permafrost, and increases water vapor (a GHG).

Card 9

What is **smog**? Differentiate between classical (London) smog and photochemical (Los Angeles) smog.

**Smog**: A type of air pollution that reduces visibility and harms health. Two main types: | Feature | **Classical (London) Smog** | **Photochemical (Los Angeles) Smog** | |----------------------|------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------| | **Primary Pollutants** | SO₂, particulate matter (soot), fog | NOₓ, VOCs (volatile organic compounds) | | **Secondary Pollutants** | Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) | Ozone (O₃), PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate) | | **Formation Conditions** | Cold, humid weather (winter) | Warm, sunny weather (summer) | | **Chemical Process** | SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (acidic) | NO₂ + sunlight → NO + O (O + O₂ → O₃) | | **Health Effects** | Respiratory irritation, bronchitis | Eye irritation, lung damage, asthma | | **Visibility** | Grayish-black haze | Brownish haze | | **Sources** | Coal combustion, industrial emissions | Vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions |
