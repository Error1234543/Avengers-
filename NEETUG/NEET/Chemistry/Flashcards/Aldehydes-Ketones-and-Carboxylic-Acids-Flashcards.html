NEET Chemistry: Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the general formula for aldehydes and ketones?

Aldehydes: R-CHO, where R is hydrogen or an alkyl/aryl group. Ketones: R-CO-R', where R and R' are alkyl or aryl groups. Both contain the carbonyl group (C=O).

Card 2

Name the reagent used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones in a laboratory setting.

Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate). Aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent to form a silver mirror, while ketones do not react.

Card 3

What is the product formed when acetaldehyde undergoes aldol condensation?

3-Hydroxybutanal (aldol). The reaction occurs in the presence of a dilute base, where the alpha-hydrogen of one acetaldehyde molecule adds to the carbonyl carbon of another.

Card 4

Explain the mechanism of nucleophilic addition to carbonyl compounds.

Nucleophilic addition involves the attack of a nucleophile (e.g., CN-, OH-) on the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, forming a tetrahedral intermediate. The pi bond of C=O breaks, and the nucleophile forms a sigma bond with the carbon. The intermediate is then protonated to yield the final product.

Card 5

Why are carboxylic acids more acidic than alcohols?

Carboxylic acids are more acidic due to the resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion (RCOO-). The negative charge is delocalized over two oxygen atoms, making the anion more stable and the acid stronger. Alcohols lack this resonance stabilization.

Card 6

What is the name of the reaction where a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst?

Esterification (Fischer esterification). The reaction produces an ester and water, e.g., ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate + H2O.

Card 7

Identify the product formed when propanoic acid is treated with LiAlH4.

Propan-1-ol. LiAlH4 is a strong reducing agent that reduces carboxylic acids to primary alcohols via the aldehyde intermediate.

Card 8

What is the role of the carbonyl group in the reactivity of aldehydes and ketones?

The carbonyl group (C=O) is polar, with the carbon being electrophilic and the oxygen nucleophilic. This polarity makes the carbonyl carbon susceptible to nucleophilic attack, driving reactions like nucleophilic addition, oxidation, and reduction.

Card 9

Name the reagent used to convert a ketone into a secondary alcohol.

Sodium borohydride (NaBH4) or lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH4). NaBH4 is milder and often preferred for ketones, while LiAlH4 is stronger and reduces carboxylic acids as well.

Card 10

What is the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction?

A reaction where carboxylic acids with alpha-hydrogens are brominated at the alpha-carbon using Br2 and PBr3 (or red phosphorus). The product is an alpha-bromo carboxylic acid, e.g., propanoic acid → 2-bromopropanoic acid.

Card 11

Explain why aldehydes are more reactive than ketones toward nucleophilic addition.

Aldehydes are more reactive due to: (1) Steric effects: Ketones have two alkyl/aryl groups, which hinder nucleophilic attack. (2) Electronic effects: Alkyl groups in ketones donate electron density to the carbonyl carbon, reducing its electrophilicity compared to aldehydes.

Card 12

What is the product of the reaction between benzaldehyde and concentrated NaOH?

Benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate (Cannizzaro reaction). Benzaldehyde lacks alpha-hydrogens, so it undergoes disproportionation in the presence of a strong base to form an alcohol and a carboxylate salt.

Card 13

Name the test used to detect the presence of a carboxylic acid group.

Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) test. Carboxylic acids react with NaHCO3 to produce effervescence (CO2 gas), e.g., RCOOH + NaHCO3 → RCOONa + H2O + CO2.

Card 14

What is the IUPAC name of the compound CH3-CH2-CO-CH2-CH3?

Pentan-3-one. The carbonyl group is at the third carbon in a five-carbon chain, making it a ketone.

Card 15

Describe the decarboxylation reaction of carboxylic acids.

Decarboxylation is the loss of CO2 from a carboxylic acid, typically requiring heat and a catalyst (e.g., soda lime, NaOH + CaO). Beta-keto acids or malonic acid derivatives undergo decarboxylation readily, e.g., malonic acid → acetic acid + CO2.

Card 16

What is the product formed when acetone is treated with iodine and sodium hydroxide?

Iodoform (CHI3) and sodium acetate. This is the iodoform test, where methyl ketones (or alcohols with CH3-CH(OH)- group) yield a yellow precipitate of iodoform.

Card 17

Explain the difference between the Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reductions.

Both reduce carbonyl compounds to hydrocarbons. Clemmensen: Uses Zn(Hg) and HCl (acidic medium), suitable for acid-stable compounds. Wolff-Kishner: Uses hydrazine (NH2NH2) and a base (e.g., KOH), suitable for base-stable compounds.

Card 18

What is the name of the reaction where an aldehyde is oxidized to a carboxylic acid using Tollen's reagent?

Silver mirror test (oxidation of aldehydes). Tollen's reagent oxidizes aldehydes to carboxylate salts while reducing Ag+ to metallic silver, forming a mirror-like deposit.

Card 19

Name the functional group present in carboxylic acids and its key properties.

Carboxyl group (-COOH). Key properties: (1) Highly polar, forms hydrogen bonds. (2) Acidic due to resonance stabilization of the carboxylate anion. (3) Undergoes esterification, reduction, and decarboxylation reactions.

Card 20

What is the product of the reaction between formaldehyde and Grignard reagent (CH3MgBr)?

Primary alcohol (ethanol). Formaldehyde reacts with Grignard reagents to form primary alcohols after hydrolysis, e.g., HCHO + CH3MgBr → CH3-CH2-OMgBr → CH3-CH2-OH.

Card 21

Explain why carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alcohols of comparable molecular weight.

Carboxylic acids form stronger hydrogen-bonded dimers in the liquid state, requiring more energy to break these interactions compared to the single hydrogen bonds in alcohols. This results in higher boiling points.
