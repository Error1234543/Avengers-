NEET Chemistry: Coordination Compounds - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define a coordination compound and give one example.

A coordination compound is a neutral or ionic species in which a central metal ion (usually a transition metal) is bonded to one or more ligands (molecules or ions) through coordinate covalent bonds. Example: [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃, where Co³⁺ is the central metal ion and NH₃ are the ligands.

Card 2

What is the difference between a ligand and a coordination entity?

A ligand is an ion or molecule that donates a pair of electrons to the central metal ion to form a coordinate bond. A coordination entity is the central metal ion along with its surrounding ligands, which may be neutral or charged (e.g., [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻).

Card 3

Explain the term 'coordination number' with an example.

The coordination number is the number of coordinate bonds formed between the central metal ion and its ligands. For example, in [PtCl₆]²⁻, the coordination number of Pt⁴⁺ is 6 because it is bonded to six Cl⁻ ligands.

Card 4

What are the rules for writing the IUPAC name of a coordination compound?

1. Cation is named before the anion. 2. Ligands are named before the metal ion. 3. Ligands are named in alphabetical order (prefixes like di-, tri- are ignored). 4. Anionic ligands end with '-o' (e.g., chloro, cyano). 5. Neutral ligands retain their names (e.g., ammonia, water). 6. The metal ion's oxidation state is indicated in Roman numerals in parentheses. 7. If the complex is anionic, the metal name ends with '-ate'.

Card 5

Name the following coordination compound: [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂.

Pentaamminechlorocobalt(III) chloride.

Card 6

What is the primary valence and secondary valence in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺?

Primary valence (oxidation state) of Co is +3. Secondary valence (coordination number) is 6, as Co is bonded to six NH₃ ligands.

Card 7

Differentiate between monodentate and polydentate ligands with examples.

Monodentate ligands bind to the central metal ion through a single donor atom (e.g., NH₃, Cl⁻). Polydentate ligands bind through multiple donor atoms (e.g., ethylenediamine (en) is bidentate, EDTA is hexadentate).

Card 8

What is chelation? Give an example of a chelating ligand.

Chelation is the formation of a ring structure when a polydentate ligand binds to a central metal ion through two or more donor atoms. Example: Ethylenediamine (en) is a chelating ligand that forms a five-membered ring with the metal ion.

Card 9

Explain the concept of isomerism in coordination compounds with types.

Isomerism in coordination compounds occurs when compounds have the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms. Types: 1. Structural isomerism (ionization, hydrate, linkage, coordination). 2. Stereoisomerism (geometric and optical isomerism).

Card 10

What are ionization isomers? Give an example.

Ionization isomers are coordination compounds that produce different ions in solution. Example: [Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br. The first gives SO₄²⁻ ions, while the second gives Br⁻ ions in solution.

Card 11

Define linkage isomerism with an example.

Linkage isomerism occurs when a ligand can bind to the central metal ion through different donor atoms. Example: Nitrite ligand (NO₂⁻) can bind through nitrogen (nitro, -NO₂) or oxygen (nitrito, -ONO).

Card 12

What is geometric isomerism? Give an example in square planar complexes.

Geometric isomerism occurs when ligands occupy different spatial positions around the central metal ion. In square planar complexes like [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], two isomers exist: cis (adjacent Cl ligands) and trans (opposite Cl ligands).

Card 13

Explain optical isomerism in coordination compounds with an example.

Optical isomerism occurs when a compound exists as non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers). Example: [Co(en)₃]³⁺ exists as two optical isomers that rotate plane-polarized light in opposite directions.

Card 14

What is the crystal field theory (CFT)?

Crystal Field Theory explains the bonding, structure, and color of coordination compounds by considering the electrostatic interaction between the central metal ion and ligands. It assumes ligands are point negative charges that split the d-orbitals of the metal ion into different energy levels.

Card 15

Describe the splitting of d-orbitals in an octahedral complex.

In an octahedral complex, the five d-orbitals split into two sets: 1. Lower energy t₂g set (dxy, dyz, dzx). 2. Higher energy eg set (dx²-y², dz²). The energy difference between them is denoted as Δ₀ (crystal field splitting energy).

Card 16

What is the spectrochemical series? Arrange the following ligands in order of increasing field strength: Cl⁻, CN⁻, H₂O, NH₃.

The spectrochemical series ranks ligands based on their ability to split d-orbitals. Order of increasing field strength: Cl⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < CN⁻.

Card 17

Explain why [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ appears violet in solution.

[Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ absorbs yellow-green light (complementary to violet) due to the d-d transition of electrons from the t₂g to eg orbitals. The energy difference (Δ₀) corresponds to the wavelength of absorbed light, resulting in the violet color observed.

Card 18

What are high-spin and low-spin complexes? Give an example of each.

High-spin complexes have electrons occupying orbitals to maximize unpaired electrons (weak field ligands, small Δ₀). Example: [CoF₆]³⁻. Low-spin complexes have electrons paired in lower energy orbitals (strong field ligands, large Δ₀). Example: [Co(CN)₆]³⁻.

Card 19

Calculate the crystal field stabilization energy (CFSE) for a d⁵ high-spin octahedral complex.

For a d⁵ high-spin octahedral complex: 1. Electrons occupy t₂g³ eg². 2. CFSE = (0.4 × 3 - 0.6 × 2) Δ₀ = (1.2 - 1.2) Δ₀ = 0 Δ₀. No stabilization energy due to half-filled configuration.

Card 20

What is the Jahn-Teller effect? Give an example of a complex showing this effect.

The Jahn-Teller effect occurs in complexes with unevenly filled eg orbitals, causing distortion to lower symmetry and energy. Example: [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (d⁹ configuration) shows elongation along the z-axis.

Card 21

Explain the valence bond theory (VBT) for coordination compounds.

Valence Bond Theory describes bonding in coordination compounds as the overlap of ligand orbitals with hybridized orbitals of the central metal ion. It explains the geometry and magnetic properties of complexes. Example: [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ uses d²sp³ hybridization for octahedral geometry.

Card 22

What is the difference between inner orbital and outer orbital complexes?

Inner orbital complexes use (n-1)d orbitals for hybridization (e.g., d²sp³), resulting in low-spin complexes. Outer orbital complexes use nd orbitals (e.g., sp³d²), resulting in high-spin complexes.

Card 23

Give the hybridization and geometry of [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻.

[Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ has dsp² hybridization (inner orbital complex) and square planar geometry. CN⁻ is a strong field ligand, causing pairing of electrons.

Card 24

What are the applications of coordination compounds in medicine?

1. Cisplatin ([Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]) is used in cancer treatment. 2. EDTA is used to treat lead poisoning. 3. Gadolinium complexes are used as MRI contrast agents. 4. Silver sulfadiazine is used in burn treatments.
