NEET Chemistry - Hydrocarbons Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the general molecular formula for alkanes?

The general molecular formula for alkanes is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, where 'n' represents the number of carbon atoms. Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with single bonds between carbon atoms.

Card 2

Define isomerism in hydrocarbons with an example.

Isomerism is the phenomenon where compounds have the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements. For example, butane (C₄H₁₀) has two isomers: n-butane (straight-chain) and isobutane (branched-chain).

Card 3

What is the primary product of the reaction between methane and chlorine in the presence of sunlight?

The primary product is chloromethane (CH₃Cl), formed via a free radical substitution reaction. Further substitution can yield dichloromethane (CH₂Cl₂), chloroform (CHCl₃), and carbon tetrachloride (CCl₄).

Card 4

Explain Markovnikov’s rule with an example.

Markovnikov’s rule states that in the addition of a protic acid (like HBr) to an unsymmetrical alkene, the hydrogen atom attaches to the carbon with the greater number of hydrogen atoms. For example, in the reaction of propene (CH₃-CH=CH₂) with HBr, the product is 2-bromopropane (CH₃-CHBr-CH₃).

Card 5

What is the difference between homolytic and heterolytic fission?

Homolytic fission involves the breaking of a covalent bond where each atom retains one electron, forming free radicals. Heterolytic fission involves the breaking of a bond where one atom retains both electrons, forming ions (cation and anion).

Card 6

Write the IUPAC name of the following compound: CH₃-CH₂-CH(CH₃)-CH=CH₂.

The IUPAC name is 3-methylpent-1-ene. The longest carbon chain containing the double bond is five carbons, and the methyl group is on the third carbon.

Card 7

What is the role of peroxides in the addition of HBr to alkenes?

Peroxides initiate an anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr to alkenes by generating bromine radicals. This results in the bromine atom attaching to the less substituted carbon, opposite to Markovnikov’s rule.

Card 8

Describe the mechanism of electrophilic addition in alkenes.

Electrophilic addition involves two steps: (1) The π-electrons of the double bond attack an electrophile, forming a carbocation intermediate. (2) A nucleophile attacks the carbocation, forming the final product. For example, in the addition of HBr to ethene, H⁺ acts as the electrophile, forming a carbocation, which is then attacked by Br⁻.

Card 9

What is the product of the ozonolysis of 2-methylbut-2-ene?

The products are acetone (CH₃-CO-CH₃) and acetaldehyde (CH₃-CHO). Ozonolysis cleaves the double bond, forming carbonyl compounds.

Card 10

Explain the term 'aromaticity' with reference to benzene.

Aromaticity refers to the stability of benzene due to its planar, cyclic structure with delocalized π-electrons (4n+2 π-electrons, where n=1 for benzene). This delocalization results in resonance energy, making benzene less reactive than typical alkenes.

Card 11

What is the difference between ortho, meta, and para positions in benzene derivatives?

Ortho (o-), meta (m-), and para (p-) describe the relative positions of substituents on a benzene ring. Ortho refers to adjacent carbons (1,2), meta to carbons separated by one carbon (1,3), and para to carbons opposite each other (1,4).

Card 12

Write the structure of the major product formed when benzene reacts with CH₃Cl in the presence of AlCl₃.

The major product is toluene (C₆H₅-CH₃). This is an example of Friedel-Crafts alkylation, where a methyl group substitutes a hydrogen atom on the benzene ring.

Card 13

What is the significance of the Huckel rule in determining aromaticity?

Huckel’s rule states that a compound is aromatic if it has a planar, cyclic structure with (4n+2) π-electrons, where n is a non-negative integer (e.g., n=1 for benzene with 6 π-electrons). This rule helps predict the stability of aromatic compounds.

Card 14

Explain the mechanism of the Wurtz reaction.

The Wurtz reaction involves the coupling of alkyl halides using sodium metal in dry ether. The mechanism proceeds via the formation of free radicals or organosodium intermediates, resulting in the formation of a new carbon-carbon bond. For example, 2CH₃Br + 2Na → CH₃-CH₃ + 2NaBr.

Card 15

What is the product of the complete combustion of ethane?

The products are carbon dioxide (CO₂) and water (H₂O). The balanced equation is: 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O.

Card 16

Describe the preparation of ethyne from calcium carbide.

Ethyne (acetylene) is prepared by the reaction of calcium carbide (CaC₂) with water: CaC₂ + 2H₂O → C₂H₂ + Ca(OH)₂. This reaction produces ethyne gas, which is collected over water.

Card 17

What is the difference between addition and substitution reactions in hydrocarbons?

Addition reactions involve the breaking of multiple bonds (e.g., double or triple bonds) to form new single bonds, increasing the saturation of the molecule. Substitution reactions involve the replacement of an atom or group in a molecule with another atom or group, typically occurring in saturated or aromatic hydrocarbons.

Card 18

Write the IUPAC name of the following compound: CH₃-C≡C-CH(CH₃)-CH₃.

The IUPAC name is 4-methylpent-2-yne. The longest chain containing the triple bond is five carbons, and the methyl group is on the fourth carbon.

Card 19

Explain the term 'hybridization' in the context of ethene and ethyne.

In ethene (C₂H₄), each carbon atom undergoes sp² hybridization, forming three sp² hybrid orbitals (one for the C-H bond and two for the C=C bond) and one unhybridized p-orbital for the π-bond. In ethyne (C₂H₂), each carbon undergoes sp hybridization, forming two sp hybrid orbitals (one for the C-H bond and one for the C≡C bond) and two unhybridized p-orbitals for the two π-bonds.

Card 20

What is the role of concentrated H₂SO₄ in the preparation of ethene from ethanol?

Concentrated H₂SO₄ acts as a dehydrating agent, removing a water molecule from ethanol (C₂H₅OH) to form ethene (C₂H₄). The reaction is: C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O (in the presence of conc. H₂SO₄ at 170°C).

Card 21

Describe the test used to distinguish between alkanes and alkenes.

Alkenes can be distinguished from alkanes using the bromine water test. Alkenes decolorize bromine water (orange to colorless) due to the addition of bromine across the double bond, while alkanes do not react and the bromine water remains orange.
