NEET Chemistry - Electrochemistry Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define electrochemical cell and classify it into two main types.

An electrochemical cell is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy or vice versa through redox reactions. The two main types are: 1. **Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell**: Spontaneous redox reaction produces electrical energy (e.g., Daniell cell). 2. **Electrolytic Cell**: Electrical energy is used to drive a non-spontaneous redox reaction (e.g., electrolysis of water).

Card 2

What is the role of a salt bridge in a galvanic cell? Explain with an example.

A salt bridge (e.g., KCl in agar) maintains electrical neutrality in the half-cells by allowing ions to migrate between them. In a Daniell cell (Zn | Zn²⁺ || Cu²⁺ | Cu), Zn²⁺ ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and SO₄²⁻ ions in the cathode compartment. The salt bridge supplies K⁺ to the cathode and Cl⁻ to the anode, preventing charge buildup and sustaining current flow.

Card 3

Write the Nernst equation for a general electrochemical cell reaction: aA + bB → cC + dD. Include the standard cell potential (E°) and reaction quotient (Q).

Nernst Equation: E_cell = E°_cell - (RT/nF) * ln(Q) Where: - E_cell = Cell potential under non-standard conditions (V). - E°_cell = Standard cell potential (V). - R = Gas constant (8.314 J/mol·K). - T = Temperature (K). - n = Moles of electrons transferred. - F = Faraday constant (96,485 C/mol). - Q = Reaction quotient = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b.

Card 4

Calculate the standard cell potential (E°_cell) for the reaction: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu. Given: E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = -0.76 V, E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V.

E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode = E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) - E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = 0.34 V - (-0.76 V) = +1.10 V. **Key Point**: The more positive reduction potential acts as the cathode (reduction), and the more negative as the anode (oxidation).

Card 5

Explain the relationship between Gibbs free energy (ΔG) and cell potential (E_cell). Derive the formula linking them.

ΔG = -nFE_cell Where: - ΔG = Gibbs free energy change (J/mol). - n = Moles of electrons transferred. - F = Faraday constant (96,485 C/mol). - E_cell = Cell potential (V). **Derivation**: Electrical work (W_elec) = -nFE_cell. For a reversible process, W_elec = ΔG. Thus, ΔG = -nFE_cell. **Implication**: A positive E_cell indicates a spontaneous reaction (ΔG < 0).

Card 6

Define standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) and its significance in electrochemistry.

SHE is a reference electrode with: - Pt electrode immersed in 1 M H⁺ solution. - H₂ gas at 1 atm pressure bubbled over the Pt surface. - Assigned E° = 0 V at 298 K. **Significance**: Used to measure standard reduction potentials of other electrodes. For example, if a metal electrode has E° = -0.76 V vs. SHE, its reduction potential is -0.76 V.

Card 7

What is overpotential in electrolysis? How does it affect the decomposition voltage of water?

Overpotential is the extra voltage required beyond the theoretical decomposition potential to drive an electrolytic reaction at a practical rate. For water electrolysis: - Theoretical E° = 1.23 V (2H₂O → O₂ + 2H₂). - Due to overpotential (especially for O₂ evolution), the actual voltage required is ~1.8–2.0 V. **Cause**: Slow electrode kinetics, resistance, and gas bubble formation.

Card 8

Differentiate between molar conductivity (Λ_m) and equivalent conductivity (Λ_eq). How do they vary with concentration for strong electrolytes?

**Molar Conductivity (Λ_m)**: Conductivity of 1 mole of electrolyte in solution (S cm² mol⁻¹). **Equivalent Conductivity (Λ_eq)**: Conductivity of 1 equivalent of electrolyte (S cm² eq⁻¹). **Variation with Concentration**: - For strong electrolytes (e.g., NaCl), Λ_m decreases linearly with √c (Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation) due to ion-ion interactions. - Λ_m → Λ°_m (limiting molar conductivity) as c → 0.

Card 9

State Faraday’s laws of electrolysis and calculate the mass of copper deposited at the cathode when 2 A current is passed for 30 minutes through CuSO₄ solution. (Atomic mass of Cu = 63.5 g/mol).

**Faraday’s Laws**: 1. Mass deposited ∝ charge passed (m ∝ Q). 2. Mass deposited ∝ equivalent weight (m ∝ E). **Calculation**: - Charge (Q) = I × t = 2 A × (30 × 60) s = 3600 C. - Moles of electrons (n) = Q/F = 3600 / 96,485 ≈ 0.0373 mol. - Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu ⇒ 1 mol Cu requires 2 mol e⁻. - Moles of Cu = 0.0373 / 2 = 0.01865 mol. - Mass of Cu = 0.01865 mol × 63.5 g/mol ≈ 1.18 g.

Card 10

Explain the concept of corrosion as an electrochemical process. How does galvanization protect iron from rusting?

**Corrosion Mechanism**: Iron rusts via electrochemical cells: - Anode: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation). - Cathode: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (reduction). - Rust (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O) forms when Fe²⁺ reacts with OH⁻ and O₂. **Galvanization**: Coating iron with zinc (more negative E°) creates a sacrificial anode. Zinc corrodes preferentially, protecting iron even if the coating is scratched.

Card 11

What is the difference between primary and secondary batteries? Give one example of each.

**Primary Batteries**: Non-rechargeable; redox reactions are irreversible. - Example: **Dry cell (Leclanché cell)**: Zn anode, MnO₂ cathode, NH₄Cl electrolyte. **Secondary Batteries**: Rechargeable; redox reactions are reversible. - Example: **Lead-acid battery**: Pb anode, PbO₂ cathode, H₂SO₄ electrolyte. Used in automobiles.

Card 12

How does the conductivity of a solution depend on temperature? Explain the trend for strong and weak electrolytes.

**Strong Electrolytes**: Conductivity increases with temperature (~2% per °C) due to: - Decreased viscosity of the solvent (easier ion movement). - Reduced ion-ion interactions. **Weak Electrolytes**: Conductivity increases more sharply with temperature because: - Higher temperature shifts the dissociation equilibrium (e.g., CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺) toward ions, increasing ion concentration.

Card 13

Define Kohlrausch’s law of independent migration of ions. Calculate the limiting molar conductivity (Λ°_m) of CH₃COOH given: Λ°_m(CH₃COONa) = 91.0 S cm² mol⁻¹, Λ°_m(HCl) = 426.2 S cm² mol⁻¹, Λ°_m(NaCl) = 123.7 S cm² mol⁻¹.

**Kohlrausch’s Law**: Λ°_m of an electrolyte = Σ λ°_+ + Σ λ°_- (λ° = limiting ionic conductivity). **Calculation for CH₃COOH**: - Λ°_m(CH₃COOH) = Λ°_m(CH₃COONa) + Λ°_m(HCl) - Λ°_m(NaCl) = 91.0 + 426.2 - 123.7 = 393.5 S cm² mol⁻¹. **Use**: Determines Λ°_m for weak electrolytes (e.g., acetic acid) where direct measurement is difficult.
