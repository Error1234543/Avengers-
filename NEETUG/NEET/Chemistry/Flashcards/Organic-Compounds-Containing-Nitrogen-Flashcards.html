NEET Chemistry: Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the general formula of aliphatic amines?

The general formula of aliphatic amines is CₙH₂ₙ₊₃N, where 'n' is the number of carbon atoms. Primary amines have the formula RNH₂, secondary amines R₂NH, and tertiary amines R₃N.

Card 2

How are amines classified based on the number of hydrogen atoms replaced in ammonia?

Amines are classified as primary (1°), secondary (2°), or tertiary (3°) based on the number of hydrogen atoms in ammonia (NH₃) replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. Primary amines have one replacement (RNH₂), secondary have two (R₂NH), and tertiary have three (R₃N).

Card 3

What is the IUPAC name of the compound CH₃CH₂NH₂?

The IUPAC name of CH₃CH₂NH₂ is ethanamine. The suffix '-amine' is used for primary amines, and the parent chain is named as an alkane with the '-e' replaced by '-amine'.

Card 4

Explain the basicity of amines in comparison to ammonia.

Amines are generally more basic than ammonia due to the electron-donating effect of alkyl groups, which increases the electron density on the nitrogen atom. However, aryl amines (e.g., aniline) are less basic than ammonia due to the resonance stabilization of the lone pair of electrons on nitrogen with the benzene ring.

Card 5

What is the product of the reaction between a primary amine and nitrous acid (HNO₂)?

Primary amines react with nitrous acid to form diazonium salts (for aromatic amines) or alcohols (for aliphatic amines) with the evolution of nitrogen gas. For example, ethylamine (CH₃CH₂NH₂) reacts with HNO₂ to form ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH), nitrogen gas (N₂), and water (H₂O).

Card 6

Describe the Hinsberg test for distinguishing between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines.

The Hinsberg test uses benzene sulfonyl chloride (C₆H₅SO₂Cl) to distinguish amines. Primary amines form a soluble sulfonamide that dissolves in alkali. Secondary amines form an insoluble sulfonamide. Tertiary amines do not react with benzene sulfonyl chloride.

Card 7

What is the structure of aniline, and how does its basicity compare to aliphatic amines?

Aniline (C₆H₅NH₂) is an aromatic amine with a benzene ring attached to an amino group. Its basicity is lower than aliphatic amines due to the resonance stabilization of the lone pair of electrons on nitrogen with the benzene ring, which reduces the electron density on nitrogen.

Card 8

What is the product of the reaction between aniline and bromine water?

Aniline reacts with bromine water to form 2,4,6-tribromoaniline, a white precipitate. This reaction occurs readily due to the high activating effect of the amino group on the benzene ring, which directs bromination to the ortho and para positions.

Card 9

Explain the preparation of amines by the Hoffmann bromamide degradation method.

The Hoffmann bromamide degradation method involves treating an amide with bromine and a strong base (e.g., NaOH or KOH). The reaction results in the formation of a primary amine with one fewer carbon atom than the starting amide. For example, ethanamide (CH₃CONH₂) yields methylamine (CH₃NH₂).

Card 10

What is the role of nitrous acid in the diazotization of aromatic amines?

Nitrous acid (HNO₂) is used to diazotize aromatic primary amines (e.g., aniline) at low temperatures (0-5°C) to form diazonium salts (e.g., benzenediazonium chloride). These salts are highly reactive and used in the synthesis of azo dyes and other organic compounds.

Card 11

What is the product of the reduction of nitrobenzene?

The reduction of nitrobenzene (C₆H₅NO₂) under different conditions yields various products. In acidic medium (e.g., Sn/HCl), it forms aniline (C₆H₅NH₂). In neutral medium (e.g., Zn/NH₄Cl), it forms phenylhydroxylamine (C₆H₅NHOH). In alkaline medium (e.g., Zn/NaOH), it forms azobenzene (C₆H₅N=NC₆H₅).

Card 12

How does the presence of an electron-withdrawing group affect the basicity of aniline?

The presence of an electron-withdrawing group (e.g., -NO₂, -CN) on the benzene ring of aniline further decreases its basicity. This is because the electron-withdrawing group reduces the electron density on the nitrogen atom, making it less available to accept a proton.

Card 13

What is the structure of urea, and to which class of organic compounds does it belong?

Urea (NH₂CONH₂) is a diamide of carbonic acid and belongs to the class of organic compounds called amides. It is the first organic compound to be synthesized artificially (by Friedrich Wöhler) and is a key component of the nitrogen cycle in living organisms.

Card 14

Explain the carbylamine test for primary amines.

The carbylamine test is used to detect primary amines. When a primary amine is heated with chloroform (CHCl₃) and alcoholic potassium hydroxide (KOH), it forms an isocyanide (carbylamine), which has a foul odor. The reaction is: RNH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH → RNC + 3KCl + 3H₂O.

Card 15

What is the product of the reaction between ethylamine and acetyl chloride?

Ethylamine (CH₃CH₂NH₂) reacts with acetyl chloride (CH₃COCl) to form N-ethylacetamide (CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃) and hydrogen chloride (HCl). This is an example of an acylation reaction, where an acyl group is introduced into the amine.

Card 16

Describe the preparation of amines by the Gabriel phthalimide synthesis.

The Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is used to prepare primary amines. Phthalimide is treated with ethanolic potassium hydroxide to form potassium phthalimide, which then reacts with an alkyl halide to form an N-alkyl phthalimide. Hydrolysis of this compound yields the primary amine. This method avoids the formation of secondary and tertiary amines.

Card 17

What is the difference between amides and amines in terms of structure and reactivity?

Amides (RCONH₂) contain a carbonyl group (C=O) attached to the nitrogen atom, while amines (RNH₂) do not. Amides are less basic and less reactive than amines due to the resonance stabilization of the lone pair of electrons on nitrogen with the carbonyl group, which reduces the electron density on nitrogen.

Card 18

What is the product of the reaction between aniline and acetic anhydride?

Aniline (C₆H₅NH₂) reacts with acetic anhydride ((CH₃CO)₂O) to form acetanilide (C₆H₅NHCOCH₃) and acetic acid (CH₃COOH). This reaction is an example of acylation, where the amino group is protected by converting it into an amide.

Card 19

Explain the role of amines in the formation of peptides.

Amines (specifically amino acids) play a crucial role in the formation of peptides. The amino group (-NH₂) of one amino acid reacts with the carboxyl group (-COOH) of another amino acid to form a peptide bond (-CONH-), releasing a molecule of water. This process is known as condensation polymerization.

Card 20

What is the effect of temperature on the diazotization of aniline?

Diazotization of aniline is typically carried out at low temperatures (0-5°C). At higher temperatures, the diazonium salt decomposes to form phenol and nitrogen gas. Maintaining low temperatures ensures the stability of the diazonium salt for further reactions.

Card 21

What is the product of the reaction between methylamine and nitrous acid?

Methylamine (CH₃NH₂) reacts with nitrous acid (HNO₂) to form methanol (CH₃OH), nitrogen gas (N₂), and water (H₂O). This reaction is characteristic of primary aliphatic amines.
