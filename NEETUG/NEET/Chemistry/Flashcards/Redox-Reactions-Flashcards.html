NEET Chemistry - Redox Reactions Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define a redox reaction.

A redox reaction is a chemical reaction in which both oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously. Oxidation involves the loss of electrons (increase in oxidation number), while reduction involves the gain of electrons (decrease in oxidation number). These reactions are fundamental in processes like respiration, combustion, and corrosion.

Card 2

What is the oxidation number of an element in its free (uncombined) state?

The oxidation number of an element in its free (uncombined) state is always zero. For example, the oxidation number of Na, O₂, and Cl₂ is 0.

Card 3

How is the oxidation number of hydrogen determined in compounds?

The oxidation number of hydrogen is +1 in most compounds, except in metal hydrides (e.g., NaH, CaH₂), where it is -1. This is because hydrogen forms a negative ion (H⁻) in hydrides due to the low electronegativity of metals.

Card 4

What is the oxidation number of oxygen in peroxides?

In peroxides (e.g., H₂O₂, Na₂O₂), the oxidation number of oxygen is -1. This is an exception to the usual -2 oxidation state of oxygen in most compounds.

Card 5

Explain the concept of a half-reaction in redox processes.

A half-reaction represents either the oxidation or reduction component of a redox reaction. It shows the electrons lost (oxidation half-reaction) or gained (reduction half-reaction). For example, in the reaction Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, the oxidation half-reaction is Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻, and the reduction half-reaction is Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu.

Card 6

What is the role of an oxidizing agent in a redox reaction?

An oxidizing agent (or oxidant) is a substance that gains electrons in a redox reaction, thereby causing the oxidation of another substance. It itself gets reduced. Common examples include KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇, and O₂.

Card 7

How do you balance a redox reaction using the ion-electron method in acidic medium?

To balance a redox reaction in acidic medium using the ion-electron method: 1) Write the oxidation and reduction half-reactions. 2) Balance atoms other than O and H. 3) Balance oxygen by adding H₂O. 4) Balance hydrogen by adding H⁺. 5) Balance charge by adding electrons. 6) Multiply half-reactions to equalize electrons. 7) Add the half-reactions and cancel common terms.

Card 8

What is the oxidation number of chromium in K₂Cr₂O₇?

The oxidation number of chromium in K₂Cr₂O₇ is +6. This is calculated as follows: Let the oxidation number of Cr be x. Then, 2(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 → 2 + 2x - 14 = 0 → 2x = 12 → x = +6.

Card 9

Define disproportionation with an example.

Disproportionation is a type of redox reaction in which a single substance undergoes both oxidation and reduction simultaneously. For example, in the reaction 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂, hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) disproportionates into water (H₂O) and oxygen (O₂). Here, oxygen is both oxidized and reduced.

Card 10

What is the significance of the electrochemical series in redox reactions?

The electrochemical series is a list of elements arranged in order of their standard electrode potentials. It helps predict the feasibility of redox reactions: a metal higher in the series can displace a metal lower in the series from its salt solution. It also indicates the strength of oxidizing and reducing agents.

Card 11

How is the equivalent weight of an oxidizing or reducing agent calculated?

The equivalent weight of an oxidizing or reducing agent is calculated as: Equivalent weight = Molar mass / Number of electrons gained or lost per molecule. For example, in KMnO₄ (acting as an oxidizing agent in acidic medium), Mn gains 5 electrons, so its equivalent weight is Molar mass / 5.

Card 12

What is the oxidation number of nitrogen in NH₃ and NO₃⁻?

In NH₃, the oxidation number of nitrogen is -3. In NO₃⁻, the oxidation number of nitrogen is +5. This is calculated as follows: For NH₃: x + 3(+1) = 0 → x = -3. For NO₃⁻: x + 3(-2) = -1 → x = +5.

Card 13

Explain the concept of auto-redox reactions with an example.

Auto-redox reactions (or self-redox reactions) are a type of disproportionation where the same element in a single compound undergoes both oxidation and reduction. For example, in the reaction 3ClO⁻ → ClO₃⁻ + 2Cl⁻, chlorine in ClO⁻ is both oxidized to ClO₃⁻ and reduced to Cl⁻.

Card 14

What is the role of a reducing agent in a redox reaction?

A reducing agent (or reductant) is a substance that loses electrons in a redox reaction, thereby causing the reduction of another substance. It itself gets oxidized. Common examples include metals like Zn, Fe, and Na, as well as compounds like NaBH₄ and LiAlH₄.

Card 15

How do you balance a redox reaction in basic medium using the ion-electron method?

To balance a redox reaction in basic medium: 1) Write the oxidation and reduction half-reactions. 2) Balance atoms other than O and H. 3) Balance oxygen by adding H₂O. 4) Balance hydrogen by adding H₂O to the side needing H and OH⁻ to the opposite side. 5) Balance charge by adding electrons. 6) Multiply half-reactions to equalize electrons. 7) Add the half-reactions and cancel common terms.

Card 16

What is the oxidation number of sulfur in H₂SO₄ and H₂S?

In H₂SO₄, the oxidation number of sulfur is +6. In H₂S, the oxidation number of sulfur is -2. This is calculated as follows: For H₂SO₄: 2(+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 → x = +6. For H₂S: 2(+1) + x = 0 → x = -2.

Card 17

What is the difference between oxidation number and valency?

Oxidation number is the apparent charge on an atom in a compound, which can be positive, negative, or zero, and may have fractional values. Valency is the combining capacity of an element, represented by a whole number without a sign. For example, carbon has a valency of 4 but can have oxidation numbers like +4, +2, or -4 in different compounds.

Card 18

Explain the concept of redox titrations with an example.

Redox titrations are volumetric methods used to determine the concentration of an oxidizing or reducing agent by reacting it with a known concentration of a reducing or oxidizing agent, respectively. For example, potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) is commonly used to titrate iron(II) ions (Fe²⁺) in acidic medium. The endpoint is detected by the appearance of a pink color due to excess KMnO₄.

Card 19

What is the oxidation number of carbon in CH₄, CH₃OH, and CO₂?

In CH₄, the oxidation number of carbon is -4. In CH₃OH, it is -2. In CO₂, it is +4. This is calculated as follows: For CH₄: x + 4(+1) = 0 → x = -4. For CH₃OH: x + 4(+1) + (-2) + (+1) = 0 → x = -2. For CO₂: x + 2(-2) = 0 → x = +4.

Card 20

What are the common indicators used in redox titrations?

Common indicators used in redox titrations include: 1) Self-indicators like KMnO₄, which turns pink at the endpoint. 2) Diphenylamine, which turns violet in the presence of excess oxidizing agent. 3) Starch, used in iodometric titrations, which turns blue-black in the presence of iodine.

Card 21

How does the oxidation number change in a combustion reaction?

In a combustion reaction, the oxidation number of carbon or other combustible elements increases (oxidation), while the oxidation number of oxygen decreases (reduction). For example, in the combustion of methane (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O), carbon is oxidized from -4 to +4, and oxygen is reduced from 0 to -2.
