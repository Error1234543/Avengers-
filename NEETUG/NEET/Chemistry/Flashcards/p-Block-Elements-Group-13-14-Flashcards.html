NEET Chemistry: p-Block Elements (Group 13 & 14) - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Which element of Group 13 exhibits the inert-pair effect most prominently?

Thallium (Tl). The inert-pair effect refers to the reluctance of the two electrons in the outermost s-orbital to participate in bonding, leading to a stable +1 oxidation state in thallium, in addition to the +3 state common to Group 13 elements.

Card 2

What is the hybridization and geometry of the boron atom in boron trifluoride (BF₃)?

sp² hybridization; trigonal planar geometry. Boron has three valence electrons, each forming a sigma bond with fluorine, resulting in three sp² hybrid orbitals arranged at 120° angles.

Card 3

Why does aluminum not react with concentrated nitric acid?

Aluminum forms a protective oxide layer (Al₂O₃) on its surface when exposed to concentrated nitric acid, which passivates the metal and prevents further reaction.

Card 4

What is the product formed when borax is heated strongly?

Sodium metaborate (NaBO₂) and boric anhydride (B₂O₃). The reaction is: Na₂B₄O₇ → 2NaBO₂ + B₂O₃.

Card 5

Which Group 13 element forms the most stable +1 oxidation state?

Thallium (Tl). Due to the inert-pair effect, thallium commonly exhibits a +1 oxidation state, which is more stable than its +3 state.

Card 6

What is the structure of diborane (B₂H₆)?

Diborane has a bridged structure with two hydrogen atoms forming three-center two-electron bonds (banana bonds) between the two boron atoms. Each boron is also bonded to two terminal hydrogen atoms.

Card 7

Why is carbon monoxide (CO) a neutral oxide while carbon dioxide (CO₂) is acidic?

CO does not react with water to form an acid, whereas CO₂ reacts with water to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), making it acidic. CO’s neutrality is due to the triple bond between carbon and oxygen, which is highly stable.

Card 8

What is the hybridization of the central atom in silicon tetrachloride (SiCl₄)?

sp³ hybridization; tetrahedral geometry. Silicon forms four sigma bonds with chlorine atoms, utilizing four sp³ hybrid orbitals.

Card 9

Which allotrope of carbon is used as a lubricant and why?

Graphite. Its layered structure allows the layers to slide over each other easily, making it an effective lubricant.

Card 10

What is the product of the reaction between silicon dioxide (SiO₂) and hydrofluoric acid (HF)?

Silicon tetrafluoride (SiF₄) and water (H₂O). The reaction is: SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O.

Card 11

Why does lead exhibit a +2 oxidation state more commonly than +4?

Due to the inert-pair effect, the 6s² electrons in lead are reluctant to participate in bonding, making the +2 oxidation state more stable than +4.

Card 12

What is the role of cryolite in the extraction of aluminum?

Cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) lowers the melting point of alumina (Al₂O₃) from ~2000°C to ~950°C, making the electrolytic extraction of aluminum economically feasible.

Card 13

What is the shape of the [AlF₆]³⁻ ion?

Octahedral. Aluminum undergoes sp³d² hybridization to form six equivalent bonds with fluorine atoms.

Card 14

Which Group 14 element forms the most stable catenated compounds?

Carbon. Due to its small size and high bond energy (C-C bond), carbon forms stable catenated compounds like alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes.

Card 15

What is the product of the reaction between tin (Sn) and concentrated hydrochloric acid?

Stannous chloride (SnCl₂) and hydrogen gas (H₂). The reaction is: Sn + 2HCl → SnCl₂ + H₂.

Card 16

Why is CO₂ a gas at room temperature while SiO₂ is a solid?

CO₂ exists as discrete linear molecules with weak van der Waals forces between them, making it a gas. SiO₂ forms a three-dimensional covalent network (quartz), resulting in a high melting point and solid state.

Card 17

What is the oxidation state of boron in borax (Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O)?

+3. In borax, boron is present in the form of [B₄O₇]²⁻, where each boron atom has an oxidation state of +3.

Card 18

Which Group 13 element is used in the manufacture of transistors and why?

Gallium (Ga). Its semiconductor properties, particularly when combined with arsenic to form gallium arsenide (GaAs), make it suitable for high-speed electronic devices like transistors.

Card 19

What is the product of the reaction between lead (Pb) and dilute nitric acid?

Lead nitrate (Pb(NO₃)₂), nitric oxide (NO), and water (H₂O). The reaction is: 3Pb + 8HNO₃ → 3Pb(NO₃)₂ + 2NO + 4H₂O.

Card 20

Why is graphite a good conductor of electricity while diamond is not?

Graphite has delocalized π-electrons in its layered structure, which are free to move and conduct electricity. Diamond has a three-dimensional covalent network with no free electrons, making it an insulator.
