NEET Chemistry: p-Block Elements (Group 15-18) - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Which element of Group 15 exhibits the highest tendency to form pπ–pπ multiple bonds?

Nitrogen. Due to its small atomic size and high electronegativity, nitrogen readily forms pπ–pπ multiple bonds (e.g., N≡N in N₂), unlike heavier congeners (P, As, Sb, Bi) which form single bonds or use d-orbitals for π-bonding.

Card 2

State the oxidation states exhibited by phosphorus in its oxides and oxoacids. Provide one example for each.

+3 in P₂O₃ (phosphorus trioxide) and H₃PO₃ (phosphorous acid); +5 in P₂O₅ (phosphorus pentoxide) and H₃PO₄ (phosphoric acid).

Card 3

Why is white phosphorus highly reactive and stored under water?

White phosphorus consists of discrete P₄ tetrahedra with 60° bond angles, causing high angle strain. It spontaneously ignites in air (autoignition temperature ~30°C), so it is stored under water to prevent oxidation.

Card 4

Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction of ammonia with excess chlorine.

NH₃ + 3Cl₂ → NCl₃ + 3HCl. Excess chlorine oxidizes ammonia to nitrogen trichloride, a yellow oily explosive liquid.

Card 5

Explain the basicity difference between NH₃ and PH₃ using hybridization and bond angle.

NH₃ (sp³, 107°) has a lone pair in an sp³ orbital with higher s-character, making it more available for donation (pKb ~4.75). PH₃ (sp³, 93.5°) has a lone pair in an orbital with higher p-character, reducing its basicity (pKb ~27).

Card 6

What is the structure of PCl₅ in the solid state?

In the solid state, PCl₅ exists as [PCl₄]⁺[PCl₆]⁻, an ionic lattice. The cation is tetrahedral (sp³), and the anion is octahedral (sp³d²).

Card 7

Why does nitrogen not form pentahalides like phosphorus?

Nitrogen lacks d-orbitals in its valence shell, preventing the expansion of its octet beyond 8 electrons. Phosphorus can use 3d orbitals to accommodate 10 electrons (e.g., PCl₅).

Card 8

Describe the preparation of nitric acid by Ostwald’s process with balanced equations.

1. Catalytic oxidation of NH₃: 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (Pt-Rh catalyst, 800°C). 2. Oxidation of NO: 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂. 3. Absorption in water: 3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO (recycled).

Card 9

What is the cause of the brown ring test for nitrates?

The brown ring is due to the formation of [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺, a nitrosonium complex. NO₃⁻ is reduced by Fe²⁺ in acidic medium to NO, which coordinates with Fe²⁺ to form the brown-colored complex.

Card 10

Explain the trend in bond angles of Group 16 hydrides (H₂O, H₂S, H₂Se, H₂Te).

Bond angles decrease down the group: H₂O (104.5°) > H₂S (92.1°) > H₂Se (91°) > H₂Te (89.5°). This is due to decreasing electronegativity and increasing atomic size, reducing lone pair-bond pair repulsion.

Card 11

Why is O₂ paramagnetic while O₃ is diamagnetic?

O₂ has two unpaired electrons in its π* molecular orbitals (MO theory), making it paramagnetic. O₃ has a closed-shell π-system with all electrons paired, making it diamagnetic.

Card 12

Write the balanced equation for the reaction of sulfur with hot concentrated nitric acid.

S + 6HNO₃ → H₂SO₄ + 6NO₂ + 2H₂O. Sulfur is oxidized to H₂SO₄, and HNO₃ is reduced to NO₂.

Card 13

What is the structure of SF₆, and why is it chemically inert?

SF₆ has an octahedral structure (sp³d² hybridization). It is inert due to the steric protection of the sulfur atom by six fluorine atoms, preventing nucleophilic attack, and the high S-F bond strength.

Card 14

Explain the bleaching action of sulfur dioxide.

SO₂ bleaches by reduction: SO₂ + 2H₂O + X (colored) → H₂SO₄ + XH₂ (colorless). It is temporary, as the reduced compound may re-oxidize in air (e.g., flowers regain color).

Card 15

Why does H₂SO₄ act as a dehydrating agent? Give one example.

H₂SO₄ has a strong affinity for water, forming hydrates (e.g., H₂SO₄·H₂O). Example: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ (sucrose) → 12C + 11H₂O (charred black mass).

Card 16

What are the products of the reaction between chlorine and cold dilute NaOH?

Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O. Chlorine disproportionates to chloride (Cl⁻) and hypochlorite (OCl⁻).

Card 17

Explain the trend in oxidizing power of Group 17 elements (F₂ to I₂).

Oxidizing power decreases down the group: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. This is due to decreasing electronegativity, increasing atomic size, and decreasing bond dissociation enthalpy (except F₂, which has a low bond dissociation enthalpy due to lone pair repulsion).

Card 18

Why is HF a weak acid while HCl, HBr, and HI are strong acids?

HF has a high bond dissociation enthalpy and strong H-F bond due to fluorine’s small size and high electronegativity. It also forms hydrogen-bonded ion pairs (e.g., HF₂⁻), reducing free H⁺ availability. Heavier hydrogen halides lack these factors.

Card 19

What is the structure of XeF₄, and what is its hybridization?

XeF₄ has a square planar structure with sp³d² hybridization. The two lone pairs occupy axial positions, minimizing repulsion (VSEPR theory).

Card 20

Write the balanced equation for the hydrolysis of XeF₆.

XeF₆ + 3H₂O → XeO₃ + 6HF. Xenon hexafluoride hydrolyzes to xenon trioxide, a highly explosive solid.

Card 21

Why are noble gases chemically inert under normal conditions?

Noble gases have a complete valence electron octet (ns²np⁶), resulting in high ionization enthalpies and negligible electron affinities. This makes them thermodynamically stable and unreactive under standard conditions.

Card 22

What is the cause of the temporary hardness of water, and how is it removed?

Temporary hardness is caused by bicarbonate ions of Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ (e.g., Ca(HCO₃)₂). It is removed by boiling: Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O + CO₂↑, precipitating insoluble carbonates.

Card 23

Explain the anomalous behavior of fluorine in Group 17.

Fluorine differs due to: (1) small size and high electronegativity, (2) absence of d-orbitals, (3) low F-F bond dissociation enthalpy (lone pair repulsion), (4) high hydration enthalpy of F⁻, and (5) formation of strong hydrogen bonds (e.g., HF).

Card 24

What is the structure of the interhalogen compound ICl₃?

ICl₃ has a T-shaped structure with sp³d hybridization. The central iodine atom has three bonding pairs and two lone pairs, minimizing repulsion (VSEPR theory).

Card 25

Why does helium have the lowest boiling point among all elements?

Helium has the weakest van der Waals forces due to its small size and low polarizability. It lacks interatomic interactions strong enough to liquefy at higher temperatures, resulting in a boiling point of 4.2 K.
