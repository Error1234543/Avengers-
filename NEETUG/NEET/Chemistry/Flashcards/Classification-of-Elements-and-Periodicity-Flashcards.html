NEET Chemistry: Classification of Elements and Periodicity - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the term 'periodicity' in the context of the modern periodic table.

Periodicity refers to the recurring trends or patterns in the properties of elements when they are arranged in order of increasing atomic number. These trends include atomic radius, ionization enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, electronegativity, and valency, which repeat at regular intervals (periods) in the periodic table.

Card 2

What is the basis of the modern periodic law proposed by Moseley?

The modern periodic law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers (number of protons), not their atomic masses. This corrected Mendeleev's periodic law, which was based on atomic masses, and resolved anomalies like the placement of tellurium and iodine.

Card 3

Explain the trend of atomic radius across a period and down a group in the periodic table.

Across a period (left to right), atomic radius decreases due to increasing nuclear charge, which pulls the electrons closer to the nucleus. Down a group, atomic radius increases because additional electron shells are added, increasing the distance between the nucleus and the outermost electrons.

Card 4

Why does the ionization enthalpy generally increase across a period?

Ionization enthalpy increases across a period because the nuclear charge increases, leading to a stronger attraction between the nucleus and the outermost electrons. This makes it harder to remove an electron, thus requiring more energy. Additionally, the atomic radius decreases, further increasing the attraction.

Card 5

What is the exception to the trend of ionization enthalpy in Group 13 (e.g., boron and aluminum) and why does it occur?

In Group 13, the first ionization enthalpy of elements like boron and aluminum is lower than that of the preceding Group 2 elements (e.g., beryllium and magnesium). This occurs because the outermost electron in Group 13 elements is in a p-orbital, which is slightly higher in energy and easier to remove compared to the s-orbital electrons in Group 2 elements.

Card 6

Define electron gain enthalpy. How does it vary across a period and down a group?

Electron gain enthalpy is the energy released when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom. Across a period, electron gain enthalpy becomes more negative (increases) due to increasing nuclear charge and smaller atomic size, which attract the incoming electron more strongly. Down a group, electron gain enthalpy becomes less negative (decreases) because the atomic size increases, reducing the attraction for the incoming electron.

Card 7

Why do noble gases have positive electron gain enthalpies?

Noble gases have completely filled electron shells, making them highly stable. Adding an electron to a noble gas requires energy to overcome the repulsion from the already stable electron configuration, resulting in a positive electron gain enthalpy.

Card 8

Explain the trend of electronegativity in the periodic table.

Electronegativity increases across a period from left to right due to increasing nuclear charge and decreasing atomic radius, which enhances the attraction for shared electrons in a bond. Down a group, electronegativity decreases because the atomic size increases, reducing the attraction for shared electrons.

Card 9

What is the difference between valency and oxidation state?

Valency is the combining capacity of an element, typically determined by the number of electrons in the outermost shell (valence electrons). Oxidation state is the apparent charge on an atom in a compound, which can vary depending on the bonding and the elements involved. For example, carbon has a valency of 4 but can exhibit oxidation states ranging from -4 to +4.

Card 10

Describe the trend of metallic and non-metallic character in the periodic table.

Metallic character decreases across a period from left to right because the tendency to lose electrons decreases as nuclear charge increases. Down a group, metallic character increases because the atomic size increases, making it easier to lose electrons. Non-metallic character follows the opposite trend: it increases across a period and decreases down a group.

Card 11

Why do elements in the same group exhibit similar chemical properties?

Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons, which determines their chemical reactivity and bonding behavior. This similarity in valence electron configuration leads to comparable chemical properties, such as forming similar types of compounds (e.g., Group 1 elements form +1 ions).

Card 12

What are the s, p, d, and f blocks in the periodic table? Give one example of each.

The periodic table is divided into blocks based on the type of orbital that receives the last electron: - s-block: Elements where the last electron enters an s-orbital (e.g., sodium, Na). - p-block: Elements where the last electron enters a p-orbital (e.g., carbon, C). - d-block: Elements where the last electron enters a d-orbital (e.g., iron, Fe). - f-block: Elements where the last electron enters an f-orbital (e.g., cerium, Ce).

Card 13

Explain the concept of effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) and its role in periodic trends.

Effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) is the net positive charge experienced by an electron in a multi-electron atom. It is less than the actual nuclear charge due to shielding or screening by inner electrons. Z_eff increases across a period because the number of protons increases while the shielding effect remains relatively constant. This increase in Z_eff is responsible for trends like decreasing atomic radius and increasing ionization enthalpy across a period.

Card 14

Why does the second ionization enthalpy of an element always higher than its first ionization enthalpy?

The second ionization enthalpy is higher because removing an electron from a positively charged ion requires more energy than removing it from a neutral atom. The increased nuclear attraction and reduced electron-electron repulsion in the ion make it harder to remove the second electron.

Card 15

What are isoelectronic species? Give an example and explain their properties.

Isoelectronic species are atoms or ions that have the same number of electrons and thus the same electronic configuration. For example, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, and Mg²⁺ all have 10 electrons. Despite having the same number of electrons, their properties differ due to variations in nuclear charge, which affects atomic/ionic radius and other properties.
