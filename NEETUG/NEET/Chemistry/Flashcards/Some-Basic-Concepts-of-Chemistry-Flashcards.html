NEET Chemistry: Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the term 'mole' in chemistry. What is its significance?

A mole is the SI unit of amount of substance, defined as the amount of any substance that contains as many elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions, or electrons) as there are atoms in 12 grams of carbon-12. This number is Avogadro's constant (6.022 × 10²³). The mole is significant because it allows chemists to count particles by weighing macroscopic amounts of substances, enabling quantitative chemical calculations such as stoichiometry, molarity, and mass-mole conversions.

Card 2

What is the difference between empirical formula and molecular formula? Provide an example.

The empirical formula represents the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound, while the molecular formula shows the actual number of atoms of each element in a molecule. For example, the empirical formula of glucose is CH₂O, but its molecular formula is C₆H₁₂O₆. The molecular formula is always a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula (in this case, 6 × CH₂O = C₆H₁₂O₆).

Card 3

State the law of conservation of mass. How is it applied in balancing chemical equations?

The law of conservation of mass states that mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction; the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products. This law is applied in balancing chemical equations by ensuring the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides of the equation. For example, in the reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, there are 4 hydrogen atoms and 2 oxygen atoms on both sides, satisfying the law.

Card 4

What is the molar mass of a substance? How is it calculated for a compound like water (H₂O)?

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance, expressed in grams per mole (g/mol). It is calculated by summing the atomic masses of all the atoms in the molecular formula. For water (H₂O), the molar mass is calculated as: (2 × atomic mass of H) + (1 × atomic mass of O) = (2 × 1.008 g/mol) + (1 × 16.00 g/mol) = 18.016 g/mol.

Card 5

Explain the concept of limiting reagent in a chemical reaction with an example.

The limiting reagent is the reactant that is completely consumed first in a chemical reaction, thereby limiting the amount of product formed. For example, in the reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, if 4 moles of H₂ react with 1 mole of O₂, O₂ is the limiting reagent because it can only produce 2 moles of H₂O (requiring 2 moles of H₂), leaving 2 moles of H₂ unreacted. The amount of product is determined by the limiting reagent.

Card 6

What is the percentage composition of an element in a compound? Calculate the percentage of carbon in carbon dioxide (CO₂).

Percentage composition is the mass percentage of each element in a compound. It is calculated as: (mass of the element in 1 mole of compound / molar mass of the compound) × 100. For carbon in CO₂: (atomic mass of C / molar mass of CO₂) × 100 = (12.01 g/mol / 44.01 g/mol) × 100 ≈ 27.29%.

Card 7

Define stoichiometry. Why is it important in chemical reactions?

Stoichiometry is the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction, based on the balanced chemical equation. It is important because it allows chemists to predict the amounts of reactants needed or products formed, calculate yields, and determine limiting reagents. For example, stoichiometry helps in scaling up reactions for industrial processes or determining the exact amount of reactants for a laboratory experiment.

Card 8

What is the difference between accuracy and precision in measurements? Provide an example.

Accuracy refers to how close a measured value is to the true or accepted value, while precision refers to how close multiple measurements of the same quantity are to each other. For example, if the true mass of a sample is 5.00 g, measurements of 4.95 g, 5.05 g, and 5.00 g are accurate and precise. Measurements of 4.50 g, 4.55 g, and 4.45 g are precise but not accurate, while 4.80 g, 5.20 g, and 4.90 g are neither precise nor accurate.

Card 9

Explain the concept of significant figures. How many significant figures are in the measurement 0.00450 kg?

Significant figures are the digits in a measurement that carry meaning contributing to its precision, including all digits except leading zeros (which are placeholders) and trailing zeros in a number without a decimal point. In 0.00450 kg, the significant figures are 4, 5, and 0 (the trailing zero after the decimal point), totaling 3 significant figures.

Card 10

What is the difference between mass and weight? Why is mass used in chemical calculations instead of weight?

Mass is a measure of the amount of matter in an object and is constant regardless of location, while weight is the force exerted by gravity on an object and varies with the strength of the gravitational field. Mass is used in chemical calculations because it is invariant and directly relates to the number of atoms or molecules in a sample, which is essential for stoichiometric calculations. Weight, being dependent on gravity, is not suitable for such calculations.

Card 11

State Avogadro's law. How does it relate to the ideal gas equation?

Avogadro's law states that equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. Mathematically, V ∝ n (where V is volume and n is the number of moles). This law is incorporated into the ideal gas equation PV = nRT, where the volume of a gas is directly proportional to the number of moles at constant temperature and pressure.

Card 12

What is the difference between a homogeneous and heterogeneous mixture? Provide examples.

A homogeneous mixture has a uniform composition throughout, with no visible boundaries between components. Examples include saltwater, air, and sugar dissolved in water. A heterogeneous mixture has a non-uniform composition with visible boundaries between components. Examples include sand in water, oil and water, and a mixture of iron filings and sulfur.

Card 13

Explain the concept of molarity. How is it calculated for a solution?

Molarity (M) is the number of moles of solute per liter of solution. It is calculated as: Molarity = (moles of solute) / (volume of solution in liters). For example, if 58.5 g of NaCl (molar mass = 58.5 g/mol) is dissolved in enough water to make 1 L of solution, the molarity is (1 mole / 1 L) = 1 M.

Card 14

What is the law of definite proportions? Provide an example.

The law of definite proportions states that a chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same proportion by mass, regardless of the source or method of preparation. For example, water (H₂O) always contains 11.19% hydrogen and 88.81% oxygen by mass, whether it is obtained from a river, synthesized in a lab, or extracted from a living organism.

Card 15

How is the percentage yield of a reaction calculated? Why is it often less than 100%?

Percentage yield is calculated as: (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100. It is often less than 100% due to factors such as incomplete reactions, side reactions, loss of product during purification, or experimental errors. For example, if the theoretical yield of a reaction is 10 g but the actual yield is 8 g, the percentage yield is (8 g / 10 g) × 100 = 80%.
