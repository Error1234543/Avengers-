NEET Chemistry - Structure of Atom: Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the significance of the Rutherford gold-foil experiment in understanding atomic structure?

The Rutherford gold-foil experiment (1911) demonstrated that atoms consist of a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by electrons. Most alpha particles passed through the foil, but some deflected at large angles, indicating the nucleus occupies a very small volume compared to the atom. This disproved Thomson's 'plum-pudding' model and led to the nuclear model of the atom.

Card 2

Define the term 'atomic number' and explain its role in determining the identity of an element.

The atomic number (Z) is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It determines the element's identity because each element has a unique atomic number. For a neutral atom, the atomic number also equals the number of electrons, which governs its chemical properties.

Card 3

What are isotopes? Give an example and explain their chemical behavior.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same atomic number (Z) but different mass numbers (A) due to varying numbers of neutrons. Example: Carbon-12 (6 protons, 6 neutrons) and Carbon-14 (6 protons, 8 neutrons). Isotopes exhibit nearly identical chemical behavior because they have the same number of electrons and electronic configuration.

Card 4

State the postulates of Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom. What limitation did it address?

Bohr's postulates: (1) Electrons revolve in fixed, quantized orbits (stationary states) without radiating energy. (2) Angular momentum of an electron in an orbit is quantized: mvr = nh/2π (n = 1, 2, 3...). (3) Energy is emitted/absorbed when electrons transition between orbits (ΔE = hν). It addressed the limitation of Rutherford's model by explaining the stability of atoms and the origin of atomic spectra.

Card 5

Calculate the wavelength of light emitted when an electron in a hydrogen atom transitions from n=3 to n=2. (Rydberg constant, R = 1.097 × 10^7 m⁻¹)

Using the Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R(1/n₁² - 1/n₂²). For n₁=2, n₂=3: 1/λ = 1.097×10⁷(1/4 - 1/9) = 1.097×10⁷(5/36) = 1.524×10⁶ m⁻¹. λ = 6.56×10⁻⁷ m (656 nm, red line in Balmer series).

Card 6

What is the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 1% the speed of light? (Mass of electron = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s)

De Broglie wavelength: λ = h/mv. v = 0.01 × 3×10⁸ m/s = 3×10⁶ m/s. λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 3×10⁶) = 2.43×10⁻¹⁰ m (0.243 nm).

Card 7

Explain Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle and its implication for atomic structure.

Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle states that it is impossible to simultaneously determine the exact position (Δx) and momentum (Δp) of a particle with absolute precision: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π. For electrons in atoms, this implies that we cannot define fixed orbits (as in Bohr's model) but must describe electron positions as probabilities (orbitals).

Card 8

What are quantum numbers? Name the four quantum numbers and their significance.

Quantum numbers describe the properties of electrons in atoms: (1) Principal quantum number (n): energy level and size of orbital (n = 1, 2, 3...). (2) Azimuthal quantum number (l): shape of orbital (l = 0 to n-1; s, p, d, f). (3) Magnetic quantum number (mₗ): orientation of orbital (mₗ = -l to +l). (4) Spin quantum number (mₛ): electron spin (mₛ = +1/2 or -1/2).

Card 9

Draw the shapes of s, p, and d orbitals. How does the number of nodes relate to the principal quantum number?

s-orbital: Spherical shape. p-orbital: Dumbbell-shaped (3 orientations: pₓ, pᵧ, p_z). d-orbital: Cloverleaf or double dumbbell (5 orientations). Number of radial nodes = n - l - 1. For example, 3s orbital (n=3, l=0) has 2 radial nodes.

Card 10

State the Pauli Exclusion Principle and explain its role in electron configuration.

Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers. This limits each orbital to a maximum of 2 electrons with opposite spins, which governs the filling order of orbitals and the structure of the periodic table.

Card 11

Write the electron configuration of chromium (Z=24) and explain its exception to the Aufbau principle.

Chromium: [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ (not [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²). Exception occurs because a half-filled d-subshell (d⁵) is more stable due to exchange energy and symmetry. Similar exceptions occur in copper (Z=29: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹).

Card 12

What is the photoelectric effect? How did Einstein's explanation support the particle nature of light?

The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency shines on it. Einstein explained it using Planck's quantum theory: light consists of discrete packets (photons) with energy E = hν. If hν ≥ work function (φ), electrons are ejected. This supported the particle nature of light (wave-particle duality).

Card 13

Calculate the energy of a photon with a wavelength of 500 nm. (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3×10⁸ m/s)

Energy of photon: E = hc/λ. λ = 500 nm = 5×10⁻⁷ m. E = (6.626×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸) / 5×10⁻⁷ = 3.976×10⁻¹⁹ J (or 2.48 eV).

Card 14

Explain the concept of effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) and its trend across a period.

Effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) is the net positive charge experienced by an electron in a multi-electron atom. Z_eff = Z - σ (where σ = shielding constant). Across a period (left to right), Z_eff increases because the number of protons increases while the shielding effect of inner electrons remains nearly constant, leading to stronger attraction for valence electrons.

Card 15

What is the difference between an orbit (Bohr's model) and an orbital (quantum mechanical model)?

Orbit (Bohr's model): Fixed, circular path of an electron with definite radius and energy. Orbital (quantum mechanical model): Three-dimensional region in space where the probability of finding an electron is maximum (90-95%). Orbitals have no fixed path and are described by wavefunctions (ψ).

Card 16

State Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity and apply it to the electron configuration of oxygen (Z=8).

Hund's Rule: Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals (same energy) singly with parallel spins before pairing. For oxygen (Z=8): 1s² 2s² 2p⁴. The 2p subshell has three orbitals; the 4 electrons occupy them as ↑↓ ↑ ↑ (not ↑↓ ↑↓), maximizing unpaired electrons.

Card 17

What is the significance of the Schrödinger wave equation in quantum mechanics?

The Schrödinger wave equation (Hψ = Eψ) describes the wave-like behavior of electrons in atoms. It provides the wavefunction (ψ), which gives the probability density (ψ²) of finding an electron in a specific region. Solutions to the equation yield quantum numbers and orbital shapes, forming the basis of the quantum mechanical model of the atom.
