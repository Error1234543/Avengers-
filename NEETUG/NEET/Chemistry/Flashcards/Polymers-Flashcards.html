NEET Chemistry - Polymers (Flashcards)

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define a polymer and distinguish it from a monomer.

A polymer is a large molecule (macromolecule) composed of repeating structural units called monomers. Monomers are small molecules that can bond to other identical molecules to form a polymer through polymerization. Polymers have high molecular weights and exhibit properties distinct from their monomeric units due to their size and structure.

Card 2

Classify polymers based on their source. Provide one example for each type.

Polymers are classified into three types based on their source: 1. Natural Polymers: Found in nature, e.g., cellulose, starch, proteins, and natural rubber. 2. Semi-synthetic Polymers: Chemically modified natural polymers, e.g., cellulose acetate (rayon) and vulcanized rubber. 3. Synthetic Polymers: Man-made polymers, e.g., polyethylene, nylon, and Bakelite.

Card 3

What is the difference between addition polymerization and condensation polymerization? Give an example of each.

Addition polymerization involves the repeated addition of monomer molecules with double or triple bonds (unsaturated compounds) to form a polymer without the loss of any small molecules. Example: Formation of polyethylene from ethylene. Condensation polymerization involves the combination of monomers with the loss of small molecules like water, ammonia, or HCl. Example: Formation of nylon-6,6 from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid, with the elimination of water.

Card 4

Explain the term 'copolymer' and provide an example.

A copolymer is a polymer derived from more than one species of monomer. The monomers are arranged in a specific sequence along the polymer chain. Example: Buna-S, a copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and styrene, used in the manufacture of tires.

Card 5

What are the two types of polymerization mechanisms? Describe the steps involved in one of them.

The two types of polymerization mechanisms are: 1. Chain-growth (Addition) polymerization 2. Step-growth (Condensation) polymerization Steps in Chain-growth polymerization: 1. Initiation: Formation of an active center (free radical, cation, or anion) from the initiator. 2. Propagation: The active center reacts with monomer molecules, adding them to the growing chain and regenerating the active center. 3. Termination: The active center is deactivated, stopping chain growth. This can occur via combination, disproportionation, or chain transfer.

Card 6

Name the polymer used in making non-stick cookware and describe its structure.

The polymer used in making non-stick cookware is polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), commonly known as Teflon. Its structure consists of a long chain of carbon atoms, each bonded to two fluorine atoms: -(CF2-CF2)n-. The strong C-F bonds and the protective sheath of fluorine atoms make PTFE chemically inert and heat-resistant.

Card 7

What is vulcanization of rubber? How does it improve the properties of natural rubber?

Vulcanization is the process of heating natural rubber with sulfur (or other equivalent curatives) to improve its properties. During vulcanization, sulfur forms cross-links between the polymer chains of natural rubber, resulting in: 1. Increased tensile strength and elasticity. 2. Improved resistance to abrasion, oxidation, and organic solvents. 3. Reduced plasticity and stickiness. 4. Enhanced durability and temperature stability.

Card 8

Differentiate between thermoplastic and thermosetting polymers with examples.

Thermoplastic polymers soften on heating and harden on cooling, making them recyclable. They have linear or branched structures with weak van der Waals forces between chains. Example: Polyethylene, PVC. Thermosetting polymers become permanently hard when heated and cannot be remolded. They have a cross-linked or network structure. Example: Bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde resin.

Card 9

What is biodegradable polymer? Give an example and mention its significance.

Biodegradable polymers are polymers that can be broken down by microorganisms into simpler substances like water, carbon dioxide, and biomass under natural environmental conditions. Example: Polyhydroxybutyrate (PHB), a polyester produced by certain bacteria. Significance: Biodegradable polymers help reduce plastic pollution and environmental waste, offering sustainable alternatives to conventional synthetic polymers.

Card 10

Describe the preparation of nylon-6,6. Mention its uses.

Nylon-6,6 is prepared by the condensation polymerization of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid. The reaction involves the elimination of water molecules: n(H2N-(CH2)6-NH2) + n(HOOC-(CH2)4-COOH) → [-(NH-(CH2)6-NH-CO-(CH2)4-CO-)n-] + 2n H2O Uses: Nylon-6,6 is used in making textiles (e.g., clothing, carpets), industrial fibers (e.g., tire cords, conveyor belts), and plastic parts (e.g., gears, bearings).

Card 11

What is the role of Ziegler-Natta catalysts in polymerization? Provide an example of a polymer synthesized using these catalysts.

Ziegler-Natta catalysts are a combination of transition metal compounds (e.g., TiCl4) and organometallic compounds (e.g., triethylaluminum). They are used to control the stereochemistry of polymerization, enabling the production of stereoregular polymers (e.g., isotactic or syndiotactic polymers) with specific properties. Example: High-density polyethylene (HDPE) and isotactic polypropylene are synthesized using Ziegler-Natta catalysts.

Card 12

Explain the term 'glass transition temperature (Tg)' in the context of polymers.

The glass transition temperature (Tg) is the temperature at which a polymer transitions from a hard, glassy state to a soft, rubbery state. Below Tg, the polymer is rigid and brittle; above Tg, it becomes flexible and elastic. Tg is a critical parameter for determining the suitability of a polymer for specific applications, as it affects mechanical properties, thermal stability, and processing conditions.

Card 13

What are elastomers? Give an example and mention their key properties.

Elastomers are polymers with viscoelasticity, meaning they exhibit both viscosity and elasticity. They can undergo significant deformation under stress and return to their original shape when the stress is removed. Example: Natural rubber and synthetic rubbers like neoprene. Key properties: 1. High elasticity and flexibility. 2. Low Young's modulus. 3. Ability to absorb and dissipate energy. 4. Resistance to abrasion and impact.

Card 14

Describe the structure and uses of Bakelite.

Bakelite is a thermosetting phenol-formaldehyde resin. Its structure consists of a highly cross-linked network of phenol and formaldehyde units, formed through condensation polymerization. Uses: Bakelite is used in electrical insulators, radio and telephone casings, kitchenware handles, and automotive components due to its heat resistance, electrical insulating properties, and mechanical strength.

Card 15

What is the difference between homopolymers and copolymers?

Homopolymers are polymers derived from a single type of monomer. Example: Polyethylene, formed from ethylene monomers. Copolymers are polymers derived from two or more different types of monomers. Example: Styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), formed from styrene and 1,3-butadiene monomers. Copolymers can exhibit properties distinct from those of their constituent homopolymers.
