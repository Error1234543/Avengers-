NEET Chemistry: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the functional group present in alcohols and write its general formula.

The functional group in alcohols is the hydroxyl group (-OH). The general formula for alcohols is R-OH, where R is an alkyl group.

Card 2

How are primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols classified? Provide an example of each.

Alcohols are classified based on the carbon atom to which the -OH group is attached. Primary (1°): -OH attached to a primary carbon (e.g., CH₃CH₂OH, ethanol). Secondary (2°): -OH attached to a secondary carbon (e.g., (CH₃)₂CHOH, isopropanol). Tertiary (3°): -OH attached to a tertiary carbon (e.g., (CH₃)₃COH, tert-butanol).

Card 3

Explain the mechanism of the reaction between ethanol and hydrogen bromide (HBr).

Ethanol reacts with HBr via an Sₙ2 mechanism for primary alcohols. The -OH group is protonated by HBr to form an oxonium ion (R-OH₂⁺), which is a good leaving group. Bromide ion (Br⁻) then attacks the carbon, displacing water and forming bromoethane (CH₃CH₂Br).

Card 4

What is the product formed when phenol reacts with bromine water? Explain the observation.

Phenol reacts with bromine water to form 2,4,6-tribromophenol, a white precipitate. The -OH group in phenol activates the benzene ring toward electrophilic substitution, making it highly reactive toward bromination even at room temperature.

Card 5

Write the reaction for the preparation of diethyl ether from ethanol using sulfuric acid.

Diethyl ether is prepared by the intermolecular dehydration of ethanol at 140°C in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid: 2 CH₃CH₂OH → (CH₃CH₂)₂O + H₂O.

Card 6

Why is phenol more acidic than ethanol? Explain with resonance structures.

Phenol is more acidic because its conjugate base (phenoxide ion) is stabilized by resonance. The negative charge is delocalized over the benzene ring, making it more stable. In ethanol, the negative charge on the ethoxide ion is localized on the oxygen atom, making it less stable and thus less acidic.

Card 7

What is the role of Lucas reagent in distinguishing between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols?

Lucas reagent (ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl) is used to distinguish alcohols based on their reactivity. Tertiary alcohols react immediately to form a cloudy solution (alkyl chloride formation). Secondary alcohols react within 5-10 minutes, while primary alcohols do not react at room temperature.

Card 8

Explain the Williamson ether synthesis with an example.

Williamson ether synthesis involves the reaction of an alkoxide ion (R-O⁻) with an alkyl halide (R'-X) to form an ether (R-O-R'). Example: CH₃CH₂O⁻Na⁺ + CH₃Br → CH₃CH₂OCH₃ + NaBr (ethoxy methane).

Card 9

What happens when phenol is treated with neutral FeCl₃ solution? Describe the observation.

Phenol reacts with neutral FeCl₃ to form a violet-colored complex. This is a characteristic test for phenols, indicating the presence of the phenolic -OH group.

Card 10

Write the reaction for the oxidation of a primary alcohol to an aldehyde and further to a carboxylic acid.

Primary alcohols are oxidized to aldehydes using mild oxidizing agents like PCC (Pyridinium chlorochromate): R-CH₂OH → R-CHO. Further oxidation with stronger agents like KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇ yields carboxylic acids: R-CHO → R-COOH.

Card 11

How does the boiling point of alcohols compare to that of alkanes of similar molecular weight? Explain.

Alcohols have higher boiling points than alkanes of similar molecular weight due to hydrogen bonding between the -OH groups of alcohol molecules. Alkanes lack hydrogen bonding and only have weak van der Waals forces.

Card 12

What is the product of the reaction between phenol and dilute nitric acid? Write the reaction.

Phenol reacts with dilute nitric acid to form a mixture of ortho-nitrophenol and para-nitrophenol: C₆H₅OH + HNO₃ (dilute) → C₆H₄(NO₂)OH (ortho/para) + H₂O.

Card 13

Explain why ethers are relatively inert compared to alcohols and phenols.

Ethers are relatively inert because the C-O-C bond is strong and less polar compared to the O-H bond in alcohols and phenols. They do not readily undergo substitution or elimination reactions under normal conditions.

Card 14

What is the product formed when anisole (methoxybenzene) is treated with HI?

Anisole reacts with HI to form phenol and methyl iodide via cleavage of the C-O bond: C₆H₅OCH₃ + HI → C₆H₅OH + CH₃I.

Card 15

Describe the industrial preparation of ethanol from ethene.

Ethanol is prepared industrially by the hydration of ethene in the presence of phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) catalyst at high temperature and pressure: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH.

Card 16

What is the difference between absolute alcohol and denatured alcohol?

Absolute alcohol is 100% ethanol with no water, while denatured alcohol is ethanol mixed with additives (e.g., methanol, pyridine) to make it unfit for consumption, often used for industrial purposes.

Card 17

Explain the Kolbe-Schmitt reaction with an example.

The Kolbe-Schmitt reaction involves the carboxylation of phenol with CO₂ under high pressure to form salicylic acid: C₆H₅OH + CO₂ → C₆H₄(OH)COOH (salicylic acid).

Card 18

What is the product of the reaction between tert-butyl alcohol and HCl? Write the mechanism.

Tert-butyl alcohol reacts with HCl via an Sₙ1 mechanism to form tert-butyl chloride: (CH₃)₃COH + HCl → (CH₃)₃CCl + H₂O. The reaction proceeds via a carbocation intermediate due to the stability of the tertiary carbocation.

Card 19

Why are phenols more soluble in aqueous NaOH than in water?

Phenols are more soluble in aqueous NaOH because they react with NaOH to form water-soluble phenoxide ions (C₆H₅O⁻Na⁺). In water, phenols form weaker hydrogen bonds, limiting their solubility.

Card 20

Write the reaction for the preparation of methyl phenyl ether (anisole) using Williamson synthesis.

Anisole is prepared by reacting sodium phenoxide with methyl iodide: C₆H₅O⁻Na⁺ + CH₃I → C₆H₅OCH₃ + NaI.
