NEET Chemistry: Hydrogen and s-Block Elements - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the position of hydrogen in the periodic table and why is it unique?

Hydrogen is placed in Group 1 of the periodic table due to its electronic configuration (1s¹), which resembles alkali metals. However, it is unique because it can also gain one electron to achieve a stable configuration, similar to halogens. Unlike alkali metals, hydrogen is a non-metal and exists as a diatomic gas (H₂) under standard conditions. Its small size and high ionization enthalpy further distinguish it from Group 1 elements.

Card 2

Explain the isotopes of hydrogen and their significance.

Hydrogen has three isotopes: protium (¹H), deuterium (²H or D), and tritium (³H or T). Protium has no neutrons, deuterium has one neutron, and tritium has two neutrons. Deuterium is used as a moderator in nuclear reactors, while tritium is radioactive and used in nuclear fusion reactions. The difference in mass between these isotopes leads to significant variations in physical properties, such as boiling points and bond energies.

Card 3

What are the methods of preparation of dihydrogen (H₂) in the laboratory?

1. **Reaction of metals with acids**: Zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid or sulfuric acid to produce H₂. Example: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂. 2. **Reaction of metals with alkalis**: Aluminum or zinc reacts with sodium hydroxide to liberate H₂. Example: 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + 3H₂. 3. **Electrolysis of water**: Passing electricity through acidified or alkaline water produces H₂ at the cathode. Example: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (in the presence of an electrolyte like H₂SO₄).

Card 4

Describe the industrial production of dihydrogen.

1. **Steam reforming of natural gas**: Methane (CH₄) reacts with steam at high temperatures (700–1000°C) in the presence of a nickel catalyst to produce syngas (CO + H₂). Example: CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂. 2. **Coal gasification**: Coal reacts with steam and oxygen at high temperatures to produce syngas. Example: C + H₂O → CO + H₂. 3. **Water-gas shift reaction**: Carbon monoxide from syngas reacts with steam to produce additional H₂. Example: CO + H₂O → CO₂ + H₂. 4. **Electrolysis of brine**: In the chlor-alkali process, H₂ is produced as a byproduct during the electrolysis of sodium chloride solution.

Card 5

What are the properties of dihydrogen that make it useful in industrial applications?

1. **High calorific value**: H₂ has a high energy content per unit mass (142 kJ/g), making it an efficient fuel. 2. **Reducing agent**: H₂ can reduce metal oxides to metals, e.g., CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O. 3. **Low density**: Its low density makes it useful in weather balloons and airships (though flammability limits this use). 4. **Reactivity with non-metals**: It forms hydrides with elements like oxygen (H₂O), nitrogen (NH₃), and halogens (HX). 5. **Use in hydrogenation**: H₂ is used to convert unsaturated hydrocarbons into saturated ones, e.g., in the production of margarine.

Card 6

Explain the types of hydrides formed by hydrogen with examples.

1. **Ionic or saline hydrides**: Formed by s-block elements (e.g., NaH, CaH₂). These are crystalline solids with high melting points and conduct electricity in the molten state. 2. **Covalent or molecular hydrides**: Formed by p-block elements (e.g., CH₄, NH₃, H₂O). These are volatile compounds with low melting and boiling points. 3. **Metallic or interstitial hydrides**: Formed by d- and f-block elements (e.g., PdH, TiH₂). These are non-stoichiometric and retain metallic properties like conductivity.

Card 7

What is the diagonal relationship between lithium and magnesium?

Lithium (Group 1) shows similarities with magnesium (Group 2) due to their comparable ionic radii and high charge-to-size ratios. Key similarities include: 1. **Formation of nitrides**: Both form nitrides (Li₃N and Mg₃N₂) when heated in nitrogen. 2. **Reaction with oxygen**: Both form oxides (Li₂O and MgO) and not peroxides or superoxides. 3. **Carbonates and hydroxides**: Both have carbonates (Li₂CO₃, MgCO₃) and hydroxides (LiOH, Mg(OH)₂) that decompose on heating. 4. **Solubility of salts**: Salts like fluorides and carbonates of both are sparingly soluble in water.

Card 8

Describe the anomalous behavior of lithium in Group 1 elements.

Lithium differs from other alkali metals due to: 1. **Small size and high polarization power**: Leads to covalent character in its compounds (e.g., LiCl is soluble in organic solvents). 2. **High ionization enthalpy**: Makes it less reactive than other alkali metals. 3. **Formation of nitride**: Only alkali metal to form Li₃N when heated in nitrogen. 4. **Thermal stability of compounds**: Li₂CO₃, LiOH, and LiNO₃ decompose on heating, unlike other alkali metal compounds. 5. **Solubility of salts**: LiF and Li₂CO₃ are sparingly soluble in water, unlike other alkali metal salts.

Card 9

What are the biological importance of sodium and potassium?

1. **Sodium (Na⁺)**: - Regulates osmotic pressure and water balance in cells. - Essential for nerve impulse transmission and muscle contraction. - Maintains blood pressure and pH balance. 2. **Potassium (K⁺)**: - Critical for nerve signal transmission and muscle function. - Activates enzymes involved in protein synthesis and carbohydrate metabolism. - Helps maintain electrical potential across cell membranes. - Deficiency of either ion can lead to muscle cramps, fatigue, or cardiac arrhythmias.

Card 10

Explain the preparation and properties of sodium hydroxide (NaOH).

**Preparation**: 1. **Electrolysis of brine (Chlor-alkali process)**: 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂ (using a mercury cathode or membrane cell). 2. **Leblanc process**: Na₂SO₄ + Ca(OH)₂ → NaOH + CaSO₄ (historical method). **Properties**: 1. **Physical**: White, deliquescent solid with a melting point of 318°C. Highly soluble in water, exothermic dissolution. 2. **Chemical**: - Strong base: Reacts with acids to form salts (e.g., NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O). - Reacts with CO₂ to form Na₂CO₃. - Saponification: Reacts with fats to produce soap. - Amphoteric metals: Dissolves metals like Al and Zn to produce H₂ (e.g., 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + 3H₂).

Card 11

What are the uses of sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃) and sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃)?

**Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)**: 1. **Glass industry**: Used as a flux to lower the melting point of silica. 2. **Paper industry**: Acts as a pH regulator and bleaching agent. 3. **Water softening**: Precipitates Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions as carbonates. 4. **Detergents**: Enhances cleaning efficiency. 5. **Textile industry**: Used in dyeing and finishing processes. **Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃)**: 1. **Baking soda**: Releases CO₂ on heating, used in baking (e.g., 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂). 2. **Antacid**: Neutralizes stomach acid (e.g., NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂). 3. **Fire extinguishers**: Produces CO₂ to smother flames. 4. **Mild abrasive**: Used in toothpaste and cleaning agents.
