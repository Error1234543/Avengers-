1. Which of the following statements correctly describes the phenomenon of adsorption?

A. It is a bulk phenomenon where the substance is uniformly distributed throughout the solid.
B. It is a surface phenomenon where the substance accumulates only on the surface of the solid.
C. It involves the penetration of one substance into the bulk of another substance.
D. It is always an exothermic process with a positive enthalpy change.
Answer: B

Adsorption is a surface phenomenon where molecules or atoms of a substance (adsorbate) accumulate on the surface of another substance (adsorbent). Unlike absorption, it does not involve penetration into the bulk. Adsorption is typically exothermic (negative enthalpy change), but the key defining feature is its surface-specific nature.

2. The extent of adsorption of a gas on a solid surface increases with:

A. Increase in temperature and decrease in pressure.
B. Decrease in temperature and increase in pressure.
C. Increase in both temperature and pressure.
D. Decrease in both temperature and pressure.
Answer: B

The extent of adsorption generally increases with a decrease in temperature (as adsorption is exothermic) and an increase in pressure (as higher pressure forces more gas molecules onto the surface). This relationship is described by adsorption isotherms like the Freundlich isotherm.

3. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of physisorption?

A. It is reversible in nature.
B. It involves weak van der Waals forces.
C. It has a high enthalpy of adsorption (80-240 kJ/mol).
D. It forms multimolecular layers at high pressures.
Answer: C

Physisorption (physical adsorption) is characterized by weak van der Waals forces, reversibility, and the formation of multimolecular layers at high pressures. The enthalpy of adsorption is low (20-40 kJ/mol), unlike chemisorption, which involves stronger chemical bonds and higher enthalpy values (80-240 kJ/mol).

4. The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by the equation x/m = kP^(1/n). What does the term '1/n' represent?

A. The total surface area of the adsorbent.
B. The fraction of the adsorbent surface covered by the adsorbate.
C. A constant that depends on the nature of the adsorbent and adsorbate, indicating the intensity of adsorption.
D. The equilibrium pressure of the gas.
Answer: C

In the Freundlich adsorption isotherm (x/m = kP^(1/n)), the term '1/n' is a constant that depends on the nature of the adsorbent and adsorbate. It indicates the intensity of adsorption and typically lies between 0 and 1. A value of 1/n closer to 0 suggests stronger adsorption.

5. Which of the following is an example of chemisorption?

A. Adsorption of nitrogen gas on activated charcoal.
B. Adsorption of hydrogen gas on the surface of nickel.
C. Adsorption of water vapor on silica gel.
D. Adsorption of methane on the surface of glass.
Answer: B

Chemisorption involves the formation of chemical bonds between the adsorbate and the adsorbent. The adsorption of hydrogen gas on the surface of nickel is an example of chemisorption, as hydrogen molecules dissociate and form chemical bonds with the nickel surface. The other options involve weak van der Waals forces (physisorption).

6. The process of removing an adsorbed substance from a surface is known as:

A. Absorption
B. Desorption
C. Sorption
D. Diffusion
Answer: B

Desorption is the process of removing an adsorbed substance from the surface of an adsorbent. It is the reverse of adsorption and can be achieved by increasing temperature or decreasing pressure, depending on the nature of the adsorption.

7. Which of the following statements about catalysts is correct in the context of surface chemistry?

A. A catalyst increases the equilibrium constant of a reaction.
B. A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy.
C. A catalyst is consumed during the reaction and must be replenished.
D. A catalyst changes the enthalpy of the reaction.
Answer: B

A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy, thereby increasing the rate of the reaction. It does not alter the equilibrium constant, is not consumed during the reaction, and does not change the enthalpy of the reaction.

8. The Langmuir adsorption isotherm assumes that:

A. Adsorption is multimolecular and occurs in layers.
B. Adsorption is limited to a monolayer and occurs at specific sites on the surface.
C. The enthalpy of adsorption decreases with increasing surface coverage.
D. The adsorbate molecules interact strongly with each other on the surface.
Answer: B

The Langmuir adsorption isotherm assumes that adsorption is limited to a monolayer and occurs at specific, equivalent sites on the surface. It also assumes that there are no interactions between adsorbed molecules and that the enthalpy of adsorption is constant.

9. Which of the following is a property of colloidal solutions?

A. They are homogeneous mixtures.
B. They show the Tyndall effect due to the scattering of light by colloidal particles.
C. They can be separated by filtration using ordinary filter paper.
D. Their particles settle down rapidly under the influence of gravity.
Answer: B

Colloidal solutions exhibit the Tyndall effect, where light is scattered by the colloidal particles, making the path of light visible. They are heterogeneous mixtures, cannot be separated by ordinary filtration, and their particles do not settle rapidly under gravity due to Brownian motion.

10. The process of peptization involves:

A. The coagulation of a colloidal solution by the addition of an electrolyte.
B. The conversion of a precipitate into a colloidal solution by the addition of a suitable electrolyte.
C. The adsorption of ions on the surface of colloidal particles.
D. The precipitation of colloidal particles due to heating.
Answer: B

Peptization is the process of converting a precipitate into a colloidal solution by adding a suitable electrolyte. The electrolyte provides ions that adsorb onto the precipitate particles, imparting a charge and causing them to disperse as a colloidal solution.

11. Which of the following is used as a catalyst in the Haber process for the synthesis of ammonia?

A. Platinum
B. Iron with promoters like Al2O3 and K2O
C. Nickel
D. Vanadium pentoxide
Answer: B

In the Haber process for the synthesis of ammonia, iron (Fe) is used as the catalyst, often promoted with small amounts of Al2O3 and K2O to enhance its activity and stability. Platinum and nickel are used in other catalytic processes, while vanadium pentoxide is used in the contact process for sulfuric acid production.

12. The Brownian motion observed in colloidal solutions is due to:

A. The gravitational force acting on the colloidal particles.
B. The collision of colloidal particles with the molecules of the dispersion medium.
C. The electrostatic repulsion between colloidal particles.
D. The adsorption of ions on the surface of colloidal particles.
Answer: B

Brownian motion in colloidal solutions is caused by the random collision of colloidal particles with the molecules of the dispersion medium. This motion helps keep the colloidal particles suspended and prevents them from settling under gravity.

13. The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by the equation x/m = kP^(1/n). Which of the following statements about this equation is correct?

A. The value of n is always less than 1.
B. The equation is valid only for chemisorption.
C. At low pressures, the equation reduces to x/m = kP.
D. The constants k and n are independent of temperature.
Answer: C

The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by x/m = kP^(1/n). At low pressures, 1/n ≈ 1, so the equation simplifies to x/m = kP. The value of n is usually greater than 1, and the constants k and n depend on temperature and the nature of the adsorbent and adsorbate.

14. Which of the following is an example of a heterogeneous catalyst?

A. Enzymes in biological systems.
B. Iron in the Haber process for ammonia synthesis.
C. Hydrochloric acid in the esterification reaction.
D. Platinum in the homogeneous hydrogenation of alkenes.
Answer: B

A heterogeneous catalyst is in a different phase than the reactants. In the Haber process, iron (solid) catalyzes the reaction between nitrogen and hydrogen (gases), making it a heterogeneous catalyst. Enzymes and hydrochloric acid are homogeneous catalysts, while platinum in homogeneous hydrogenation is also homogeneous.

15. Colloidal solutions exhibit the Tyndall effect because:

A. The particles are large enough to scatter light.
B. The particles are charged and repel each other.
C. The particles undergo Brownian motion.
D. The particles are in a true solution state.
Answer: A

The Tyndall effect is observed in colloidal solutions because the particles are large enough (though not visible to the naked eye) to scatter light. This scattering makes the path of light visible through the solution.

16. Which of the following methods is used to prepare a lyophilic colloidal solution?

A. Mechanical dispersion
B. Peptization
C. Double decomposition
D. Reduction
Answer: B

Lyophilic colloidal solutions are prepared by peptization, where a freshly precipitated substance is dispersed into a colloidal solution by adding a suitable electrolyte (peptizing agent). Mechanical dispersion is used for lyophobic colloids, while double decomposition and reduction are chemical methods for preparing lyophobic colloids.

17. The gold number is a measure of:

A. The protective power of a lyophilic colloid.
B. The charge on colloidal particles.
C. The size of colloidal particles.
D. The stability of a lyophobic colloid.
Answer: A

The gold number is defined as the minimum amount of a protective colloid (in milligrams) required to prevent the coagulation of 10 mL of a standard gold sol when 1 mL of 10% NaCl solution is added. It is a measure of the protective power of a lyophilic colloid.

18. Which of the following is NOT a method of coagulation of a colloidal solution?

A. Addition of an electrolyte
B. Electrophoresis
C. Dialysis
D. Heating
Answer: C

Dialysis is a method used to purify colloidal solutions by removing dissolved impurities, not to coagulate them. Coagulation can be achieved by adding electrolytes, electrophoresis (movement of charged particles under an electric field), or heating, which increases the kinetic energy of particles and leads to coagulation.

19. In the adsorption of acetic acid on activated charcoal, the amount of acetic acid adsorbed decreases with:

A. Increase in temperature
B. Increase in concentration of acetic acid
C. Increase in surface area of charcoal
D. Decrease in pressure
Answer: A

Adsorption is an exothermic process, so it decreases with an increase in temperature. The amount of acetic acid adsorbed increases with its concentration and the surface area of the charcoal. Pressure changes are more relevant for gas adsorption and have negligible effect on liquid adsorption.

20. Which of the following statements correctly describes the phenomenon of adsorption isotherms as per the Freundlich adsorption isotherm?

A. The extent of adsorption is independent of pressure at low temperatures.
B. The extent of adsorption (x/m) is directly proportional to the pressure (P) at all pressures.
C. The extent of adsorption (x/m) is proportional to P^(1/n), where n is a constant greater than 1, at moderate pressures.
D. The Freundlich isotherm fails to explain adsorption at high pressures but is accurate at extremely low pressures.
Answer: C

The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is mathematically expressed as x/m = k * P^(1/n), where x/m is the extent of adsorption, P is the pressure, and k and n are constants. Here, n is typically greater than 1, indicating that the adsorption increases with pressure but not linearly. This relationship holds true at moderate pressures. At very low pressures, the isotherm approximates a linear relationship (x/m ∝ P), while at high pressures, it deviates as the surface becomes saturated. Thus, option C correctly captures the essence of the Freundlich isotherm.

21. Which of the following statements correctly describes the role of a promoter in catalytic reactions?

A. A promoter increases the surface area of the catalyst, thereby enhancing its activity.
B. A promoter itself acts as a catalyst and provides additional active sites for the reaction.
C. A promoter modifies the electronic structure of the catalyst, increasing its efficiency without participating directly in the reaction.
D. A promoter prevents the adsorption of reactants on the catalyst surface, reducing side reactions.
Answer: C

A promoter in catalytic reactions is a substance that enhances the efficiency of a catalyst. It does not act as a catalyst itself but modifies the electronic or crystal structure of the catalyst, improving its activity. For example, in the Haber process, molybdenum or potassium oxide acts as a promoter for the iron catalyst, increasing the rate of ammonia synthesis without directly participating in the reaction.

22. The adsorption of a gas on a solid surface follows the Freundlich adsorption isotherm. If the pressure of the gas is doubled at constant temperature, the amount of gas adsorbed per unit mass of the adsorbent will:

A. Remain unchanged.
B. Double.
C. Increase by a factor less than 2.
D. Increase by a factor greater than 2.
Answer: C

The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by the equation: x/m = k * P^(1/n), where x/m is the amount of gas adsorbed per unit mass of adsorbent, P is the pressure, and k and n are constants (with n > 1). When the pressure is doubled, the new adsorption becomes x'/m = k * (2P)^(1/n) = 2^(1/n) * (k * P^(1/n)). Since n > 1, 2^(1/n) is less than 2, meaning the amount adsorbed increases by a factor less than 2.

23. In the context of colloids, which of the following phenomena is responsible for the scattering of light observed in a colloidal solution?

A. Electrophoresis
B. Tyndall effect
C. Brownian motion
D. Coagulation
Answer: B

The Tyndall effect is the scattering of light by colloidal particles suspended in a transparent medium. This phenomenon occurs because the size of colloidal particles is comparable to the wavelength of visible light, causing the light to scatter in all directions. This is why colloidal solutions appear turbid or milky when a beam of light passes through them, unlike true solutions, which do not exhibit this effect.
