This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on the topic of Polymers, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer from the options provided.

1. Which of the following polymers is formed by the condensation polymerization of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid?

A. Nylon-6
B. Nylon-6,6
C. Bakelite
D. Terylene
Answer: B

Nylon-6,6 is formed by the condensation polymerization of hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid, resulting in the formation of amide linkages. The name '6,6' indicates the number of carbon atoms in the diamine and diacid, respectively.

2. The polymer used in the manufacture of unbreakable crockery is:

A. Melamine-formaldehyde
B. Urea-formaldehyde
C. Bakelite
D. Polystyrene
Answer: A

Melamine-formaldehyde is a thermosetting polymer known for its hardness and resistance to heat, making it ideal for manufacturing unbreakable crockery.

3. Which of the following is a biodegradable polymer?

A. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
B. Polythene
C. Polyhydroxybutyrate-co-hydroxyvalerate (PHBV)
D. Polystyrene
Answer: C

PHBV is a biodegradable polymer produced by certain bacteria. It degrades naturally in the environment, unlike synthetic polymers like PVC, polythene, and polystyrene.

4. The process of vulcanization of rubber involves the addition of:

A. Sulphur
B. Oxygen
C. Nitrogen
D. Carbon
Answer: A

Vulcanization is the process of adding sulphur to natural rubber to improve its elasticity, strength, and durability by forming cross-links between polymer chains.

5. Which of the following statements about thermosetting polymers is correct?

A. They soften on heating and harden on cooling.
B. They cannot be remoulded once set.
C. They are formed by addition polymerization.
D. They are soluble in organic solvents.
Answer: B

Thermosetting polymers undergo irreversible chemical changes when heated, forming a rigid three-dimensional network. Once set, they cannot be remoulded or reshaped.

6. The monomer of natural rubber is:

A. Isoprene
B. Chloroprene
C. Neoprene
D. Styrene
Answer: A

Natural rubber is a polymer of isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene). The repeating unit in natural rubber is derived from isoprene molecules.

7. Which of the following polymers is used in the manufacture of paints and lacquers?

A. Glyptal
B. Buna-N
C. Neoprene
D. Polypropene
Answer: A

Glyptal, a polyester formed from ethylene glycol and phthalic acid, is commonly used in the manufacture of paints and lacquers due to its adhesive properties.

8. The polymer chain in high-density polythene (HDPE) is:

A. Highly branched
B. Linear with minimal branching
C. Cross-linked
D. Amorphous
Answer: B

High-density polythene (HDPE) has a linear structure with minimal branching, which allows the polymer chains to pack closely, resulting in higher density and strength.

9. Which of the following is an example of a copolymer?

A. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
B. Buna-S
C. Polystyrene
D. Polyethene
Answer: B

Buna-S is a copolymer of butadiene and styrene, used in the manufacture of synthetic rubber. Copolymers are formed from two or more different monomers.

10. The polymer used in the manufacture of non-stick cookware is:

A. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
B. Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)
C. Bakelite
D. Melamine-formaldehyde
Answer: B

Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), commonly known as Teflon, is used in non-stick cookware due to its high heat resistance and low friction properties.

11. Which of the following statements about addition polymerization is incorrect?

A. It involves the repeated addition of monomer molecules with double bonds.
B. It results in the formation of a polymer without the loss of any small molecules.
C. It typically requires a catalyst or initiator.
D. It is used to form condensation polymers like nylon.
Answer: D

Addition polymerization is used to form polymers like polythene and polystyrene, not condensation polymers like nylon. Condensation polymerization involves the loss of small molecules like water or methanol.

12. The polymer formed by the polymerization of caprolactam is:

A. Nylon-6,6
B. Nylon-6
C. Terylene
D. Bakelite
Answer: B

Nylon-6 is formed by the ring-opening polymerization of caprolactam, a cyclic amide. The number '6' indicates the number of carbon atoms in the monomer unit.

13. Which of the following polymers is an example of a thermosetting polymer?

A. Polyethylene
B. Bakelite
C. Nylon-6,6
D. Polystyrene
Answer: B

Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer formed from the condensation of phenol and formaldehyde. Once set, it cannot be remelted or reshaped, unlike thermoplastic polymers.

14. Which of the following is a copolymer?

A. Polyethylene
B. Buna-S
C. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
D. Polystyrene
Answer: B

Buna-S is a copolymer of butadiene and styrene. Copolymers are formed from two or more different monomers, unlike homopolymers, which are formed from a single type of monomer.

15. The polymer used in the manufacture of bulletproof vests is:

A. Kevlar
B. Nylon-6,6
C. Bakelite
D. Terylene
Answer: A

Kevlar is a high-strength synthetic polymer used in bulletproof vests due to its exceptional tensile strength and resistance to impact.

16. Which of the following polymers is used in the manufacture of electrical switches and plugs?

A. Polyethylene
B. Bakelite
C. Polystyrene
D. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
Answer: B

Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer used in electrical switches and plugs due to its excellent insulating properties and heat resistance.

17. The polymer chain growth mechanism that involves the formation of a reactive intermediate like a free radical or ion is called:

A. Condensation polymerization
B. Addition polymerization
C. Step-growth polymerization
D. Coordination polymerization
Answer: B

Addition polymerization involves the formation of a reactive intermediate (e.g., free radical, cation, or anion) that propagates the polymer chain by adding monomer units sequentially.

18. Assertion (A): Natural rubber is a polymer of isoprene. Reason (R): Isoprene units in natural rubber are linked in a cis-configuration, imparting elasticity. Choose the correct option:

A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
C. A is true, but R is false
D. A is false, but R is true
Answer: A

Natural rubber is indeed a polymer of isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene). The elasticity of natural rubber arises from the cis-configuration of the isoprene units, which prevents tight packing and allows the polymer chains to stretch. Thus, both the assertion and reason are correct, and the reason explains the assertion.

19. A polymer sample has a number-average molecular weight (Mn) of 50,000 and a weight-average molecular weight (Mw) of 100,000. The polydispersity index (PDI) of the sample is:

A. 0.5
B. 1.0
C. 2.0
D. 5.0
Answer: C

The polydispersity index (PDI) is calculated as the ratio of the weight-average molecular weight (Mw) to the number-average molecular weight (Mn). Here, PDI = Mw / Mn = 100,000 / 50,000 = 2.0. A PDI of 1 indicates a monodisperse polymer, while values greater than 1 indicate polydispersity.

20. Assertion: Natural rubber is a polymer of isoprene and is cis-1,4-polyisoprene. Reason: The cis-configuration imparts elasticity to natural rubber by preventing close packing of polymer chains.

A. Both assertion and reason are true, and the reason is the correct explanation of the assertion.
B. Both assertion and reason are true, but the reason is not the correct explanation of the assertion.
C. The assertion is true, but the reason is false.
D. The assertion is false, but the reason is true.
Answer: A

Natural rubber is indeed a polymer of isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) and exists as cis-1,4-polyisoprene. The cis-configuration prevents the polymer chains from packing closely, which allows the chains to stretch and recoil, imparting elasticity. Thus, both the assertion and reason are correct, and the reason explains the assertion.

21. A polymer sample has the following characteristics: it is resistant to heat, chemicals, and electrical insulation. It is formed by the step-growth polymerization of phenol and formaldehyde. Identify the polymer.

A. Melamine
B. Bakelite
C. Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)
D. Polystyrene
Answer: B

Bakelite is a thermosetting polymer formed by the step-growth polymerization of phenol and formaldehyde. It is known for its resistance to heat, chemicals, and electrical insulation properties. Melamine is formed from melamine and formaldehyde, PVC is an addition polymer of vinyl chloride, and polystyrene is an addition polymer of styrene.

22. Which of the following statements about biodegradable polymers is INCORRECT?

A. Poly-β-hydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate (PHBV) is a biodegradable polymer used in medical applications.
B. Biodegradable polymers are typically broken down by enzymatic action of microorganisms.
C. Polythene is a biodegradable polymer due to its simple hydrocarbon structure.
D. Polylactic acid (PLA) is derived from renewable resources like corn starch and is biodegradable.
Answer: C

Polythene (polyethylene) is a non-biodegradable polymer due to its stable hydrocarbon structure, which resists microbial degradation. The other statements are correct: PHBV is biodegradable and used in medical applications, biodegradable polymers are broken down by microorganisms, and PLA is derived from renewable resources and is biodegradable.

23. Which of the following statements correctly differentiates thermoplastic and thermosetting polymers?

A. Thermoplastic polymers soften on heating and harden on cooling reversibly, while thermosetting polymers become infusible and insoluble on heating due to extensive cross-linking.
B. Thermoplastic polymers are always linear or branched, while thermosetting polymers are always network polymers with no exceptions.
C. Thermosetting polymers can be recycled multiple times, whereas thermoplastic polymers degrade after a single heating cycle.
D. Thermoplastic polymers are formed by condensation polymerization, while thermosetting polymers are formed exclusively by addition polymerization.
Answer: A

Thermoplastic polymers, such as polyethylene and PVC, soften upon heating and can be reshaped due to weak van der Waals forces or hydrogen bonds between chains, which are reversible. Thermosetting polymers, like bakelite and vulcanized rubber, undergo extensive cross-linking during heating, forming a rigid three-dimensional network that cannot be remelted or reshaped. Option B is incorrect because some thermosetting polymers may initially be linear before cross-linking. Option C is incorrect because thermosetting polymers cannot be recycled due to their irreversible cross-linking. Option D is incorrect as both thermoplastic and thermosetting polymers can be formed by either addition or condensation polymerization, depending on the monomer and reaction conditions.
