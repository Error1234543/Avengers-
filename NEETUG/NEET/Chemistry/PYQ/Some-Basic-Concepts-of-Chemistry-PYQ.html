1. Which of the following statements about the law of definite proportions is correct?

A. It states that a chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in a fixed ratio by mass, regardless of the source.
B. It was proposed by John Dalton to explain the law of multiple proportions.
C. It applies only to gaseous compounds and not to solids or liquids.
D. It is also known as the law of conservation of mass.
Answer: A

The law of definite proportions, proposed by Joseph Proust, states that a chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in a fixed ratio by mass, irrespective of its source or method of preparation. This is a fundamental principle in stoichiometry and does not apply exclusively to gases.

2. The number of atoms present in 0.5 moles of nitrogen gas (N₂) is:

A. 3.011 × 10²³ atoms
B. 6.022 × 10²³ atoms
C. 1.204 × 10²⁴ atoms
D. 1.505 × 10²³ atoms
Answer: B

1 mole of any substance contains Avogadro's number (6.022 × 10²³) of particles. Nitrogen gas (N₂) is diatomic, so 0.5 moles of N₂ contain 0.5 × 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 6.022 × 10²³ atoms.

3. Which of the following represents the correct relationship between molarity (M) and molality (m) for a solution?

A. M = m × density of solution (in g/mL)
B. M = (m × density of solution) / (1 + m × molar mass of solute)
C. m = M × density of solution
D. M = m / (1 - m × molar mass of solute)
Answer: B

Molarity (M) is moles of solute per liter of solution, while molality (m) is moles of solute per kilogram of solvent. The relationship between them is given by: M = (m × density of solution) / (1 + m × molar mass of solute), where density is in g/mL.

4. A compound contains 40% carbon, 6.67% hydrogen, and 53.33% oxygen by mass. Its empirical formula is:

A. CH₂O
B. C₂H₄O₂
C. CH₃O
D. C₃H₆O₃
Answer: A

Assume 100 g of the compound: Carbon = 40 g, Hydrogen = 6.67 g, Oxygen = 53.33 g. Moles of C = 40/12 = 3.33, H = 6.67/1 = 6.67, O = 53.33/16 = 3.33. Divide by the smallest number of moles (3.33): C = 1, H = 2, O = 1. Thus, the empirical formula is CH₂O.

5. The limiting reagent in a chemical reaction is the one that:

A. Has the highest molar mass.
B. Is present in the smallest stoichiometric amount.
C. Is completely consumed first, limiting the amount of product formed.
D. Is in excess after the reaction completes.
Answer: C

The limiting reagent is the reactant that is completely consumed first in a reaction, thereby determining the maximum amount of product that can be formed. It is not necessarily the reactant with the smallest mass or molar amount but the one that is stoichiometrically deficient.

6. Which of the following expressions correctly represents the mole fraction (χ) of a solute in a solution?

A. χ = moles of solute / moles of solvent
B. χ = moles of solute / (moles of solute + moles of solvent)
C. χ = moles of solute / volume of solution (in liters)
D. χ = mass of solute / mass of solution
Answer: B

Mole fraction (χ) of a solute is defined as the ratio of the moles of solute to the total moles of all components in the solution (solute + solvent). It is a dimensionless quantity.

7. The percentage composition of oxygen in water (H₂O) is approximately:

A. 88.89%
B. 11.11%
C. 50%
D. 80%
Answer: A

The molar mass of water (H₂O) is 18 g/mol (2 × 1 + 16). The mass of oxygen in one mole of water is 16 g. Percentage of oxygen = (16 / 18) × 100 = 88.89%.

8. In the reaction: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, if 4 moles of hydrogen react with 2 moles of oxygen, the amount of water produced is:

A. 2 moles
B. 4 moles
C. 6 moles
D. 8 moles
Answer: B

The balanced equation shows that 2 moles of H₂ react with 1 mole of O₂ to produce 2 moles of H₂O. Here, 4 moles of H₂ would react with 2 moles of O₂ (which is in excess) to produce 4 moles of H₂O.

9. Which of the following is NOT a unit of concentration?

A. Molarity (M)
B. Molality (m)
C. Normality (N)
D. Mole (mol)
Answer: D

Mole (mol) is a unit of amount of substance, not concentration. Molarity (M), molality (m), and normality (N) are all units of concentration used to express the amount of solute in a solution.

10. The equivalent weight of an acid is given by:

A. Molar mass / number of replaceable hydrogen atoms
B. Molar mass × number of replaceable hydrogen atoms
C. Molar mass / basicity
D. Both A and C
Answer: D

The equivalent weight of an acid is calculated as its molar mass divided by the number of replaceable hydrogen atoms (also called basicity). For example, the equivalent weight of H₂SO₄ (molar mass = 98 g/mol) is 98 / 2 = 49 g/equiv.

11. A solution is prepared by dissolving 18 g of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) in 90 g of water. The mole fraction of glucose in the solution is:

A. 0.018
B. 0.020
C. 0.091
D. 0.100
Answer: B

Moles of glucose = 18 g / 180 g/mol = 0.1 mol. Moles of water = 90 g / 18 g/mol = 5 mol. Mole fraction of glucose = 0.1 / (0.1 + 5) = 0.1 / 5.1 ≈ 0.020.

12. The law of multiple proportions is illustrated by which of the following pairs of compounds?

A. CO and CO₂
B. H₂O and H₂O₂
C. NO and NO₂
D. All of the above
Answer: D

The law of multiple proportions states that when two elements form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other element are in the ratio of small whole numbers. This is illustrated by all the given pairs: CO and CO₂, H₂O and H₂O₂, and NO and NO₂.

13. The mass of one mole of a substance is equal to its:

A. Molecular mass expressed in grams
B. Atomic mass expressed in kilograms
C. Equivalent mass expressed in grams
D. Molar mass expressed in milligrams
Answer: A

One mole of any substance is defined as the amount of that substance which contains as many particles (atoms, molecules, or ions) as there are atoms in exactly 12 grams of carbon-12. This quantity is numerically equal to the molecular mass of the substance expressed in grams.

14. Which of the following statements about the mole concept is incorrect?

A. One mole of oxygen gas (O₂) contains 6.022 × 10²³ molecules.
B. The molar mass of water (H₂O) is 18 g/mol.
C. One mole of sodium chloride (NaCl) contains 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ ions.
D. The number of atoms in one mole of helium gas (He) is 6.022 × 10²³.
Answer: C

One mole of sodium chloride (NaCl) dissociates into one mole of Na⁺ ions and one mole of Cl⁻ ions, totaling 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ ions. However, the statement is misleading because it implies that NaCl exists as ions in its solid state, which is incorrect. The correct interpretation is for its dissociated form in solution.

15. The number of significant figures in 0.002500 is:

A. 2
B. 3
C. 4
D. 5
Answer: C

Significant figures are the digits in a number that carry meaning contributing to its precision. Leading zeros are not significant, but trailing zeros after the decimal point are. Thus, 0.002500 has 4 significant figures (2, 5, 0, 0).

16. A compound contains 40% carbon, 6.67% hydrogen, and 53.33% oxygen by mass. If its molecular mass is 180 g/mol, what is the molecular formula of the compound? (Atomic masses: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

A. C₆H₁₂O₆
B. C₅H₁₀O₅
C. C₃H₆O₃
D. C₄H₈O₄
Answer: A

Step 1: Calculate the empirical formula. Assume 100 g of the compound: Carbon = 40 g, Hydrogen = 6.67 g, Oxygen = 53.33 g. Moles of C = 40/12 = 3.33, H = 6.67/1 = 6.67, O = 53.33/16 = 3.33. Divide by the smallest number of moles (3.33): C = 1, H = 2, O = 1. Empirical formula = CH₂O. Step 2: Calculate the empirical formula mass = 12 + 2(1) + 16 = 30 g/mol. Step 3: Molecular mass = 180 g/mol. n = Molecular mass / Empirical formula mass = 180 / 30 = 6. Molecular formula = (CH₂O)₆ = C₆H₁₂O₆.

17. The mass of one molecule of a substance is 4.65 × 10⁻²³ g. The molar mass of the substance (in g mol⁻¹) is closest to: (Avogadro constant = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

A. 28
B. 32
C. 44
D. 56
Answer: A

Molar mass = mass of one molecule × Avogadro constant = 4.65 × 10⁻²³ g × 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ ≈ 28 g mol⁻¹. This matches the molar mass of nitrogen (N₂).

18. A solution is prepared by dissolving 5.85 g of NaCl in 500 g of water. The molality of the solution is: (Molar mass of NaCl = 58.5 g mol⁻¹)

A. 0.1 mol kg⁻¹
B. 0.2 mol kg⁻¹
C. 0.5 mol kg⁻¹
D. 1.0 mol kg⁻¹
Answer: B

Molality = moles of solute / mass of solvent (kg). Moles of NaCl = 5.85 g / 58.5 g mol⁻¹ = 0.1 mol. Mass of water = 0.5 kg. Molality = 0.1 mol / 0.5 kg = 0.2 mol kg⁻¹.

19. In a reaction, 2.8 g of nitrogen gas (N₂) reacts completely with hydrogen gas (H₂) to form ammonia (NH₃). The mass of ammonia formed is: (Molar masses: N₂ = 28 g mol⁻¹, NH₃ = 17 g mol⁻¹)

A. 1.7 g
B. 3.4 g
C. 5.1 g
D. 6.8 g
Answer: B

Balanced equation: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Moles of N₂ = 2.8 g / 28 g mol⁻¹ = 0.1 mol. Moles of NH₃ formed = 2 × 0.1 mol = 0.2 mol. Mass of NH₃ = 0.2 mol × 17 g mol⁻¹ = 3.4 g.

20. The percentage composition of sulfur in sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) is: (Molar mass of H₂SO₄ = 98 g mol⁻¹, S = 32 g mol⁻¹)

A. 32.65%
B. 33.33%
C. 65.31%
D. 98.00%
Answer: A

Percentage of sulfur = (Mass of S / Molar mass of H₂SO₄) × 100 = (32 / 98) × 100 ≈ 32.65%.

21. A gas occupies 2.24 L at STP. The number of molecules present in the gas is: (Avogadro constant = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, Molar volume at STP = 22.4 L mol⁻¹)

A. 6.022 × 10²¹
B. 6.022 × 10²²
C. 6.022 × 10²³
D. 1.204 × 10²³
Answer: B

Moles of gas = Volume at STP / Molar volume = 2.24 L / 22.4 L mol⁻¹ = 0.1 mol. Number of molecules = 0.1 mol × 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ = 6.022 × 10²².

22. Which of the following represents the correct relationship between molarity (M) and molality (m) for a solution? (ρ = density of solution in g mL⁻¹, M₂ = molar mass of solute in g mol⁻¹)

A. M = m × ρ / (1 + m × M₂)
B. m = M × ρ / (1 + M × M₂)
C. M = m / (1 + m × M₂ × ρ)
D. m = M / (1 + M × M₂ × ρ)
Answer: A

Molarity (M) = moles of solute / volume of solution (L). Molality (m) = moles of solute / mass of solvent (kg). The correct relationship accounting for density is M = m × ρ / (1 + m × M₂), where ρ is the density of the solution.

23. A compound contains 40% carbon, 6.67% hydrogen, and the rest is oxygen by mass. If the molecular mass of the compound is 180 g/mol, what is its molecular formula? (Atomic masses: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

A. C6H12O6
B. C5H10O5
C. C4H8O4
D. C3H6O3
Answer: A

First, calculate the empirical formula: Carbon = 40/12 = 3.33, Hydrogen = 6.67/1 = 6.67, Oxygen = 53.33/16 = 3.33. Divide by the smallest value (3.33): C = 1, H = 2, O = 1. Thus, the empirical formula is CH2O. Empirical mass = 12 + 2 + 16 = 30 g/mol. Molecular mass = 180 g/mol, so n = 180/30 = 6. Molecular formula = (CH2O)6 = C6H12O6.
