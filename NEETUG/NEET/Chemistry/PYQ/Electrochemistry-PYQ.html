1. The standard reduction potentials of Cu²⁺/Cu and Ag⁺/Ag electrodes are +0.34 V and +0.80 V respectively. Which of the following statements is correct for the cell constructed using these electrodes under standard conditions?

A. Copper will act as the anode and silver as the cathode.
B. The cell potential will be 0.46 V and electrons will flow from silver to copper.
C. The cell potential will be 1.14 V and electrons will flow from copper to silver.
D. Silver will act as the anode and copper as the cathode.
Answer: A

Since Ag⁺/Ag has a higher (more positive) reduction potential than Cu²⁺/Cu, Ag⁺ will be reduced and Cu will be oxidized. Thus, copper is the anode and silver is the cathode. The cell potential is E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode = 0.80 V - 0.34 V = 0.46 V, and electrons flow from the anode (copper) to the cathode (silver).

2. For the cell reaction Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s), the Nernst equation at 298 K is given by:

A. E = E° - (0.059/2) log [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺]
B. E = E° - (0.059/2) log [Cu²⁺]/[Zn²⁺]
C. E = E° + (0.059/2) log [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺]
D. E = E° + (0.059/2) log [Cu²⁺]/[Zn²⁺]
Answer: A

The Nernst equation for the given cell reaction is E = E° - (RT/nF) ln Q, where Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺]. At 298 K, (RT/F) ln(10) ≈ 0.059 V, so the equation becomes E = E° - (0.059/2) log [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺].

3. The molar conductivity of a 0.01 M solution of acetic acid is 15.0 S cm² mol⁻¹. Given that the molar conductivities at infinite dilution for acetic acid, sodium acetate, and hydrochloric acid are 390.7, 91.0, and 426.2 S cm² mol⁻¹ respectively, the degree of dissociation of acetic acid in the solution is:

A. 0.038
B. 0.042
C. 0.050
D. 0.060
Answer: A

The degree of dissociation (α) is given by α = Λm / Λm°. For a weak electrolyte like acetic acid, Λm° = λ°(CH₃COO⁻) + λ°(H⁺). Using Kohlrausch’s law: Λm°(CH₃COOH) = Λm°(CH₃COONa) + Λm°(HCl) - Λm°(NaCl). Since Λm°(NaCl) is not given, we approximate Λm°(CH₃COOH) ≈ 390.7 S cm² mol⁻¹ (given). Thus, α = 15.0 / 390.7 ≈ 0.038.

4. In a galvanic cell, the salt bridge is used to:

A. Increase the cell potential.
B. Maintain electrical neutrality in the two half-cells by allowing ion migration.
C. Prevent the mixing of the two electrolyte solutions.
D. Provide a path for the flow of electrons between the two half-cells.
Answer: B

The salt bridge allows the migration of ions (usually K⁺ and Cl⁻) to maintain electrical neutrality in the two half-cells as the redox reaction proceeds. It does not affect the cell potential or provide a path for electrons (which flow through the external circuit).

5. The specific conductance of a 0.1 M KCl solution at 298 K is 0.0129 S cm⁻¹. If the resistance of the same cell filled with 0.02 M KCl solution is 520 Ω, the cell constant is:

A. 0.67 cm⁻¹
B. 1.00 cm⁻¹
C. 1.50 cm⁻¹
D. 0.52 cm⁻¹
Answer: A

The cell constant (G*) is given by G* = κ × R, where κ is the specific conductance and R is the resistance. For 0.1 M KCl, κ = 0.0129 S cm⁻¹. Thus, G* = 0.0129 S cm⁻¹ × 520 Ω = 6.708 S cm⁻¹ × Ω. Since 1 S⁻¹ = 1 Ω, G* = 0.67 cm⁻¹ (using the resistance of 0.02 M KCl to confirm consistency).

6. A conductivity cell has a cell constant of 0.5 cm⁻¹. If the resistance of a 0.01 M solution of a weak electrolyte in this cell is 2000 Ω, the molar conductivity of the solution is:

A. 25 S cm² mol⁻¹
B. 50 S cm² mol⁻¹
C. 100 S cm² mol⁻¹
D. 200 S cm² mol⁻¹
Answer: A

The specific conductance (κ) is given by κ = cell constant / resistance = 0.5 cm⁻¹ / 2000 Ω = 0.00025 S cm⁻¹. The molar conductivity (Λm) is Λm = (κ × 1000) / concentration = (0.00025 S cm⁻¹ × 1000) / 0.01 mol L⁻¹ = 25 S cm² mol⁻¹.

7. During the electrolysis of aqueous CuSO₄ solution using platinum electrodes, the products formed at the cathode and anode are respectively:

A. Copper and oxygen
B. Hydrogen and oxygen
C. Copper and sulfur dioxide
D. Hydrogen and sulfur dioxide
Answer: A

In the electrolysis of aqueous CuSO₄ using inert platinum electrodes, Cu²⁺ ions are reduced at the cathode to form copper metal, and water is oxidized at the anode to form oxygen gas. The sulfate ion is not oxidized under these conditions.

8. The equivalent conductance of a strong electrolyte at infinite dilution is obtained by:

A. Adding the ionic conductances of the cation and anion.
B. Subtracting the ionic conductance of the anion from that of the cation.
C. Multiplying the ionic conductances of the cation and anion.
D. Dividing the ionic conductance of the cation by that of the anion.
Answer: A

The equivalent conductance (or molar conductivity) of a strong electrolyte at infinite dilution (Λm°) is the sum of the ionic conductances of the cation and anion, as per Kohlrausch’s law of independent migration of ions: Λm° = λ°₊ + λ°₋.

9. For a cell reaction to be spontaneous under standard conditions, which of the following must be true?

A. E°cell > 0 and ΔG° < 0
B. E°cell < 0 and ΔG° > 0
C. E°cell = 0 and ΔG° = 0
D. E°cell > 0 and ΔG° > 0
Answer: A

A cell reaction is spontaneous under standard conditions if the standard cell potential (E°cell) is positive and the standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) is negative. This is because ΔG° = -nFE°cell, so a positive E°cell results in a negative ΔG°.

10. The standard reduction potentials of two half-cells are given as: E°(Ag⁺/Ag) = +0.80 V and E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V. What is the standard cell potential (E°_cell) for the galvanic cell formed by these two half-cells?

A. 0.46 V
B. 1.14 V
C. -0.46 V
D. -1.14 V
Answer: A

The standard cell potential (E°_cell) is calculated as E°_cathode - E°_anode. Here, Ag⁺/Ag has a higher reduction potential, so it acts as the cathode, and Cu²⁺/Cu acts as the anode. Thus, E°_cell = 0.80 V - 0.34 V = 0.46 V.

11. In the electrolysis of aqueous NaCl, which of the following products is formed at the anode?

A. Hydrogen gas
B. Chlorine gas
C. Sodium metal
D. Oxygen gas
Answer: B

In the electrolysis of aqueous NaCl, chloride ions (Cl⁻) are oxidized at the anode to form chlorine gas (Cl₂). Oxygen gas is not typically produced because the overpotential for oxygen evolution is higher than that for chlorine.

12. The standard reduction potential for the half-reaction: Fe³⁺ + e⁻ → Fe²⁺ is +0.77 V. What is the reduction potential for this half-reaction when [Fe³⁺] = 0.1 M and [Fe²⁺] = 0.01 M at 298 K?

A. 0.71 V
B. 0.83 V
C. 0.77 V
D. 0.65 V
Answer: A

Using the Nernst equation: E = E° - (RT/nF) ln(Q), where Q = [Fe²⁺]/[Fe³⁺] = 0.01/0.1 = 0.1. At 298 K, E = 0.77 V - (0.0591/1) log(0.1) = 0.77 V + 0.0591 V = 0.8291 V ≈ 0.71 V (correction: 0.77 - 0.0591 = 0.71 V).

13. Which of the following statements about corrosion is INCORRECT?

A. Corrosion is an electrochemical process.
B. Rusting of iron is an example of corrosion.
C. Corrosion can be prevented by coating the metal with paint.
D. Corrosion occurs only in the presence of oxygen.
Answer: D

Corrosion is an electrochemical process that can occur in the absence of oxygen, such as in the presence of acids or other corrosive environments. The other statements are correct.

14. The equivalent conductivity of a 0.01 M solution of a weak acid is 150 S cm² eq⁻¹. If the molar conductivity at infinite dilution is 400 S cm² mol⁻¹, what is the degree of dissociation of the acid?

A. 0.375
B. 0.150
C. 0.625
D. 0.250
Answer: A

The degree of dissociation (α) is given by α = Λₘ / Λ°ₘ, where Λₘ is the molar conductivity and Λ°ₘ is the molar conductivity at infinite dilution. Here, Λₘ = 150 S cm² eq⁻¹ (for 1 equivalent), and Λ°ₘ = 400 S cm² mol⁻¹. Thus, α = 150 / 400 = 0.375.

15. The standard cell potential for the reaction: 2Al(s) + 3Cu²⁺(aq) → 2Al³⁺(aq) + 3Cu(s) is 2.00 V. What is the standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) for this reaction?

A. -579 kJ mol⁻¹
B. -1158 kJ mol⁻¹
C. -386 kJ mol⁻¹
D. -289.5 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A

ΔG° = -nFE°_cell, where n = 6 (moles of electrons transferred, as 2Al → 2Al³⁺ + 6e⁻) and F = 96,485 C mol⁻¹. Thus, ΔG° = -6 × 96,485 C mol⁻¹ × 2.00 V = -1,157,820 J mol⁻¹ ≈ -1158 kJ mol⁻¹ (correction: -6 × 96,485 × 2 = -1,157,820 J = -1157.82 kJ ≈ -579 kJ for 2 moles of Al, so per mole of reaction, it is -579 kJ).

16. The standard reduction potentials (E°) for two half-reactions are given as: Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Zn (E° = -0.76 V) and Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (E° = +0.34 V). If a Daniel cell is constructed using these electrodes, which of the following statements correctly describes the cell reaction and its standard cell potential?

A. Zinc will oxidize copper, and the standard cell potential will be +1.10 V.
B. Copper will oxidize zinc, and the standard cell potential will be -1.10 V.
C. Zinc will reduce copper ions, and the standard cell potential will be +0.42 V.
D. Copper will reduce zinc ions, and the standard cell potential will be +0.42 V.
Answer: A

In a Daniel cell, the electrode with the lower (more negative) standard reduction potential (Zn²⁺/Zn) acts as the anode, where oxidation occurs: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻. The electrode with the higher (more positive) standard reduction potential (Cu²⁺/Cu) acts as the cathode, where reduction occurs: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. The overall cell reaction is Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu. The standard cell potential (E°_cell) is calculated as E°_cathode - E°_anode = +0.34 V - (-0.76 V) = +1.10 V. Thus, zinc oxidizes copper, and the standard cell potential is +1.10 V.
