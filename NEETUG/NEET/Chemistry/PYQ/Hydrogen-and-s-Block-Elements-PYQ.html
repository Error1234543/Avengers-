This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Hydrogen and s-Block Elements, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. The temporary hardness of water is due to the presence of:

A. Chlorides of calcium and magnesium
B. Sulphates of calcium and magnesium
C. Bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium
D. Nitrates of calcium and magnesium
Answer: C

Temporary hardness of water is caused by the presence of bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium (e.g., Ca(HCO₃)₂ and Mg(HCO₃)₂). These can be removed by boiling, which decomposes the bicarbonates into insoluble carbonates. Permanent hardness is caused by chlorides, sulphates, and nitrates of calcium and magnesium.

2. Which of the following alkali metals has the highest hydration enthalpy?

A. Lithium
B. Sodium
C. Potassium
D. Rubidium
Answer: A

Lithium has the highest hydration enthalpy among the alkali metals due to its small size and high charge density. This allows it to attract water molecules more strongly, releasing more energy during hydration. The hydration enthalpy decreases down the group as the size of the alkali metal ion increases.

3. The correct order of basicity of the following oxides is:

A. Na₂O < MgO < Al₂O₃ < SiO₂
B. SiO₂ < Al₂O₃ < MgO < Na₂O
C. Al₂O₃ < SiO₂ < MgO < Na₂O
D. MgO < Na₂O < Al₂O₃ < SiO₂
Answer: B

The basicity of oxides decreases across a period from left to right in the periodic table. Na₂O (alkali metal oxide) is the most basic, followed by MgO (alkaline earth metal oxide), Al₂O₃ (amphoteric), and SiO₂ (acidic). Thus, the correct order is SiO₂ < Al₂O₃ < MgO < Na₂O.

4. Which of the following statements about the diagonal relationship between lithium and magnesium is incorrect?

A. Both lithium and magnesium form nitrides when heated in nitrogen.
B. The carbonates of lithium and magnesium decompose on heating to give their oxides and CO₂.
C. Both lithium and magnesium react with oxygen to form peroxides.
D. The hydroxides of lithium and magnesium are weak bases.
Answer: C

Lithium and magnesium do not form peroxides when reacting with oxygen. Lithium forms lithium oxide (Li₂O) and lithium peroxide (Li₂O₂) only under specific conditions, while magnesium forms magnesium oxide (MgO). The other statements are correct: both form nitrides (Li₃N, Mg₃N₂), their carbonates decompose on heating, and their hydroxides are weak bases.

5. The correct order of solubility of the sulphates of alkaline earth metals in water is:

A. BeSO₄ > MgSO₄ > CaSO₄ > SrSO₄ > BaSO₄
B. BaSO₄ > SrSO₄ > CaSO₄ > MgSO₄ > BeSO₄
C. MgSO₄ > CaSO₄ > SrSO₄ > BaSO₄ > BeSO₄
D. BeSO₄ > BaSO₄ > SrSO₄ > CaSO₄ > MgSO₄
Answer: A

The solubility of sulphates of alkaline earth metals decreases down the group. Beryllium sulphate (BeSO₄) is highly soluble, while barium sulphate (BaSO₄) is nearly insoluble. The correct order is BeSO₄ > MgSO₄ > CaSO₄ > SrSO₄ > BaSO₄.

6. Which of the following is NOT a use of sodium hydroxide?

A. Manufacture of soap
B. Paper industry
C. Production of high-purity metals
D. Food preservation
Answer: D

Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) is not used for food preservation. It is used in the manufacture of soap, the paper industry (pulping and bleaching), and the production of high-purity metals (e.g., aluminum via the Bayer process). Food preservation typically involves substances like sodium benzoate or citric acid.

7. The reaction of sodium with water is highly exothermic. Which of the following observations is incorrect?

A. Sodium floats on water due to its low density.
B. The reaction produces hydrogen gas, which may catch fire.
C. The solution formed is acidic due to the formation of sodium hydroxide.
D. The reaction is vigorous and may cause spattering.
Answer: C

The solution formed when sodium reacts with water is alkaline, not acidic, due to the formation of sodium hydroxide (NaOH). The other observations are correct: sodium floats on water, hydrogen gas is produced (which may ignite), and the reaction is vigorous with potential spattering.

8. Which of the following compounds is used as a drying agent in the laboratory?

A. Calcium chloride
B. Sodium chloride
C. Potassium chloride
D. Magnesium sulphate heptahydrate
Answer: A

Calcium chloride (CaCl₂) is commonly used as a drying agent in laboratories due to its hygroscopic nature. It absorbs moisture from the air and forms hydrates. Sodium chloride and potassium chloride are not typically used as drying agents, and magnesium sulphate heptahydrate (MgSO₄·7H₂O) is a hydrated compound, not a drying agent.

9. The correct order of thermal stability of the carbonates of alkaline earth metals is:

A. BeCO₃ < MgCO₃ < CaCO₃ < SrCO₃ < BaCO₃
B. BaCO₃ < SrCO₃ < CaCO₃ < MgCO₃ < BeCO₃
C. MgCO₃ < CaCO₃ < SrCO₃ < BaCO₃ < BeCO₃
D. BeCO₃ > MgCO₃ > CaCO₃ > SrCO₃ > BaCO₃
Answer: A

The thermal stability of carbonates of alkaline earth metals increases down the group. Beryllium carbonate (BeCO₃) is the least stable and decomposes easily, while barium carbonate (BaCO₃) is the most stable. The correct order is BeCO₃ < MgCO₃ < CaCO₃ < SrCO₃ < BaCO₃.

10. Which of the following statements about the preparation of hydrogen in the laboratory is correct?

A. Hydrogen is prepared by the reaction of zinc with concentrated sulphuric acid.
B. The reaction of calcium with water is used to prepare hydrogen in the laboratory.
C. Hydrogen is collected by the downward displacement of water.
D. The reaction of sodium with water is commonly used for laboratory preparation of hydrogen.
Answer: C

In the laboratory, hydrogen is typically prepared by the reaction of zinc with dilute sulphuric acid (not concentrated) and collected by the downward displacement of water. Calcium and sodium react too vigorously with water for safe laboratory use. The reaction of zinc with dilute sulphuric acid is the standard method.

11. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic property of alkali metals?

A. They have low ionization enthalpies.
B. They exhibit +1 oxidation state in their compounds.
C. They form peroxides and superoxides when reacted with excess oxygen.
D. They are stored under water to prevent reaction with air.
Answer: D

Alkali metals are not stored under water because they react violently with water. They are typically stored under kerosene or mineral oil to prevent reaction with air and moisture. The other statements are correct: alkali metals have low ionization enthalpies, exhibit a +1 oxidation state, and can form peroxides (e.g., Na₂O₂) and superoxides (e.g., KO₂) with excess oxygen.

12. The correct order of ionic mobility of the alkali metal ions in aqueous solution is:

A. Li⁺ > Na⁺ > K⁺ > Rb⁺ > Cs⁺
B. Cs⁺ > Rb⁺ > K⁺ > Na⁺ > Li⁺
C. Na⁺ > Li⁺ > K⁺ > Rb⁺ > Cs⁺
D. Li⁺ > Cs⁺ > Rb⁺ > K⁺ > Na⁺
Answer: B

The ionic mobility of alkali metal ions in aqueous solution increases down the group due to decreasing hydration enthalpy. Larger ions (e.g., Cs⁺) have lower charge density and are less hydrated, allowing them to move faster in solution. The correct order is Cs⁺ > Rb⁺ > K⁺ > Na⁺ > Li⁺.

13. The correct order of increasing ionic character in the hydrides of alkali metals is:

A. LiH < NaH < KH < RbH < CsH
B. CsH < RbH < KH < NaH < LiH
C. LiH < KH < NaH < RbH < CsH
D. RbH < CsH < KH < NaH < LiH
Answer: A

The ionic character of alkali metal hydrides increases down the group due to the decreasing ionization enthalpy of the alkali metals. Thus, the correct order is LiH < NaH < KH < RbH < CsH.

14. Which of the following reactions is NOT a method for the preparation of hydrogen in the laboratory?

A. Zn + dilute H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂
B. 2Al + 2NaOH + 6H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] + 3H₂
C. 2H₂O + 2Na → 2NaOH + H₂
D. CaH₂ + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + 2H₂
Answer: C

The reaction of sodium with water (2H₂O + 2Na → 2NaOH + H₂) is highly vigorous and not typically used as a laboratory method for preparing hydrogen due to safety concerns. The other reactions are standard laboratory methods.

15. The solubility of alkaline earth metal hydroxides in water increases down the group. Which of the following is the correct reason for this trend?

A. Increase in lattice enthalpy dominates over hydration enthalpy.
B. Decrease in lattice enthalpy dominates over hydration enthalpy.
C. Both lattice enthalpy and hydration enthalpy decrease, but the decrease in lattice enthalpy is more significant.
D. Both lattice enthalpy and hydration enthalpy increase, but the increase in hydration enthalpy is more significant.
Answer: C

The solubility of alkaline earth metal hydroxides increases down the group because the decrease in lattice enthalpy (which favors solubility) is more significant than the decrease in hydration enthalpy (which opposes solubility).

16. Which of the following properties of beryllium is NOT shared by the other members of Group 2?

A. Formation of covalent compounds.
B. Amphoteric nature of its oxide.
C. High ionization enthalpy.
D. Formation of peroxides when burned in oxygen.
Answer: D

Beryllium does not form peroxides when burned in oxygen; it forms beryllium oxide (BeO). The other properties—formation of covalent compounds, amphoteric oxide, and high ionization enthalpy—are unique to beryllium among Group 2 elements.

17. Which of the following statements about the reaction of alkali metals with liquid ammonia is correct?

A. The solution is colorless and diamagnetic.
B. The solution is blue and paramagnetic due to solvated electrons.
C. The solution is red and conducts electricity poorly.
D. The solution forms metal hydrides immediately.
Answer: B

Alkali metals dissolve in liquid ammonia to form a blue-colored solution due to the presence of solvated electrons, which makes the solution paramagnetic. The solution also conducts electricity well.

18. The correct order of basic strength of the hydroxides of alkaline earth metals is:

A. Be(OH)₂ < Mg(OH)₂ < Ca(OH)₂ < Sr(OH)₂ < Ba(OH)₂
B. Ba(OH)₂ < Sr(OH)₂ < Ca(OH)₂ < Mg(OH)₂ < Be(OH)₂
C. Be(OH)₂ < Ca(OH)₂ < Mg(OH)₂ < Sr(OH)₂ < Ba(OH)₂
D. Mg(OH)₂ < Be(OH)₂ < Ca(OH)₂ < Sr(OH)₂ < Ba(OH)₂
Answer: A

The basic strength of the hydroxides of alkaline earth metals increases down the group due to the increasing ionic character and decreasing lattice enthalpy. Thus, the correct order is Be(OH)₂ < Mg(OH)₂ < Ca(OH)₂ < Sr(OH)₂ < Ba(OH)₂.

19. The correct order of decreasing ionic radius for the following species is: Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Rb⁺, Cs⁺, Be²⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺.

A. Cs⁺ > Rb⁺ > K⁺ > Na⁺ > Li⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Be²⁺
B. Cs⁺ > Rb⁺ > K⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Li⁺ > Be²⁺
C. Be²⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Li⁺ > Na⁺ > K⁺ > Rb⁺ > Cs⁺
D. Cs⁺ > K⁺ > Rb⁺ > Na⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Li⁺ > Be²⁺
Answer: B

The ionic radius increases down the group and decreases with increasing nuclear charge for isoelectronic species. Thus, the correct order is Cs⁺ > Rb⁺ > K⁺ > Ca²⁺ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Li⁺ > Be²⁺.

20. Which of the following statements about the reaction of calcium with water is correct?

A. Calcium reacts vigorously with cold water to produce calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.
B. Calcium does not react with water at room temperature.
C. Calcium reacts slowly with cold water to produce calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.
D. Calcium reacts with water to form calcium oxide and hydrogen gas.
Answer: C

Calcium reacts slowly with cold water to produce calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas. The reaction is less vigorous than that of alkali metals like sodium or potassium.

21. The correct order of increasing reducing power of the alkali metals in aqueous solution is:

A. Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs
B. Cs < Rb < K < Na < Li
C. Li < K < Na < Rb < Cs
D. Na < Li < K < Rb < Cs
Answer: B

The reducing power of alkali metals in aqueous solution decreases down the group due to the increasing size and decreasing hydration enthalpy. Thus, the correct order is Cs < Rb < K < Na < Li.

22. A sample of hydrogen gas is collected over water at 25°C and 745 mm Hg pressure. If the vapor pressure of water at 25°C is 24 mm Hg, what is the partial pressure of dry hydrogen gas in the sample?

A. 721 mm Hg
B. 745 mm Hg
C. 769 mm Hg
D. 729 mm Hg
Answer: A

The total pressure of the gas mixture (hydrogen + water vapor) is 745 mm Hg. The vapor pressure of water at 25°C is 24 mm Hg. The partial pressure of dry hydrogen gas is calculated by subtracting the vapor pressure of water from the total pressure: 745 mm Hg - 24 mm Hg = 721 mm Hg. Thus, the correct answer is 721 mm Hg.

23. Which of the following statements correctly explains the anomalous behavior of lithium compared to other alkali metals?

A. Lithium has the smallest atomic size and highest ionization enthalpy among alkali metals, leading to the formation of covalent compounds rather than ionic ones.
B. Lithium exhibits a +2 oxidation state due to the presence of two valence electrons, unlike other alkali metals.
C. Lithium hydroxide is a strong base, similar to other alkali metal hydroxides, and does not decompose upon heating.
D. Lithium carbonate is highly soluble in water, whereas carbonates of other alkali metals are insoluble.
Answer: A

Lithium exhibits anomalous behavior due to its small atomic and ionic size, high ionization enthalpy, and high polarization power. This results in the formation of compounds with significant covalent character (e.g., LiCl is soluble in organic solvents). Unlike other alkali metals, lithium forms nitrides (Li₃N) and reacts directly with carbon to form carbides. Its hydroxide (LiOH) is a weaker base compared to other alkali metal hydroxides and decomposes upon heating to form Li₂O and H₂O. Additionally, lithium carbonate (Li₂CO₃) is less soluble in water compared to other alkali metal carbonates.
