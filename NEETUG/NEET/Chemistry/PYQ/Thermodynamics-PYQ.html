This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Thermodynamics, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. For a spontaneous process at constant temperature and pressure, which of the following is always true?

A. ΔG < 0
B. ΔH < 0
C. ΔS > 0
D. ΔU = 0
Answer: A

For a process to be spontaneous at constant temperature and pressure, the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) must be negative (ΔG < 0). While ΔH < 0 or ΔS > 0 may contribute to spontaneity, they are not universally true for all spontaneous processes. ΔU = 0 applies only to isolated systems or specific conditions, not all spontaneous processes.

2. The enthalpy of combustion of methane (CH₄) is -890 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the enthalpy change when 16 g of methane is completely burned?

A. -445 kJ
B. -890 kJ
C. -222.5 kJ
D. -1780 kJ
Answer: B

The molar mass of methane (CH₄) is 16 g mol⁻¹. The enthalpy of combustion is given for 1 mole (16 g) of methane, so burning 16 g releases -890 kJ. The correct answer is -890 kJ.

3. Which of the following statements about the first law of thermodynamics is correct?

A. Energy can be created but not destroyed.
B. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.
C. Heat is always converted completely into work.
D. The internal energy of a system decreases in an adiabatic process.
Answer: B

The first law of thermodynamics states that the total energy of an isolated system remains constant (energy is conserved). Energy cannot be created or destroyed, and heat is not always completely converted into work. In an adiabatic process, the internal energy may increase or decrease depending on the work done.

4. For an ideal gas undergoing an isothermal expansion, which of the following is true?

A. ΔU = 0 and q = -w
B. ΔU > 0 and q = 0
C. ΔU < 0 and w = 0
D. ΔU = 0 and q = 0
Answer: A

In an isothermal process for an ideal gas, the temperature remains constant, so the internal energy change (ΔU) is zero. According to the first law of thermodynamics, ΔU = q + w. Since ΔU = 0, it follows that q = -w.

5. The standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f) of water is -286 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the enthalpy change for the reaction: 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)?

A. -286 kJ
B. -572 kJ
C. +572 kJ
D. +286 kJ
Answer: B

The standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH°f) of water is given for the formation of 1 mole of H₂O. For the reaction producing 2 moles of H₂O, the enthalpy change is twice the ΔH°f value: 2 × (-286 kJ) = -572 kJ.

6. Which of the following processes is accompanied by a decrease in entropy?

A. Melting of ice
B. Dissolution of sugar in water
C. Condensation of water vapor
D. Expansion of a gas into vacuum
Answer: C

Entropy (S) is a measure of disorder. Condensation of water vapor (gas → liquid) results in a decrease in entropy because the liquid state is more ordered than the gaseous state. The other processes (melting, dissolution, expansion) increase entropy.

7. For a reaction, if ΔH = -50 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = -100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, at what temperature will the reaction be at equilibrium (ΔG = 0)?

A. 500 K
B. 250 K
C. 50 K
D. 1000 K
Answer: A

At equilibrium, ΔG = 0. Using the equation ΔG = ΔH - TΔS, we set ΔG = 0: 0 = -50,000 J mol⁻¹ - T(-100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹). Solving for T: T = 50,000 / 100 = 500 K.

8. The work done in a reversible isothermal expansion of an ideal gas is given by:

A. w = -nRT ln(V₂/V₁)
B. w = -PΔV
C. w = -nCvΔT
D. w = -nCpΔT
Answer: A

For a reversible isothermal expansion of an ideal gas, the work done is given by w = -nRT ln(V₂/V₁), where n is the number of moles, R is the gas constant, T is the temperature, and V₂/V₁ is the ratio of final to initial volume. The other options apply to different processes (e.g., isobaric or adiabatic).

9. For a reaction, if ΔG° = -RT ln K, what does a positive value of ΔG° indicate about the equilibrium constant (K)?

A. K > 1
B. K < 1
C. K = 1
D. K = 0
Answer: B

The equation ΔG° = -RT ln K relates the standard Gibbs free energy change to the equilibrium constant. If ΔG° is positive, ln K is negative, which means K < 1. This indicates that the reaction favors reactants at equilibrium.

10. In an adiabatic process, which of the following is true?

A. q = 0 and ΔU = w
B. q = w and ΔU = 0
C. q = ΔU and w = 0
D. ΔU = 0 and w = 0
Answer: A

In an adiabatic process, no heat is exchanged with the surroundings (q = 0). According to the first law of thermodynamics, ΔU = q + w, so ΔU = w.

11. The standard enthalpy of formation of CO₂(g) is -393.5 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the enthalpy change for the combustion of 1 mole of carbon (graphite) to CO₂(g)?

A. -393.5 kJ
B. +393.5 kJ
C. -787 kJ
D. +787 kJ
Answer: A

The standard enthalpy of formation of CO₂(g) is defined as the enthalpy change for the formation of 1 mole of CO₂ from its constituent elements in their standard states. For carbon (graphite) combusting to CO₂, the enthalpy change is equal to the standard enthalpy of formation of CO₂, which is -393.5 kJ mol⁻¹.

12. The enthalpy of combustion of methane (CH₄) is -890 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the heat released when 4 g of methane is completely burned in excess oxygen?

A. -222.5 kJ
B. -445 kJ
C. -890 kJ
D. -1780 kJ
Answer: A

The molar mass of methane (CH₄) is 16 g mol⁻¹. For 4 g of methane, the moles burned = 4 g / 16 g mol⁻¹ = 0.25 mol. Heat released = 0.25 mol × (-890 kJ mol⁻¹) = -222.5 kJ.

13. For an ideal gas, the relationship between molar heat capacities at constant pressure (Cₚ) and constant volume (Cᵥ) is given by:

A. Cₚ - Cᵥ = R
B. Cₚ + Cᵥ = R
C. Cₚ / Cᵥ = R
D. Cₚ × Cᵥ = R
Answer: A

For an ideal gas, the difference between molar heat capacities at constant pressure and constant volume is equal to the universal gas constant (R), i.e., Cₚ - Cᵥ = R. This is derived from the first law of thermodynamics and the definition of enthalpy.

14. A reaction has ΔH = -50 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = -100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. At what temperature will the reaction become non-spontaneous?

A. 500 K
B. 250 K
C. 300 K
D. 400 K
Answer: A

The reaction becomes non-spontaneous when ΔG = ΔH - TΔS ≥ 0. Substituting the values: -50,000 J mol⁻¹ - T(-100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹) ≥ 0 → -50,000 + 100T ≥ 0 → T ≥ 500 K. Thus, the reaction is non-spontaneous above 500 K.

15. The standard enthalpy of formation (ΔH_f°) of water is -286 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the enthalpy change for the formation of 1.5 moles of water?

A. -429 kJ
B. -143 kJ
C. -286 kJ
D. -572 kJ
Answer: A

The enthalpy change for the formation of 1.5 moles of water = 1.5 mol × (-286 kJ mol⁻¹) = -429 kJ.

16. For a cyclic process, which of the following is always zero?

A. ΔU
B. ΔH
C. ΔS
D. q (heat)
Answer: A

In a cyclic process, the system returns to its initial state, so the change in internal energy (ΔU) is zero. ΔH, ΔS, and q are not necessarily zero for a cyclic process.

17. The work done in the isothermal reversible expansion of an ideal gas from volume V₁ to V₂ is given by:

A. w = -nRT ln(V₂/V₁)
B. w = -nRT (V₂ - V₁)
C. w = -P(V₂ - V₁)
D. w = -nCᵥΔT
Answer: A

For an isothermal reversible expansion of an ideal gas, the work done is given by w = -nRT ln(V₂/V₁), where n is the number of moles, R is the gas constant, T is the temperature, and V₂/V₁ is the ratio of final to initial volume.

18. Which of the following is true for an adiabatic process?

A. q = 0
B. ΔU = 0
C. ΔH = 0
D. w = 0
Answer: A

In an adiabatic process, no heat is exchanged with the surroundings (q = 0). ΔU, ΔH, and w are not necessarily zero; for example, work can be done on or by the system, changing its internal energy.

19. The standard Gibbs free energy change (ΔG°) for a reaction is -200 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the equilibrium constant (K) for the reaction at 298 K?

A. K ≈ 10³⁵
B. K ≈ 10¹⁰
C. K ≈ 10⁵
D. K ≈ 1
Answer: A

The relationship between ΔG° and the equilibrium constant (K) is given by ΔG° = -RT ln K. Substituting the values: -200,000 J mol⁻¹ = -(8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)(298 K) ln K → ln K = 200,000 / (8.314 × 298) ≈ 80.7 → K ≈ e⁸⁰.⁷ ≈ 10³⁵.
