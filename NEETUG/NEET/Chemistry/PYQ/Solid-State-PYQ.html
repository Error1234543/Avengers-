1. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the coordination number in a crystal lattice?

A. In a simple cubic lattice, the coordination number is 8.
B. In a body-centered cubic (bcc) lattice, the coordination number is 12.
C. In a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice, the coordination number is 12.
D. In a hexagonal close-packed (hcp) lattice, the coordination number is 6.
Answer: C

The coordination number refers to the number of nearest neighbor atoms surrounding a central atom in a crystal lattice. In a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice, each atom is surrounded by 12 nearest neighbors, giving it a coordination number of 12. The other options are incorrect: simple cubic has a coordination number of 6, bcc has 8, and hcp also has 12 (not 6).

2. A compound crystallizes in a cubic lattice with edge length 400 pm. If the density of the compound is 8.0 g/cm³ and it contains 4 formula units per unit cell, what is the molar mass of the compound? (Use N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

A. 48 g/mol
B. 96 g/mol
C. 192 g/mol
D. 24 g/mol
Answer: C

The density (ρ) of a cubic crystal is given by the formula: ρ = (Z × M) / (a³ × N_A), where Z is the number of formula units per unit cell, M is the molar mass, a is the edge length, and N_A is Avogadro's number. Rearranging for M: M = (ρ × a³ × N_A) / Z. Substituting the given values: ρ = 8.0 g/cm³, a = 400 pm = 4 × 10⁻⁸ cm, Z = 4, N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. M = (8.0 × (4 × 10⁻⁸)³ × 6.022 × 10²³) / 4 = 192 g/mol.

3. Which type of defect is responsible for the electrical conductivity in pure silicon at room temperature?

A. Frenkel defect
B. Schottky defect
C. Interstitial defect
D. Electronic defect (intrinsic semiconductor)
Answer: D

Pure silicon is an intrinsic semiconductor, and its electrical conductivity at room temperature arises due to the thermal excitation of electrons from the valence band to the conduction band, creating electron-hole pairs. This is an electronic defect, not a point defect like Frenkel or Schottky defects. Interstitial defects do not contribute to conductivity in this context.

4. In a crystalline solid, the arrangement of particles is such that the voids are minimized. Which of the following packing efficiencies corresponds to a hexagonal close-packed (hcp) structure?

A. 52.4%
B. 68%
C. 74%
D. 78%
Answer: C

Hexagonal close-packed (hcp) and face-centered cubic (fcc) structures both have the highest packing efficiency of 74%. This is because they are close-packed structures where the spheres occupy the maximum possible space, leaving minimal voids. The other options correspond to less efficient packing arrangements.

5. A metal crystallizes in a body-centered cubic (bcc) lattice. If the radius of the metal atom is 125 pm, what is the edge length of the unit cell?

A. 250 pm
B. 289 pm
C. 306 pm
D. 354 pm
Answer: B

In a body-centered cubic (bcc) lattice, the relationship between the atomic radius (r) and the edge length (a) of the unit cell is given by: a = (4r) / √3. Substituting r = 125 pm: a = (4 × 125) / √3 = 500 / 1.732 ≈ 289 pm.

6. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of ionic solids?

A. High melting and boiling points
B. Good electrical conductivity in the solid state
C. Solubility in polar solvents like water
D. Hard and brittle nature
Answer: B

Ionic solids are characterized by high melting and boiling points, solubility in polar solvents, and a hard and brittle nature due to strong electrostatic forces between ions. However, they do NOT conduct electricity in the solid state because the ions are fixed in their lattice positions. Electrical conductivity is only possible when the solid is melted or dissolved in water, allowing the ions to move freely.

7. The number of tetrahedral voids present in a hexagonal close-packed (hcp) structure containing N atoms is:

A. N
B. 2N
C. N/2
D. 4N
Answer: B

In a close-packed structure (either hcp or fcc), the number of tetrahedral voids is always twice the number of atoms (N) present in the structure. This is because each atom in the lattice contributes to the formation of two tetrahedral voids. Thus, the correct answer is 2N.

8. Which of the following statements about Frenkel defects is correct?

A. It involves the migration of an ion from its lattice site to an interstitial site, creating a vacancy.
B. It results in a decrease in the density of the crystal.
C. It is commonly observed in highly ionic compounds like NaCl.
D. It involves the complete removal of ions from the crystal lattice.
Answer: A

A Frenkel defect is a type of point defect where an ion (usually a cation) migrates from its original lattice site to an interstitial position within the crystal, creating a vacancy at the original site and an interstitial defect. This defect does not change the density of the crystal because no ions are lost. It is commonly observed in compounds with large differences in ionic radii, such as AgCl, not in highly ionic compounds like NaCl.

9. A crystal of NaCl has a density of 2.165 g/cm³. If the edge length of the unit cell is 564 pm, how many formula units of NaCl are present in one unit cell? (Molar mass of NaCl = 58.5 g/mol, N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8
Answer: B

The density (ρ) of a crystal is given by: ρ = (Z × M) / (a³ × N_A), where Z is the number of formula units per unit cell, M is the molar mass, a is the edge length, and N_A is Avogadro's number. Rearranging for Z: Z = (ρ × a³ × N_A) / M. Substituting the given values: ρ = 2.165 g/cm³, a = 564 pm = 5.64 × 10⁻⁸ cm, M = 58.5 g/mol, N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Z = (2.165 × (5.64 × 10⁻⁸)³ × 6.022 × 10²³) / 58.5 ≈ 4.

10. Which of the following is an example of a covalent solid?

A. Sodium chloride (NaCl)
B. Diamond (C)
C. Iron (Fe)
D. Calcium fluoride (CaF₂)
Answer: B

Covalent solids are composed of atoms held together by covalent bonds in a continuous network. Diamond is a classic example of a covalent solid, where each carbon atom is covalently bonded to four other carbon atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement. The other options are ionic solids (NaCl, CaF₂) or a metallic solid (Fe).

11. In a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice, what fraction of the total volume of the unit cell is occupied by the atoms?

A. 0.52
B. 0.68
C. 0.74
D. 0.80
Answer: C

The packing efficiency of a face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice is 74%, meaning that 74% of the total volume of the unit cell is occupied by the atoms. This is the highest packing efficiency for a close-packed structure, along with hexagonal close-packed (hcp). The fraction of the volume occupied is thus 0.74.

12. Which of the following defects can be observed in a crystal of zinc oxide (ZnO) at high temperatures?

A. Metal excess defect due to anion vacancies
B. Metal deficiency defect due to cation vacancies
C. Frenkel defect involving Zn²⁺ ions
D. Schottky defect involving both Zn²⁺ and O²⁻ ions
Answer: A

Zinc oxide (ZnO) exhibits a metal excess defect at high temperatures due to the loss of oxygen atoms, leaving behind anion vacancies. The excess zinc ions occupy interstitial sites, and the electrons released during this process are trapped in the anion vacancies, forming F-centers. This defect is responsible for the non-stoichiometric nature of ZnO at high temperatures.

13. The Bragg equation for diffraction of X-rays by a crystal is given by: nλ = 2d sinθ. If the wavelength of the X-rays is 1.54 Å and the angle of diffraction (θ) is 15°, what is the interplanar spacing (d) for n = 1?

A. 1.54 Å
B. 2.98 Å
C. 3.08 Å
D. 4.52 Å
Answer: B

Using the Bragg equation: nλ = 2d sinθ. For n = 1, λ = 1.54 Å, and θ = 15°, we have: 1 × 1.54 = 2d sin(15°). Solving for d: d = 1.54 / (2 × sin(15°)) = 1.54 / (2 × 0.2588) ≈ 2.98 Å.

14. In a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice, the number of atoms present in the unit cell is:

A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8
Answer: B

In a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice, atoms are located at all the corners and the centers of all the faces of the cube. Each corner atom is shared by 8 unit cells, contributing 1/8th of an atom per corner, while each face-centered atom is shared by 2 unit cells, contributing 1/2 of an atom per face. Thus, the total number of atoms per unit cell is (8 corners × 1/8) + (6 faces × 1/2) = 1 + 3 = 4 atoms.

15. Which of the following defects in crystals does NOT alter the density of the solid?

A. Frenkel defect
B. Schottky defect
C. Interstitial defect
D. Vacancy defect
Answer: A

A Frenkel defect is a combination of a vacancy defect and an interstitial defect, where an ion moves from its lattice site to an interstitial position. Since the number of ions remains the same, the density of the crystal does not change. In contrast, Schottky defects (vacancy pairs) and vacancy/interstitial defects alter the density by changing the total mass or volume of the crystal.

16. The coordination number of a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure is:

A. 4
B. 6
C. 8
D. 12
Answer: C

In a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, each atom at the center of the cube is in contact with 8 corner atoms. Thus, the coordination number, which is the number of nearest neighbors, is 8.

17. Which of the following statements about amorphous solids is INCORRECT?

A. They have a short-range order.
B. They do not have a sharp melting point.
C. They exhibit anisotropy.
D. They are isotropic in nature.
Answer: C

Amorphous solids lack long-range order and are isotropic, meaning their properties are the same in all directions. They do not have a sharp melting point and only exhibit short-range order. The incorrect statement is that they exhibit anisotropy, as anisotropy is a property of crystalline solids.

18. The packing efficiency of a simple cubic (SC) unit cell is approximately:

A. 52%
B. 68%
C. 74%
D. 90%
Answer: A

In a simple cubic (SC) unit cell, atoms are located only at the corners of the cube. The packing efficiency is calculated as the volume occupied by the atoms divided by the total volume of the unit cell. For SC, it is approximately 52%, as the atoms touch along the edges and leave significant empty space.

19. Which type of solid is characterized by high electrical conductivity in the molten state but not in the solid state?

A. Ionic solid
B. Metallic solid
C. Covalent network solid
D. Molecular solid
Answer: A

Ionic solids consist of ions held together by strong electrostatic forces. In the solid state, the ions are fixed in position and do not conduct electricity. However, in the molten state, the ions become mobile and can conduct electricity. Metallic solids conduct in both states, while covalent network and molecular solids do not conduct in either state.

20. The radius ratio for an octahedral void in a close-packed structure is:

A. 0.155 - 0.225
B. 0.225 - 0.414
C. 0.414 - 0.732
D. 0.732 - 1.000
Answer: C

The radius ratio (r₊/r₋) for an octahedral void in a close-packed structure is between 0.414 and 0.732. This range ensures that the smaller ion fits perfectly into the octahedral void without distorting the lattice.

21. Which defect is responsible for the pink color of LiCl crystals when heated in lithium vapor?

A. Frenkel defect
B. Schottky defect
C. Metal excess defect due to anionic vacancies
D. Metal excess defect due to interstitial cations
Answer: C

When LiCl crystals are heated in lithium vapor, lithium atoms deposit on the surface and lose electrons to form Li⁺ ions. The released electrons occupy anionic vacancies (F-centers), which absorb visible light and impart a pink color to the crystal. This is an example of a metal excess defect due to anionic vacancies.

22. The edge length of a unit cell of a metal with atomic radius 'r' in a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure is:

A. 2r
B. 4r/√3
C. 4r/√2
D. 2√2 r
Answer: B

In a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, the atoms touch along the body diagonal of the cube. The body diagonal is equal to 4 times the atomic radius (4r). The relationship between the edge length (a) and the body diagonal is √3a = 4r, so a = 4r/√3.

23. Which of the following statements about Bragg's Law is correct?

A. It relates the wavelength of X-rays to the angle of diffraction and the interplanar spacing in a crystal.
B. It states that the intensity of diffracted X-rays is proportional to the number of atoms in the unit cell.
C. It is used to determine the coordination number of a crystal lattice.
D. It applies only to amorphous solids.
Answer: A

Bragg's Law states that nλ = 2d sinθ, where n is an integer, λ is the wavelength of X-rays, d is the interplanar spacing, and θ is the angle of diffraction. It relates the wavelength of X-rays to the angle of diffraction and the interplanar spacing in a crystalline solid, enabling the determination of crystal structures.
