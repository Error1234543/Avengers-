This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Hydrocarbons, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following hydrocarbons will decolorize bromine water most rapidly?

A. Ethane
B. Ethene
C. Ethyne
D. Benzene
Answer: C

Ethyne (acetylene) is an alkyne with a triple bond, which is more reactive than the double bond in ethene or the single bonds in ethane. Benzene, though unsaturated, is stabilized by resonance and does not readily decolorize bromine water. Thus, ethyne reacts most rapidly with bromine water.

2. The major product formed when 2-butene is treated with HBr in the presence of peroxides is:

A. 1-Bromobutane
B. 2-Bromobutane
C. 1,2-Dibromobutane
D. 2-Bromo-1-butene
Answer: A

In the presence of peroxides, HBr adds to alkenes via an anti-Markovnikov mechanism. For 2-butene, the bromine atom attaches to the less substituted carbon, yielding 1-bromobutane as the major product.

3. The reaction of propene with HCl in the absence of peroxides follows:

A. Markovnikov's rule
B. Anti-Markovnikov's rule
C. Free radical mechanism
D. Nucleophilic addition
Answer: A

In the absence of peroxides, the addition of HCl to propene follows Markovnikov's rule, where the hydrogen atom attaches to the carbon with the greater number of hydrogen atoms, and the chlorine attaches to the more substituted carbon.

4. The compound formed by the catalytic hydrogenation of 2-butyne is:

A. Butane
B. 1-Butene
C. cis-2-Butene
D. trans-2-Butene
Answer: A

Catalytic hydrogenation of alkynes using a metal catalyst (e.g., Pd, Pt, or Ni) adds hydrogen across the triple bond to form an alkane. Thus, 2-butyne is fully hydrogenated to butane.

5. Which of the following is the correct order of stability of carbocations?

A. Tertiary > Secondary > Primary
B. Primary > Secondary > Tertiary
C. Secondary > Tertiary > Primary
D. Primary > Tertiary > Secondary
Answer: A

Carbocation stability follows the order: tertiary > secondary > primary. This is due to the electron-donating inductive effect and hyperconjugation, which stabilize the positive charge on the more substituted carbon.

6. The product obtained when benzene is treated with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light is:

A. Chlorobenzene
B. Hexachlorobenzene
C. Benzene hexachloride (BHC)
D. 1,2-Dichlorobenzene
Answer: C

In the presence of ultraviolet light, benzene undergoes an addition reaction with chlorine to form benzene hexachloride (BHC), also known as 1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane. This is a free radical addition reaction, not an electrophilic substitution.

7. The reaction of 1,3-butadiene with one equivalent of bromine at low temperature yields predominantly:

A. 3,4-Dibromo-1-butene
B. 1,4-Dibromo-2-butene
C. 1,2-Dibromo-3-butene
D. 1,2,3,4-Tetrabromobutane
Answer: B

At low temperatures, 1,3-butadiene undergoes 1,4-addition with bromine to form 1,4-dibromo-2-butene as the major product. This is due to the stability of the intermediate allylic carbocation, which favors the 1,4-addition pathway.

8. The addition of HBr to propene in the presence of peroxides follows:

A. Markovnikov's rule
B. Anti-Markovnikov's rule
C. Kharasch effect
D. Both B and C
Answer: D

In the presence of peroxides, the addition of HBr to propene follows the anti-Markovnikov rule (also known as the Kharasch effect or peroxide effect), resulting in the formation of 1-bromopropane instead of 2-bromopropane.

9. Which of the following compounds is aromatic?

A. Cyclopentadiene
B. Cycloheptatriene
C. Benzene
D. Cyclooctatetraene
Answer: C

Benzene is aromatic because it satisfies Hückel's rule (4n + 2 π electrons, where n is an integer), is planar, and has a cyclic structure with continuous delocalization of π electrons. The other options do not meet all criteria for aromaticity.

10. The correct order of reactivity of the following hydrocarbons towards addition of HBr is: (I) Ethene (II) Propene (III) 2-Methylpropene

A. I < II < III
B. III < II < I
C. II < I < III
D. I < III < II
Answer: A

The reactivity of alkenes towards addition of HBr increases with the stability of the intermediate carbocation. 2-Methylpropene forms a tertiary carbocation, propene forms a secondary carbocation, and ethene forms a primary carbocation. Thus, the order is I < II < III.

11. The product formed when benzene is treated with excess chlorine in the presence of FeCl3 is:

A. Chlorobenzene
B. 1,2-Dichlorobenzene
C. 1,4-Dichlorobenzene
D. Hexachlorobenzene
Answer: D

In the presence of FeCl3 (a Lewis acid catalyst), benzene undergoes electrophilic substitution with excess chlorine to form hexachlorobenzene. This is due to the progressive substitution of hydrogen atoms by chlorine atoms.

12. Which of the following reagents is used to convert an alkene into an alkane?

A. H2/Pd
B. Br2/CCl4
C. KMnO4
D. O3
Answer: A

Hydrogen gas in the presence of a palladium (Pd) catalyst is used to reduce alkenes to alkanes through a process called catalytic hydrogenation. The other reagents are used for different reactions (e.g., bromination, oxidation, ozonolysis).

13. An alkene on ozonolysis yields two moles of ethanal and one mole of methanal. The structure of the alkene is:

A. CH3-CH=CH-CH3
B. CH2=CH-CH2-CH3
C. CH3-CH=C(CH3)2
D. (CH3)2C=CH-CH3
Answer: D

Ozonolysis of an alkene cleaves the double bond, producing carbonyl compounds. The given products are two moles of ethanal (CH3CHO) and one mole of methanal (HCHO). This indicates the original alkene must have had two ethylidene (CH3-CH=) groups and one methylene (=CH2) group attached to the double bond. The only structure that fits this requirement is (CH3)2C=CH-CH3 (2-methylbut-2-ene), which yields two moles of ethanal from the two methyl groups attached to the double-bonded carbon and one mole of methanal from the terminal methylene group.
