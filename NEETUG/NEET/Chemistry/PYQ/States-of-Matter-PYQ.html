This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on the topic 'States of Matter' for NEET preparation. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following statements correctly describes the behavior of an ideal gas according to the kinetic theory of gases?

A. The volume occupied by the gas molecules is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas.
B. The gas molecules exert attractive forces on each other at all temperatures.
C. The average kinetic energy of the gas molecules is inversely proportional to the absolute temperature.
D. The collisions between gas molecules are inelastic.
Answer: A

According to the kinetic theory of gases, an ideal gas is characterized by the following assumptions: (1) The volume occupied by the gas molecules is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas. (2) There are no intermolecular forces (attractive or repulsive) between the gas molecules. (3) The average kinetic energy of the gas molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature. (4) The collisions between gas molecules and with the walls of the container are perfectly elastic. Thus, option A is correct.

2. At constant temperature, the pressure of a given mass of gas is doubled. What happens to its volume?

A. It remains the same.
B. It is halved.
C. It is doubled.
D. It becomes four times the original volume.
Answer: B

According to Boyle's Law, for a given mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure of the gas is inversely proportional to its volume. Mathematically, P ∝ 1/V or PV = constant. If the pressure is doubled, the volume must be halved to maintain the product PV constant. Thus, the correct answer is B.

3. The root mean square (rms) speed of gas molecules is given by the expression:

A. √(3RT/M)
B. √(2RT/M)
C. √(8RT/πM)
D. √(3kT/m)
Answer: A

The root mean square (rms) speed of gas molecules is derived from the kinetic theory of gases and is given by the formula: urms = √(3RT/M), where R is the universal gas constant, T is the absolute temperature, and M is the molar mass of the gas. This formula is crucial for understanding the speed distribution of gas molecules. Thus, option A is correct.

4. Which of the following gases will have the highest rate of diffusion at a given temperature and pressure?

A. Oxygen (O₂)
B. Nitrogen (N₂)
C. Hydrogen (H₂)
D. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
Answer: C

According to Graham's Law of Diffusion, the rate of diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass. The lighter the gas, the faster it diffuses. Among the given options, hydrogen (H₂) has the lowest molar mass (2 g/mol), so it will have the highest rate of diffusion. Thus, the correct answer is C.

5. A gas occupies a volume of 500 mL at 760 mm Hg pressure. If the pressure is increased to 1520 mm Hg at constant temperature, what will be the new volume of the gas?

A. 250 mL
B. 500 mL
C. 750 mL
D. 1000 mL
Answer: A

Using Boyle's Law (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂), where P₁ = 760 mm Hg, V₁ = 500 mL, and P₂ = 1520 mm Hg, we can solve for V₂: V₂ = (P₁V₁)/P₂ = (760 × 500)/1520 = 250 mL. Thus, the new volume is 250 mL, making option A correct.

6. The van der Waals equation for real gases introduces corrections for:

A. The volume of gas molecules and intermolecular forces.
B. The temperature and pressure of the gas.
C. The kinetic energy of the gas molecules.
D. The ideal gas constant and molar mass.
Answer: A

The van der Waals equation modifies the ideal gas law to account for the behavior of real gases. It introduces two corrections: (1) The volume occupied by the gas molecules themselves (b), which reduces the available volume for molecular motion. (2) The intermolecular attractive forces (a), which reduce the pressure exerted by the gas. Thus, option A is correct.

7. Which of the following is NOT a postulate of the kinetic theory of gases?

A. Gas molecules are in continuous random motion.
B. The volume of gas molecules is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas.
C. The collisions between gas molecules are perfectly inelastic.
D. The average kinetic energy of gas molecules is proportional to the absolute temperature.
Answer: C

The kinetic theory of gases assumes that collisions between gas molecules are perfectly elastic, meaning there is no net loss of kinetic energy during collisions. The other options (A, B, and D) are correct postulates of the kinetic theory. Thus, option C is the correct answer.

8. At what temperature will the root mean square speed of oxygen molecules (O₂) be equal to the root mean square speed of hydrogen molecules (H₂) at 300 K?

A. 4800 K
B. 1200 K
C. 300 K
D. 2400 K
Answer: A

The root mean square speed (urms) of a gas is given by urms = √(3RT/M). For the urms of O₂ and H₂ to be equal at different temperatures, the following must hold: √(3RT₁/M_O₂) = √(3RT₂/M_H₂). Simplifying, T₁/M_O₂ = T₂/M_H₂. Given T₂ = 300 K, M_O₂ = 32 g/mol, and M_H₂ = 2 g/mol, we get T₁ = (T₂ × M_O₂)/M_H₂ = (300 × 32)/2 = 4800 K. Thus, the correct answer is A.

9. The compressibility factor (Z) for an ideal gas is:

A. Always equal to 1.
B. Greater than 1 at all temperatures.
C. Less than 1 at all temperatures.
D. Equal to 1 only at high pressures.
Answer: A

The compressibility factor (Z) is defined as Z = PV/RT. For an ideal gas, PV = RT, so Z = 1 at all temperatures and pressures. Real gases deviate from this behavior, but for an ideal gas, Z is always equal to 1. Thus, option A is correct.

10. Which of the following statements about the critical temperature of a gas is correct?

A. It is the temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied, regardless of the pressure applied.
B. It is the temperature at which the gas and liquid phases coexist at any pressure.
C. It is the lowest temperature at which a gas can be liquefied by applying pressure.
D. It is the temperature at which the gas molecules have zero kinetic energy.
Answer: A

The critical temperature of a gas is defined as the temperature above which the gas cannot be liquefied, no matter how much pressure is applied. Below the critical temperature, the gas can be liquefied by applying sufficient pressure. Thus, option A is correct.

11. A gas mixture contains 2 moles of oxygen (O₂) and 3 moles of nitrogen (N₂). What is the mole fraction of oxygen in the mixture?

A. 0.2
B. 0.4
C. 0.6
D. 0.8
Answer: B

The mole fraction of a component in a mixture is given by the ratio of the moles of that component to the total moles of the mixture. Here, the total moles = 2 (O₂) + 3 (N₂) = 5 moles. The mole fraction of oxygen = moles of O₂ / total moles = 2/5 = 0.4. Thus, the correct answer is B.

12. Which of the following gases is most likely to deviate from ideal behavior at high pressures and low temperatures?

A. Helium (He)
B. Hydrogen (H₂)
C. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
D. Nitrogen (N₂)
Answer: C

Real gases deviate from ideal behavior at high pressures and low temperatures due to the presence of intermolecular forces and the finite volume occupied by gas molecules. Among the given options, carbon dioxide (CO₂) has the strongest intermolecular forces (van der Waals forces) and the largest molecular size, making it most likely to deviate from ideal behavior. Thus, the correct answer is C.

13. Which of the following gases will have the highest rate of diffusion under identical conditions of temperature and pressure?

A. O₂
B. CO₂
C. NH₃
D. CH₄
Answer: C

According to Graham's Law of Diffusion, the rate of diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass. NH₃ has the lowest molar mass (17 g/mol) among the given options, so it will diffuse the fastest.

14. At constant temperature, the pressure of a fixed amount of gas is doubled. The volume of the gas will:

A. Remain the same
B. Be halved
C. Be doubled
D. Become four times
Answer: B

Boyle's Law states that at constant temperature, the pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume (P ∝ 1/V). If pressure is doubled, volume is halved.

15. The van der Waals equation corrects the ideal gas law for:

A. Only the volume occupied by gas molecules
B. Only the intermolecular forces between gas molecules
C. Both the volume occupied by gas molecules and intermolecular forces
D. Neither volume nor intermolecular forces
Answer: C

The van der Waals equation introduces two corrections to the ideal gas law: (1) the volume occupied by gas molecules (b) and (2) the intermolecular forces of attraction (a/V²).

16. A gas occupies 500 mL at 27°C. What volume will it occupy at 127°C if the pressure remains constant?

A. 333 mL
B. 666 mL
C. 750 mL
D. 1000 mL
Answer: B

Using Charles's Law (V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂), where temperatures must be in Kelvin: T₁ = 273 + 27 = 300 K, T₂ = 273 + 127 = 400 K. V₂ = V₁ × (T₂/T₁) = 500 × (400/300) = 666.67 mL ≈ 666 mL.

17. A mixture of gases contains 2 moles of O₂, 3 moles of N₂, and 5 moles of CO₂. What is the mole fraction of N₂ in the mixture?

A. 0.2
B. 0.3
C. 0.5
D. 0.6
Answer: B

Mole fraction of N₂ = moles of N₂ / total moles = 3 / (2 + 3 + 5) = 3/10 = 0.3.

18. The root mean square speed (u_rms) of gas molecules is given by:

A. √(3RT/M)
B. √(2RT/M)
C. √(8RT/πM)
D. √(3kT/m)
Answer: A

The root mean square speed (u_rms) of gas molecules is given by u_rms = √(3RT/M), where R is the gas constant, T is the absolute temperature, and M is the molar mass of the gas.

19. At what temperature will the volume of a gas be twice its volume at 0°C, if the pressure remains constant?

A. 273 K
B. 373 K
C. 546 K
D. 273°C
Answer: C

Using Charles's Law (V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂), where V₂ = 2V₁ and T₁ = 273 K (0°C). T₂ = V₂ × T₁ / V₁ = 2 × 273 = 546 K.

20. A gas at 300 K and 1 atm pressure occupies a volume of 10 L. If the pressure is increased to 2 atm at constant temperature, what will be the new volume of the gas? Which gas law is directly applied here?

A. 5 L, Boyle's Law
B. 20 L, Charles's Law
C. 5 L, Charles's Law
D. 10 L, Avogadro's Law
Answer: A

According to Boyle's Law, for a given mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure of the gas is inversely proportional to its volume (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂). Here, P₁ = 1 atm, V₁ = 10 L, P₂ = 2 atm. Solving for V₂: V₂ = (P₁V₁)/P₂ = (1 × 10)/2 = 5 L. Thus, the new volume is 5 L, and the law applied is Boyle's Law.

21. A gas at 27°C and 1 atm pressure occupies a volume of 10 L. If the temperature is increased to 127°C at constant pressure, the new volume of the gas will be:

A. 12.2 L
B. 13.3 L
C. 15.0 L
D. 16.6 L
Answer: B

Using Charles's Law (V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂), where temperature must be in Kelvin. Convert temperatures: T₁ = 27°C = 300 K, T₂ = 127°C = 400 K. Substitute values: 10 L / 300 K = V₂ / 400 K. Solving for V₂ gives V₂ = (10 × 400) / 300 = 13.3 L. Common mistake: Forgetting to convert Celsius to Kelvin.

22. Which of the following statements regarding the kinetic theory of gases is INCORRECT?

A. The average kinetic energy of gas molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature of the gas.
B. Gas molecules are in constant random motion and collide with the walls of the container, exerting pressure.
C. The volume occupied by the gas molecules themselves is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas.
D. The collisions between gas molecules are inelastic, leading to loss of kinetic energy over time.
Answer: D

According to the kinetic theory of gases, collisions between gas molecules are perfectly elastic, meaning no kinetic energy is lost during collisions. The other statements (A, B, C) are correct postulates of the kinetic theory. Common confusion: Misinterpreting elastic collisions as inelastic.
