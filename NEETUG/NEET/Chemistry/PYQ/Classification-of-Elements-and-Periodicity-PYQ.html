This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) modeled on previous NEET patterns for the topic 'Classification of Elements and Periodicity'. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. Which of the following elements is classified as a metalloid in the modern periodic table?

A. Aluminium
B. Silicon
C. Phosphorus
D. Sulphur
Answer: B

Metalloids exhibit properties intermediate between metals and non-metals. Silicon is a classic metalloid, located along the staircase line in the p-block of the periodic table. Aluminium is a metal, while phosphorus and sulphur are non-metals.

2. The correct order of increasing atomic radius among the following elements is:

A. O < F < N < C
B. F < O < N < C
C. C < N < O < F
D. N < C < F < O
Answer: B

Atomic radius decreases across a period due to increasing nuclear charge. Among the second-period elements listed, the order of atomic numbers is C (6) < N (7) < O (8) < F (9), so the atomic radius decreases as F < O < N < C.

3. Which property of elements generally increases down a group in the periodic table?

A. Ionisation enthalpy
B. Electronegativity
C. Atomic radius
D. Electron gain enthalpy
Answer: C

Down a group, the number of electron shells increases, leading to an increase in atomic radius. Ionisation enthalpy, electronegativity, and electron gain enthalpy generally decrease down a group due to increased shielding and distance from the nucleus.

4. An element with atomic number 34 belongs to which block and group in the periodic table?

A. p-block, Group 16
B. p-block, Group 17
C. d-block, Group 6
D. s-block, Group 2
Answer: A

The element with atomic number 34 is selenium (Se). Its electronic configuration is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁴, placing it in the p-block, Group 16 (chalcogens).

5. The first ionisation enthalpy of aluminium is lower than that of magnesium because:

A. Aluminium has a larger atomic radius than magnesium
B. The electron removed from aluminium is from a 3p orbital, which is higher in energy than the 3s orbital of magnesium
C. Magnesium has a higher nuclear charge than aluminium
D. Aluminium has a completely filled 3s orbital
Answer: B

The first ionisation enthalpy of aluminium is lower because the electron removed is from the 3p orbital, which is higher in energy and thus easier to remove compared to the 3s electron of magnesium. This outweighs the effect of aluminium's slightly higher nuclear charge.

6. Which of the following statements regarding the modern periodic law is correct?

A. Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic weights
B. Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers
C. Properties of elements repeat every 8 elements
D. Properties of elements are determined solely by their group number
Answer: B

The modern periodic law, proposed by Moseley, states that the properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers, not atomic weights. This corrected the anomalies in Mendeleev's periodic table.

7. The element with the highest electronegativity in the periodic table is:

A. Oxygen
B. Chlorine
C. Fluorine
D. Nitrogen
Answer: C

Fluorine is the most electronegative element in the periodic table, with an electronegativity value of 3.98 on the Pauling scale. This is due to its small atomic size and high effective nuclear charge.

8. Which of the following elements has the most negative electron gain enthalpy?

A. Fluorine
B. Chlorine
C. Bromine
D. Iodine
Answer: B

Chlorine has the most negative electron gain enthalpy among the halogens. While fluorine is highly electronegative, its small size leads to significant electron-electron repulsion, making its electron gain enthalpy less negative than that of chlorine.

9. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of transition elements?

A. Variable oxidation states
B. Formation of coloured compounds
C. High ionisation enthalpies
D. Presence of electrons in the outermost d-orbitals
Answer: D

Transition elements are defined by the presence of electrons in the (n-1)d orbitals, not the outermost d-orbitals. They exhibit variable oxidation states, form coloured compounds, and generally have high ionisation enthalpies.

10. The element with atomic number 56 is placed in which period and group of the periodic table?

A. Period 6, Group 2
B. Period 5, Group 2
C. Period 6, Group 1
D. Period 5, Group 1
Answer: A

The element with atomic number 56 is barium (Ba). Its electronic configuration is [Xe] 6s², placing it in Period 6 and Group 2 (alkaline earth metals).

11. Which of the following trends is observed for the ionic radii of isoelectronic species?

A. Increases with increasing nuclear charge
B. Decreases with increasing nuclear charge
C. Remains constant regardless of nuclear charge
D. Increases with increasing atomic number
Answer: B

For isoelectronic species (same number of electrons), the ionic radius decreases with increasing nuclear charge. This is because a higher nuclear charge pulls the electrons closer to the nucleus, reducing the ionic radius.

12. Which of the following elements has the highest first ionization enthalpy?

A. Aluminium (Al)
B. Silicon (Si)
C. Phosphorus (P)
D. Sulfur (S)
Answer: C

First ionization enthalpy generally increases across a period from left to right due to increasing nuclear charge and decreasing atomic radius. However, phosphorus (P) has a half-filled 3p subshell (3p³), which is more stable than the 3p⁴ configuration of sulfur (S). This extra stability results in a higher first ionization enthalpy for phosphorus compared to sulfur. Aluminium (Al) and silicon (Si) have lower ionization enthalpies as they appear earlier in the period.

13. The correct order of increasing electron gain enthalpy (more negative) for the elements O, S, and Se is:

A. Se < S < O
B. O < S < Se
C. S < Se < O
D. Se < O < S
Answer: A

Electron gain enthalpy becomes less negative as we move down a group due to an increase in atomic size and shielding effect, which reduces the attraction for the incoming electron. However, oxygen (O) has a less negative electron gain enthalpy than sulfur (S) because of its small size, which leads to significant electron-electron repulsion in the relatively compact 2p orbital. Thus, the correct order is Se < S < O.

14. An element X has the following successive ionization enthalpies (in kJ/mol): 577, 1816, 2744, 11576, 14829. To which group of the periodic table does element X most likely belong?

A. Group 1
B. Group 2
C. Group 13
D. Group 14
Answer: B

The large jump in ionization enthalpy occurs after the second ionization enthalpy (from 2744 kJ/mol to 11576 kJ/mol), indicating that the element loses 2 electrons relatively easily but resists losing a third electron strongly. This suggests that the element has 2 valence electrons and belongs to Group 2 (alkaline earth metals).

15. Which of the following elements is expected to have the largest atomic radius?

A. Chlorine (Cl)
B. Potassium (K)
C. Calcium (Ca)
D. Bromine (Br)
Answer: B

Atomic radius increases down a group and decreases across a period from left to right. Potassium (K) is in Group 1 and Period 4, while calcium (Ca) is in Group 2 and Period 4. Chlorine (Cl) and bromine (Br) are in Group 17, with bromine below chlorine. Among the given options, potassium has the largest atomic radius as it is the farthest left and has fewer protons pulling the electrons inward compared to calcium.

16. The element with the electronic configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵ belongs to:

A. Group 15 and Period 3
B. Group 17 and Period 3
C. Group 16 and Period 3
D. Group 17 and Period 2
Answer: B

The electronic configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵ corresponds to 7 valence electrons (2 in 3s and 5 in 3p), placing the element in Group 17 (halogens). The principal quantum number of the outermost shell is 3, so the element belongs to Period 3. This configuration matches chlorine (Cl).

17. Which of the following trends is observed for electronegativity in the periodic table?

A. Increases down a group and decreases across a period.
B. Decreases down a group and increases across a period.
C. Increases down a group and increases across a period.
D. Decreases down a group and decreases across a period.
Answer: B

Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract a shared pair of electrons. It decreases down a group due to an increase in atomic size and shielding effect, which reduces the attraction for electrons. It increases across a period from left to right due to increasing nuclear charge and decreasing atomic radius, which enhances the attraction for electrons.

18. The correct order of increasing metallic character for the elements Na, Mg, Al, and Si is:

A. Si < Al < Mg < Na
B. Na < Mg < Al < Si
C. Si < Mg < Al < Na
D. Al < Si < Mg < Na
Answer: A

Metallic character decreases across a period from left to right and increases down a group. Among the given elements, sodium (Na) is the most metallic, followed by magnesium (Mg), aluminium (Al), and silicon (Si), which is the least metallic (or most non-metallic). Thus, the correct order is Si < Al < Mg < Na.

19. Which of the following pairs of elements exhibits diagonal relationship in the periodic table?

A. Li and Mg
B. Be and Al
C. B and Si
D. All of the above
Answer: D

A diagonal relationship occurs between certain pairs of elements in the second and third periods of the periodic table. These pairs exhibit similar properties due to comparable atomic and ionic radii, as well as similar charge-to-radius ratios. The pairs Li-Mg, Be-Al, and B-Si all exhibit diagonal relationships.

20. The element with the highest melting point in the third period is:

A. Sodium (Na)
B. Magnesium (Mg)
C. Silicon (Si)
D. Chlorine (Cl)
Answer: C

Silicon (Si) is a metalloid with a giant covalent structure, which results in a very high melting point. In the third period, sodium (Na) and magnesium (Mg) are metals with relatively low melting points, while chlorine (Cl) is a gas at room temperature. Thus, silicon has the highest melting point among the given options.

21. Which of the following statements about the lanthanide series is correct?

A. The lanthanide series includes elements with atomic numbers 58 to 71.
B. The lanthanides are characterized by the filling of the 4d subshell.
C. All lanthanides exhibit a +2 oxidation state.
D. The atomic radii of lanthanides increase as the atomic number increases.
Answer: A

The lanthanide series consists of elements with atomic numbers 58 to 71, where the 4f subshell is progressively filled. The lanthanides are characterized by the filling of the 4f subshell, not the 4d subshell. They primarily exhibit a +3 oxidation state, though some can show +2 or +4 states. The atomic radii of lanthanides decrease as the atomic number increases, a phenomenon known as the lanthanide contraction.

22. An element has the following properties: high ionization enthalpy, high electronegativity, and forms acidic oxides. This element is most likely:

A. A metal
B. A non-metal
C. A metalloid
D. An alkali metal
Answer: B

Non-metals typically have high ionization enthalpies and high electronegativities. They also form acidic oxides. Metals, on the other hand, have low ionization enthalpies, low electronegativities, and form basic oxides. Alkali metals are highly reactive metals and do not fit the given properties. Thus, the element is most likely a non-metal.

23. The correct order of increasing atomic size for the elements O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, and Mg²⁺ is:

A. Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ < Ne < F⁻ < O²⁻
B. O²⁻ < F⁻ < Ne < Na⁺ < Mg²⁺
C. Ne < F⁻ < O²⁻ < Na⁺ < Mg²⁺
D. Mg²⁺ < O²⁻ < Na⁺ < F⁻ < Ne
Answer: A

All the given species are isoelectronic (have the same number of electrons: 10). For isoelectronic species, the size decreases as the nuclear charge (atomic number) increases because the greater nuclear charge pulls the electrons closer to the nucleus. The order of increasing nuclear charge is O²⁻ (8) < F⁻ (9) < Ne (10) < Na⁺ (11) < Mg²⁺ (12). Thus, the correct order of increasing atomic size is Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ < Ne < F⁻ < O²⁻.
