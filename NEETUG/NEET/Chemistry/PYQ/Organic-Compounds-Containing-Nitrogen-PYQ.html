This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) based on the NEET exam pattern for the topic 'Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen'. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following reagents is used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines via the Hinsberg test?

A. Benzene sulfonyl chloride (C₆H₅SO₂Cl)
B. Acetyl chloride (CH₃COCl)
C. Nitrous acid (HNO₂)
D. Bromine water (Br₂/H₂O)
Answer: A

The Hinsberg test uses benzene sulfonyl chloride (C₆H₅SO₂Cl) to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines. Primary amines form a soluble sulfonamide that dissolves in alkali, secondary amines form an insoluble sulfonamide, and tertiary amines do not react.

2. The correct order of basicity among the following amines in aqueous solution is: (I) Aniline, (II) Methylamine, (III) Dimethylamine, (IV) Trimethylamine

A. IV > III > II > I
B. III > IV > II > I
C. II > III > IV > I
D. I > II > III > IV
Answer: B

In aqueous solution, the basicity order is dimethylamine (III) > trimethylamine (IV) > methylamine (II) > aniline (I). This is due to the combined effects of inductive effects (+I effect of alkyl groups) and solvation effects, where steric hindrance reduces the solvation of trimethylamine.

3. Which of the following compounds will NOT give a positive isocyanide test?

A. Ethylamine
B. Diethylamine
C. Aniline
D. Triethylamine
Answer: D

The isocyanide test is positive for primary amines (ethylamine and aniline) but negative for secondary and tertiary amines. Triethylamine is a tertiary amine and will not form an isocyanide with chloroform and alcoholic KOH.

4. The product formed when nitrobenzene is reduced with Sn/HCl is:

A. Nitrosobenzene
B. Hydrazobenzene
C. Aniline
D. Phenylhydroxylamine
Answer: C

Reduction of nitrobenzene with Sn/HCl yields aniline. This is a common laboratory method for the preparation of aromatic amines, where the nitro group is fully reduced to an amino group.

5. Which of the following statements about diazonium salts is INCORRECT?

A. They are stable at low temperatures (0-5°C).
B. They undergo coupling reactions with phenols and aromatic amines.
C. They can be used to prepare aryl halides via the Sandmeyer reaction.
D. They are highly stable at room temperature and can be stored for long periods.
Answer: D

Diazonium salts are unstable at room temperature and decompose readily. They are typically prepared and used at low temperatures (0-5°C) to prevent decomposition.

6. The reaction of aniline with bromine water results in the formation of:

A. 2-Bromoaniline
B. 3-Bromoaniline
C. 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline
D. 4-Bromoaniline
Answer: C

Aniline is highly activating due to the lone pair on nitrogen, leading to the formation of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline when treated with bromine water. This is a characteristic reaction of aniline due to its strong ortho/para-directing effect.

7. Which of the following compounds is used as a stabilizer in the preparation of diazonium salts?

A. Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
B. Sodium nitrite (NaNO₂)
C. Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
D. Copper(I) chloride (CuCl)
Answer: A

Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is used to stabilize diazonium salts by providing an acidic medium, which prevents the decomposition of the diazonium ion. It also reacts with sodium nitrite to generate nitrous acid in situ.

8. Which of the following reactions is used to convert a primary amine into an alcohol?

A. Hofmann elimination
B. Carbylamine reaction
C. Diazotization followed by hydrolysis
D. Acetylation
Answer: C

Diazotization of a primary amine followed by hydrolysis converts the amine into an alcohol. The diazonium salt formed is unstable and decomposes to form a phenol (or alcohol in aliphatic cases) upon heating with water.

9. The compound formed when ethylamine is treated with nitrous acid is:

A. Ethanol
B. Ethyl nitrite
C. Ethane
D. Nitroethane
Answer: A

Ethylamine reacts with nitrous acid (HNO₂) to form ethanol. This is a characteristic reaction of primary aliphatic amines, where the amino group is replaced by a hydroxyl group.

10. Which of the following statements about amides is CORRECT?

A. Amides are more basic than amines due to resonance stabilization.
B. Amides can be reduced to primary amines using LiAlH₄.
C. Amides undergo the Hofmann degradation to form primary amines with one fewer carbon atom.
D. Amides are highly reactive towards nucleophilic substitution reactions.
Answer: B

Amides can be reduced to primary amines using LiAlH₄. This is a common method for the preparation of amines from amides. The other statements are incorrect: amides are less basic than amines, Hofmann degradation forms amines with one fewer carbon, and amides are relatively unreactive towards nucleophilic substitution.

11. The correct order of reactivity of the following compounds towards nucleophilic substitution is: (I) Chlorobenzene, (II) Benzyl chloride, (III) Vinyl chloride, (IV) Allyl chloride

A. II > IV > I > III
B. IV > II > III > I
C. II > I > IV > III
D. III > I > IV > II
Answer: A

The order of reactivity towards nucleophilic substitution is benzyl chloride (II) > allyl chloride (IV) > chlorobenzene (I) > vinyl chloride (III). Benzyl and allyl chlorides are highly reactive due to resonance stabilization of the intermediate carbocation, while chlorobenzene and vinyl chloride are less reactive due to partial double-bond character.

12. The product formed when aniline is treated with bromine water at room temperature is:

A. 2-Bromoaniline
B. 3-Bromoaniline
C. 2,4,6-Tribromoaniline
D. 4-Bromoaniline
Answer: C

Aniline undergoes bromination readily due to the activating effect of the -NH₂ group, leading to the formation of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline as a white precipitate.

13. The correct order of basicity among the following amines in aqueous solution is:

A. Aniline > Methylamine > Ammonia
B. Methylamine > Ammonia > Aniline
C. Ammonia > Methylamine > Aniline
D. Methylamine > Aniline > Ammonia
Answer: B

In aqueous solution, alkylamines (e.g., methylamine) are more basic than ammonia due to the +I effect of the alkyl group. Aniline is less basic due to the resonance stabilization of the lone pair on nitrogen with the benzene ring.

14. Which of the following compounds will not undergo coupling with benzenediazonium chloride?

A. Phenol
B. Aniline
C. Nitrobenzene
D. N,N-Dimethylaniline
Answer: C

Coupling reactions of benzenediazonium chloride occur with activated aromatic rings (e.g., phenol, aniline, N,N-dimethylaniline). Nitrobenzene is deactivated due to the -NO₂ group and does not undergo coupling.

15. The reaction of ethylamine with nitrous acid yields:

A. Ethanol and nitrogen gas
B. Ethyl nitrite
C. Ethyl diazonium salt
D. Nitroethane
Answer: A

Primary aliphatic amines (e.g., ethylamine) react with nitrous acid to form alcohols and nitrogen gas. The reaction proceeds via a diazonium intermediate that decomposes to form ethanol and N₂.

16. Which of the following is the correct structure of the product formed when acetamide is treated with bromine in the presence of sodium hydroxide (Hofmann bromamide reaction)?

A. Methylamine
B. Ethylamine
C. Dimethylamine
D. Methyl isocyanate
Answer: A

The Hofmann bromamide reaction converts amides to amines with one fewer carbon atom. Acetamide (CH₃CONH₂) yields methylamine (CH₃NH₂) under these conditions.

17. The correct order of reactivity of the following compounds towards nucleophilic substitution (Sₙ2) is:

A. CH₃CH₂Br > CH₃CH₂NH₂ > CH₃CH₂OH
B. CH₃CH₂Br > CH₃CH₂OH > CH₃CH₂NH₂
C. CH₃CH₂NH₂ > CH₃CH₂Br > CH₃CH₂OH
D. CH₃CH₂OH > CH₃CH₂Br > CH₃CH₂NH₂
Answer: A

Alkyl halides (e.g., CH₃CH₂Br) are the most reactive towards Sₙ2 due to the good leaving group (Br⁻). Amines (CH₃CH₂NH₂) are less reactive because the -NH₂ group is a poor leaving group, and alcohols (CH₃CH₂OH) are the least reactive due to the poor leaving group (OH⁻).

18. In the reaction sequence below, identify the product **Z** formed when **X** is treated with HNO₂ followed by heating with water: X (C₆H₅NH₂) → (HNO₂, 0-5°C) Y → (H₂O, Δ) Z

A. C₆H₅OH (Phenol)
B. C₆H₅NO₂ (Nitrobenzene)
C. C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ (Benzenediazonium chloride)
D. C₆H₅NHNH₂ (Phenylhydrazine)
Answer: A

When aniline (C₆H₅NH₂, **X**) reacts with nitrous acid (HNO₂) at 0-5°C, it forms benzenediazonium chloride (**Y**). Upon heating with water, the diazonium salt undergoes hydrolysis, replacing the diazonium group (–N₂⁺) with a hydroxyl group (–OH), yielding phenol (**Z**). This is a classic example of the diazotization reaction followed by hydrolysis, a key transformation in the chemistry of aromatic amines. The other options are incorrect: nitrobenzene (B) is formed by nitration of benzene, not this sequence; benzenediazonium chloride (C) is the intermediate **Y**, not the final product; and phenylhydrazine (D) is not formed under these conditions.

19. Which of the following reagents is most suitable for the conversion of benzamide to aniline under mild conditions?

A. LiAlH₄ in ether
B. Br₂ in aqueous NaOH (Hofmann bromamide reaction)
C. Sn/HCl (reduction)
D. NaOH/I₂ (iodoform test)
Answer: B

The Hofmann bromamide reaction (Br₂ in aqueous NaOH) is specifically used to convert amides to amines with one fewer carbon atom under mild conditions. Here, benzamide (C₆H₅CONH₂) is converted to aniline (C₆H₅NH₂). LiAlH₄ reduces amides to amines but does not remove the carbonyl carbon, yielding benzylamine (C₆H₅CH₂NH₂) instead. Sn/HCl is used for nitro group reduction, not amides. NaOH/I₂ is for the iodoform test, unrelated to amide conversion.

20. A compound with molecular formula C₃H₉N exhibits two isomeric forms that are both soluble in water and react with nitrous acid to give different products. Which of the following pairs correctly represents these isomers?

A. Propan-1-amine and propan-2-amine
B. Trimethylamine and propan-1-amine
C. N-Methylethanamine and propan-2-amine
D. Propan-1-amine and N,N-dimethylmethanamine
Answer: A

C₃H₉N has two primary amine isomers: propan-1-amine (1°) and propan-2-amine (1°). Both are water-soluble and react with HNO₂: propan-1-amine yields propan-1-ol (with N₂ gas), while propan-2-amine yields propan-2-ol. Trimethylamine (3°) does not react with HNO₂ to give an alcohol. N-Methylethanamine (2°) gives a nitrosoamine, not an alcohol. N,N-Dimethylmethanamine is C₂H₇N, not C₃H₉N.

21. In the diazotization of aniline, the temperature must be maintained below 5°C. What is the primary reason for this strict temperature control?

A. To prevent the formation of phenol via hydrolysis of the diazonium salt
B. To avoid the decomposition of sodium nitrite into nitric oxide and nitrogen dioxide
C. To ensure the complete conversion of aniline to benzenediazonium chloride without side reactions
D. To stabilize the intermediate nitrosamine formed during the reaction
Answer: A

Benzenediazonium chloride is unstable above 5°C and readily hydrolyzes to phenol (C₆H₅OH) with the release of N₂ gas. This side reaction reduces the yield of the diazonium salt, which is essential for further coupling or substitution reactions. While low temperature does slow decomposition of NaNO₂, the primary concern is the diazonium salt's stability. Nitrosamine formation is not a key issue in diazotization.

22. Which of the following statements about the basicity of amines is correct in the context of NEET?

A. Aliphatic amines are generally less basic than ammonia due to the -I effect of alkyl groups
B. Aryl amines are more basic than alkyl amines because the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized into the benzene ring
C. In aqueous solution, the basicity order of methylamines is: (CH₃)₃N > (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > NH₃
D. The basicity of amines is determined by the availability of the lone pair on nitrogen and its stability in the protonated form
Answer: D

Basicity of amines depends on the availability of the lone pair on nitrogen and the stability of the resulting ammonium ion. Aliphatic amines are more basic than ammonia due to the +I effect of alkyl groups, which increases electron density on nitrogen. Aryl amines are less basic because the lone pair is delocalized into the benzene ring (resonance), reducing its availability. In aqueous solution, the basicity order is (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ due to solvation effects. Statement D is the only correct general principle.

23. A compound X (C₇H₇NO) dissolves in dilute HCl and gives a brisk effervescence with NaHCO₃. On treatment with Br₂ in aqueous NaOH, it yields a compound Y (C₆H₇N). What is the most likely structure of X?

A. Benzamide (C₆H₅CONH₂)
B. p-Aminobenzoic acid (H₂NC₆H₄COOH)
C. N-Methylbenzamide (C₆H₅CONHCH₃)
D. Benzylamine (C₆H₅CH₂NH₂)
Answer: B
