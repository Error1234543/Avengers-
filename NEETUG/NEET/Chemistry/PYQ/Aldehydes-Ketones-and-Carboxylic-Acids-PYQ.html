PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed to match the style, difficulty, and coverage of NEET previous-year questions (PYQ) on the topic 'Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids'. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Choose the correct option (A, B, C, or D) for each question.

1. Which of the following reagents is used to distinguish between benzaldehyde and acetophenone?

A. Tollens' reagent
B. Fehling's solution
C. 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP)
D. Sodium bisulphite
Answer: A

Tollens' reagent is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones. Benzaldehyde (an aldehyde) gives a positive Tollens' test (silver mirror), while acetophenone (a ketone) does not react. Fehling's solution also distinguishes aldehydes from ketones but is less commonly used for aromatic aldehydes like benzaldehyde. 2,4-DNP and sodium bisulphite react with both aldehydes and ketones, so they cannot distinguish between them.

2. The product formed when propanal is treated with dilute NaOH followed by heating is:

A. Propanol
B. 2-Methyl-2-pentenal
C. 3-Hydroxy-2-methylpentanal
D. Propanoic acid
Answer: B

Propanal undergoes aldol condensation in the presence of dilute NaOH. The initial product is 3-hydroxy-2-methylpentanal, which upon heating loses a water molecule to form 2-methyl-2-pentenal (an α,β-unsaturated aldehyde). This is a classic example of aldol condensation followed by dehydration.

3. Which of the following carboxylic acids does NOT undergo decarboxylation when heated with soda lime?

A. Formic acid
B. Benzoic acid
C. Oxalic acid
D. Malonic acid
Answer: B

Decarboxylation with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) typically occurs for carboxylic acids with a β-carbonyl group or those that are unstable (e.g., formic acid, oxalic acid, malonic acid). Benzoic acid, being a stable aromatic carboxylic acid, does not undergo decarboxylation under these conditions.

4. The correct order of reactivity of the following compounds towards nucleophilic addition reactions is: (I) Formaldehyde, (II) Acetone, (III) Benzaldehyde, (IV) Acetophenone

A. I > III > II > IV
B. I > II > III > IV
C. III > I > II > IV
D. II > I > IV > III
Answer: A

The reactivity of carbonyl compounds towards nucleophilic addition depends on steric and electronic factors. Formaldehyde (I) is the most reactive due to minimal steric hindrance and high electrophilicity. Benzaldehyde (III) is more reactive than acetone (II) because the phenyl group stabilizes the transition state through resonance. Acetophenone (IV) is the least reactive due to steric hindrance from both the phenyl and methyl groups.

5. Which of the following statements about the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction is INCORRECT?

A. It involves the α-halogenation of carboxylic acids.
B. It requires the presence of phosphorus (P) or phosphorus halides.
C. The reaction is specific to carboxylic acids with α-hydrogen atoms.
D. It can be used to introduce a halogen at the β-carbon of a carboxylic acid.
Answer: D

The Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction is used for the α-halogenation of carboxylic acids. It requires phosphorus or phosphorus halides (e.g., PBr₃) and is specific to carboxylic acids with α-hydrogen atoms. The reaction does NOT introduce halogens at the β-carbon; it exclusively substitutes α-hydrogens with halogens.

6. Identify the product formed when cyclohexanone is treated with excess methyl magnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis:

A. 1-Methylcyclohexanol
B. Cyclohexanol
C. 1,1-Dimethylcyclohexanol
D. Methylenecyclohexane
Answer: C

Grignard reagents (e.g., methyl magnesium bromide) react with ketones to form tertiary alcohols. Cyclohexanone reacts with excess methyl magnesium bromide to form 1,1-dimethylcyclohexanol after hydrolysis. The reaction involves nucleophilic addition of two methyl groups to the carbonyl carbon.

7. The reaction of benzoyl chloride with ammonia followed by heating yields:

A. Benzamide
B. Benzonitrile
C. Benzoic acid
D. Benzylamine
Answer: A

Benzoyl chloride reacts with ammonia to form benzamide. Upon heating, benzamide does not directly convert to benzonitrile under these conditions. The reaction is a nucleophilic acyl substitution where the -Cl group is replaced by -NH₂ to form an amide.

8. Which of the following reagents is used to reduce carboxylic acids to primary alcohols?

A. LiAlH₄
B. NaBH₄
C. H₂/Pd
D. Zn/Hg with HCl
Answer: A

Lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH₄) is a strong reducing agent that reduces carboxylic acids to primary alcohols. Sodium borohydride (NaBH₄) is not strong enough to reduce carboxylic acids. H₂/Pd and Zn/Hg with HCl are used for reducing other functional groups (e.g., alkenes, carbonyls) but not carboxylic acids.

9. The correct IUPAC name of the compound CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH is:

A. 2-Methylbutanoic acid
B. 3-Methylbutanoic acid
C. 2-Methylpropanoic acid
D. Pentanoic acid
Answer: A

The compound CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH has the longest carbon chain of 4 carbons with a methyl group at the 2nd carbon. The correct IUPAC name is 2-methylbutanoic acid. The carboxyl group (-COOH) gets priority in numbering, so the chain is numbered starting from the carboxyl carbon.

10. The compound formed when acetic acid is treated with PCl₅ is:

A. Acetyl chloride
B. Methyl chloride
C. Chloroacetic acid
D. Ethyl chloride
Answer: A

Phosphorus pentachloride (PCl₅) reacts with carboxylic acids to replace the -OH group with a -Cl group, forming acyl chlorides. Thus, acetic acid (CH₃COOH) reacts with PCl₅ to form acetyl chloride (CH₃COCl). This is a nucleophilic acyl substitution reaction.

11. The product formed when propanal is treated with dilute NaOH under aldol condensation conditions is:

A. 3-Hydroxy-2-methylpentanal
B. 2-Methyl-2-pentenal
C. 3-Hydroxyhexanal
D. 2-Ethyl-3-hydroxybutanal
Answer: A

Propanal undergoes aldol condensation in the presence of dilute NaOH. The α-hydrogen of one molecule attacks the carbonyl carbon of another, forming 3-hydroxy-2-methylpentanal. The product retains the hydroxyl group and forms a new carbon-carbon bond.

12. Which of the following is the major product when cyclohexanone is treated with methyl magnesium bromide followed by hydrolysis?

A. 1-Methylcyclohexanol
B. Cyclohexanol
C. 1-Methylcyclohexene
D. Cyclohexanecarboxylic acid
Answer: A

Grignard reagents (methyl magnesium bromide) add to the carbonyl group of cyclohexanone, forming a tertiary alcohol (1-methylcyclohexanol) after hydrolysis. The reaction does not produce alkenes or carboxylic acids under these conditions.

13. Which of the following reagents is used to convert a carboxylic acid to an acid chloride?

A. SOCl₂
B. PCl₃
C. PCl₅
D. All of the above
Answer: D

Thionyl chloride (SOCl₂), phosphorus trichloride (PCl₃), and phosphorus pentachloride (PCl₅) are all commonly used to convert carboxylic acids to acid chlorides. SOCl₂ is often preferred because the byproducts (SO₂ and HCl) are gases, simplifying purification.

14. The reaction of benzaldehyde with concentrated NaOH results in:

A. Cannizzaro reaction
B. Aldol condensation
C. Perkin reaction
D. Claisen condensation
Answer: A

Benzaldehyde lacks α-hydrogens, so it cannot undergo aldol condensation. Instead, it undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction in the presence of concentrated NaOH, producing benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate.

15. Which of the following compounds will NOT form a cyclic acetal when treated with ethylene glycol in the presence of an acid catalyst?

A. Acetone
B. Benzaldehyde
C. Formaldehyde
D. Acetophenone
Answer: D

Acetophenone is a ketone with bulky phenyl and methyl groups, which hinder the formation of a cyclic acetal with ethylene glycol. Aldehydes (formaldehyde, benzaldehyde) and less hindered ketones (acetone) readily form cyclic acetals under these conditions.

16. The correct order of acidity of the following carboxylic acids is: (I) Formic acid, (II) Acetic acid, (III) Benzoic acid, (IV) Chloroacetic acid

A. IV > I > III > II
B. I > IV > III > II
C. IV > III > I > II
D. III > IV > I > II
Answer: A

Chloroacetic acid is the most acidic due to the electron-withdrawing chlorine atom, which stabilizes the carboxylate anion. Formic acid is more acidic than benzoic acid because the phenyl group in benzoic acid has a weaker electron-withdrawing effect. Acetic acid is the least acidic among the given options.

17. Which of the following reagents can distinguish between benzaldehyde and acetophenone by giving a positive Tollens’ test?

A. 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine
B. Fehling’s solution
C. Tollens’ reagent
D. Sodium bisulphite
Answer: C

Tollens’ reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate) is used to distinguish aldehydes from ketones. Aldehydes, including benzaldehyde, are oxidized to carboxylic acids and reduce Tollens’ reagent to metallic silver (silver mirror test), giving a positive result. Ketones like acetophenone do not react with Tollens’ reagent. While Fehling’s solution (B) also tests for aldehydes, it is generally ineffective for aromatic aldehydes like benzaldehyde. 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (A) and sodium bisulphite (D) react with both aldehydes and ketones, so they cannot distinguish between them.

18. The correct order of acidity among the following carboxylic acids is: I. Chloroacetic acid II. Acetic acid III. Trichloroacetic acid IV. Formic acid

A. III > I > IV > II
B. III > IV > I > II
C. I > III > II > IV
D. IV > III > I > II
Answer: A

The acidity of carboxylic acids is influenced by the electron-withdrawing groups (EWGs) attached to the alpha carbon. EWGs stabilize the carboxylate anion, increasing acidity. Trichloroacetic acid (III) has three chlorine atoms (strong EWGs), making it the most acidic. Chloroacetic acid (I) has one chlorine atom, making it more acidic than formic acid (IV) and acetic acid (II). Formic acid is more acidic than acetic acid due to the absence of an electron-donating methyl group. Thus, the correct order is III > I > IV > II.

19. In the reaction sequence: Ethanol → (P) → Ethanal → (Q) → Ethanoic acid, the reagents P and Q are respectively:

A. KMnO₄ (acidic) and LiAlH₄
B. PCC and Tollens' reagent
C. CrO₃ in anhydrous medium and K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄
D. K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ and Ag₂O/NH₄OH
Answer: C

The conversion of ethanol to ethanal (acetaldehyde) requires controlled oxidation, which is achieved using CrO₃ in anhydrous medium (PCC is also correct but not listed in the options). The further oxidation of ethanal to ethanoic acid (acetic acid) is done using a strong oxidizing agent like K₂Cr₂O₇ in acidic medium. Option C correctly identifies CrO₃ in anhydrous medium (P) and K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄ (Q). The other options either reverse the reagents or use incorrect ones for the given transformations.
