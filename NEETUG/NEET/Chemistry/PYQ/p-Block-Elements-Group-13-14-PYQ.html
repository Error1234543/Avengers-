This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed to reflect the style and difficulty of NEET questions on p-Block Elements (Group 13 & 14). Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate answer from the options provided.

1. Which of the following statements regarding boron is incorrect?

A. Boron forms stable covalent compounds due to its high ionization enthalpy.
B. Boron exhibits allotropy and exists in amorphous and crystalline forms.
C. Boron has a very low melting point compared to other Group 13 elements.
D. Boron does not react directly with hydrogen to form boranes.
Answer: C

Boron has a very high melting point (2300°C) due to its strong covalent network structure, unlike other Group 13 elements like aluminum, which have lower melting points. The other statements are correct: boron forms covalent compounds, exhibits allotropy, and does not directly react with hydrogen to form boranes.

2. The correct order of acidic strength among the following oxides is:

A. B₂O₃ < Al₂O₃ < Ga₂O₃ < In₂O₃
B. B₂O₃ > Al₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > In₂O₃
C. Al₂O₃ > B₂O₃ > Ga₂O₃ > In₂O₃
D. Ga₂O₃ > B₂O₃ > Al₂O₃ > In₂O₃
Answer: B

The acidic strength of Group 13 oxides decreases down the group due to the increasing metallic character. Boron oxide (B₂O₃) is the most acidic, followed by Al₂O₃, Ga₂O₃, and In₂O₃, which is amphoteric or basic.

3. The hybridization of boron in diborane (B₂H₆) is:

A. sp
B. sp²
C. sp³
D. sp³d
Answer: C

In diborane (B₂H₆), each boron atom is sp³ hybridized. The structure involves two bridging hydrogen atoms, forming banana bonds (3-center-2-electron bonds), which are characteristic of sp³ hybridization in boron.

4. Which of the following elements forms the most stable +2 oxidation state?

A. Boron
B. Aluminum
C. Gallium
D. Thallium
Answer: D

Thallium exhibits the inert pair effect most prominently in Group 13, making its +1 oxidation state more stable than +3. However, among the given options, thallium is the only element that commonly forms a stable +2 oxidation state (though +1 is more stable). The other elements primarily exhibit +3.

5. The correct statement about the structure of boric acid (H₃BO₃) is:

A. It is a strong tribasic acid.
B. It forms a layered structure with hydrogen bonding.
C. It exists as a discrete molecule in the solid state.
D. It does not react with alcohols to form esters.
Answer: B

Boric acid (H₃BO₃) forms a layered structure in the solid state, held together by hydrogen bonding. It is a weak monobasic acid (not tribasic) and reacts with alcohols to form borate esters. It does not exist as discrete molecules.

6. Which of the following is the correct order of atomic radii for Group 14 elements?

A. C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb
B. C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb
C. Si < C < Ge < Sn < Pb
D. Pb < Sn < Ge < Si < C
Answer: A

The atomic radii of Group 14 elements increase down the group due to the addition of new electron shells. The correct order is C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb.

7. The element that does NOT exhibit allotropy among the following is:

A. Carbon
B. Silicon
C. Germanium
D. Tin
Answer: C

Germanium does not exhibit allotropy. Carbon (diamond, graphite, graphene), silicon (crystalline and amorphous), and tin (gray tin, white tin) all exhibit allotropy.

8. Which of the following statements about carbon monoxide (CO) is correct?

A. It is a linear molecule with a triple bond between carbon and oxygen.
B. It is a strong oxidizing agent.
C. It does not form complexes with transition metals.
D. It is highly soluble in water.
Answer: A

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a linear molecule with a triple bond between carbon and oxygen (one sigma and two pi bonds). It is a strong reducing agent (not oxidizing), forms complexes with transition metals (e.g., metal carbonyls), and is only slightly soluble in water.

9. The correct order of bond angles in the hydrides of Group 14 elements is:

A. CH₄ > SiH₄ > GeH₄ > SnH₄
B. CH₄ < SiH₄ < GeH₄ < SnH₄
C. CH₄ = SiH₄ = GeH₄ = SnH₄
D. SiH₄ > CH₄ > GeH₄ > SnH₄
Answer: A

The bond angles in Group 14 hydrides decrease down the group due to decreasing s-character in the hybrid orbitals and increasing size of the central atom. The correct order is CH₄ (109.5°) > SiH₄ > GeH₄ > SnH₄.

10. Which of the following is the primary reason for the high melting point of silicon carbide (SiC)?

A. Ionic bonding
B. Metallic bonding
C. Covalent network structure
D. Van der Waals forces
Answer: C

Silicon carbide (SiC) has a very high melting point due to its covalent network structure, where each silicon atom is covalently bonded to four carbon atoms and vice versa, forming a rigid 3D lattice.

11. The correct statement about the inert pair effect in Group 14 is:

A. It becomes less pronounced down the group.
B. It is responsible for the stability of the +2 oxidation state in lead.
C. It is observed only in carbon and silicon.
D. It increases the tendency to form +4 oxidation state.
Answer: B

The inert pair effect becomes more pronounced down Group 14, leading to the increased stability of the +2 oxidation state in heavier elements like lead (Pb). This effect is due to the reluctance of the s-electrons in the outermost shell to participate in bonding.

12. The correct order of atomic radii for the elements Al, Ga, In, and Tl (Group 13) is:

A. Al < Ga < In < Tl
B. Ga < Al < In < Tl
C. Al < In < Ga < Tl
D. Ga < In < Al < Tl
Answer: B

The atomic radius generally increases down the group due to the addition of new electron shells. However, gallium (Ga) has a smaller atomic radius than aluminum (Al) due to the poor shielding effect of the 3d electrons in Ga, which increases the effective nuclear charge. Thus, the correct order is Ga < Al < In < Tl.

13. Which of the following compounds of boron is used as a mild antiseptic?

A. Borax
B. Boric acid
C. Boron nitride
D. Diborane
Answer: B

Boric acid (H₃BO₃) is commonly used as a mild antiseptic and insecticide. It is also used in the manufacture of heat-resistant borosilicate glass.

14. The hybridization of the central atom in AlCl₃ is:

A. sp³
B. sp²
C. sp
D. dsp²
Answer: B

In AlCl₃, aluminum (Al) is the central atom and forms three bonds with chlorine atoms. The molecule has a trigonal planar geometry, which corresponds to sp² hybridization.

15. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic property of carbon?

A. Catenation
B. Formation of multiple bonds
C. High electronegativity
D. Formation of ionic carbides
Answer: D

Carbon primarily forms covalent bonds due to its high electronegativity and small size. While it can form ionic carbides (e.g., CaC₂), this is not a characteristic property of carbon. Catenation, multiple bonding, and high electronegativity are key properties.

16. The correct order of stability of +2 oxidation state for Group 14 elements is:

A. C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb
B. Si < Ge < Sn < Pb < C
C. Pb < Sn < Ge < Si < C
D. Ge < Si < C < Sn < Pb
Answer: A

The stability of the +2 oxidation state increases down Group 14 due to the inert pair effect. This effect becomes more pronounced as the atomic size increases, making Pb²⁺ the most stable. The correct order is C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb.

17. Which of the following statements about silicones is correct?

A. Silicones are inorganic polymers with a backbone of Si-O-Si linkages.
B. Silicones are highly reactive and decompose easily in water.
C. Silicones are used as reducing agents in organic synthesis.
D. Silicones are soluble in water due to their polar nature.
Answer: A

Silicones are inorganic polymers with a backbone of alternating silicon and oxygen atoms (Si-O-Si). They are chemically inert, water-repellent, and thermally stable, making them useful in a variety of applications, including lubricants and sealants.

18. The compound used in the manufacture of optical glasses and as a drying agent is:

A. Silica
B. Silicon carbide
C. Silicon tetrachloride
D. Borax
Answer: A

Silica (SiO₂) is used in the manufacture of optical glasses due to its high transparency and thermal stability. It is also used as a drying agent in the form of silica gel.

19. Which of the following elements forms the most stable carbide?

A. Silicon
B. Carbon
C. Germanium
D. Tin
Answer: A

Silicon forms silicon carbide (SiC), which is an extremely hard and stable compound used as an abrasive and in high-temperature applications. Carbon itself does not form carbides; instead, it forms compounds with other elements.

20. The correct statement about the structure of diborane (B₂H₆) is:

A. It has a planar structure with all atoms in the same plane.
B. It contains two bridging hydrogen atoms and four terminal hydrogen atoms.
C. It has a linear structure with boron atoms at the ends.
D. It contains only terminal hydrogen atoms and no bridging hydrogens.
Answer: B

Diborane (B₂H₆) has a unique structure with two bridging hydrogen atoms that form three-center two-electron bonds with the boron atoms. The remaining four hydrogen atoms are terminal and form normal covalent bonds with boron.

21. The element that exhibits the highest catenation tendency in Group 14 is:

A. Carbon
B. Silicon
C. Germanium
D. Tin
Answer: A

Carbon exhibits the highest catenation tendency among Group 14 elements due to its small size, high bond energy (C-C bond), and ability to form strong covalent bonds with itself, leading to the formation of long chains and complex structures.

22. Which of the following statements regarding the diagonal relationship between boron (Group 13) and silicon (Group 14) is INCORRECT?

A. Both boron and silicon form covalent hydrides that are volatile and flammable in air.
B. Both boron and silicon exhibit allotropy and exist in amorphous as well as crystalline forms.
C. Both boron and silicon react with alkalies to liberate hydrogen gas and form borates/silicates.
D. Both boron and silicon have high melting points due to their strong ionic bonds in the solid state.
Answer: D

The diagonal relationship between boron and silicon is due to similar atomic and ionic sizes, electronegativities, and charge densities. Both form covalent hydrides (e.g., diborane and silanes), exhibit allotropy, and react with alkalies to liberate hydrogen. However, neither boron nor silicon forms ionic bonds in their solid states; their high melting points arise from strong covalent network structures, not ionic bonds. Thus, statement D is incorrect.
