PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Chemistry in Everyday Life (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed to reflect the style, difficulty, and coverage of NEET previous-year questions on the topic 'Chemistry in Everyday Life'. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Choose the most appropriate answer from the given options.

1. Which of the following is a broad-spectrum antibiotic commonly used to treat bacterial infections such as pneumonia and urinary tract infections?

A. Aspirin
B. Chloramphenicol
C. Paracetamol
D. Ibuprofen
Answer: B

Chloramphenicol is a broad-spectrum antibiotic effective against a wide range of bacteria, including those causing pneumonia and urinary tract infections. Aspirin and ibuprofen are non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), while paracetamol is an analgesic and antipyretic, not an antibiotic.

2. The class of drugs used to reduce pain without causing unconsciousness is known as:

A. Antipyretics
B. Analgesics
C. Antibiotics
D. Antiseptics
Answer: B

Analgesics are drugs specifically designed to relieve pain without inducing loss of consciousness. Antipyretics reduce fever, antibiotics treat bacterial infections, and antiseptics prevent the growth of disease-causing microorganisms.

3. Which of the following is NOT a mode of action of antacids?

A. Neutralizing excess stomach acid
B. Inhibiting the proton pump in gastric cells
C. Forming a protective layer over the stomach lining
D. Reducing the secretion of gastric acid
Answer: B

Antacids work by neutralizing excess stomach acid (e.g., sodium bicarbonate, magnesium hydroxide) or forming a protective layer (e.g., aluminum hydroxide). Inhibiting the proton pump is the mechanism of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) like omeprazole, not antacids.

4. Sulpha drugs are structural analogs of:

A. Para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA)
B. Folic acid
C. Penicillin
D. Tetracycline
Answer: A

Sulpha drugs (e.g., sulfanilamide) are structural analogs of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA). They competitively inhibit the bacterial enzyme responsible for synthesizing folic acid, thereby disrupting bacterial growth.

5. Which of the following is a synthetic detergent commonly used in toothpaste?

A. Sodium lauryl sulfate
B. Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide
C. Sodium stearate
D. Calcium carbonate
Answer: A

Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) is an anionic synthetic detergent widely used in toothpaste for its foaming and cleaning properties. Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide is a cationic detergent, sodium stearate is a soap, and calcium carbonate is an abrasive.

6. The drug that blocks the action of histamine, thereby reducing allergic symptoms, is classified as a/an:

A. Antihistamine
B. Antacid
C. Antibiotic
D. Antidepressant
Answer: A

Antihistamines (e.g., diphenhydramine, loratadine) block histamine receptors, alleviating allergic symptoms such as itching, sneezing, and runny nose. They do not neutralize stomach acid (antacids) or treat bacterial infections (antibiotics).

7. Which of the following is a non-narcotic analgesic?

A. Morphine
B. Codeine
C. Aspirin
D. Heroin
Answer: C

Aspirin is a non-narcotic analgesic, meaning it relieves pain without causing addiction or dependence. Morphine, codeine, and heroin are narcotic analgesics derived from opium and have high potential for abuse.

8. The artificial sweetener that is approximately 200 times sweeter than sucrose and is stable at cooking temperatures is:

A. Saccharin
B. Aspartame
C. Sucralose
D. Alitame
Answer: D

Alitame is an artificial sweetener about 2000 times sweeter than sucrose and remains stable at high temperatures, making it suitable for cooking. Saccharin is 300–400 times sweeter but less heat-stable. Aspartame loses sweetness when heated, and sucralose is 600 times sweeter but not as heat-stable as alitame.

9. Which of the following is a tranquilizer used to reduce anxiety and tension?

A. Equanil
B. Seconal
C. Luminal
D. Morphine
Answer: A

Equanil (meprobamate) is a tranquilizer used to relieve anxiety and tension. Seconal and Luminal are barbiturates used as sedatives or hypnotics, while morphine is a narcotic analgesic.

10. The substance added to soap to enhance its cleansing properties in hard water is called a:

A. Builder
B. Filler
C. Antioxidant
D. Preservative
Answer: A

Builders (e.g., sodium tripolyphosphate) are added to soaps and detergents to soften hard water by sequestering calcium and magnesium ions, thereby improving cleansing efficiency. Fillers add bulk, antioxidants prevent rancidity, and preservatives extend shelf life.

11. Which of the following is a narrow-spectrum antibiotic?

A. Penicillin G
B. Tetracycline
C. Chloramphenicol
D. Streptomycin
Answer: A

Penicillin G is a narrow-spectrum antibiotic, primarily effective against Gram-positive bacteria. Tetracycline, chloramphenicol, and streptomycin are broad-spectrum antibiotics, targeting a wider range of bacteria.

12. The class of drugs used to treat depression by increasing the levels of serotonin and norepinephrine in the brain is known as:

A. Benzodiazepines
B. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)
C. Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)
D. Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
Answer: C

Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) (e.g., imipramine) increase the levels of serotonin and norepinephrine by inhibiting their reuptake. SSRIs (e.g., fluoxetine) primarily target serotonin, benzodiazepines are anxiolytics, and MAOIs inhibit the enzyme monoamine oxidase.

13. Which of the following is a food preservative that inhibits the growth of molds and yeasts?

A. Sodium benzoate
B. Sodium metabisulfite
C. Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA)
D. Propionic acid
Answer: D

Propionic acid and its salts (e.g., calcium propionate) are food preservatives that inhibit the growth of molds and yeasts, commonly used in bread and baked goods. Sodium benzoate is effective against bacteria and fungi but less so for molds. Sodium metabisulfite is an antioxidant, and BHA is an antioxidant used to prevent rancidity.

14. Which of the following drugs is classified as a tranquilizer and is commonly used to treat anxiety and insomnia?

A. Equanil
B. Seconal
C. Luminal
D. Morphine
Answer: A

Equanil (meprobamate) is a well-known tranquilizer used to relieve anxiety and induce sleep. Seconal and Luminal are barbiturates (hypnotics), while morphine is a narcotic analgesic.

15. The artificial sweetener aspartame is unstable at cooking temperatures. Which of the following sweeteners is a suitable alternative for use in baked goods?

A. Saccharin
B. Sucralose
C. Alitame
D. Both B and C
Answer: D

Sucralose and alitame are heat-stable artificial sweeteners, making them suitable for cooking and baking. Saccharin is also heat-stable but has a bitter aftertaste, while aspartame degrades at high temperatures.

16. Which class of antibiotics is structurally characterized by a β-lactam ring and is commonly used to treat bacterial infections?

A. Tetracyclines
B. Penicillins
C. Macrolides
D. Aminoglycosides
Answer: B

Penicillins are β-lactam antibiotics, characterized by the presence of a β-lactam ring in their structure. They inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis. Tetracyclines, macrolides, and aminoglycosides have different mechanisms and structures.

17. Which of the following is NOT a mechanism of action for antimicrobial drugs?

A. Inhibition of cell wall synthesis
B. Inhibition of protein synthesis
C. Disruption of nucleic acid synthesis
D. Stimulation of host immune response
Answer: D

Antimicrobial drugs act by inhibiting bacterial processes such as cell wall synthesis, protein synthesis, or nucleic acid synthesis. They do not directly stimulate the host immune response, though some may modulate it indirectly.

18. Which of the following detergents is biodegradable and environmentally friendly?

A. Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS)
B. Linear alkylbenzene sulfonates (LAS)
C. Branched alkylbenzene sulfonates (ABS)
D. Both A and B
Answer: D

Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) and linear alkylbenzene sulfonates (LAS) are biodegradable detergents. Branched alkylbenzene sulfonates (ABS) are non-biodegradable and harmful to the environment.

19. Which of the following is a common side effect of prolonged use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like aspirin?

A. Peptic ulcers
B. Hypertension
C. Kidney damage
D. All of the above
Answer: D

Prolonged use of NSAIDs like aspirin can lead to peptic ulcers (due to irritation of the stomach lining), hypertension (due to fluid retention), and kidney damage (due to reduced blood flow to the kidneys).

20. Which of the following chemicals is used as a food preservative to prevent the growth of fungi and bacteria in acidic foods?

A. Sodium benzoate
B. Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA)
C. Sodium metabisulfite
D. Ascorbic acid
Answer: A

Sodium benzoate is a common food preservative used in acidic foods (e.g., soft drinks, pickles) to inhibit the growth of fungi and bacteria. BHA is an antioxidant, sodium metabisulfite is used as a preservative in wines and dried fruits, and ascorbic acid is a vitamin C supplement.

21. Which of the following is a broad-spectrum antibiotic effective against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria?

A. Penicillin G
B. Streptomycin
C. Tetracycline
D. Erythromycin
Answer: C

Tetracycline is a broad-spectrum antibiotic effective against a wide range of Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. Penicillin G is primarily effective against Gram-positive bacteria, streptomycin against Gram-negative bacteria, and erythromycin against Gram-positive bacteria.

22. Which of the following drugs is used as an antipyretic (fever reducer) and analgesic (pain reliever) but is NOT an anti-inflammatory agent?

A. Ibuprofen
B. Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
C. Aspirin
D. Naproxen
Answer: B

Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is an antipyretic and analgesic but has weak anti-inflammatory effects. Ibuprofen, aspirin, and naproxen are NSAIDs with anti-inflammatory properties.

23. Which of the following is a common example of a cationic detergent used in fabric softeners and hair conditioners?

A. Sodium lauryl sulfate
B. Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB)
C. Linear alkylbenzene sulfonate (LAS)
D. Sodium stearate
Answer: B

Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) is a cationic detergent commonly used in fabric softeners and hair conditioners. Sodium lauryl sulfate and LAS are anionic detergents, while sodium stearate is a soap.
