This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Coordination Compounds, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the correct option for each question.

1. The IUPAC name of the complex [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is:

A. Tetraamminedichlorocobalt(III) ion
B. Tetraamminedichlorocobaltate(III) ion
C. Diamminetetrachlorocobalt(III) ion
D. Tetraamminedichlorocobalt(II) ion
Answer: A

The complex [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ has cobalt in the +3 oxidation state. Ligands are named alphabetically: 'ammine' for NH₃ and 'chloro' for Cl⁻. The coordination sphere is cationic, so the metal name remains 'cobalt' with oxidation state in Roman numerals. The correct IUPAC name is tetraamminedichlorocobalt(III) ion.

2. The coordination number and oxidation state of cobalt in the complex K₃[Co(C₂O₄)₃] are:

A. 6 and +3
B. 4 and +2
C. 6 and +2
D. 4 and +3
Answer: A

The oxalate ligand (C₂O₄²⁻) is bidentate, so three oxalate ligands occupy 6 coordination sites. The complex is [Co(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻, so cobalt’s oxidation state is +3 (since K⁺ is +1 and the overall charge is -3). Coordination number is 6.

3. Which of the following is a chelating ligand?

A. NH₃
B. Cl⁻
C. H₂O
D. en (ethylenediamine)
Answer: D

A chelating ligand binds to the central metal ion through two or more donor atoms, forming a ring structure. Ethylenediamine (en) is a bidentate chelating ligand, while NH₃, Cl⁻, and H₂O are monodentate.

4. The correct order of stability for the following complexes in aqueous solution is: (I) [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, (II) [Co(en)₃]³⁺, (III) [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺

A. I < II < III
B. III < I < II
C. II < I < III
D. III < II < I
Answer: B

The stability of complexes follows the chelate effect: chelating ligands (like en) form more stable complexes than monodentate ligands (like NH₃ or H₂O). Among monodentate ligands, NH₃ forms more stable complexes than H₂O due to stronger ligand field. Thus, the order is [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺ < [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ < [Co(en)₃]³⁺.

5. The magnetic moment of [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ is zero. This is because:

A. Fe is in +2 oxidation state and all electrons are paired
B. Fe is in +3 oxidation state and all electrons are unpaired
C. CN⁻ is a weak-field ligand causing high spin complex
D. The complex is tetrahedral
Answer: A

In [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, Fe is in +2 oxidation state (d⁶ configuration). CN⁻ is a strong-field ligand, causing pairing of electrons in the t₂g orbitals, resulting in a diamagnetic complex with zero magnetic moment.

6. Which of the following complexes is optically active?

A. cis-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺
B. trans-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺
C. [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺
D. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺
Answer: A

Optical activity occurs in complexes that are chiral and lack a plane of symmetry. The cis isomer of [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ is optically active because it exists as a pair of non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers). The trans isomer is achiral.

7. The primary valence and secondary valence of cobalt in [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ are, respectively:

A. 3 and 6
B. 6 and 3
C. 3 and 3
D. 6 and 6
Answer: A

Primary valence refers to the oxidation state of the metal (Co is +3 in this complex). Secondary valence refers to the coordination number (6, as Co is bonded to 6 NH₃ ligands). Thus, primary valence is 3 and secondary valence is 6.

8. The correct formula of the complex formed when potassium hexacyanoferrate(II) reacts with iron(III) chloride is:

A. Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃
B. KFe[Fe(CN)₆]
C. Fe₃[Fe(CN)₆]₂
D. K₃Fe[Fe(CN)₆]
Answer: A

The reaction between K₄[Fe(CN)₆] (potassium hexacyanoferrate(II)) and FeCl₃ (iron(III) chloride) produces Prussian blue, which has the formula Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃. This is a well-known coordination compound used as a pigment.

9. Which of the following ligands can exhibit linkage isomerism?

A. NH₃
B. NO₂⁻
C. H₂O
D. Cl⁻
Answer: B

Linkage isomerism occurs when a ligand can bind to the central metal ion through different donor atoms. The nitrite ligand (NO₂⁻) can bind through nitrogen (nitro, -NO₂) or oxygen (nitrito, -ONO), exhibiting linkage isomerism.

10. The crystal field splitting energy (Δ₀) for [CoF₆]³⁻ is 18,200 cm⁻¹. The complex is:

A. High spin and paramagnetic
B. Low spin and diamagnetic
C. High spin and diamagnetic
D. Low spin and paramagnetic
Answer: A

F⁻ is a weak-field ligand, so the crystal field splitting energy (Δ₀) is small. For Co³⁺ (d⁶), this results in a high-spin complex with 4 unpaired electrons, making it paramagnetic. The given Δ₀ value is consistent with a weak-field ligand.

11. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the complex [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻?

A. It is square planar and diamagnetic.
B. It is tetrahedral and paramagnetic.
C. It is square planar and paramagnetic.
D. It is tetrahedral and diamagnetic.
Answer: A

The complex [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ involves Ni²⁺ (d⁸ configuration) with strong-field CN⁻ ligands, which cause pairing of electrons. This results in a square planar geometry and diamagnetic behavior due to the absence of unpaired electrons.

12. Which of the following complexes will show optical isomerism?

A. [Co(en)₃]³⁺
B. [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺
C. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺
D. [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺
Answer: A

Optical isomerism occurs in complexes that are chiral and lack a plane of symmetry. The complex [Co(en)₃]³⁺ has a non-superimposable mirror image due to the arrangement of the bidentate ethylenediamine (en) ligands, making it optically active.

13. Which of the following is a π-acceptor ligand?

A. CO
B. NH₃
C. H₂O
D. Cl⁻
Answer: A

π-acceptor ligands are those that can accept electron density from the metal into their empty π* orbitals. Carbon monoxide (CO) is a classic example of a π-acceptor ligand due to its ability to form π-backbonding with the metal.

14. Which of the following complexes is expected to have the highest crystal field splitting energy (Δ₀)?

A. [Co(CN)₆]³⁻
B. [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺
C. [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺
D. [CoF₆]³⁻
Answer: A

The crystal field splitting energy (Δ₀) depends on the strength of the ligand. CN⁻ is a strong-field ligand, causing greater splitting compared to NH₃, H₂O, and F⁻. Thus, [Co(CN)₆]³⁻ has the highest Δ₀.

15. The complex [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] exists in two isomeric forms. These isomers are an example of:

A. Geometric isomerism
B. Optical isomerism
C. Linkage isomerism
D. Ionization isomerism
Answer: A

The complex [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] exhibits geometric isomerism because the ligands can be arranged in either a cis or trans configuration around the central platinum ion, leading to two distinct isomers.

16. The coordination number and oxidation state of cobalt in the complex [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl are respectively:

A. 6 and +3
B. 4 and +2
C. 6 and +2
D. 4 and +3
Answer: A

In the complex [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl, the cobalt ion is surrounded by four NH₃ molecules and two Cl⁻ ions as ligands, giving a coordination number of 6. The overall charge of the coordination sphere [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is +1 (since one Cl⁻ acts as a counter ion). NH₃ is a neutral ligand, and each Cl⁻ ligand contributes -1 charge. Let the oxidation state of cobalt be x. Then, x + 4(0) + 2(-1) = +1 → x - 2 = +1 → x = +3. Thus, the oxidation state of cobalt is +3, and the coordination number is 6.
