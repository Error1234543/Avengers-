PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed to match the style, difficulty, and coverage of NEET previous-year questions on Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. Which of the following reagents is used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols via the Lucas test?

A. Concentrated HCl and ZnCl₂
B. Dilute H₂SO₄ and KMnO₄
C. Bromine water
D. FeCl₃ solution
Answer: A

The Lucas test uses concentrated HCl and anhydrous ZnCl₂ (Lucas reagent) to distinguish alcohols based on the rate of formation of alkyl chlorides. Tertiary alcohols react immediately (turbidity), secondary alcohols take 5–10 minutes, and primary alcohols do not react at room temperature.

2. The compound that gives a positive iodoform test but does not reduce Tollens' reagent is:

A. Ethanol
B. Methanol
C. Propanone
D. Propanal
Answer: A

Ethanol contains the CH₃CH(OH)– group and gives a positive iodoform test (yellow precipitate of CHI₃). However, it does not reduce Tollens' reagent because it is a primary alcohol, not an aldehyde.

3. The major product formed when tert-butyl methyl ether is heated with HI is:

A. tert-Butyl alcohol and methyl iodide
B. tert-Butyl iodide and methanol
C. Isobutylene and methanol
D. tert-Butyl alcohol and methane
Answer: B

Cleavage of ethers with HI follows the Sₙ1 mechanism for tertiary alkyl groups. The tert-butyl group forms a stable carbocation, leading to tert-butyl iodide, while the methyl group forms methanol.

4. Which of the following compounds will exhibit the highest boiling point?

A. Ethanol
B. Dimethyl ether
C. Propan-1-ol
D. Methoxymethane
Answer: C

Propan-1-ol has the highest molecular weight among the options and forms hydrogen bonds, leading to a higher boiling point compared to ethanol and ethers, which lack hydrogen bonding (or have weaker hydrogen bonding in the case of ethanol).

5. The reaction of phenol with bromine water yields:

A. 2-Bromophenol
B. 3-Bromophenol
C. 2,4,6-Tribromophenol
D. 4-Bromophenol
Answer: C

Phenol undergoes electrophilic bromination readily due to the activating –OH group. In bromine water, it forms a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol due to substitution at the ortho and para positions.

6. Which of the following is the correct order of acidity among the given compounds?

A. Phenol > p-Nitrophenol > p-Cresol
B. p-Cresol > Phenol > p-Nitrophenol
C. p-Nitrophenol > Phenol > p-Cresol
D. Phenol > p-Cresol > p-Nitrophenol
Answer: C

The acidity of phenols is influenced by substituents. The nitro group (–NO₂) is electron-withdrawing and increases acidity, while the methyl group (–CH₃) is electron-donating and decreases acidity. Thus, p-nitrophenol is the most acidic, followed by phenol, and p-cresol is the least acidic.

7. The compound used as an antiseptic and known as 'carbolic acid' is:

A. Benzyl alcohol
B. Phenol
C. Glycerol
D. Ethylene glycol
Answer: B

Phenol, also known as carbolic acid, is widely used as an antiseptic and disinfectant due to its ability to denature proteins and disrupt cell membranes of microorganisms.

8. Which of the following reactions is used to prepare diethyl ether from ethanol?

A. Dehydration of ethanol at 140°C in the presence of H₂SO₄
B. Oxidation of ethanol with KMnO₄
C. Reduction of ethanol with LiAlH₄
D. Reaction of ethanol with PCl₅
Answer: A

Diethyl ether is prepared by the intermolecular dehydration of ethanol at 140°C in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄. At higher temperatures (170°C), ethylene is formed instead due to intramolecular dehydration.

9. The compound that does not react with sodium metal is:

A. Ethanol
B. Phenol
C. Diethyl ether
D. Glycerol
Answer: C

Diethyl ether does not contain an –OH group and thus does not react with sodium metal. Ethanol, phenol, and glycerol all contain –OH groups and react with sodium to liberate hydrogen gas.

10. The IUPAC name of the compound CH₃-O-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃ is:

A. 1-Methoxypropane
B. 2-Methoxypropane
C. Methoxyethane
D. 1-Ethoxypropane
Answer: A

The compound CH₃-O-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃ is an ether. The longest carbon chain attached to the oxygen is propane, and the methoxy group is attached to the first carbon. Thus, the IUPAC name is 1-methoxypropane.

11. The reaction of ethanol with concentrated sulfuric acid at 140°C produces:

A. Ethene
B. Diethyl ether
C. Ethyl hydrogen sulfate
D. Acetaldehyde
Answer: B

At 140°C, ethanol undergoes intermolecular dehydration in the presence of concentrated sulfuric acid to form diethyl ether. At higher temperatures (170°C), it forms ethene via intramolecular dehydration.

12. The compound obtained by the oxidation of secondary alcohols is:

A. Aldehyde
B. Ketone
C. Carboxylic acid
D. Ester
Answer: B

Secondary alcohols undergo oxidation to form ketones. Primary alcohols oxidize to aldehydes and further to carboxylic acids, while tertiary alcohols do not undergo oxidation under normal conditions.

13. Which of the following ethers is most likely to undergo cleavage with HI?

A. CH₃-O-CH₃
B. CH₃-O-C₂H₅
C. C₂H₅-O-C₂H₅
D. C₆H₅-O-CH₃
Answer: D

Ethers with aryl groups (e.g., C₆H₅-O-CH₃) undergo cleavage with HI to form phenol and an alkyl iodide. This is because the C-O bond in aryl alkyl ethers is weaker due to resonance stabilization of the phenol.

14. The Williamson ether synthesis involves the reaction of:

A. An alcohol with a carboxylic acid
B. An alkoxide ion with an alkyl halide
C. An alkene with an alcohol
D. A phenol with a ketone
Answer: B

The Williamson ether synthesis is a nucleophilic substitution reaction where an alkoxide ion (from an alcohol) reacts with an alkyl halide to form an ether. It is a key method for preparing unsymmetrical ethers.

15. Which of the following is the correct product of the reaction between phenol and chloroform in the presence of NaOH?

A. Salicylaldehyde
B. Benzaldehyde
C. p-Hydroxybenzaldehyde
D. o-Chlorophenol
Answer: A

The reaction of phenol with chloroform in the presence of NaOH is known as the Reimer-Tiemann reaction. It introduces an aldehyde group (-CHO) at the ortho position of phenol, forming salicylaldehyde.

16. The compound CH₃-CH₂-O-CH₂-CH₃ is classified as:

A. A symmetrical ether
B. An unsymmetrical ether
C. A mixed ether
D. A cyclic ether
Answer: A

The compound CH₃-CH₂-O-CH₂-CH₃ (diethyl ether) is a symmetrical ether because both alkyl groups attached to the oxygen atom are identical (ethyl groups).

17. Which of the following reagents is most suitable for the conversion of phenol to 2,4,6-tribromophenol?

A. Br₂ in CS₂ at 0°C
B. Br₂ in water at room temperature
C. Br₂ in glacial acetic acid
D. Br₂ in the presence of FeBr₃
Answer: B

Phenol undergoes bromination readily due to the activating effect of the hydroxyl group. When phenol is treated with bromine water (Br₂ in water) at room temperature, it results in the formation of 2,4,6-tribromophenol as a white precipitate. This reaction occurs due to the high electron density at the ortho and para positions of the phenol ring, which facilitates electrophilic substitution. The other reagents either do not provide the necessary conditions (e.g., CS₂ or glacial acetic acid) or lead to different products (e.g., FeBr₃ catalyzes bromination at the meta position for benzene, not phenol).

18. The correct order of acidity among the following compounds is: (I) p-Nitrophenol (II) Phenol (III) p-Cresol (IV) p-Chlorophenol

A. (I) > (IV) > (II) > (III)
B. (IV) > (I) > (II) > (III)
C. (I) > (II) > (IV) > (III)
D. (II) > (IV) > (I) > (III)
Answer: A

Acidity in phenols is influenced by the nature of substituents on the benzene ring. Electron-withdrawing groups (e.g., -NO₂, -Cl) increase acidity by stabilizing the phenoxide ion through resonance or inductive effects, while electron-donating groups (e.g., -CH₃) decrease acidity. p-Nitrophenol (I) is the most acidic due to the strong -R and -I effects of the nitro group. p-Chlorophenol (IV) is next due to the -I effect of chlorine, followed by phenol (II) itself. p-Cresol (III) is the least acidic due to the electron-donating methyl group. Thus, the correct order is (I) > (IV) > (II) > (III).

19. An organic compound 'X' with molecular formula C₄H₁₀O reacts with sodium metal to evolve hydrogen gas. It does not react with Lucas reagent at room temperature but gives a positive iodoform test. The compound 'X' is most likely:

A. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol
B. Butan-1-ol
C. Butan-2-ol
D. 2-Methylpropan-1-ol
Answer: C

The molecular formula C₄H₁₀O suggests 'X' is an alcohol. Reaction with sodium metal to evolve hydrogen gas confirms the presence of an -OH group. The compound does not react with Lucas reagent at room temperature, indicating it is a primary or secondary alcohol (tertiary alcohols react immediately). A positive iodoform test indicates the presence of a CH₃-CH(OH)- group, which is characteristic of secondary alcohols like butan-2-ol. 2-Methylpropan-2-ol (A) is tertiary and would react immediately with Lucas reagent. Butan-1-ol (B) and 2-methylpropan-1-ol (D) are primary alcohols and do not give a positive iodoform test. Thus, 'X' is butan-2-ol.
