PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Biomolecules (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Biomolecules, styled after NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following is NOT a monosaccharide?

A. Glucose
B. Fructose
C. Sucrose
D. Galactose
Answer: C

Sucrose is a disaccharide composed of glucose and fructose. Monosaccharides are the simplest form of carbohydrates and cannot be hydrolyzed further. Glucose, fructose, and galactose are all monosaccharides.

2. The peptide bond in proteins is formed between which of the following groups?

A. Two amino groups
B. Two carboxyl groups
C. An amino group and a carboxyl group
D. An amino group and a hydroxyl group
Answer: C

A peptide bond is formed between the carboxyl group (-COOH) of one amino acid and the amino group (-NH2) of another amino acid, resulting in the release of a water molecule (dehydration synthesis).

3. Which of the following statements about starch and cellulose is correct?

A. Both are polymers of glucose and have the same glycosidic linkages.
B. Starch is a polymer of α-glucose, while cellulose is a polymer of β-glucose.
C. Cellulose is digestible by humans, while starch is not.
D. Both starch and cellulose are used as structural components in plant cells.
Answer: B

Starch is a polymer of α-glucose with α-1,4 and α-1,6 glycosidic linkages, making it digestible by humans. Cellulose is a polymer of β-glucose with β-1,4 glycosidic linkages, which humans cannot digest due to the lack of the enzyme cellulase.

4. The secondary structure of a protein is primarily stabilized by:

A. Peptide bonds
B. Hydrogen bonds
C. Disulfide bonds
D. Ionic bonds
Answer: B

The secondary structure of proteins, such as α-helices and β-pleated sheets, is stabilized by hydrogen bonds formed between the amino hydrogen and carboxyl oxygen atoms of the peptide backbone.

5. Which of the following is a non-reducing sugar?

A. Maltose
B. Lactose
C. Sucrose
D. Glucose
Answer: C

Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar because the glycosidic bond is formed between the reducing ends of glucose and fructose, leaving no free aldehyde or ketone group to act as a reducing agent.

6. The nitrogenous base found in RNA but not in DNA is:

A. Adenine
B. Guanine
C. Uracil
D. Cytosine
Answer: C

RNA contains uracil instead of thymine, which is found in DNA. Adenine, guanine, and cytosine are common to both DNA and RNA.

7. Which of the following vitamins is water-soluble?

A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin C
C. Vitamin D
D. Vitamin K
Answer: B

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is a water-soluble vitamin. Vitamins A, D, and K are fat-soluble vitamins.

8. The process by which a protein loses its native structure and becomes biologically inactive is called:

A. Denaturation
B. Hydrolysis
C. Polymerization
D. Deamination
Answer: A

Denaturation refers to the loss of a protein's native structure due to external stressors such as heat, pH changes, or chemical agents, leading to loss of biological activity. Hydrolysis involves breaking peptide bonds, while deamination is the removal of an amino group.

9. Which of the following is a storage polysaccharide in animals?

A. Starch
B. Cellulose
C. Glycogen
D. Chitin
Answer: C

Glycogen is the primary storage polysaccharide in animals, stored in the liver and muscles. Starch is the storage polysaccharide in plants, cellulose is a structural polysaccharide in plants, and chitin is a structural polysaccharide in the exoskeletons of arthropods.

10. The bond formed between two nucleotides in a nucleic acid is called:

A. Peptide bond
B. Glycosidic bond
C. Phosphodiester bond
D. Hydrogen bond
Answer: C

A phosphodiester bond is formed between the phosphate group of one nucleotide and the hydroxyl group of the sugar of another nucleotide, linking them together in a nucleic acid chain. Peptide bonds are found in proteins, glycosidic bonds in carbohydrates, and hydrogen bonds stabilize secondary structures.

11. Which of the following amino acids contains a sulfur atom?

A. Alanine
B. Cysteine
C. Valine
D. Phenylalanine
Answer: B

Cysteine contains a sulfur atom in its side chain (thiol group, -SH). This sulfur atom plays a crucial role in forming disulfide bonds, which stabilize protein structures. Alanine, valine, and phenylalanine do not contain sulfur.

12. The enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of starch into maltose is:

A. Lipase
B. Amylase
C. Protease
D. Cellulase
Answer: B

Amylase is the enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of starch into maltose. Lipase breaks down lipids, protease breaks down proteins, and cellulase breaks down cellulose.

13. Which of the following is NOT a function of lipids in biological systems?

A. Energy storage
B. Structural component of cell membranes
C. Catalysis of biochemical reactions
D. Insulation and protection of organs
Answer: C

Lipids serve as energy storage (e.g., triglycerides), structural components of cell membranes (e.g., phospholipids), and insulation/protection (e.g., adipose tissue). However, they do not catalyze biochemical reactions; enzymes (proteins or RNA) perform this role.

14. In DNA, adenine pairs with thymine through how many hydrogen bonds?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4
Answer: B

Adenine (A) pairs with thymine (T) in DNA via two hydrogen bonds, while guanine (G) pairs with cytosine (C) via three hydrogen bonds. This complementary base pairing is crucial for DNA replication and transcription.

15. Which of the following vitamins is water-soluble and acts as a coenzyme in redox reactions?

A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin C
C. Vitamin D
D. Vitamin K
Answer: B

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is water-soluble and acts as a coenzyme in redox reactions, such as the synthesis of collagen. Vitamins A, D, and K are fat-soluble and have different functions (e.g., vision, calcium absorption, blood clotting).

16. The secondary structure of a protein is stabilized primarily by:

A. Peptide bonds
B. Disulfide bonds
C. Hydrogen bonds
D. Ionic bonds
Answer: C

The secondary structure of proteins (e.g., α-helices and β-pleated sheets) is stabilized primarily by hydrogen bonds between the amino hydrogen and carboxyl oxygen atoms of the peptide backbone. Peptide bonds link amino acids, disulfide bonds stabilize tertiary/quaternary structures, and ionic bonds are less common in secondary structure.

17. Which of the following carbohydrates is a non-reducing sugar?

A. Glucose
B. Fructose
C. Sucrose
D. Lactose
Answer: C

Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar because its glycosidic bond is formed between the anomeric carbons of glucose and fructose, leaving no free aldehyde or ketone group to act as a reducing agent. Glucose, fructose, and lactose are reducing sugars.

18. The process by which a protein loses its native structure and function is called:

A. Denaturation
B. Hydrolysis
C. Polymerization
D. Deamination
Answer: A

Denaturation refers to the loss of a protein's native structure (and thus function) due to external stressors like heat, pH changes, or chemicals. Hydrolysis breaks peptide bonds, polymerization links monomers, and deamination removes amino groups.

19. Which of the following is a pyrimidine base found in RNA but not in DNA?

A. Adenine
B. Guanine
C. Uracil
D. Thymine
Answer: C

Uracil is a pyrimidine base found in RNA but not in DNA, where thymine replaces it. Adenine and guanine are purines found in both DNA and RNA.

20. The bond formed between the glycerol and fatty acids in a triglyceride is called:

A. Peptide bond
B. Glycosidic bond
C. Ester bond
D. Phosphodiester bond
Answer: C

Triglycerides are formed by ester bonds between the hydroxyl groups of glycerol and the carboxyl groups of fatty acids. Peptide bonds link amino acids, glycosidic bonds link sugars, and phosphodiester bonds link nucleotides.

21. Which of the following statements correctly describes the structural difference between starch and cellulose?

A. Starch is a linear polymer of α-D-glucose, while cellulose is a branched polymer of β-D-glucose.
B. Starch contains α-1,4-glycosidic linkages with occasional α-1,6-branches, while cellulose contains only β-1,4-glycosidic linkages in a linear arrangement.
C. Cellulose is a storage polysaccharide in plants, while starch is a structural polysaccharide in the cell wall.
D. Starch and cellulose are both polymers of β-D-glucose but differ in the type of glycosidic linkages.
Answer: B

Starch is a storage polysaccharide in plants and consists of two components: amylose (linear polymer with α-1,4-glycosidic linkages) and amylopectin (branched polymer with α-1,4 and α-1,6-glycosidic linkages). Cellulose, on the other hand, is a structural polysaccharide in plant cell walls and is composed of linear chains of β-D-glucose units linked by β-1,4-glycosidic bonds. This structural difference makes cellulose indigestible by humans, while starch is digestible. Option B correctly captures this distinction.
