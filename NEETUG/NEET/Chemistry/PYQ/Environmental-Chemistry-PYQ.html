This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed to reflect the style, difficulty, and coverage of actual NEET Environmental Chemistry questions. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer, consistent with NEET's marking scheme. Answer all questions based on the standard NEET syllabus for Environmental Chemistry.

1. Which of the following is the primary greenhouse gas responsible for global warming?

A. Carbon monoxide (CO)
B. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
C. Methane (CH₄)
D. Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
Answer: B

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the primary greenhouse gas contributing to global warming due to its high concentration in the atmosphere and its ability to trap infrared radiation. While methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O) are also potent greenhouse gases, CO₂ is the most significant in terms of overall impact due to human activities like burning fossil fuels and deforestation.

2. The process of eutrophication in water bodies is primarily caused by the excessive presence of:

A. Heavy metals like lead and mercury
B. Nutrients like nitrates and phosphates
C. Chlorinated hydrocarbons
D. Suspended particulate matter
Answer: B

Eutrophication is the enrichment of water bodies with nutrients, primarily nitrates and phosphates, which leads to excessive growth of algae and other aquatic plants. This process depletes oxygen in the water, harming aquatic life. Heavy metals and chlorinated hydrocarbons cause pollution but are not the primary cause of eutrophication.

3. Which of the following pollutants is NOT a primary pollutant?

A. Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)
B. Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)
C. Ozone (O₃)
D. Carbon monoxide (CO)
Answer: C

Ozone (O₃) is a secondary pollutant formed by the reaction of primary pollutants like nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in the presence of sunlight. Primary pollutants, such as SO₂, NO₂, and CO, are directly emitted from sources like vehicles and industrial processes.

4. The pH of normal rainwater is approximately 5.6 due to the dissolution of:

A. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
B. Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)
C. Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)
D. Ammonia (NH₃)
Answer: A

Normal rainwater is slightly acidic (pH ~5.6) due to the dissolution of atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO₂) to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃). Acid rain, which has a pH below 5.6, is caused by the dissolution of sulphur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), leading to the formation of sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃).

5. Which of the following is a major source of particulate matter (PM) in urban areas?

A. Volcanic eruptions
B. Automobile exhaust
C. Ocean spray
D. Photosynthesis in plants
Answer: B

In urban areas, automobile exhaust is a major source of particulate matter (PM), including fine particles (PM₂.₅) and coarse particles (PM₁₀). These particles are emitted from the combustion of fossil fuels in vehicles. While volcanic eruptions and ocean spray can contribute to PM, they are not the primary sources in urban environments.

6. Which of the following statements about photochemical smog is correct?

A. It is formed in the presence of sunlight and involves the reaction of NOₓ and VOCs.
B. It primarily consists of sulphur dioxide and particulate matter.
C. It is most common in rural areas with low vehicle traffic.
D. It does not affect human health but damages vegetation.
Answer: A

Photochemical smog is formed in the presence of sunlight when nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) react to produce ozone (O₃) and other secondary pollutants. It is most common in urban areas with high vehicle traffic and can cause respiratory issues in humans and damage to vegetation.

7. The BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) of a water sample is a measure of:

A. The amount of dissolved oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter
B. The concentration of heavy metals in the water
C. The pH level of the water
D. The salinity of the water
Answer: A

Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) is a measure of the amount of dissolved oxygen required by aerobic microorganisms to decompose organic matter in a water sample. It is an important indicator of water quality, as high BOD levels indicate high organic pollution, which can deplete oxygen and harm aquatic life.

8. Which of the following is NOT a consequence of acid rain?

A. Corrosion of buildings and monuments
B. Increase in the pH of soil and water bodies
C. Damage to aquatic ecosystems
D. Leaching of heavy metals into water sources
Answer: B

Acid rain decreases the pH of soil and water bodies, making them more acidic. This can lead to the corrosion of buildings and monuments, damage to aquatic ecosystems, and the leaching of heavy metals like aluminum into water sources. An increase in pH is not a consequence of acid rain.

9. The term 'bioaccumulation' refers to:

A. The process by which pollutants are broken down by microorganisms
B. The gradual build-up of substances, such as pesticides, in the tissues of an organism
C. The release of pollutants into the atmosphere
D. The movement of pollutants from water to soil
Answer: B

Bioaccumulation is the process by which substances, such as pesticides or heavy metals, gradually accumulate in the tissues of an organism over time. This occurs when the rate of intake of a substance exceeds the organism's ability to metabolize or excrete it. Bioaccumulation can lead to toxic effects in organisms, particularly at higher trophic levels in the food chain.

10. The process of 'denitrification' in the nitrogen cycle involves:

A. The conversion of ammonia into nitrates by nitrifying bacteria
B. The conversion of nitrates into nitrogen gas by denitrifying bacteria
C. The fixation of atmospheric nitrogen by leguminous plants
D. The uptake of nitrates by plants for protein synthesis
Answer: B

Denitrification is the process by which denitrifying bacteria convert nitrates (NO₃⁻) into nitrogen gas (N₂), which is released back into the atmosphere. This process is a crucial part of the nitrogen cycle, as it helps maintain the balance of nitrogen in ecosystems. The other options describe different stages of the nitrogen cycle, such as nitrification (A), nitrogen fixation (C), and assimilation (D).

11. Which of the following is the primary cause of photochemical smog?

A. High concentration of nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds in the presence of sunlight
B. Excessive emission of carbon dioxide from vehicles
C. Industrial release of sulfur dioxide and particulate matter
D. Combustion of fossil fuels in power plants
Answer: A

Photochemical smog is formed when nitrogen oxides (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) react in the presence of sunlight, leading to the formation of ozone and other secondary pollutants. This is distinct from classical smog, which is caused by sulfur dioxide and particulate matter.

12. Which of the following gases is NOT a greenhouse gas?

A. Carbon dioxide (CO2)
B. Methane (CH4)
C. Nitrogen (N2)
D. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
Answer: C

Nitrogen (N2) is not a greenhouse gas because it does not absorb infrared radiation effectively. Greenhouse gases like CO2, CH4, and CFCs trap heat in the atmosphere, contributing to global warming.

13. The depletion of the ozone layer is primarily caused by:

A. Carbon dioxide emissions from vehicles
B. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and halons
C. Sulfur dioxide from industrial processes
D. Methane from agricultural activities
Answer: B

Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and halons release chlorine and bromine atoms in the stratosphere, which catalytically destroy ozone molecules. This leads to the thinning of the ozone layer, particularly over the polar regions.

14. Which of the following is a secondary pollutant?

A. Carbon monoxide (CO)
B. Sulfur dioxide (SO2)
C. Ozone (O3)
D. Nitrogen dioxide (NO2)
Answer: C

Ozone (O3) is a secondary pollutant because it is not directly emitted into the atmosphere. Instead, it is formed by the reaction of primary pollutants like nitrogen oxides (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in the presence of sunlight.

15. The pH of acid rain is typically:

A. Above 7.0
B. Between 5.6 and 7.0
C. Below 5.6
D. Exactly 7.0
Answer: C

Acid rain is defined as precipitation with a pH below 5.6. It is caused by the dissolution of sulfur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen oxides (NOx) in water, forming sulfuric acid and nitric acid, which lower the pH of rainwater.

16. Which of the following is a major source of indoor air pollution in urban households?

A. Radon gas from building materials
B. Carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion of biomass fuels
C. Ozone from photocopy machines
D. All of the above
Answer: D

Indoor air pollution can arise from multiple sources, including radon gas from building materials, carbon monoxide from biomass fuels, and ozone from office equipment like photocopy machines. These pollutants can have significant health impacts.

17. Which of the following statements about biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) is correct?

A. BOD is a measure of the amount of oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water
B. High BOD indicates clean water with low organic pollution
C. BOD is unaffected by the presence of inorganic pollutants
D. BOD is measured in parts per million (ppm) of carbon dioxide
Answer: A

Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) is a measure of the amount of dissolved oxygen required by aerobic microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water. High BOD values indicate high levels of organic pollution, which can deplete oxygen and harm aquatic life.

18. Which of the following is a consequence of global warming?

A. Increase in the pH of ocean water
B. Rise in sea levels due to thermal expansion and melting of glaciers
C. Decrease in the frequency of extreme weather events
D. Expansion of the ozone layer
Answer: B

Global warming leads to a rise in sea levels due to the thermal expansion of seawater and the melting of glaciers and ice caps. This poses a significant threat to coastal communities and ecosystems.

19. The presence of which of the following in water can cause 'blue baby syndrome' (methemoglobinemia)?

A. Lead (Pb)
B. Nitrates (NO3-)
C. Arsenic (As)
D. Mercury (Hg)
Answer: B

Blue baby syndrome, or methemoglobinemia, is caused by the presence of high levels of nitrates in drinking water. Nitrates are converted to nitrites in the body, which oxidize hemoglobin to methemoglobin, reducing its oxygen-carrying capacity.

20. Which of the following statements correctly describes the role of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) in environmental chemistry?

A. CFCs react with ozone in the troposphere to produce oxygen, leading to ozone depletion in the stratosphere.
B. CFCs are stable in the troposphere but dissociate in the stratosphere under UV radiation, releasing chlorine atoms that catalyze ozone depletion.
C. CFCs directly absorb infrared radiation, contributing significantly to global warming without affecting the ozone layer.
D. CFCs are broken down by water vapor in the troposphere, releasing chlorine atoms that immediately destroy ozone molecules.
Answer: B

Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are stable compounds in the troposphere but migrate to the stratosphere, where they are broken down by ultraviolet (UV) radiation. This process releases chlorine atoms, which catalyze the breakdown of ozone (O₃) into oxygen (O₂), leading to ozone layer depletion. This mechanism is well-documented and is the primary reason for the phase-out of CFCs under the Montreal Protocol. Options A, C, and D are incorrect because CFCs do not react with ozone in the troposphere, do not primarily contribute to global warming via infrared absorption, and are not broken down by water vapor in the troposphere.
