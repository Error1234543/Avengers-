This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Haloalkanes and Haloarenes, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET Chemistry. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following haloalkanes will undergo Sₙ2 reaction most readily?

A. 2-Bromo-2-methylpropane
B. 1-Bromobutane
C. 2-Bromobutane
D. 2-Bromo-3-methylbutane
Answer: B

Sₙ2 reactions are favored by primary haloalkanes due to less steric hindrance around the carbon atom bonded to the halogen. 1-Bromobutane is a primary haloalkane, making it the most reactive in Sₙ2 reactions among the given options.

2. The reaction of chlorobenzene with sodium hydroxide at high temperature and pressure yields:

A. Phenol
B. Benzyl alcohol
C. Anisole
D. Benzaldehyde
Answer: A

Chlorobenzene undergoes nucleophilic aromatic substitution with sodium hydroxide at high temperature (around 300°C) and pressure to form phenol. This is known as the Dow process.

3. Which of the following is the correct order of reactivity of alkyl halides towards β-elimination (E2) reactions?

A. R-F > R-Cl > R-Br > R-I
B. R-I > R-Br > R-Cl > R-F
C. R-Cl > R-Br > R-I > R-F
D. R-Br > R-I > R-Cl > R-F
Answer: B

The reactivity of alkyl halides in E2 reactions follows the order R-I > R-Br > R-Cl > R-F. This is because the leaving group ability increases down the group in the periodic table, with iodide being the best leaving group.

4. The compound formed by the reaction of bromoethane with silver nitrite is:

A. Nitroethane
B. Ethyl nitrite
C. A mixture of nitroethane and ethyl nitrite
D. Ethyl nitrate
Answer: C

Bromoethane reacts with silver nitrite to form a mixture of nitroethane (C₂H₅NO₂) and ethyl nitrite (C₂H₅ONO). This is due to the ambident nature of the nitrite ion, which can attack through nitrogen or oxygen.

5. Which of the following statements about the Fittig reaction is correct?

A. It involves the reaction of an aryl halide with an alkyl halide in the presence of sodium metal.
B. It is used to prepare alkylbenzenes from aryl halides and sodium metal.
C. It results in the formation of a new carbon-carbon bond between two aryl halides.
D. It is a nucleophilic substitution reaction.
Answer: C

The Fittig reaction involves the coupling of two aryl halides in the presence of sodium metal to form a biaryl compound, creating a new carbon-carbon bond between the aryl groups.

6. The major product formed when 2-chlorobutane is treated with alcoholic KOH is:

A. But-1-ene
B. But-2-ene (major) and But-1-ene (minor)
C. Butan-2-ol
D. Butan-1-ol
Answer: B

Treatment of 2-chlorobutane with alcoholic KOH results in a β-elimination (E2) reaction, producing a mixture of alkenes. But-2-ene is the major product due to the formation of the more stable alkene (Zaitsev's rule).

7. Which of the following is the correct IUPAC name for the compound CH₃-CH(Br)-CH₂-CH(Cl)-CH₃?

A. 2-Bromo-4-chloropentane
B. 4-Chloro-2-bromopentane
C. 2-Bromo-3-chloropentane
D. 3-Chloro-2-bromopentane
Answer: A

The IUPAC naming rules prioritize the halogen with the lower locant number. Here, bromine is at position 2 and chlorine at position 4, so the correct name is 2-Bromo-4-chloropentane.

8. The reaction of tert-butyl bromide with aqueous NaOH primarily yields:

A. tert-Butyl alcohol via Sₙ1 mechanism
B. Isobutylene via E2 mechanism
C. tert-Butyl alcohol via Sₙ2 mechanism
D. Isobutylene via Sₙ1 mechanism
Answer: A

Tert-butyl bromide, being a tertiary alkyl halide, undergoes substitution via the Sₙ1 mechanism with aqueous NaOH to form tert-butyl alcohol. Elimination (E2) is less favored in aqueous conditions.

9. Which of the following reagents can be used to distinguish between chlorobenzene and benzyl chloride?

A. Aqueous NaOH
B. Alcoholic AgNO₃
C. Bromine water
D. Fehling's solution
Answer: B

Benzyl chloride is more reactive due to the stability of the benzyl carbocation and will react with alcoholic AgNO₃ to form a white precipitate of AgCl. Chlorobenzene does not react under these conditions.

10. The correct order of boiling points of the following compounds is: (1) CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl, (2) CH₃CH₂CH₂Br, (3) CH₃CH₂CH₂I

A. 1 < 2 < 3
B. 3 < 2 < 1
C. 2 < 1 < 3
D. 1 < 3 < 2
Answer: A

The boiling points of haloalkanes increase with the size of the halogen due to increased van der Waals forces. Thus, the order is CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl < CH₃CH₂CH₂Br < CH₃CH₂CH₂I.

11. The major product formed in the following reaction is: CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-Br + NaCN (alcoholic) →

A. CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CN
B. CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-NC
C. CH₃-CH=CH₂
D. CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-OH
Answer: A

The reaction of 1-bromopropane with sodium cyanide (NaCN) in alcoholic solution results in a nucleophilic substitution (Sₙ2) to form butanenitrile (CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CN). The cyanide ion attacks through carbon, not nitrogen.

12. Which of the following statements correctly describes the reactivity of alkyl halides in nucleophilic substitution reactions?

A. Tertiary alkyl halides react faster than primary alkyl halides via S<sub>N</sub>2 mechanism due to steric hindrance.
B. Primary alkyl halides react faster than tertiary alkyl halides via S<sub>N</sub>2 mechanism due to less steric hindrance.
C. Secondary alkyl halides cannot undergo S<sub>N</sub>1 or S<sub>N</sub>2 reactions due to intermediate stability.
D. Tertiary alkyl halides react faster than primary alkyl halides via S<sub>N</sub>2 mechanism due to carbocation stability.
Answer: B

Primary alkyl halides react faster via S<sub>N</sub>2 mechanism because the nucleophile can easily approach the carbon atom bonded to the halogen due to minimal steric hindrance. Tertiary alkyl halides, however, react faster via S<sub>N</sub>1 mechanism due to the stability of the carbocation intermediate, not S<sub>N</sub>2.

13. Which of the following is the correct order of boiling points of isomeric alkyl halides?

A. Primary > Secondary > Tertiary
B. Tertiary > Secondary > Primary
C. Secondary > Primary > Tertiary
D. Primary = Secondary = Tertiary
Answer: A

The boiling points of isomeric alkyl halides decrease with increasing branching (steric hindrance) because van der Waals forces weaken as the surface area of the molecule decreases. Thus, primary alkyl halides have the highest boiling points, followed by secondary and then tertiary.

14. In the preparation of alkyl halides from alcohols using thionyl chloride (SOCl<sub>2</sub>), the byproducts are:

A. SO<sub>2</sub> and HCl
B. SO<sub>3</sub> and HCl
C. Cl<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O
D. SO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O
Answer: A

The reaction of alcohols with thionyl chloride (SOCl<sub>2</sub>) produces alkyl chlorides along with sulfur dioxide (SO<sub>2</sub>) and hydrogen chloride (HCl) as byproducts. This method is preferred because the byproducts are gases and escape easily, driving the reaction to completion.

15. Which of the following reagents is most suitable for converting bromoethane to ethene?

A. Alcoholic KOH
B. Aqueous KOH
C. Zn/HCl
D. NaNH<sub>2</sub>
Answer: A

Alcoholic KOH (potassium hydroxide in alcohol) is used for the elimination reaction (dehydrohalogenation) of alkyl halides to form alkenes. Aqueous KOH, on the other hand, promotes substitution reactions to form alcohols.

16. The correct IUPAC name for the compound CH<sub>3</sub>-CH(Cl)-CH(Br)-CH<sub>3</sub> is:

A. 2-Bromo-3-chlorobutane
B. 3-Bromo-2-chlorobutane
C. 2-Chloro-3-bromobutane
D. 3-Chloro-2-bromobutane
Answer: C

In IUPAC nomenclature, the carbon chain is numbered such that the substituents (halogens in this case) get the lowest possible numbers. Here, chlorine is given priority over bromine alphabetically, so the correct name is 2-chloro-3-bromobutane.

17. The compound that does NOT exhibit optical isomerism is:

A. 2-Chlorobutane
B. 1-Chloro-2-methylbutane
C. 2-Chloro-2-methylbutane
D. 3-Chlorohexane
Answer: C

Optical isomerism occurs in compounds that have a chiral carbon (a carbon atom bonded to four different groups). In 2-chloro-2-methylbutane, the second carbon is bonded to two methyl groups, making it achiral and thus incapable of exhibiting optical isomerism.

18. The reaction of benzene with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light yields:

A. Chlorobenzene
B. Benzyl chloride
C. Hexachlorocyclohexane
D. p-Dichlorobenzene
Answer: C

In the presence of ultraviolet (UV) light, benzene undergoes an addition reaction with chlorine to form hexachlorocyclohexane (also known as benzene hexachloride or BHC). This is a free-radical addition reaction, not an electrophilic substitution reaction.

19. The correct order of dipole moments for the following compounds is: (I) CH<sub>3</sub>Cl, (II) CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>, (III) CHCl<sub>3</sub>, (IV) CCl<sub>4</sub>

A. I > II > III > IV
B. IV > III > II > I
C. II > I > III > IV
D. III > II > I > IV
Answer: A

The dipole moment of a molecule depends on the polarity of the bonds and the molecular geometry. CH<sub>3</sub>Cl has the highest dipole moment because it has one polar C-Cl bond and a tetrahedral geometry that does not cancel out the dipole. As the number of chlorine atoms increases, the dipole moments of CH<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>, CHCl<sub>3</sub>, and CCl<sub>4</sub> decrease due to symmetrical cancellation of bond dipoles. Thus, the order is I > II > III > IV.

20. The reaction of chlorobenzene with chlorine in the presence of anhydrous FeCl₃ gives predominantly:

A. o-dichlorobenzene
B. p-dichlorobenzene
C. m-dichlorobenzene
D. A mixture of o- and p-dichlorobenzene with p-isomer as the major product
Answer: D

Chlorobenzene undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution with chlorine in the presence of anhydrous FeCl₃ (a Lewis acid catalyst). The chlorine substituent is ortho-para directing, but due to steric hindrance at the ortho position, the para isomer is formed in greater proportion. Thus, the reaction yields a mixture of o-dichlorobenzene and p-dichlorobenzene, with the p-isomer as the major product. This is a classic example of the directing effects of substituents in haloarenes.

21. The reaction of tert-butyl bromide with aqueous sodium hydroxide proceeds via which of the following mechanisms?

A. Sₙ1 mechanism
B. Sₙ2 mechanism
C. E1 mechanism
D. E2 mechanism
Answer: A

Tert-butyl bromide is a tertiary alkyl halide, which forms a stable carbocation intermediate due to the +I effect of the three methyl groups. This favors the Sₙ1 (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular) mechanism, where the rate-determining step is the formation of the carbocation. Sₙ2 is unlikely due to steric hindrance, and elimination (E1/E2) would require a strong base or high temperature, which is not indicated here.

22. Which of the following statements correctly explains the difference in reactivity between chlorobenzene and benzyl chloride towards nucleophilic substitution?

A. Chlorobenzene undergoes Sₙ2 substitution due to the sp² hybridized carbon, while benzyl chloride undergoes Sₙ1 substitution.
B. Benzyl chloride is more reactive due to the stability of the benzyl carbocation, while chlorobenzene is less reactive due to resonance stabilization of the C-Cl bond.
C. Chlorobenzene is more reactive because the chlorine atom is directly attached to the benzene ring, facilitating nucleophilic attack.
D. Both compounds undergo Sₙ2 substitution at similar rates due to the presence of the benzene ring.
Answer: B

Benzyl chloride is highly reactive towards nucleophilic substitution because the benzyl carbocation intermediate is stabilized by resonance with the benzene ring. In contrast, chlorobenzene is less reactive due to the resonance stabilization of the C-Cl bond (the lone pair of electrons on chlorine delocalizes into the benzene ring, strengthening the bond and making it less susceptible to nucleophilic attack). Sₙ2 is not favored in chlorobenzene due to the sp² hybridized carbon.

23. In the preparation of alkyl halides from alcohols using thionyl chloride (SOCl₂), which of the following is a key advantage of this method?

A. The reaction proceeds via a free radical mechanism, ensuring high yield.
B. The byproducts (SO₂ and HCl) are gases, which escape from the reaction mixture, driving the reaction to completion.
C. Thionyl chloride can be used with primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols without rearrangement.
D. The reaction requires a strong base to neutralize the HCl formed, preventing side reactions.
Answer: B

The key advantage of using thionyl chloride (SOCl₂) for the preparation of alkyl halides from alcohols is that the byproducts (SO₂ and HCl) are gaseous and escape from the reaction mixture. This drives the reaction to completion, making the process efficient and yielding pure alkyl chlorides. The reaction typically proceeds via an Sₙi (Substitution Nucleophilic Internal) mechanism, not a free radical mechanism, and rearrangements can still occur with certain alcohols.
