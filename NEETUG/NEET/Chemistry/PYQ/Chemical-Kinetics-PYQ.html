PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Chemical Kinetics (Part 1 of 2)

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Chemical Kinetics, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks with 1 mark deducted for incorrect answers. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. In a zero-order reaction, the concentration of the reactant decreases linearly with time. Which of the following plots will be a straight line?

A. [A] vs t
B. ln[A] vs t
C. 1/[A] vs t
D. [A]² vs t
Answer: A

For a zero-order reaction, the rate law is [A] = [A]₀ - kt. This is a linear equation of the form y = mx + c, where [A] is plotted against time (t), yielding a straight line with a negative slope.

2. The rate law for the reaction 2A + B → C is given by rate = k[A]²[B]. If the concentration of A is doubled and that of B is halved, the new rate will be:

A. Same as the original rate
B. Twice the original rate
C. Half the original rate
D. Four times the original rate
Answer: B

Original rate = k[A]²[B]. New rate = k[2A]²[B/2] = k × 4[A]² × [B]/2 = 2k[A]²[B]. Thus, the new rate is twice the original rate.

3. In the reaction 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂, the rate law is rate = k[NO]²[O₂]. Which of the following mechanisms is consistent with this rate law?

A. NO + O₂ → NO₂ (slow); NO + NO₂ → 2NO₂ (fast)
B. 2NO ⇌ N₂O₂ (fast); N₂O₂ + O₂ → 2NO₂ (slow)
C. NO + O₂ → NO₃ (slow); NO₃ + NO → 2NO₂ (fast)
D. 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ (one-step)
Answer: B

The rate law suggests that the slow step involves 2 molecules of NO and 1 molecule of O₂. Mechanism B proposes a fast equilibrium between 2NO and N₂O₂, followed by a slow step involving N₂O₂ and O₂, which matches the rate law rate = k[NO]²[O₂].

4. For the reaction A → B, the rate constant is 0.01 min⁻¹. If the initial concentration of A is 0.1 M, what will be the concentration of A after 100 minutes?

A. 0.037 M
B. 0.010 M
C. 0.001 M
D. 0.050 M
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, [A] = [A]₀ e⁻ᵏᵗ. Here, [A]₀ = 0.1 M, k = 0.01 min⁻¹, t = 100 min. [A] = 0.1 × e⁻⁰·⁰¹×¹⁰⁰ = 0.1 × e⁻¹ = 0.1 × 0.3679 ≈ 0.037 M.

5. In a first-order reaction, the time taken for 75% completion is:

A. Equal to two half-lives
B. Less than two half-lives
C. More than two half-lives
D. Equal to one half-life
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, 50% completion occurs in one half-life, and 75% completion occurs in two half-lives (since 25% of the reactant remains after two half-lives).

6. The rate constant for a reaction is 3.0 × 10⁻² s⁻¹. What is the order of the reaction?

A. Zero order
B. First order
C. Second order
D. Cannot be determined
Answer: B

The units of the rate constant (s⁻¹) indicate that the reaction is first-order. Zero-order reactions have units of M s⁻¹, and second-order reactions have units of M⁻¹ s⁻¹.

7. In the reaction 2A + B → C, the rate law is determined to be rate = k[A][B]². If the concentration of A is doubled and that of B is halved, the new rate will be:

A. Half of the original rate
B. Same as the original rate
C. Twice the original rate
D. One-fourth of the original rate
Answer: A

The original rate is rate = k[A][B]². After changing concentrations: new rate = k[2A][(B/2)]² = k × 2[A] × (B²/4) = (1/2)k[A][B]². Thus, the new rate is half of the original rate.

8. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the molecularity of a reaction?

A. Molecularity can be a fraction.
B. Molecularity is always equal to the order of the reaction.
C. Molecularity is the number of reacting species in the slowest step of the reaction mechanism.
D. Molecularity can be determined experimentally.
Answer: C

Molecularity is defined as the number of reacting species (atoms, ions, or molecules) involved in the rate-determining step of a reaction mechanism. It is always a whole number and cannot be a fraction or determined experimentally like the order of a reaction.

9. For a zero-order reaction, which of the following plots will be linear?

A. [A] vs time
B. ln[A] vs time
C. 1/[A] vs time
D. [A]² vs time
Answer: A

For a zero-order reaction, the rate law is rate = k, and the integrated rate law is [A] = [A]₀ - kt. This equation is linear in [A] vs time, with a slope of -k and intercept [A]₀.

10. The rate constant of a reaction increases by 100% when the temperature is increased from 25°C to 35°C. What is the temperature coefficient of the reaction?

A. 1.5
B. 2.0
C. 2.5
D. 3.0
Answer: B

The temperature coefficient is defined as the ratio of the rate constants at two temperatures differing by 10°C. Here, the rate constant doubles (100% increase) when the temperature increases by 10°C, so the temperature coefficient is 2.00.

11. In the reaction mechanism: Step 1: A + B → C (slow); Step 2: C + D → E (fast), the rate law for the overall reaction is:

A. rate = k[A][B]
B. rate = k[A][B][D]
C. rate = k[C][D]
D. rate = k[A][D]
Answer: A

The rate law is determined by the slowest step (rate-determining step) in the reaction mechanism. Here, the slow step is Step 1: A + B → C, so the rate law is rate = k[A][B]. The concentration of D does not appear in the rate law because it is involved in the fast step.

12. For a first-order reaction, the time required for 99% completion is twice the time required for 90% completion. If the rate constant of the reaction is k, which of the following expressions correctly relates the half-life (t₁/₂) of the reaction to k?

A. t₁/₂ = 0.693/k
B. t₁/₂ = 1/k
C. t₁/₂ = 2.303/k
D. t₁/₂ = k/0.693
Answer: A

For a first-order reaction, the half-life (t₁/₂) is given by t₁/₂ = 0.693/k. The time for 99% completion (t₉₉) and 90% completion (t₉₀) can be derived using the first-order integrated rate law: t = (2.303/k) log([A]₀/[A]). For 99% completion, [A] = 0.01[A]₀, so t₉₉ = (2.303/k) log(100) = (2.303/k) × 2. For 90% completion, [A] = 0.1[A]₀, so t₉₀ = (2.303/k) log(10) = (2.303/k) × 1. Given t₉₉ = 2 × t₉₀, this confirms the first-order kinetics. The half-life formula remains t₁/₂ = 0.693/k, independent of the percentage completion.

13. The rate of a reaction increases by a factor of 2.25 when the temperature is raised from 25°C to 35°C. If the activation energy of the reaction is 50 kJ/mol, what is the rate constant at 35°C? (Use R = 8.314 J/mol·K and log(2.25) = 0.352)

A. k₂ = k₁ × 10^(0.352 × 50000 / (2.303 × 8.314 × 298))
B. k₂ = k₁ × e^(50000 / (8.314 × 308))
C. k₂ = k₁ × 2.25
D. k₂ = k₁ × 10^(50000 / (2.303 × 8.314 × 308))
Answer: A

The Arrhenius equation relates the rate constant (k) to temperature (T): log(k₂/k₁) = (Eₐ/2.303R) × (1/T₁ - 1/T₂). Here, k₂/k₁ = 2.25, Eₐ = 50 kJ/mol = 50000 J/mol, T₁ = 298 K (25°C), T₂ = 308 K (35°C), and R = 8.314 J/mol·K. Substituting the values: log(2.25) = (50000/2.303 × 8.314) × (1/298 - 1/308). Simplifying, 0.352 = (50000/2.303 × 8.314) × (10/298 × 308), which confirms the expression in option A. The rate constant at 35°C (k₂) is thus k₁ × 10^(0.352 × 50000 / (2.303 × 8.314 × 298)).

14. In the reaction 2A + B → C, the rate law is determined experimentally to be rate = k[A][B]². If the concentration of A is doubled and the concentration of B is halved, by what factor will the rate of the reaction change?

A. Rate remains unchanged
B. Rate increases by a factor of 2
C. Rate decreases by a factor of 2
D. Rate increases by a factor of 4
Answer: C

The rate law for the reaction is rate = k[A][B]². Initially, let the rate be rate₁ = k[A][B]². After changing the concentrations: [A] becomes 2[A] and [B] becomes 0.5[B]. The new rate (rate₂) is rate₂ = k[2A][0.5B]² = k × 2[A] × 0.25[B]² = 0.5k[A][B]². Thus, rate₂ = 0.5 × rate₁, meaning the rate decreases by a factor of 2.
