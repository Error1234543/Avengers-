NEET Physics - Physical World and Measurement: Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the term 'Physics' and state its two main branches as per NEET syllabus.

Physics is the natural science that studies matter, its motion and behavior through space and time, and the related entities of energy and force. The two main branches relevant to NEET are: (1) **Classical Physics** (covering mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, and optics), and (2) **Modern Physics** (including relativity, quantum mechanics, atomic and nuclear physics).

Card 2

What is the SI unit of length, and how is it defined?

The SI unit of length is the **meter (m)**. It is defined as the distance traveled by light in a vacuum in **1/299,792,458 of a second**. This definition links the meter to the speed of light, a fundamental constant of nature.

Card 3

Explain the difference between fundamental and derived units with examples.

**Fundamental units** are independent and cannot be expressed in terms of other units (e.g., meter for length, kilogram for mass, second for time). **Derived units** are combinations of fundamental units (e.g., newton (N) for force = kg·m/s², joule (J) for energy = kg·m²/s²).

Card 4

What is dimensional analysis, and how is it used in physics?

Dimensional analysis is a method to check the consistency of physical equations by comparing the dimensions (units) on both sides. It helps in: (1) **Deriving relationships** between physical quantities, (2) **Converting units** across systems, and (3) **Identifying errors** in equations. For example, the dimension of force is [M L T⁻²].

Card 5

State the dimensions of the following quantities: (a) Work, (b) Power, (c) Pressure.

(a) Work: [M L² T⁻²] (force × distance), (b) Power: [M L² T⁻³] (work/time), (c) Pressure: [M L⁻¹ T⁻²] (force/area).

Card 6

What are the limitations of dimensional analysis?

Dimensional analysis cannot: (1) Determine **dimensionless constants** (e.g., π, ½ in equations), (2) Distinguish between quantities with **same dimensions** (e.g., work and torque), (3) Provide **directional information** (e.g., vector vs. scalar), or (4) Validate **numerical factors** in equations.

Card 7

Define accuracy and precision with an example relevant to NEET measurements.

**Accuracy** refers to how close a measured value is to the true value (e.g., hitting the bullseye). **Precision** refers to the consistency of repeated measurements (e.g., multiple arrows landing close to each other but not necessarily on the bullseye). Example: A thermometer reading 37.1°C, 37.2°C, and 37.0°C for a true temperature of 37.0°C is **precise and accurate**.

Card 8

What are systematic and random errors? How can they be minimized?

**Systematic errors** are consistent and repeatable (e.g., zero error in instruments, calibration issues). Minimized by **calibration** and **correction factors**. **Random errors** are unpredictable (e.g., fluctuations in readings, human reaction time). Minimized by **averaging multiple measurements** and using **statistical methods**.

Card 9

Explain the concept of significant figures and state the rules for their determination.

Significant figures (SF) represent the digits in a measurement that are known reliably, including the first uncertain digit. Rules: (1) All **non-zero digits** are significant, (2) **Zeros between non-zero digits** are significant, (3) **Leading zeros** (before first non-zero digit) are **not** significant, (4) **Trailing zeros** in a decimal number are significant, (5) In scientific notation, all digits in the coefficient are significant. Example: 0.00450 has **3 SF** (4, 5, 0).

Card 10

Perform the following operation and express the result with the correct significant figures: 12.11 + 18.0 + 1.012.

Step 1: Align decimal places: 12.11 + 18.00 + 1.012 = 31.122. Step 2: The least precise measurement is 18.0 (1 decimal place). Step 3: Round the result to **1 decimal place**: **31.1**.

Card 11

What is the difference between absolute and relative error?

**Absolute error** is the magnitude of the difference between the measured value and the true value (e.g., |3.2 cm - 3.0 cm| = 0.2 cm). **Relative error** is the ratio of absolute error to the true value, often expressed as a percentage (e.g., (0.2 cm / 3.0 cm) × 100 = 6.67%). Relative error provides a **sense of scale** for the error.

Card 12

State the principle of homogeneity of dimensions and its application in physics.

The principle states that **only quantities with the same dimensions can be added, subtracted, or equated**. Application: (1) **Checking equation validity** (e.g., v = u + at is valid because all terms have dimension [L T⁻¹]), (2) **Deriving formulas** (e.g., period of a simple pendulum: T ∝ √(L/g) → dimensions [T] = [L]^½ [L T⁻²]⁻½).

Card 13

Define the following terms: (a) Parallax, (b) Least count, (c) Vernier constant.

(a) **Parallax**: The apparent shift in the position of an object when viewed from different angles. Minimized by reading instruments at eye level. (b) **Least count**: The smallest measurement that can be made with an instrument (e.g., 0.1 cm for a meter scale). (c) **Vernier constant**: The difference between one main scale division and one vernier scale division (e.g., 0.01 cm for a vernier caliper).

Card 14

How is the error in a product or quotient of quantities calculated?

For quantities A and B with errors ΔA and ΔB: (1) **Product (A × B)**: Relative error = (ΔA/A + ΔB/B). Absolute error = (A × B) × (ΔA/A + ΔB/B). (2) **Quotient (A/B)**: Relative error = (ΔA/A + ΔB/B). Absolute error = (A/B) × (ΔA/A + ΔB/B). Example: If A = 5.0 ± 0.1 cm and B = 2.0 ± 0.1 cm, then A × B = 10.0 ± 0.7 cm (relative error = 0.1/5 + 0.1/2 = 0.07).

Card 15

What are the dimensions of the universal gravitational constant (G)?

From Newton’s law of gravitation: F = G (m₁ m₂)/r². Rearranging, G = F r² / (m₁ m₂). Dimensions: [G] = [M L T⁻²] [L²] / [M²] = [M⁻¹ L³ T⁻²].

Card 16

Explain the concept of order of magnitude with an example.

Order of magnitude is the power of 10 closest to a quantity, used to express its scale. Example: The mass of the Earth is ~5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, so its order of magnitude is **10²⁵ kg**. For 0.0034, the order of magnitude is **10⁻³** (since 0.0034 ≈ 3.4 × 10⁻³).

Card 17

What is the difference between mass and weight? State their SI units.

**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object (intrinsic property, SI unit: **kilogram (kg)**). **Weight** is the force exerted by gravity on an object (W = m × g, SI unit: **newton (N)**). Mass is constant; weight varies with gravitational acceleration (e.g., weight on the Moon is 1/6th of Earth’s weight).
