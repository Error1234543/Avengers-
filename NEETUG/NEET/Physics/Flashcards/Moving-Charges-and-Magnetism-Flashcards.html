NEET Physics: Moving Charges and Magnetism - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the magnetic force on a charge q moving with velocity v in a magnetic field B. State the formula, variables, and SI units.

The magnetic force (F) on a charge q moving with velocity v in a magnetic field B is given by the Lorentz force law: F = q(v × B). Here, F is the force vector (SI unit: newton, N), q is the charge (coulomb, C), v is the velocity vector (metre per second, m/s), and B is the magnetic field vector (tesla, T). The force is perpendicular to both v and B, and its magnitude is F = qvB sinθ, where θ is the angle between v and B.

Card 2

What is the condition for a charged particle to move in a circular path in a uniform magnetic field? Derive the radius of the path.

A charged particle moves in a circular path in a uniform magnetic field when its velocity is perpendicular to the field (θ = 90°). The magnetic force provides the centripetal force: qvB = mv²/r. Solving for the radius (r), we get r = mv/(qB), where m is the mass of the particle (kg), v is its velocity (m/s), q is its charge (C), and B is the magnetic field strength (T).

Card 3

Explain the working principle of a cyclotron. Why is the frequency of the applied electric field independent of the particle's velocity?

A cyclotron accelerates charged particles using a perpendicular magnetic field and an alternating electric field. The magnetic field forces particles into a circular path, while the electric field accelerates them each time they cross the gap between the dees. The frequency of the electric field (cyclotron frequency) is f = qB/(2πm), which depends only on the charge (q), magnetic field (B), and mass (m) of the particle, not on its velocity or the radius of the path. This allows synchronization of the electric field with the particle's motion.

Card 4

State Biot-Savart Law for the magnetic field due to a current-carrying conductor. Write the formula and explain the variables.

Biot-Savart Law states that the magnetic field dB at a point due to a small current element Idl is: dB = (μ₀/4π) * (Idl × r̂)/r². Here, μ₀ is the permeability of free space (4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A), Idl is the current element vector (A·m), r̂ is the unit vector pointing from the current element to the point, and r is the distance (m) between them. The direction of dB is given by the right-hand rule.

Card 5

Derive the magnetic field at the center of a circular current loop of radius R carrying current I. State the formula and direction.

For a circular loop, the magnetic field at the center is derived by integrating Biot-Savart Law. Each element Idl contributes dB = (μ₀/4π) * (Idl)/R² (since θ = 90° and r = R). Integrating over the loop: B = (μ₀I/4πR²) * ∫dl = (μ₀I/4πR²) * 2πR = μ₀I/(2R). The direction is perpendicular to the plane of the loop, given by the right-hand rule (curl fingers in the direction of I; thumb points to B).

Card 6

State Ampere's Circuital Law. How is it used to find the magnetic field inside a long solenoid?

Ampere's Circuital Law states that the line integral of B around a closed loop is μ₀ times the total current (I_enc) passing through the loop: ∮B·dl = μ₀I_enc. For a long solenoid with n turns per unit length carrying current I, the magnetic field inside is uniform and parallel to the axis. Choosing a rectangular loop, the integral simplifies to B * l = μ₀(nl)I, giving B = μ₀nI, where l is the length of the loop inside the solenoid.

Card 7

What is the force per unit length between two long, parallel current-carrying wires? Derive the formula and state the condition for attraction/repulsion.

The force per unit length (F/L) between two parallel wires separated by distance d, carrying currents I₁ and I₂, is F/L = (μ₀I₁I₂)/(2πd). The force is attractive if the currents are in the same direction and repulsive if they are in opposite directions. Derivation: Wire 1 produces a magnetic field B₁ = μ₀I₁/(2πd) at wire 2. The force on wire 2 is F = I₂LB₁, giving F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd).

Card 8

Define the torque on a current loop in a uniform magnetic field. Write the formula and explain the variables.

The torque (τ) on a current loop of area A carrying current I in a uniform magnetic field B is τ = NI(A × B), where N is the number of turns. The magnitude is τ = NIBAsinθ, where θ is the angle between the normal to the loop and B. The torque tends to align the loop's magnetic moment (μ = NIA) with the field. The direction is given by the right-hand rule (curl fingers in the direction of I; thumb points to μ).

Card 9

What is the magnetic moment of a current loop? How is it related to the torque experienced in a magnetic field?

The magnetic moment (μ) of a current loop is a vector quantity defined as μ = NIA, where N is the number of turns, I is the current (A), and A is the area vector (m²) of the loop (direction given by the right-hand rule). In a magnetic field B, the torque on the loop is τ = μ × B, with magnitude τ = μBsinθ, where θ is the angle between μ and B. The torque tends to align μ with B.

Card 10

Explain the principle of a moving coil galvanometer. How is its current sensitivity defined?

A moving coil galvanometer measures small currents using the torque on a current-carrying coil in a radial magnetic field. The coil experiences a torque τ = NIBAsinθ, where θ = 90° (radial field), so τ = NBIA. The coil deflects until the restoring torque of the suspension equals the magnetic torque. Current sensitivity is defined as the deflection per unit current: θ/I = NAB/k, where k is the torsional constant of the suspension. Higher N, A, or B increases sensitivity.

Card 11

What is the Hall effect? Derive the Hall voltage for a conductor of thickness t carrying current I in a magnetic field B.

The Hall effect is the development of a voltage (Hall voltage, V_H) across a conductor when a magnetic field is applied perpendicular to the current. Charge carriers (charge q) moving with drift velocity v_d experience a magnetic force qv_dB, causing a transverse electric field E_H = v_dB. The Hall voltage is V_H = E_H * width = v_dB * t (for thickness t). Since I = nqAv_d, V_H = IB/(nqt), where n is the charge carrier density. The sign of V_H indicates the type of charge carriers.

Card 12

A proton moves perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T with a speed of 2 × 10⁶ m/s. Calculate the radius of its circular path. (Mass of proton = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, charge = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)

Using r = mv/(qB): r = (1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)(2 × 10⁶ m/s) / [(1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)(0.5 T)] = (3.34 × 10⁻²¹) / (8 × 10⁻²⁰) = 0.04175 m = 4.175 cm.

Card 13

A circular coil of radius 10 cm and 100 turns carries a current of 5 A. Calculate the magnetic field at its center.

Using B = μ₀NI/(2R): B = (4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A)(100)(5 A) / (2 × 0.1 m) = (2 × 10⁻⁴ π) T = 6.28 × 10⁻⁴ T.

Card 14

Two long parallel wires are 2 cm apart and carry currents of 3 A and 5 A in the same direction. Calculate the force per unit length on each wire.

Using F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd): F/L = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(3)(5) / (2π × 0.02) = (6 × 10⁻⁵) N/m. The force is attractive.
