Card 1

Define Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM).

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) is a type of periodic motion where the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement from the mean position and acts in the opposite direction. Mathematically, it is expressed as F = -kx, where F is the restoring force, k is the force constant, and x is the displacement. The motion is sinusoidal in nature, described by equations of the form x(t) = A sin(ωt + φ) or x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ), where A is the amplitude, ω is the angular frequency, and φ is the phase constant.

Card 2

What is the relationship between angular frequency (ω), frequency (f), and time period (T) in SHM?

In Simple Harmonic Motion, the angular frequency (ω), frequency (f), and time period (T) are related as follows: ω = 2πf and T = 1/f. Therefore, ω = 2π/T. The time period (T) is the time taken to complete one full oscillation, while the frequency (f) is the number of oscillations per unit time. The angular frequency (ω) represents the rate of change of the phase of the oscillation.

Card 3

Derive the expression for the time period of a simple pendulum in SHM.

For a simple pendulum of length L and small angular displacement θ, the restoring torque is τ = -mgL sinθ. For small angles, sinθ ≈ θ, so τ ≈ -mgLθ. The moment of inertia of the pendulum bob about the pivot is I = mL². Using τ = Iα, we get -mgLθ = mL²α, which simplifies to α = -(g/L)θ. This is analogous to the SHM equation a = -ω²x, where ω² = g/L. Thus, the angular frequency ω = √(g/L), and the time period T = 2π/ω = 2π√(L/g).

Card 4

What is the total energy of a particle executing SHM, and how is it distributed between kinetic and potential energy?

The total energy (E) of a particle in SHM is constant and is the sum of its kinetic energy (K) and potential energy (U). For a particle of mass m, amplitude A, and angular frequency ω, the total energy is E = (1/2)mω²A². At any displacement x, the potential energy is U = (1/2)mω²x², and the kinetic energy is K = (1/2)mω²(A² - x²). At the mean position (x = 0), the energy is entirely kinetic, while at the extreme positions (x = ±A), the energy is entirely potential.

Card 5

Explain the concept of phase in SHM with an example.

Phase in SHM refers to the argument of the sine or cosine function that describes the motion, given by (ωt + φ), where φ is the phase constant (or initial phase). The phase determines the position and direction of motion of the particle at any given time. For example, two particles executing SHM with the same amplitude and frequency but different phase constants will reach their maximum displacement at different times. If φ = 0, the motion is described by x = A sin(ωt), and the particle starts from the mean position moving in the positive direction. If φ = π/2, the motion is x = A cos(ωt), and the particle starts from the maximum displacement.

Card 6

What is the difference between free, forced, and damped oscillations?

Free oscillations occur when a system oscillates with its natural frequency without any external force or damping. The amplitude remains constant over time. Forced oscillations occur when an external periodic force drives the system, causing it to oscillate at the frequency of the driving force. Damped oscillations occur when a resistive force (e.g., friction or air resistance) acts on the system, causing the amplitude to decrease exponentially over time. The energy of the system is gradually dissipated as heat. In damped oscillations, the angular frequency is reduced compared to the natural frequency, and the motion is described by x(t) = A e^(-bt) sin(ω't + φ), where b is the damping coefficient.

Card 7

A particle executes SHM with an amplitude of 0.1 m and a time period of 2 s. Calculate its maximum velocity.

The maximum velocity (v_max) of a particle in SHM occurs at the mean position and is given by v_max = Aω, where A is the amplitude and ω is the angular frequency. The angular frequency ω = 2π/T, where T is the time period. Here, T = 2 s, so ω = 2π/2 = π rad/s. Thus, v_max = 0.1 m × π rad/s = 0.1π m/s ≈ 0.314 m/s.

Card 8

What is resonance in forced oscillations?

Resonance is a phenomenon that occurs in forced oscillations when the frequency of the external driving force matches the natural frequency of the system. At resonance, the amplitude of the oscillations becomes very large, and the system absorbs maximum energy from the driving force. This can lead to large oscillations that may cause structural damage in mechanical systems. Resonance is observed in various physical systems, such as bridges, buildings, and musical instruments.

Card 9

How does the time period of a spring-mass system depend on the mass and spring constant?

The time period (T) of a spring-mass system executing SHM is given by T = 2π√(m/k), where m is the mass of the particle and k is the spring constant. The time period is independent of the amplitude of oscillation. It increases with an increase in mass (m) and decreases with an increase in the spring constant (k). This relationship shows that a stiffer spring (higher k) results in a shorter time period, while a larger mass results in a longer time period.

Card 10

A particle in SHM has a displacement of x = 0.05 m at t = 0 and a velocity of 0.1 m/s. If the angular frequency is 2 rad/s, find the amplitude of the motion.

The displacement and velocity in SHM are given by x = A sin(ωt + φ) and v = Aω cos(ωt + φ). At t = 0, x = A sinφ = 0.05 m and v = Aω cosφ = 0.1 m/s. Given ω = 2 rad/s, we have A sinφ = 0.05 and A cosφ = 0.1/2 = 0.05. Squaring and adding these equations: (A sinφ)² + (A cosφ)² = 0.05² + 0.05² → A²(sin²φ + cos²φ) = 0.005 → A² = 0.005 → A = √0.005 ≈ 0.0707 m.

Card 11

Explain the concept of superposition of two SHMs in the same direction with the same frequency.

When two SHMs of the same frequency and direction are superposed, the resultant motion is also SHM with the same frequency. If the two motions are x₁ = A₁ sin(ωt + φ₁) and x₂ = A₂ sin(ωt + φ₂), the resultant displacement is x = x₁ + x₂ = A sin(ωt + φ), where A = √(A₁² + A₂² + 2A₁A₂ cos(φ₂ - φ₁)) and φ is the phase of the resultant motion. The amplitude of the resultant motion depends on the phase difference (φ₂ - φ₁) between the two motions. If the phase difference is 0, the amplitudes add up (constructive interference), and if it is π, the amplitudes subtract (destructive interference).

Card 12

What is the difference between displacement, velocity, and acceleration in SHM?

In SHM, displacement (x), velocity (v), and acceleration (a) are related but differ in phase. Displacement is given by x = A sin(ωt + φ), velocity is the time derivative of displacement: v = Aω cos(ωt + φ), and acceleration is the time derivative of velocity: a = -Aω² sin(ωt + φ) = -ω²x. The velocity leads the displacement by a phase of π/2 (90°), and the acceleration leads the velocity by another π/2 (90°). Thus, acceleration is 180° out of phase with displacement, meaning it is always directed toward the mean position.
