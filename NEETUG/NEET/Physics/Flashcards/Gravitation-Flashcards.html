Card 1

State Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation and write its mathematical formula. Include the meaning of each variable and the SI unit of the gravitational constant (G).

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation states that every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers. The formula is: F = G * (m₁ * m₂) / r², where F is the gravitational force (N), m₁ and m₂ are the masses of the two particles (kg), r is the distance between their centers (m), and G is the universal gravitational constant. The SI unit of G is N m² kg⁻², and its value is approximately 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².

Card 2

Derive the expression for the acceleration due to gravity (g) on the surface of the Earth in terms of the Earth's mass (M) and radius (R).

From Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation, the gravitational force on an object of mass m on the Earth's surface is F = G * (M * m) / R², where M is the Earth's mass and R is its radius. By Newton's Second Law, F = m * g. Equating the two expressions: m * g = G * (M * m) / R². The mass m cancels out, yielding g = G * M / R². This shows that g depends on the Earth's mass and radius but is independent of the object's mass.

Card 3

Explain how the value of acceleration due to gravity (g) changes with altitude (h) above the Earth's surface. Provide the formula for g at height h.

As altitude (h) increases, the distance from the Earth's center becomes R + h, where R is the Earth's radius. Using the formula g = G * M / r², where r = R + h, the acceleration due to gravity at height h is g' = G * M / (R + h)². Since (R + h)² > R², g' decreases with increasing altitude. The formula can also be written as g' = g * [R / (R + h)]², where g is the value at the surface.

Card 4

Define gravitational potential energy (U) for two masses m₁ and m₂ separated by distance r. Write its formula and explain the significance of the negative sign.

Gravitational potential energy (U) is the work done to bring two masses from infinite separation to a distance r apart. The formula is U = -G * (m₁ * m₂) / r. The negative sign indicates that the gravitational force is attractive: the potential energy decreases (becomes more negative) as the masses approach each other, reflecting a bound system. At infinite separation, U = 0, which is the reference point.

Card 5

What is the escape velocity of a body from the surface of the Earth? Derive its formula in terms of the Earth's mass (M) and radius (R).

Escape velocity is the minimum velocity required for a body to escape the gravitational pull of the Earth without further propulsion. Using the principle of conservation of energy: (1/2) * m * vₑ² = G * (M * m) / R, where vₑ is the escape velocity, m is the body's mass, M is the Earth's mass, and R is its radius. Simplifying, vₑ = √(2 * G * M / R). This shows escape velocity depends on the Earth's mass and radius but not on the body's mass.

Card 6

State Kepler's three laws of planetary motion and briefly explain their significance.

1. **Law of Orbits**: All planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus. This describes the shape of planetary orbits. 2. **Law of Areas**: A line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time. This implies planets move faster when closer to the Sun (perihelion) and slower when farther away (aphelion). 3. **Law of Periods**: The square of the orbital period (T) of a planet is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (a) of its orbit: T² ∝ a³. This relates the orbital period to the orbit's size.

Card 7

Explain the concept of orbital velocity (vₒ) for a satellite revolving around the Earth. Derive its formula in terms of the Earth's mass (M) and the satellite's orbital radius (r).

Orbital velocity is the velocity required for a satellite to maintain a stable circular orbit around the Earth. For a satellite of mass m at radius r, the centripetal force is provided by the gravitational force: m * vₒ² / r = G * (M * m) / r². Simplifying, vₒ = √(G * M / r). This shows orbital velocity decreases with increasing orbital radius and is independent of the satellite's mass.

Card 8

What is the time period (T) of a satellite in a circular orbit around the Earth? Derive its formula in terms of the orbital radius (r) and the Earth's mass (M).

The time period (T) is the time taken for one complete orbit. For a circular orbit, the orbital velocity vₒ = 2πr / T. From the orbital velocity formula, vₒ = √(G * M / r). Equating the two: 2πr / T = √(G * M / r). Squaring both sides and solving for T gives T = 2π * √(r³ / (G * M)). This shows the time period depends on the orbital radius and the Earth's mass.

Card 9

Define geostationary orbit. What are its key characteristics, and what is its approximate height above the Earth's surface?

A geostationary orbit is a circular orbit directly above the Earth's equator where a satellite's orbital period matches the Earth's rotational period (24 hours). Key characteristics: 1. The satellite appears stationary relative to a point on Earth. 2. It lies in the equatorial plane. 3. The orbital radius is approximately 42,164 km from the Earth's center, which corresponds to a height of about 35,786 km above the Earth's surface. Used for communication and weather satellites.

Card 10

Explain the concept of weightlessness in a satellite orbiting the Earth. Why do astronauts feel weightless despite being under Earth's gravity?

Weightlessness in a satellite occurs because both the satellite and the astronauts inside are in free fall toward the Earth. The gravitational force provides the centripetal acceleration required for circular motion, so there is no normal force (reaction) acting on the astronauts. Since weight is the force exerted by a surface (normal force), its absence creates the sensation of weightlessness. The astronauts and the satellite are accelerating at the same rate, so they appear to float.

Card 11

What is the difference between gravitational mass and inertial mass? How are they experimentally verified to be equivalent?

Gravitational mass (m_g) determines the strength of the gravitational force on an object (F = m_g * g), while inertial mass (m_i) determines an object's resistance to acceleration (F = m_i * a). Experimentally, they are verified to be equivalent through the Eötvös experiment, which shows that all objects fall with the same acceleration in a gravitational field, implying m_g = m_i. This equivalence is a cornerstone of Einstein's General Theory of Relativity.

Card 12

A planet has a mass twice that of Earth and a radius 1.5 times that of Earth. Calculate the acceleration due to gravity on the planet's surface in terms of g (Earth's surface gravity).

Using the formula g = G * M / R², for Earth: g = G * M_E / R_E². For the planet: g' = G * (2M_E) / (1.5R_E)² = G * 2M_E / (2.25R_E²) = (2 / 2.25) * (G * M_E / R_E²) = (8/9) * g. Thus, the acceleration due to gravity on the planet's surface is (8/9)g, or approximately 0.89g.

Card 13

If the Earth's radius were to shrink to half its current size while its mass remains unchanged, how would the escape velocity from the Earth's surface change?

Escape velocity vₑ = √(2 * G * M / R). If the radius R becomes R/2, the new escape velocity vₑ' = √(2 * G * M / (R/2)) = √(4 * G * M / R) = √2 * √(2 * G * M / R) = √2 * vₑ. Thus, the escape velocity would increase by a factor of √2.
