NEET Physics - Work, Energy and Power (Exam-Focused Flashcards)

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define work done by a constant force. State the formula and the SI unit.

Work done by a constant force is defined as the product of the magnitude of the force and the displacement of the object in the direction of the force. Formula: W = F · s = F s cosθ, where F is the force, s is the displacement, and θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. SI unit: joule (J), where 1 J = 1 N·m.

Card 2

What is the work-energy theorem? Derive it for a constant force.

The work-energy theorem states that the work done by the net force on a particle equals the change in its kinetic energy. Derivation: W_net = ∫ F_net · ds = ∫ m a · ds = m ∫ (dv/dt) · v dt = m ∫ v · dv = (1/2) m v² | from initial to final = ΔK. Thus, W_net = ΔK.

Card 3

Explain the difference between conservative and non-conservative forces with examples.

Conservative forces: Work done is independent of the path taken and depends only on initial and final positions. Mechanical energy is conserved. Example: gravitational force, electrostatic force. Non-conservative forces: Work done depends on the path taken. Mechanical energy is not conserved. Example: frictional force, air resistance.

Card 4

State the principle of conservation of mechanical energy. Under what condition is it valid?

The principle of conservation of mechanical energy states that the total mechanical energy (sum of kinetic and potential energy) of a system remains constant if only conservative forces are acting on it. Condition: No non-conservative forces (e.g., friction) do work on the system.

Card 5

Define power. State its formula and SI unit. How is average power different from instantaneous power?

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. Formula: P = dW/dt. SI unit: watt (W), where 1 W = 1 J/s. Average power: P_avg = W_total / t_total. Instantaneous power: P_inst = dW/dt = F · v (for constant force).

Card 6

A block of mass 2 kg is pulled by a force of 10 N at an angle of 30° to the horizontal. If the block moves 5 m, calculate the work done by the force.

Work done W = F s cosθ = 10 N × 5 m × cos(30°) = 10 × 5 × (√3/2) = 25√3 J ≈ 43.3 J.

Card 7

A car of mass 1000 kg accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s. Calculate the average power delivered by the engine.

First, find acceleration: a = (v - u)/t = (20 - 0)/10 = 2 m/s². Force: F = m a = 1000 × 2 = 2000 N. Displacement: s = ut + (1/2) a t² = 0 + (1/2) × 2 × 10² = 100 m. Work done: W = F s = 2000 × 100 = 2 × 10⁵ J. Average power: P_avg = W/t = 2 × 10⁵ / 10 = 2 × 10⁴ W = 20 kW.

Card 8

What is the difference between kinetic energy and potential energy? Give one example of each.

Kinetic energy (K): Energy possessed by an object due to its motion. Formula: K = (1/2) m v². Example: A moving bullet. Potential energy (U): Energy possessed by an object due to its position or configuration. Example: A book placed on a table has gravitational potential energy U = m g h.

Card 9

A spring with spring constant k = 200 N/m is compressed by 0.1 m. Calculate the potential energy stored in the spring.

Potential energy stored in a spring: U = (1/2) k x² = (1/2) × 200 × (0.1)² = 1 J.

Card 10

Explain the concept of elastic and inelastic collisions. How is kinetic energy conserved in each?

Elastic collision: Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. Example: Collision between atomic particles. Inelastic collision: Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not conserved (some is converted to other forms like heat or sound). Example: A bullet embedding in a block. Perfectly inelastic collision: Objects stick together after collision, maximum kinetic energy loss.

Card 11

A ball of mass 0.5 kg is dropped from a height of 10 m. Calculate its kinetic energy just before it hits the ground. (Take g = 10 m/s²)

Using conservation of mechanical energy: Initial energy (potential) = Final energy (kinetic). m g h = (1/2) m v². Thus, K = m g h = 0.5 × 10 × 10 = 50 J.

Card 12

What is the work done by a centripetal force on an object moving in a circular path? Explain.

Work done by a centripetal force is zero. Reason: The centripetal force is always perpendicular to the displacement of the object (θ = 90°, cos90° = 0). Thus, W = F s cosθ = 0.

Card 13

Define the term 'watt'. How is it related to horsepower?

Watt (W) is the SI unit of power, defined as 1 joule of work done per second. Relation to horsepower: 1 horsepower (hp) ≈ 746 W.

Card 14

A machine does 500 J of work in 10 s. Another machine does 1000 J of work in 5 s. Which machine is more powerful?

Power of first machine: P₁ = 500 J / 10 s = 50 W. Power of second machine: P₂ = 1000 J / 5 s = 200 W. The second machine is more powerful.

Card 15

What is the efficiency of a machine? State the formula and explain its significance.

Efficiency (η) of a machine is the ratio of useful output work (or power) to the input work (or power). Formula: η = (Output Work / Input Work) × 100%. Significance: Measures how well a machine converts input energy into useful output energy; always less than 100% due to energy losses (e.g., friction, heat).
