NEET Physics - Kinematics Flashcards (Exam-Focused)

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define **displacement** in kinematics. How is it different from distance?

**Displacement** is the shortest straight-line vector from the initial to the final position of an object, having both magnitude and direction. **Distance** is the total path length traveled, a scalar quantity with only magnitude. Displacement can be zero even if distance is non-zero (e.g., returning to the starting point).

Card 2

State the **three equations of motion** for uniformly accelerated motion. Specify the variables and their SI units.

1. **v = u + at** (Final velocity = Initial velocity + Acceleration × Time) 2. **s = ut + ½at²** (Displacement = Initial velocity × Time + ½ × Acceleration × Time²) 3. **v² = u² + 2as** (Final velocity² = Initial velocity² + 2 × Acceleration × Displacement) Variables: - u, v: Initial/final velocity (m/s) - a: Acceleration (m/s²) - t: Time (s) - s: Displacement (m)

Card 3

A car accelerates from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 seconds. Calculate its acceleration and displacement during this time.

**Acceleration (a):** Using v = u + at → 30 = 10 + a(5) → a = (30 - 10)/5 = **4 m/s²**. **Displacement (s):** Using s = ut + ½at² → s = (10)(5) + ½(4)(5)² = 50 + 50 = **100 m**.

Card 4

What is **relative velocity**? Derive the formula for the relative velocity of object A with respect to object B (v_AB).

**Relative velocity** is the velocity of an object as observed from another moving object. If **v_A** and **v_B** are the velocities of objects A and B in a common frame: **v_AB = v_A - v_B** (vector subtraction). Example: If A moves at 5 m/s east and B at 3 m/s east, v_AB = 5 - 3 = **2 m/s east**.

Card 5

Explain **projectile motion**. What are the horizontal and vertical components of velocity at the highest point?

**Projectile motion** is the motion of an object under gravity after being projected at an angle. It has two independent components: 1. **Horizontal (v_x):** Constant (no acceleration), equal to u cosθ. 2. **Vertical (v_y):** Changes due to gravity (a = -g). At the highest point, v_y = **0** (momentarily at rest vertically). Total velocity at the highest point = **v_x = u cosθ**.

Card 6

A ball is thrown horizontally from a height of 20 m with a speed of 10 m/s. Calculate the time of flight and horizontal range. (g = 10 m/s²)

**Time of flight (t):** Vertical motion: s = ut + ½gt² → 20 = 0 + ½(10)t² → t² = 4 → t = **2 s**. **Horizontal range (R):** R = v_x × t = 10 × 2 = **20 m**.

Card 7

What is **uniform circular motion**? Define centripetal acceleration and its formula.

**Uniform circular motion** is motion along a circular path at constant speed. Though speed is constant, velocity changes direction continuously, requiring **centripetal acceleration (a_c)** directed toward the center. Formula: **a_c = v²/r** - v: Linear speed (m/s) - r: Radius of the circle (m). Note: Centripetal force (F_c = mv²/r) causes this acceleration.

Card 8

A stone tied to a string of length 1 m is whirled in a circle at 2 revolutions per second. Calculate its centripetal acceleration. (π = 3.14)

**Linear speed (v):** v = 2πr × frequency = 2 × 3.14 × 1 × 2 = **12.56 m/s**. **Centripetal acceleration (a_c):** a_c = v²/r = (12.56)²/1 = **157.75 m/s²** (toward the center).

Card 9

What is the **angle of projection** for maximum range in projectile motion? Derive the formula for maximum range.

The **angle of projection (θ)** for maximum range is **45°** (on level ground). **Range (R):** R = (u² sin2θ)/g. For maximum R, sin2θ = 1 → 2θ = 90° → θ = **45°**. Maximum range: **R_max = u²/g**.

Card 10

A projectile is fired at 50 m/s at 30° to the horizontal. Calculate its (a) time of flight, (b) maximum height, and (c) range. (g = 10 m/s²)

**Initial components:** u_x = 50 cos30° = 43.3 m/s, u_y = 50 sin30° = 25 m/s. (a) **Time of flight (T):** T = 2u_y/g = 2 × 25/10 = **5 s**. (b) **Maximum height (H):** H = u_y²/2g = (25)²/20 = **31.25 m**. (c) **Range (R):** R = u_x × T = 43.3 × 5 = **216.5 m**.

Card 11

Explain the **difference between speed and velocity** with an example.

**Speed** is a scalar quantity representing the rate of motion (e.g., 60 km/h). **Velocity** is a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction (e.g., 60 km/h **north**). Example: A car moving in a circle at 60 km/h has constant speed but changing velocity (direction changes continuously).

Card 12

What is **average velocity**? How is it calculated for a journey with varying speeds?

**Average velocity** is the total displacement divided by total time taken. It is a vector quantity. Formula: **v_avg = (Total displacement)/(Total time)**. Example: If a car travels 100 km east in 2 hours and 50 km west in 1 hour, total displacement = 50 km east, total time = 3 hours → v_avg = 50/3 ≈ **16.67 km/h east**.

Card 13

Define **instantaneous velocity**. How is it determined graphically?

**Instantaneous velocity** is the velocity of an object at a specific instant of time. It is the slope of the tangent to the **position-time graph** at that instant. Example: If the position-time graph is a curve, the slope of the tangent at t = 2 s gives the instantaneous velocity at t = 2 s.

Card 14

A train decelerates uniformly from 30 m/s to rest in 15 seconds. Calculate its deceleration and the distance covered during this time.

**Deceleration (a):** Using v = u + at → 0 = 30 + a(15) → a = -30/15 = **-2 m/s²** (negative sign indicates deceleration). **Distance (s):** Using s = ut + ½at² → s = (30)(15) + ½(-2)(15)² = 450 - 225 = **225 m**.

Card 15

What is **free-fall motion**? State the equations of motion for an object in free fall (taking upward as positive).

**Free-fall motion** is the motion of an object under the influence of gravity alone (no air resistance). Taking upward as positive, acceleration (a) = **-g** (g ≈ 9.8 m/s²). Equations: 1. v = u - gt 2. s = ut - ½gt² 3. v² = u² - 2gs Note: If dropped from rest (u = 0), s = -½gt² (negative sign indicates downward displacement).
