Card 1

Define the **First Law of Thermodynamics** and state its mathematical form.

The First Law of Thermodynamics states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or converted from one form to another. It is a statement of the **conservation of energy** for thermodynamic systems. Mathematically, it is expressed as: ΔU = Q - W Where: - ΔU = Change in internal energy of the system (Joules, J) - Q = Heat added **to** the system (J) - W = Work done **by** the system (J) **Key Points for NEET:** - Sign conventions: Q is positive if heat is added to the system; W is positive if work is done by the system. - Applies to all processes (isochoric, isobaric, isothermal, adiabatic). - Internal energy (U) is a **state function**, meaning it depends only on the initial and final states of the system, not the path taken.

Card 2

What is an **isothermal process**? Derive the work done by an ideal gas in such a process.

An **isothermal process** is a thermodynamic process in which the **temperature of the system remains constant** (ΔT = 0). For an ideal gas, this implies that the internal energy (U) remains constant (since U depends only on T for an ideal gas), and thus ΔU = 0. **Work Done in an Isothermal Process:** From the First Law: ΔU = Q - W → 0 = Q - W → Q = W Work done by the gas is equal to the heat added to the system. **Derivation of Work Done (W):** For an ideal gas, PV = nRT. Work done by the gas during expansion from volume V₁ to V₂ is: W = ∫ P dV (from V₁ to V₂) Substitute P = nRT/V: W = ∫ (nRT/V) dV = nRT ∫ (1/V) dV = nRT [ln V] from V₁ to V₂ W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) **Key Points for NEET:** - Isothermal processes are **slow** (quasi-static) to allow heat transfer and maintain constant temperature. - For expansion (V₂ > V₁), W is positive (work done by the gas). - For compression (V₂ < V₁), W is negative (work done on the gas).

Card 3

Explain the **Second Law of Thermodynamics** using the **Clausius statement** and the **Kelvin-Planck statement**.

The Second Law of Thermodynamics imposes **limitations on the direction of heat flow and the efficiency of heat engines**. It has two classical statements: 1. **Clausius Statement:** - It is **impossible** to construct a device that, operating in a cycle, produces no effect other than the transfer of heat from a **colder body to a hotter body**. - Implication: Heat **cannot spontaneously flow** from a colder body to a hotter body without external work. 2. **Kelvin-Planck Statement:** - It is **impossible** to construct a heat engine that, operating in a cycle, produces no effect other than the **absorption of heat from a single reservoir and the performance of an equivalent amount of work**. - Implication: **No heat engine can be 100% efficient**; some heat must always be rejected to a colder reservoir. **Key Points for NEET:** - Both statements are **equivalent**; violation of one implies violation of the other. - The Second Law introduces the concept of **entropy (S)**, a measure of disorder or randomness in a system. - For a **reversible process**, ΔS = ∫ (dQ_rev / T). For an **irreversible process**, ΔS > ∫ (dQ_irrev / T).

Card 4

Define **entropy (S)**. How does it change in a **reversible adiabatic process** and an **irreversible adiabatic process**?

**Entropy (S)** is a **state function** that quantifies the **degree of disorder or randomness** in a thermodynamic system. It is defined for a reversible process as: ΔS = ∫ (dQ_rev / T) Where: - ΔS = Change in entropy (J/K) - dQ_rev = Infinitesimal heat transfer in a reversible process (J) - T = Absolute temperature (K) **Entropy Change in Processes:** 1. **Reversible Adiabatic Process:** - In a reversible adiabatic process, **dQ_rev = 0** (no heat transfer). - Thus, ΔS = ∫ (dQ_rev / T) = 0. - **Entropy remains constant** (isentropic process). 2. **Irreversible Adiabatic Process:** - For an irreversible process, the inequality ΔS ≥ ∫ (dQ_irrev / T) holds. - Since dQ_irrev = 0 (adiabatic), ΔS ≥ 0. - **Entropy always increases** in an irreversible adiabatic process. **Key Points for NEET:** - Entropy is a **state function**, so ΔS depends only on initial and final states. - The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** can be stated as: **The entropy of an isolated system never decreases** (ΔS ≥ 0). - For the **universe (isolated system)**, entropy always increases over time.

Card 5

What is a **heat engine**? Derive the expression for its **efficiency (η)**.

A **heat engine** is a device that **converts heat energy into mechanical work** by operating in a **cyclic process** between two thermal reservoirs: - A **hot reservoir** (at temperature T_H) from which heat (Q_H) is absorbed. - A **cold reservoir** (at temperature T_C) to which heat (Q_C) is rejected. **Efficiency (η) of a Heat Engine:** Efficiency is defined as the **ratio of net work output (W_net) to the heat input (Q_H)**: η = W_net / Q_H From the First Law, for a cyclic process: ΔU = 0 → W_net = Q_H - Q_C Thus, η = (Q_H - Q_C) / Q_H = 1 - (Q_C / Q_H) **Carnot Efficiency (Maximum Possible Efficiency):** For a **reversible Carnot engine**, the efficiency is: η_Carnot = 1 - (T_C / T_H) **Key Points for NEET:** - Efficiency is **always less than 100%** (Second Law of Thermodynamics). - Real engines (e.g., petrol, diesel) have efficiencies **lower than Carnot efficiency** due to irreversibilities. - The **Carnot cycle** consists of two isothermal processes and two adiabatic processes.
