NEET Physics - Electromagnetic Induction Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define electromagnetic induction.

Electromagnetic induction is the process of generating an electromotive force (emf) or voltage in a conductor by changing the magnetic flux linked with it. This phenomenon was discovered by Michael Faraday and is the working principle behind generators, transformers, and inductors.

Card 2

State Faraday's First Law of Electromagnetic Induction.

Faraday's First Law states that whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed loop or circuit changes, an emf is induced in the loop. The magnitude of the induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux.

Card 3

State Faraday's Second Law of Electromagnetic Induction.

Faraday's Second Law states that the magnitude of the induced emf in a coil is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux through the coil. Mathematically, it is expressed as: ε = -dΦ/dt, where ε is the induced emf and Φ is the magnetic flux.

Card 4

What is Lenz's Law?

Lenz's Law states that the direction of the induced emf or current in a circuit is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. This is reflected in the negative sign in Faraday's Second Law (ε = -dΦ/dt).

Card 5

Explain the significance of the negative sign in Faraday's Second Law (ε = -dΦ/dt).

The negative sign in Faraday's Second Law represents Lenz's Law. It indicates that the induced emf opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it. This ensures the conservation of energy, as the induced current creates a magnetic field that counteracts the original change in flux.

Card 6

What is magnetic flux? Give its formula and SI unit.

Magnetic flux (Φ) is a measure of the quantity of magnetic field passing through a given surface. It is given by the formula: Φ = B · A = BA cosθ, where B is the magnetic field, A is the area of the surface, and θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal to the surface. The SI unit of magnetic flux is the weber (Wb).

Card 7

Derive the expression for induced emf in a coil of N turns.

For a coil of N turns, the total magnetic flux linked with the coil is NΦ, where Φ is the flux through one turn. According to Faraday's Second Law, the induced emf (ε) is given by: ε = -d(NΦ)/dt = -N(dΦ/dt). This shows that the induced emf is proportional to the number of turns in the coil.

Card 8

What is motional emf? Give its formula for a conductor moving in a magnetic field.

Motional emf is the emf induced in a conductor moving through a magnetic field. For a straight conductor of length l moving with velocity v perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field B, the motional emf (ε) is given by: ε = Blv. This is derived from the Lorentz force acting on the free charges in the conductor.

Card 9

Explain how a generator works using the principle of electromagnetic induction.

A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy using electromagnetic induction. It consists of a coil rotating in a magnetic field. As the coil rotates, the magnetic flux through it changes continuously, inducing an emf according to Faraday's Law. The induced emf drives a current through an external circuit, producing electricity. The direction of the induced current alternates with the rotation, resulting in alternating current (AC).

Card 10

What is self-induction? Define self-inductance and give its SI unit.

Self-induction is the phenomenon where a changing current in a coil induces an emf in the same coil, opposing the change in current. Self-inductance (L) is the property of a coil that quantifies its ability to oppose changes in current. It is defined as the ratio of the induced emf (ε) to the rate of change of current (dI/dt): ε = -L(dI/dt). The SI unit of self-inductance is the henry (H).

Card 11

What is mutual induction? Define mutual inductance and give its SI unit.

Mutual induction is the phenomenon where a changing current in one coil induces an emf in a nearby coil. Mutual inductance (M) is the property that quantifies the ability of one coil to induce an emf in another coil. It is defined as the ratio of the induced emf (ε₂) in the secondary coil to the rate of change of current (dI₁/dt) in the primary coil: ε₂ = -M(dI₁/dt). The SI unit of mutual inductance is the henry (H).

Card 12

Derive the expression for the energy stored in an inductor.

The energy stored in an inductor is due to the work done against the induced emf while establishing the current. For an inductor of inductance L carrying current I, the power supplied is P = εI = LI(dI/dt). The total energy (U) stored is the integral of power over time: U = ∫₀ᴵ LI dI = ½LI². Thus, the energy stored in an inductor is U = ½LI².

Card 13

What is eddy current? Mention its applications and disadvantages.

Eddy currents are loops of electrical current induced within conductors by a changing magnetic field. Applications include: electromagnetic braking, induction heating, and metal detectors. Disadvantages include energy loss as heat in transformers and electrical machines, which reduces efficiency.

Card 14

Explain the working principle of a transformer.

A transformer works on the principle of mutual induction. It consists of two coils, primary and secondary, wound on a common magnetic core. When an alternating current flows through the primary coil, it creates a changing magnetic flux in the core, which induces an emf in the secondary coil. The ratio of the induced emfs in the primary and secondary coils is equal to the ratio of their number of turns: ε₁/ε₂ = N₁/N₂. Transformers are used to step up or step down AC voltages.

Card 15

What is the difference between self-inductance and mutual inductance?

Self-inductance refers to the property of a single coil to induce an emf in itself due to a change in current through it. Mutual inductance refers to the property of one coil to induce an emf in another nearby coil due to a change in current in the first coil. Self-inductance depends on the geometry and material of the coil, while mutual inductance also depends on the relative position and orientation of the two coils.

Card 16

A coil of area 0.1 m² is placed perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.5 T. If the magnetic field is reduced to zero in 0.1 s, calculate the induced emf in the coil.

Using Faraday's Second Law: ε = -dΦ/dt. Magnetic flux Φ = BA = 0.5 T × 0.1 m² = 0.05 Wb. Change in flux ΔΦ = 0 - 0.05 Wb = -0.05 Wb. Time interval Δt = 0.1 s. Induced emf ε = -(-0.05 Wb / 0.1 s) = 0.5 V.

Card 17

A conductor of length 0.5 m moves with a velocity of 2 m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.2 T. Calculate the motional emf induced in the conductor.

Motional emf ε = Blv = 0.2 T × 0.5 m × 2 m/s = 0.2 V.

Card 18

A coil of 100 turns has a magnetic flux of 0.02 Wb passing through it. If the flux is reduced to zero in 0.05 s, calculate the induced emf in the coil.

Using Faraday's Second Law for N turns: ε = -N(dΦ/dt). Change in flux ΔΦ = 0 - 0.02 Wb = -0.02 Wb. Time interval Δt = 0.05 s. Induced emf ε = -100 × (-0.02 Wb / 0.05 s) = 40 V.

Card 19

An inductor of inductance 0.5 H carries a current of 2 A. Calculate the energy stored in the inductor.

Energy stored in an inductor U = ½LI² = 0.5 × 0.5 H × (2 A)² = 1 J.

Card 20

What is the direction of the induced current in a loop when the north pole of a magnet is moved towards the loop?

According to Lenz's Law, the induced current in the loop will flow in such a direction that it opposes the motion of the magnet. Thus, the face of the loop towards the magnet will behave as a north pole, repelling the magnet. The induced current will flow in an anticlockwise direction when viewed from the side of the magnet.
