Card 1

Define **stress** in the context of mechanical properties of solids. Include its formula, SI unit, and types.

**Stress** is the restoring force per unit area developed inside a body when subjected to an external deforming force. It quantifies the internal resistance of the material to deformation. **Formula**: Stress (σ) = Force (F) / Area (A) **SI Unit**: Pascal (Pa) or N/m² **Types of Stress**: 1. **Tensile Stress**: Acts perpendicular to the surface, tending to elongate the body. 2. **Compressive Stress**: Acts perpendicular to the surface, tending to compress the body. 3. **Shear Stress**: Acts parallel to the surface, causing layers of the material to slide over each other. 4. **Hydraulic Stress**: Stress due to fluid pressure, acting uniformly in all directions (e.g., in a spherical object submerged in fluid).

Card 2

What is **strain**? Explain its formula, unit, and types. How does it differ from stress?

**Strain** is the measure of deformation representing the displacement between particles in a material body. It is a dimensionless quantity (no unit) as it is a ratio of lengths. **Formula**: Strain (ε) = Change in Length (ΔL) / Original Length (L) **Types of Strain**: 1. **Longitudinal Strain**: Change in length per unit original length (due to tensile/compressive stress). 2. **Volumetric Strain**: Change in volume per unit original volume (due to hydraulic stress). 3. **Shear Strain**: Angular deformation (change in shape) due to shear stress, measured as the tangent of the angle of deformation (tanθ). **Difference from Stress**: Stress is a measure of force per unit area (internal resistance), while strain is a measure of deformation (relative change in dimension). Stress causes strain.

Card 3

State **Hooke’s Law** and its limitations. What is the proportionality constant called?

**Hooke’s Law** states that, within the elastic limit of a material, the stress developed is directly proportional to the strain produced. **Formula**: Stress (σ) ∝ Strain (ε) ⇒ σ = E × ε **Proportionality Constant (E)**: Called **Young’s Modulus** (for longitudinal stress-strain) or **Modulus of Elasticity**. It measures the stiffness of the material. **Limitations**: 1. Applies only within the **elastic limit** of the material. Beyond this, the material deforms permanently (plastic deformation). 2. Not valid for large deformations or non-linear materials (e.g., rubber). 3. Assumes the material is homogeneous and isotropic.

Card 4

Define **Young’s Modulus (Y)**. Derive its formula and state its SI unit. What does a higher Young’s Modulus indicate?

**Young’s Modulus (Y)** is a measure of the stiffness of a solid material. It defines the relationship between tensile/compressive stress and longitudinal strain within the elastic limit. **Derivation**: From Hooke’s Law: σ = Y × ε For longitudinal strain: ε = ΔL / L For tensile stress: σ = F / A Substituting: F / A = Y × (ΔL / L) ⇒ Y = (F × L) / (A × ΔL) **SI Unit**: Pascal (Pa) or N/m² **Interpretation**: A higher Young’s Modulus indicates a stiffer material (e.g., steel has a higher Y than rubber), meaning it requires more stress to produce the same strain.

Card 5

Explain **bulk modulus (B)** and **shear modulus (G)**. Include their formulas, SI units, and physical significance.

**Bulk Modulus (B)**: Measures the resistance of a material to uniform compression (hydraulic stress). **Formula**: B = (Hydraulic Stress) / (Volumetric Strain) = (ΔP) / (ΔV / V) **SI Unit**: Pascal (Pa) **Physical Significance**: Higher B indicates greater resistance to volume change (e.g., solids have higher B than gases). **Shear Modulus (G)**: Measures the resistance of a material to shear stress (shape deformation). **Formula**: G = (Shear Stress) / (Shear Strain) = (F / A) / tanθ **SI Unit**: Pascal (Pa) **Physical Significance**: Higher G indicates greater rigidity (e.g., metals have higher G than plastics). **Note**: For isotropic materials, Y, B, and G are related by the equation: 9/Y = 3/G + 1/B.

Card 6

What is **Poisson’s Ratio (σ)**? State its formula, range of values, and significance.

**Poisson’s Ratio (σ)** is the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain when a material is subjected to tensile or compressive stress. It quantifies the transverse deformation. **Formula**: σ = - (Lateral Strain) / (Longitudinal Strain) = - (ΔD / D) / (ΔL / L) **Range of Values**: - Theoretically: -1 < σ < 0.5 - Practically for most materials: 0 < σ < 0.5 (e.g., cork: ~0, rubber: ~0.5, steel: ~0.3) **Significance**: - Indicates how much a material contracts laterally when stretched longitudinally. - Materials with σ ≈ 0.5 (e.g., rubber) are nearly incompressible. - Negative Poisson’s ratio (auxetic materials) expand laterally when stretched.

Card 7

Describe **elastic limit**, **yield point**, **plastic deformation**, and **breaking stress** with a stress-strain curve.

**Stress-Strain Curve Terms**: 1. **Elastic Limit**: The maximum stress a material can withstand without permanent deformation. Beyond this, the material does not return to its original shape. 2. **Yield Point**: The stress at which the material begins to deform plastically. Beyond this, strain increases rapidly even with small increases in stress. 3. **Plastic Deformation**: Permanent deformation occurring after the yield point. The material does not return to its original shape when stress is removed. 4. **Breaking Stress (Ultimate Tensile Strength)**: The maximum stress a material can withstand before fracture. Beyond this, the material fails. **Curve Regions**: - **Elastic Region**: Linear (Hooke’s Law valid), material returns to original shape. - **Plastic Region**: Non-linear, permanent deformation occurs. - **Fracture Point**: Material breaks. **Note**: Brittle materials (e.g., glass) have little to no plastic region, while ductile materials (e.g., copper) have a pronounced plastic region.

Card 8

A steel wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm² is stretched by 1 mm under a load of 100 N. Calculate its Young’s Modulus. If the elastic limit of steel is 2.5 × 10⁸ Pa, will the wire return to its original length when the load is removed?

**Given**: - Original Length (L) = 2 m - Cross-Sectional Area (A) = 1 mm² = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m² - Extension (ΔL) = 1 mm = 1 × 10⁻³ m - Force (F) = 100 N - Elastic Limit of Steel = 2.5 × 10⁸ Pa **Step 1: Calculate Stress (σ)** σ = F / A = 100 N / (1 × 10⁻⁶ m²) = 1 × 10⁸ Pa **Step 2: Calculate Strain (ε)** ε = ΔL / L = (1 × 10⁻³ m) / 2 m = 5 × 10⁻⁴ **Step 3: Calculate Young’s Modulus (Y)** Y = σ / ε = (1 × 10⁸ Pa) / (5 × 10⁻⁴) = 2 × 10¹¹ Pa **Step 4: Check Elastic Limit** Stress (1 × 10⁸ Pa) < Elastic Limit (2.5 × 10⁸ Pa) **Conclusion**: The wire is within the elastic limit and will return to its original length when the load is removed.
