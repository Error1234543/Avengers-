Card 1

What is the key postulate of Rutherford's nuclear model of the atom?

Rutherford proposed that an atom consists of a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus at the center, with electrons revolving around it in circular orbits, similar to planets around the sun. The model was based on the gold foil experiment, where most alpha particles passed through the foil, but some were deflected at large angles, indicating the presence of a concentrated positive charge.

Card 2

Why did Rutherford's model fail to explain the stability of the atom?

According to classical electromagnetic theory, an accelerating charged particle (like an electron in circular motion) should continuously emit radiation, lose energy, and spiral into the nucleus. Rutherford's model could not account for why atoms remain stable and do not collapse.

Card 3

State Bohr's first postulate for the hydrogen atom.

Bohr's first postulate states that an electron in an atom can revolve in certain stable orbits without radiating energy. These orbits are called stationary orbits and are defined by the condition that the angular momentum of the electron is quantized: L = n(h/2π), where n is a positive integer (n = 1, 2, 3, ...), and h is Planck's constant.

Card 4

Derive the expression for the radius of the nth orbit in Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom.

In Bohr's model, the centripetal force for the electron is provided by the electrostatic attraction between the electron and the nucleus. Equating the two: (mv²)/r = (1/4πε₀)(e²/r²). Using Bohr's quantization condition, mvr = n(h/2π). Solving these equations, the radius of the nth orbit is rₙ = (4πε₀)(n²h²)/(4π²me²) = n²a₀, where a₀ = (4πε₀)(h²)/(4π²me²) ≈ 0.529 Å is the Bohr radius.

Card 5

What is the formula for the energy of the electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom according to Bohr's model?

The total energy Eₙ of the electron in the nth orbit is the sum of its kinetic and potential energy: Eₙ = - (1/4πε₀)² (2π²me⁴)/(n²h²) = - (13.6 eV)/n². Here, m is the mass of the electron, e is the charge of the electron, ε₀ is the permittivity of free space, and h is Planck's constant.

Card 6

Explain the origin of spectral lines in the hydrogen spectrum using Bohr's model.

According to Bohr's model, when an electron transitions from a higher energy orbit (n = n₂) to a lower energy orbit (n = n₁), it emits a photon with energy equal to the difference in energy between the two orbits: ΔE = Eₙ₂ - Eₙ₁ = 13.6 eV (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²). The wavelength of the emitted photon is given by λ = hc/ΔE. Different series of spectral lines (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, etc.) correspond to transitions to different lower energy levels (n₁ = 1, 2, 3, ...).

Card 7

What is the Rydberg formula for the hydrogen spectrum?

The Rydberg formula gives the wavelength of the spectral lines in the hydrogen spectrum: 1/λ = R (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²), where R is the Rydberg constant (R ≈ 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹), n₁ is the principal quantum number of the lower energy level, and n₂ is the principal quantum number of the higher energy level (n₂ > n₁).

Card 8

What is the significance of the Lyman series in the hydrogen spectrum?

The Lyman series corresponds to transitions where the electron falls to the ground state (n₁ = 1) from higher energy levels (n₂ = 2, 3, 4, ...). The spectral lines in this series lie in the ultraviolet region of the electromagnetic spectrum. The Lyman series is important for studying the energy levels of the hydrogen atom and for astronomical observations of hydrogen in space.

Card 9

How does Bohr's model explain the Balmer series?

The Balmer series corresponds to transitions where the electron falls to the second energy level (n₁ = 2) from higher energy levels (n₂ = 3, 4, 5, ...). The wavelengths of these transitions lie in the visible region of the spectrum, making the Balmer series the first to be observed experimentally. Bohr's model successfully predicted the wavelengths of the Balmer lines using the energy difference formula.

Card 10

What are the limitations of Bohr's model of the atom?

Bohr's model has several limitations: (1) It only works for hydrogen and hydrogen-like atoms (e.g., He⁺, Li²⁺) and fails for multi-electron atoms. (2) It does not explain the fine structure of spectral lines (observed under high resolution). (3) It violates the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle by assuming fixed orbits for electrons. (4) It does not account for the wave nature of electrons. (5) It cannot explain the Zeeman effect (splitting of spectral lines in a magnetic field) or the Stark effect (splitting in an electric field).

Card 11

What is the de Broglie wavelength of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom?

According to de Broglie's hypothesis, the wavelength of a particle is given by λ = h/p, where p is the momentum. For an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom, the circumference of the orbit is 2πrₙ = nλ (from Bohr's quantization condition). Substituting rₙ = n²a₀, we get λ = 2πa₀n. Thus, the de Broglie wavelength of the electron in the nth orbit is λ = 2πa₀n.

Card 12

Explain the concept of excitation and ionization energy in the context of the hydrogen atom.

Excitation energy is the energy required to move an electron from the ground state (n = 1) to a higher energy level (n > 1). For example, the excitation energy to move an electron from n = 1 to n = 2 is ΔE = 13.6 eV (1 - 1/4) = 10.2 eV. Ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron completely from the atom (i.e., move it from n = 1 to n = ∞). For hydrogen, the ionization energy is 13.6 eV.

Card 13

What is the energy of the photon emitted when an electron transitions from n = 3 to n = 2 in a hydrogen atom?

Using the energy formula for the nth orbit: Eₙ = -13.6 eV/n². The energy of the photon emitted is ΔE = E₃ - E₂ = -13.6 eV (1/9 - 1/4) = -13.6 eV (-5/36) = 1.89 eV. The positive value indicates energy is emitted.

Card 14

How does the energy of the electron vary with the principal quantum number n in Bohr's model?

In Bohr's model, the energy of the electron in the nth orbit is given by Eₙ = -13.6 eV/n². As n increases, the energy becomes less negative, meaning the electron is less tightly bound to the nucleus. The energy levels get closer together as n increases, approaching zero (ionization) as n approaches infinity.

Card 15

What is the angular momentum of an electron in the first orbit (n = 1) of a hydrogen atom?

According to Bohr's quantization condition, the angular momentum L of an electron in the nth orbit is L = n(h/2π). For n = 1, L = h/2π ≈ 1.054 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s.

Card 16

Explain the term 'ground state' and 'excited state' in the context of an atom.

The ground state of an atom is the state in which all electrons are in their lowest possible energy levels. For hydrogen, this corresponds to the electron being in the n = 1 orbit. An excited state is any state where one or more electrons are in higher energy levels (n > 1). Atoms in excited states are unstable and tend to return to the ground state by emitting photons.
