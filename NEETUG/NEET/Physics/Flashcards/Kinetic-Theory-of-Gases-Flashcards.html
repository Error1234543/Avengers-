NEET Physics: Kinetic Theory of Gases - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

State the basic assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases.

1. Gases consist of a large number of identical molecules in random, rapid motion. 2. The volume occupied by the molecules themselves is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas. 3. Intermolecular forces are negligible except during collisions. 4. Collisions between molecules and with the container walls are perfectly elastic. 5. The average kinetic energy of the molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature of the gas.

Card 2

Define mean free path (λ) and write its formula.

Mean free path (λ) is the average distance a gas molecule travels between successive collisions. Formula: λ = 1 / (√2 π d² n), where d is the molecular diameter and n is the number density of molecules (number per unit volume).

Card 3

Derive the expression for pressure exerted by an ideal gas using kinetic theory.

Consider N molecules of mass m in a cubic container of side L. The change in momentum of a molecule colliding with a wall is 2mv_x. The number of collisions per unit time on a wall is (v_x / 2L). Force on one wall = (2mv_x)(v_x / 2L) = mv_x² / L. Total force = Σ mv_x² / L. Pressure P = Force / Area = (Σ mv_x²) / L³ = (N m v_x²_avg) / V. Since v²_avg = 3 v_x²_avg, P = (1/3)(N m v_rms² / V).

Card 4

What is the relationship between the root mean square speed (v_rms), average speed (v_avg), and most probable speed (v_mp) of gas molecules?

v_rms : v_avg : v_mp = √(3RT/M) : √(8RT/πM) : √(2RT/M) = √3 : √(8/π) : √2 ≈ 1.732 : 1.596 : 1.414. v_rms > v_avg > v_mp.

Card 5

Explain the concept of degrees of freedom for a gas molecule and state the equipartition theorem.

Degrees of freedom (f) are the independent ways a molecule can possess energy. For a monatomic gas, f = 3 (translational). For a diatomic gas, f = 5 (3 translational + 2 rotational). The equipartition theorem states that each degree of freedom contributes (1/2)k_B T of energy per molecule, where k_B is Boltzmann's constant and T is absolute temperature.

Card 6

Write the formula for the total internal energy (U) of n moles of an ideal gas and explain its dependence on temperature.

U = (f/2) n R T, where f is the number of degrees of freedom, n is the number of moles, R is the universal gas constant, and T is the absolute temperature. For a monatomic gas, U = (3/2) n R T; for a diatomic gas, U = (5/2) n R T. Internal energy depends only on temperature for an ideal gas.

Card 7

What is the Maxwell-Boltzmann speed distribution? Sketch its general shape and label key points.

The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution gives the probability of finding gas molecules with a given speed. The curve starts at zero, rises to a peak (most probable speed, v_mp), then falls off exponentially. The average speed (v_avg) is to the right of v_mp, and the root mean square speed (v_rms) is further right. The area under the curve represents the total number of molecules.

Card 8

Explain why real gases deviate from ideal gas behavior at high pressures and low temperatures.

At high pressures, the volume occupied by gas molecules becomes significant compared to the container volume, violating the kinetic theory assumption of negligible molecular volume. At low temperatures, intermolecular forces become significant, violating the assumption of negligible forces. These conditions cause real gases to deviate from the ideal gas law PV = nRT.

Card 9

A gas at 300 K has a root mean square speed of 500 m/s. Calculate its root mean square speed at 600 K.

v_rms ∝ √T. v_rms2 / v_rms1 = √(T2 / T1) = √(600 / 300) = √2. v_rms2 = 500 × √2 ≈ 707 m/s.

Card 10

What is the kinetic interpretation of temperature?

The average kinetic energy of the molecules in a gas is directly proportional to the absolute temperature of the gas. Specifically, (1/2) m v_rms² = (3/2) k_B T, where m is the molecular mass, v_rms is the root mean square speed, k_B is Boltzmann's constant, and T is the absolute temperature. Temperature is thus a measure of the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules.

Card 11

Explain the concept of mean free time and how it relates to mean free path.

Mean free time (τ) is the average time between successive collisions of a gas molecule. It is related to the mean free path (λ) and the average speed (v_avg) by τ = λ / v_avg. A longer mean free path or a slower average speed results in a longer mean free time.

Card 12

For a diatomic gas at room temperature, why is the molar heat capacity at constant volume (C_V) approximately (5/2)R instead of (7/2)R?

At room temperature, vibrational modes of a diatomic molecule are not excited (they require higher temperatures). Thus, only translational (3 degrees) and rotational (2 degrees) modes contribute to the internal energy, giving f = 5. C_V = (f/2)R = (5/2)R. Vibrational modes contribute an additional R at higher temperatures, making C_V = (7/2)R.

Card 13

Calculate the root mean square speed of oxygen molecules (O₂) at 27°C. (Molar mass of O₂ = 32 g/mol, R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

v_rms = √(3RT / M). T = 27°C = 300 K, M = 32 × 10⁻³ kg/mol. v_rms = √(3 × 8.314 × 300 / 32 × 10⁻³) = √(233,831.25) ≈ 483.6 m/s.

Card 14

What is the significance of the van der Waals equation in the context of the kinetic theory of gases?

The van der Waals equation (P + a(n/V)²)(V - nb) = nRT corrects the ideal gas law for real gases by accounting for intermolecular forces (a term) and the finite volume of molecules (b term). It addresses the two main assumptions of kinetic theory that break down for real gases: negligible intermolecular forces and negligible molecular volume.

Card 15

Explain how the kinetic theory of gases accounts for the phenomenon of gas pressure.

Gas pressure arises from the continuous, random collisions of gas molecules with the walls of the container. Each collision imparts a small force to the wall. The cumulative effect of billions of such collisions per second results in a steady force, which, when divided by the area of the wall, gives the pressure of the gas. This is quantitatively expressed as P = (1/3)(N m v_rms² / V).
