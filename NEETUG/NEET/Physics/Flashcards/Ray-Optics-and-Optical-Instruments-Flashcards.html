NEET Physics - Ray Optics and Optical Instruments: Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define the term 'refractive index' of a medium. State its mathematical expression and the condition under which it is measured.

The refractive index (n) of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (c) to the speed of light in the medium (v). Mathematically, n = c/v. It is measured under the condition that light travels from vacuum into the medium at a specified wavelength (usually sodium D-line, 589 nm).

Card 2

State Snell's Law of refraction. What are the variables involved and their units?

Snell's Law states that the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence (i) to the sine of the angle of refraction (r) is constant and equal to the ratio of the refractive indices of the two media. Mathematically: n₁ sin(i) = n₂ sin(r). Here, n₁ and n₂ are the refractive indices of the first and second medium (dimensionless), and i and r are angles measured in degrees or radians from the normal.

Card 3

A ray of light passes from air (n = 1) into water (n = 1.33) at an angle of incidence of 45°. Calculate the angle of refraction. What common mistake should be avoided in this calculation?

Using Snell's Law: 1 * sin(45°) = 1.33 * sin(r). Thus, sin(r) = sin(45°)/1.33 ≈ 0.7071/1.33 ≈ 0.5317. Therefore, r = sin⁻¹(0.5317) ≈ 32.1°. Common mistake: Forgetting to use the inverse sine function or confusing degrees with radians in the calculator.

Card 4

What is the critical angle for total internal reflection? Derive its expression for a ray traveling from a denser medium (n₁) to a rarer medium (n₂).

The critical angle (θ_c) is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is 90°. Using Snell's Law: n₁ sin(θ_c) = n₂ sin(90°). Since sin(90°) = 1, θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁). This applies only when n₁ > n₂.

Card 5

Explain the formation of a real image by a concave mirror. Draw a ray diagram and label the key points: object, image, pole, focus, and center of curvature.

A real image is formed by a concave mirror when the object is placed beyond the focus (F). Rays parallel to the principal axis reflect through F, and rays passing through F reflect parallel to the principal axis. The intersection of these reflected rays forms the real, inverted image. Key points: Pole (P) is the mirror's center; Focus (F) is midway between P and the Center of Curvature (C). The image is real, inverted, and can be magnified or diminished depending on the object's position.

Card 6

Derive the mirror formula for a spherical mirror. What are the sign conventions used in the derivation?

Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u, where f is the focal length, v is the image distance, and u is the object distance. Sign conventions: Distances are measured from the pole (P) along the principal axis. Distances in the direction of incident light are negative (real objects), and distances opposite to incident light are positive (virtual images). Focal length is negative for concave mirrors and positive for convex mirrors.

Card 7

A concave mirror has a focal length of 15 cm. An object is placed 20 cm in front of it. Calculate the image distance and magnification. Is the image real or virtual?

Using the mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u → 1/(-15) = 1/v + 1/(-20). Solving: 1/v = -1/15 + 1/20 = (-4 + 3)/60 = -1/60 → v = -60 cm. Magnification (m) = -v/u = -(-60)/(-20) = -3. The image is real (v is negative) and inverted (m is negative), with a magnification of 3 (enlarged).

Card 8

What is the lens maker's formula? Explain the variables and the conditions under which it is valid.

Lens maker's formula: 1/f = (n - 1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂), where f is the focal length, n is the refractive index of the lens material, and R₁ and R₂ are the radii of curvature of the two surfaces. It is valid for thin lenses in air, where the lens thickness is negligible compared to R₁ and R₂. Sign convention: R is positive if the center of curvature is to the right of the surface and negative otherwise.

Card 9

A convex lens has a focal length of 20 cm. An object is placed 30 cm from the lens. Calculate the image distance and describe the nature of the image.

Using the lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u → 1/20 = 1/v - 1/(-30) → 1/v = 1/20 - 1/30 = (3 - 2)/60 = 1/60 → v = 60 cm. Magnification (m) = v/u = 60/(-30) = -2. The image is real (v is positive), inverted (m is negative), and magnified (|m| > 1).

Card 10

Explain the concept of power of a lens. What is its unit and how is it related to focal length?

The power of a lens (P) is the reciprocal of its focal length (f) measured in meters. Mathematically, P = 1/f. The unit is dioptre (D), where 1 D = 1 m⁻¹. A lens with a shorter focal length has higher power. For example, a lens with f = 0.5 m has P = 2 D.

Card 11

Describe the working principle of a compound microscope. Draw a ray diagram and label the objective lens, eyepiece, and final image.

A compound microscope uses two convex lenses: the objective lens (short focal length) and the eyepiece (longer focal length). The object is placed just beyond the focus of the objective, forming a real, inverted, and magnified image. This image acts as the object for the eyepiece, which further magnifies it to form a virtual, inverted, and highly magnified final image. The total magnification is the product of the magnifications of the objective and eyepiece.

Card 12

Derive the expression for the magnifying power of a simple microscope (magnifying glass) when the final image is formed at the least distance of distinct vision (D).

Magnifying power (M) = 1 + D/f, where D is the least distance of distinct vision (typically 25 cm) and f is the focal length of the lens. Derivation: The object is placed within the focus of the lens, forming a virtual image at D. Using the lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u → 1/f = 1/(-D) - 1/u → 1/u = -1/D - 1/f. Magnification (m) = v/u = (-D)/u = D(1/D + 1/f) = 1 + D/f.

Card 13

What is the difference between angular magnification and linear magnification? Which one is relevant for optical instruments like microscopes and telescopes?

Linear magnification (m) is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object (m = h'/h). Angular magnification (M) is the ratio of the angle subtended by the image at the eye to the angle subtended by the object at the eye when placed at the least distance of distinct vision. For optical instruments like microscopes and telescopes, angular magnification is relevant because it measures how much larger the image appears to the eye.

Card 14

Explain the working principle of an astronomical refracting telescope. How is its magnifying power calculated?

An astronomical refracting telescope consists of two convex lenses: the objective (long focal length) and the eyepiece (short focal length). The objective forms a real, inverted image of a distant object at its focus. The eyepiece magnifies this image to form a virtual, inverted final image at infinity. Magnifying power (M) = f₀/fₑ, where f₀ is the focal length of the objective and fₑ is the focal length of the eyepiece.
