NEET Physics - Alternating Current (Exam-Focused Flashcards)

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define alternating current (AC).

Alternating current (AC) is an electric current that periodically reverses its direction, typically sinusoidally, with a frequency measured in hertz (Hz). In India, the standard AC frequency is 50 Hz, meaning the current completes 50 cycles per second. AC is used for power transmission because it can be easily stepped up or down using transformers, reducing energy loss over long distances.

Card 2

What is the instantaneous value of an alternating voltage given by v = V₀ sin(ωt)?

The instantaneous value of the alternating voltage is represented by v = V₀ sin(ωt), where V₀ is the peak voltage (maximum amplitude), ω is the angular frequency (ω = 2πf), and t is time. This equation describes how the voltage varies sinusoidally with time.

Card 3

Define the root mean square (RMS) value of an AC current. How is it related to the peak value?

The RMS value of an AC current (I_rms) is the equivalent direct current (DC) that would dissipate the same average power in a resistive load. It is related to the peak value (I₀) by the formula: I_rms = I₀ / √2. Similarly, for voltage: V_rms = V₀ / √2. RMS values are used to express AC quantities in practical applications.

Card 4

What is the phase relationship between voltage and current in a purely resistive AC circuit?

In a purely resistive AC circuit, the voltage and current are in phase with each other. This means they reach their maximum and minimum values simultaneously, and the phase difference (φ) between them is zero. The power dissipated in the resistor is given by P = V_rms * I_rms.

Card 5

Describe the phase relationship between voltage and current in a purely inductive AC circuit.

In a purely inductive AC circuit, the current lags behind the voltage by a phase angle of 90° (π/2 radians). This is because the inductor opposes changes in current, causing the current to reach its peak after the voltage. The inductive reactance (X_L) is given by X_L = ωL, where L is the inductance.

Card 6

What is inductive reactance? How does it depend on frequency?

Inductive reactance (X_L) is the opposition offered by an inductor to the flow of alternating current. It is given by X_L = ωL = 2πfL, where ω is the angular frequency, f is the frequency, and L is the inductance. Inductive reactance increases linearly with frequency, meaning higher frequencies result in greater opposition to current flow.

Card 7

Describe the phase relationship between voltage and current in a purely capacitive AC circuit.

In a purely capacitive AC circuit, the current leads the voltage by a phase angle of 90° (π/2 radians). This is because the capacitor charges and discharges in response to the voltage, causing the current to reach its peak before the voltage. The capacitive reactance (X_C) is given by X_C = 1/(ωC).

Card 8

What is capacitive reactance? How does it depend on frequency?

Capacitive reactance (X_C) is the opposition offered by a capacitor to the flow of alternating current. It is given by X_C = 1/(ωC) = 1/(2πfC), where ω is the angular frequency, f is the frequency, and C is the capacitance. Capacitive reactance decreases with increasing frequency, meaning higher frequencies result in less opposition to current flow.

Card 9

Define impedance in an AC circuit. How is it calculated for an R-L-C series circuit?

Impedance (Z) is the total opposition offered by an AC circuit to the flow of current, combining resistance (R), inductive reactance (X_L), and capacitive reactance (X_C). For an R-L-C series circuit, impedance is calculated using the formula: Z = √(R² + (X_L - X_C)²). The unit of impedance is ohms (Ω).

Card 10

What is the condition for resonance in an R-L-C series circuit? What is the resonant frequency?

Resonance in an R-L-C series circuit occurs when the inductive reactance (X_L) equals the capacitive reactance (X_C), i.e., X_L = X_C. At resonance, the impedance is minimum and equal to the resistance (Z = R), and the current in the circuit is maximum. The resonant frequency (f₀) is given by f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)).

Card 11

What is the power factor of an AC circuit? How is it calculated?

The power factor (cos φ) of an AC circuit is the ratio of the real power (P) dissipated in the circuit to the apparent power (S). It is calculated as cos φ = R / Z, where R is the resistance and Z is the impedance. The power factor indicates the efficiency of the circuit in utilizing the supplied power and ranges from 0 to 1.

Card 12

What is the difference between real power, reactive power, and apparent power in an AC circuit?

1. Real power (P): The actual power dissipated in the circuit, measured in watts (W). It is given by P = V_rms * I_rms * cos φ. 2. Reactive power (Q): The power stored and released by inductors and capacitors, measured in volt-amperes reactive (VAR). It is given by Q = V_rms * I_rms * sin φ. 3. Apparent power (S): The product of RMS voltage and RMS current, measured in volt-amperes (VA). It is given by S = V_rms * I_rms and represents the total power supplied to the circuit.

Card 13

What is a transformer? Describe its working principle.

A transformer is a static electrical device used to transfer electrical energy between two or more circuits through electromagnetic induction. It works on the principle of Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction and consists of two coils, the primary and secondary, wound on a common magnetic core. When an alternating voltage is applied to the primary coil, it induces an alternating magnetic flux in the core, which in turn induces an alternating voltage in the secondary coil. The voltage transformation ratio is given by V_s / V_p = N_s / N_p, where V_s and V_p are the secondary and primary voltages, and N_s and N_p are the number of turns in the secondary and primary coils, respectively.

Card 14

What is the difference between a step-up and a step-down transformer?

1. Step-up transformer: Increases the voltage from the primary to the secondary coil. The number of turns in the secondary coil (N_s) is greater than in the primary coil (N_p), i.e., N_s > N_p. 2. Step-down transformer: Decreases the voltage from the primary to the secondary coil. The number of turns in the secondary coil (N_s) is less than in the primary coil (N_p), i.e., N_s < N_p. Both types of transformers work on the same principle of electromagnetic induction.

Card 15

What is the efficiency of a transformer? How is it calculated?

The efficiency (η) of a transformer is the ratio of the output power (P_out) to the input power (P_in), expressed as a percentage. It is calculated using the formula: η = (P_out / P_in) * 100%. The efficiency of an ideal transformer is 100%, but real transformers have efficiencies typically between 95% and 99% due to losses such as copper losses (I²R losses) and iron losses (hysteresis and eddy current losses).

Card 16

What are the main types of losses in a transformer?

The main types of losses in a transformer are: 1. Copper losses (I²R losses): Power dissipated as heat due to the resistance of the primary and secondary windings. 2. Iron losses: Power lost in the magnetic core, which includes: a. Hysteresis loss: Energy lost due to the repeated magnetization and demagnetization of the core material. b. Eddy current loss: Energy lost due to circulating currents induced in the core by the changing magnetic flux. These losses reduce the efficiency of the transformer.

Card 17

What is the quality factor (Q) of an R-L-C series circuit? How is it calculated?

The quality factor (Q) of an R-L-C series circuit is a measure of the sharpness of the resonance peak. It is defined as the ratio of the resonant frequency (f₀) to the bandwidth (Δf) of the circuit. The quality factor can be calculated using the formula: Q = f₀ / Δf = ω₀L / R = 1 / (ω₀CR), where ω₀ is the angular resonant frequency, L is the inductance, R is the resistance, and C is the capacitance. A higher Q factor indicates a sharper resonance peak and lower energy loss.
