NEET Physics - Wave Optics Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define wavefront. How is it related to a ray of light?

A wavefront is the locus of all points in a wave that are in the same phase of oscillation. For a point source, wavefronts are spherical; for a distant source, they approximate plane wavefronts. A ray of light is a line perpendicular to the wavefront, indicating the direction of wave propagation.

Card 2

State Huygens' Principle and its significance in wave optics.

Huygens' Principle states that every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets. The new wavefront at a later time is the envelope of these secondary wavelets. This principle explains reflection, refraction, and diffraction of light by treating light as a wave.

Card 3

Derive the law of reflection using Huygens' Principle.

Consider a plane wavefront AB incident on a reflecting surface. At time t=0, point A emits a secondary wavelet. By the time the wavefront reaches point C, the wavelet from A has traveled a distance AD = BC. Triangles ABC and ADC are congruent (right-angled, common hypotenuse AC, AD=BC). Thus, angle of incidence (i) = angle of reflection (r).

Card 4

Derive Snell's law of refraction using Huygens' Principle.

Let a plane wavefront AB travel from medium 1 (speed v₁) to medium 2 (speed v₂). In time t, the wavelet from A travels distance AD = v₂t, while the wavelet from B travels distance BC = v₁t. From right triangles ABD and ABC: sin(i) = BC/AC = v₁t/AC, sin(r) = AD/AC = v₂t/AC. Thus, sin(i)/sin(r) = v₁/v₂ = μ₂/μ₁, where μ is the refractive index.

Card 5

What is the condition for constructive interference in Young's double-slit experiment?

Constructive interference occurs when the path difference between two waves is an integer multiple of the wavelength: Δx = nλ, where n = 0, 1, 2, 3,... This results in a bright fringe (maxima) on the screen.

Card 6

Derive the fringe width (β) in Young's double-slit experiment.

For a double slit with separation d, screen distance D, and wavelength λ: Path difference for nth bright fringe = d sinθ ≈ d tanθ = d(y/D), where y is the fringe distance from the center. For constructive interference: d(y/D) = nλ → y = nλD/d. Fringe width β = yₙ₊₁ - yₙ = λD/d.

Card 7

Explain the effect of introducing a thin transparent plate in one slit of Young's experiment.

The plate introduces an additional optical path length (μ-1)t, where μ is the refractive index and t is the thickness. This shifts the central maxima by a distance Δy = (μ-1)tD/d. The fringe width remains unchanged, but the entire fringe pattern shifts.

Card 8

What is the difference between interference and diffraction?

Interference is the superposition of waves from a finite number of coherent sources (e.g., two slits), producing equally spaced fringes. Diffraction is the bending of waves around obstacles or apertures, producing a central maximum with diminishing side maxima (e.g., single-slit diffraction).

Card 9

Derive the condition for minima in single-slit diffraction.

For a slit of width a, divide it into zones of width a/2. Destructive interference occurs when the path difference between the top and bottom rays is λ: a sinθ = nλ, where n = ±1, ±2, ±3,... This gives the positions of dark fringes (minima).

Card 10

What is the angular width of the central maximum in single-slit diffraction?

The first minima occur at a sinθ = ±λ. For small angles, sinθ ≈ θ, so θ = ±λ/a. The angular width of the central maximum is 2θ = 2λ/a.

Card 11

Define resolving power of a diffraction grating. How does it depend on the number of slits?

Resolving power (R) is the ability of a grating to distinguish between two closely spaced wavelengths: R = λ/Δλ = nN, where n is the order of diffraction and N is the total number of slits. Higher N increases resolving power, allowing finer wavelength separation.

Card 12

Explain the phenomenon of polarization. What is the condition for plane-polarized light?

Polarization is the restriction of light vibrations to a single plane. In unpolarized light, the electric field oscillates in all directions perpendicular to propagation. Plane-polarized light has its electric field confined to one plane, achieved using polarizers (e.g., Polaroid sheets).

Card 13

State Malus' Law and its significance.

Malus' Law states that the intensity of plane-polarized light after passing through an analyzer is I = I₀ cos²θ, where I₀ is the initial intensity and θ is the angle between the polarizer and analyzer. This law quantifies the reduction in intensity due to polarization.

Card 14

What is Brewster's Law? Derive the relationship between the angle of polarization and refractive index.

Brewster's Law states that the angle of incidence (iₚ) for which reflected light is completely plane-polarized is given by tan(iₚ) = μ, where μ is the refractive index of the medium. At this angle, the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular: iₚ + r = 90° → sin(r) = cos(iₚ). Using Snell's law: μ = sin(iₚ)/sin(r) = sin(iₚ)/cos(iₚ) = tan(iₚ).

Card 15

Explain the concept of optical path length. How does it differ from geometric path length?

Optical path length (OPL) is the product of geometric path length (d) and refractive index (μ): OPL = μd. It accounts for the slower speed of light in a medium (v = c/μ), making the effective distance traveled equivalent to that in vacuum. This is critical in interference and diffraction calculations.

Card 16

What is the difference between coherent and incoherent sources?

Coherent sources maintain a constant phase difference over time, producing stable interference patterns (e.g., laser light). Incoherent sources have random phase fluctuations, leading to rapidly changing interference patterns that average to uniform illumination (e.g., sunlight).

Card 17

A double-slit experiment uses light of wavelength 500 nm and slit separation 0.5 mm. If the screen is 1 m away, calculate the fringe width.

Fringe width β = λD/d = (500 × 10⁻⁹ m × 1 m) / (0.5 × 10⁻³ m) = 1 × 10⁻³ m = 1 mm.

Card 18

In Young's experiment, the 3rd bright fringe is observed at 4.5 mm from the central maxima. If the slit separation is 0.2 mm and screen distance is 1.5 m, find the wavelength of light.

For nth bright fringe: y = nλD/d → λ = yd/(nD) = (4.5 × 10⁻³ m × 0.2 × 10⁻³ m) / (3 × 1.5 m) = 2 × 10⁻⁷ m = 200 nm.

Card 19

A single slit of width 0.1 mm is illuminated by light of wavelength 600 nm. Calculate the angular position of the first minima.

For first minima: a sinθ = λ → sinθ = λ/a = (600 × 10⁻⁹ m) / (0.1 × 10⁻³ m) = 6 × 10⁻³. For small angles, θ ≈ sinθ = 6 × 10⁻³ radians = 0.344°.

Card 20

Unpolarized light of intensity 32 W/m² passes through two Polaroids with their axes at 60°. Calculate the transmitted intensity.

After first Polaroid: I₁ = I₀/2 = 16 W/m². After second Polaroid (Malus' Law): I = I₁ cos²θ = 16 × cos²(60°) = 16 × (0.5)² = 4 W/m².

Card 21

What is the Brewster angle for light traveling from air (μ=1) to glass (μ=1.5)?

Brewster's Law: tan(iₚ) = μ → iₚ = tan⁻¹(1.5) = 56.31°.
