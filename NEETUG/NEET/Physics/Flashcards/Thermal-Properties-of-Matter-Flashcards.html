NEET Physics: Thermal Properties of Matter - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define **temperature** and state its SI unit.

Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. It indicates the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. The SI unit of temperature is the **kelvin (K)**. Other commonly used units include Celsius (°C) and Fahrenheit (°F).

Card 2

What is the **zeroth law of thermodynamics**? Explain its significance.

The zeroth law of thermodynamics states that if two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This law establishes the concept of temperature and provides the basis for temperature measurement using thermometers.

Card 3

Write the formula for **linear expansion** of a solid and explain each term.

The formula for linear expansion is: **ΔL = αL₀ΔT**, where: - ΔL = change in length, - α = coefficient of linear expansion (K⁻¹ or °C⁻¹), - L₀ = original length, - ΔT = change in temperature. This formula describes how the length of a solid changes with temperature.

Card 4

How does the **coefficient of volume expansion (γ)** relate to the coefficient of linear expansion (α) for a solid?

For isotropic solids (materials with uniform properties in all directions), the coefficient of volume expansion (γ) is approximately **three times** the coefficient of linear expansion (α): **γ ≈ 3α**. This is because volume expansion accounts for expansion in all three dimensions.

Card 5

Define **specific heat capacity (c)** and state its SI unit.

Specific heat capacity (c) is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of **1 kg** of a substance by **1 K (or 1°C)**. Its SI unit is **J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**. It is a measure of how much heat a substance can absorb per unit mass without a significant temperature change.

Card 6

What is the **calorimetry principle**? Write the formula used for heat exchange in calorimetry.

The calorimetry principle states that when two bodies at different temperatures are placed in thermal contact, the heat lost by the hotter body is equal to the heat gained by the colder body, assuming no heat is lost to the surroundings. The formula is: **Q = mcΔT**, where: - Q = heat energy (J), - m = mass of the substance (kg), - c = specific heat capacity (J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹), - ΔT = change in temperature (K or °C).

Card 7

Explain the **anomalous expansion of water** and its ecological significance.

Water exhibits anomalous expansion between **0°C and 4°C**, where its volume decreases as temperature increases (instead of increasing like most substances). This means water has its **maximum density at 4°C**. Ecologically, this ensures that lakes and ponds freeze from the top down, allowing aquatic life to survive in liquid water beneath the ice layer during winter.

Card 8

What is **latent heat**? Differentiate between latent heat of fusion and latent heat of vaporization.

Latent heat is the heat energy absorbed or released by a substance during a **phase change** (e.g., solid to liquid or liquid to gas) **without a change in temperature**. - **Latent heat of fusion (Lf)**: Heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from solid to liquid at its melting point (e.g., ice to water). - **Latent heat of vaporization (Lv)**: Heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from liquid to gas at its boiling point (e.g., water to steam). SI unit: **J kg⁻¹**.

Card 9

Write the formula for **thermal conductivity (k)** and explain its significance.

The formula for thermal conductivity is: **Q = (kAΔT)t / d**, where: - Q = heat transferred (J), - k = thermal conductivity (W m⁻¹ K⁻¹), - A = cross-sectional area (m²), - ΔT = temperature difference (K or °C), - t = time (s), - d = thickness of the material (m). Thermal conductivity measures a material's ability to conduct heat. Higher **k** means better heat conduction (e.g., metals), while lower **k** means better insulation (e.g., wood, air).

Card 10

What is **Newton’s Law of Cooling**? Write its formula and explain the terms.

Newton’s Law of Cooling states that the rate of heat loss of a body is **directly proportional** to the difference in temperatures between the body and its surroundings, provided the temperature difference is small. The formula is: **dQ/dt = -hA(T - Tₛ)**, where: - dQ/dt = rate of heat loss (W), - h = heat transfer coefficient (W m⁻² K⁻¹), - A = surface area of the body (m²), - T = temperature of the body (K or °C), - Tₛ = temperature of the surroundings (K or °C). The negative sign indicates heat loss.

Card 11

Explain the **triple point of water** and its importance in defining the Kelvin scale.

The triple point of water is the **unique temperature and pressure** at which water exists in **solid, liquid, and gas phases simultaneously**. For water, this occurs at **273.16 K (0.01°C)** and **611.657 Pa pressure**. The Kelvin scale is defined such that the triple point of water is exactly **273.16 K**, making it a fundamental fixed point for temperature measurement.

Card 12

What is the **ideal gas equation**? Write it and explain each term.

The ideal gas equation is: **PV = nRT**, where: - P = pressure of the gas (Pa or atm), - V = volume of the gas (m³ or L), - n = number of moles of the gas, - R = universal gas constant (8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ or 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹), - T = temperature of the gas (K). This equation relates the macroscopic properties of an ideal gas and is derived from the kinetic theory of gases.

Card 13

Differentiate between **heat capacity (C)** and **specific heat capacity (c)**.

Heat capacity (C) is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of an **entire object** by 1 K (or 1°C). Its SI unit is **J K⁻¹**. Specific heat capacity (c) is the heat required to raise the temperature of **1 kg of a substance** by 1 K (or 1°C). Its SI unit is **J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**. The relationship is: **C = mc**, where **m** is the mass of the object.

Card 14

What is **thermal stress**? Write the formula and explain its significance.

Thermal stress is the **stress induced in a material** due to a change in temperature, which causes expansion or contraction. The formula is: **σ = YαΔT**, where: - σ = thermal stress (Pa), - Y = Young’s modulus of the material (Pa), - α = coefficient of linear expansion (K⁻¹), - ΔT = change in temperature (K or °C). Thermal stress is significant in engineering (e.g., railway tracks, bridges) to prevent structural damage due to temperature changes.

Card 15

Explain the **Greenhouse Effect** in terms of thermal properties of matter.

The Greenhouse Effect is a natural process where certain gases (e.g., CO₂, methane) in Earth’s atmosphere **trap infrared radiation** emitted by the Earth’s surface, preventing it from escaping into space. This occurs because: 1. Solar radiation (shortwave) passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth’s surface. 2. The Earth emits **infrared radiation (longwave)**. 3. Greenhouse gases **absorb and re-emit** this infrared radiation, warming the atmosphere. This effect is crucial for maintaining Earth’s temperature but is amplified by human activities, leading to global warming.

Card 16

What is **blackbody radiation**? State **Stefan-Boltzmann Law** and its formula.

Blackbody radiation is the **electromagnetic radiation emitted by a perfect blackbody** (an idealized object that absorbs all incident radiation) at a given temperature. The **Stefan-Boltzmann Law** states that the total energy radiated per unit surface area of a blackbody is **proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature**. The formula is: **P = σAT⁴**, where: - P = total power radiated (W), - σ = Stefan-Boltzmann constant (5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴), - A = surface area of the blackbody (m²), - T = absolute temperature (K).
