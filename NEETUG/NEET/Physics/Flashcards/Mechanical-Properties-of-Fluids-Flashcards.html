NEET Physics: Mechanical Properties of Fluids - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define fluid. How does it differ from a solid in terms of mechanical properties?

A fluid is a substance that can flow and does not have a fixed shape. Unlike solids, fluids cannot sustain shear stress indefinitely; they deform continuously under shear stress. Solids have definite shape and volume, while fluids take the shape of their container and may or may not have a fixed volume (liquids have fixed volume, gases do not).

Card 2

State Pascal's Law. What is its mathematical expression and practical application?

Pascal's Law states that the pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished throughout the fluid in all directions. Mathematically, ΔP = F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂, where ΔP is the change in pressure, F₁ and F₂ are forces, and A₁ and A₂ are areas. Practical application: hydraulic lifts and brakes, where a small force applied on a small area generates a large force on a larger area.

Card 3

Derive the expression for pressure at a depth 'h' in a liquid of density 'ρ' under gravity 'g'.

Consider a fluid column of height h, cross-sectional area A, and density ρ. The weight of the column is W = mass × g = (ρ × A × h) × g. Pressure at depth h is force per unit area: P = W/A = (ρ × A × h × g)/A = ρgh. Thus, P = P₀ + ρgh, where P₀ is atmospheric pressure at the surface.

Card 4

What is atmospheric pressure? Give its value in SI units and its significance in fluid mechanics.

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of the Earth's atmosphere. Its standard value is 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa (or 1 atm). It acts as the reference pressure (P₀) in fluid mechanics, especially in problems involving pressure at depth, buoyancy, and manometer readings.

Card 5

Explain the principle of a hydraulic lift using Pascal's Law. Why is the force output larger than the input force?

A hydraulic lift consists of two pistons connected by a fluid-filled pipe. When a small force F₁ is applied to the smaller piston (area A₁), it creates pressure P = F₁/A₁. By Pascal's Law, this pressure is transmitted to the larger piston (area A₂), producing force F₂ = P × A₂ = (F₁/A₁) × A₂. Since A₂ > A₁, F₂ > F₁, amplifying the input force.

Card 6

Define gauge pressure and absolute pressure. How are they related?

Gauge pressure is the pressure measured relative to atmospheric pressure (P_gauge = P_absolute - P_atm). Absolute pressure is the total pressure, including atmospheric pressure (P_absolute = P_gauge + P_atm). Gauge pressure can be negative (vacuum), but absolute pressure is always non-negative.

Card 7

What is Archimedes' Principle? Derive the expression for buoyant force.

Archimedes' Principle states that a body immersed in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body. Derivation: Consider a submerged object of volume V. The fluid exerts pressure on all surfaces, but the net upward force (buoyant force) is F_b = (P_bottom - P_top) × A = (ρ_fluid × g × h) × A = ρ_fluid × g × V, where V is the displaced volume.

Card 8

A block of wood floats in water with 60% of its volume submerged. Calculate its density. (Density of water = 1000 kg/m³)

For floating bodies, buoyant force = weight of the body. Thus, ρ_wood × V × g = ρ_water × (0.6V) × g. Simplifying, ρ_wood = 0.6 × ρ_water = 0.6 × 1000 = 600 kg/m³.

Card 9

Define streamline flow and turbulent flow. What is the significance of Reynolds number?

Streamline flow (laminar flow) is smooth, orderly fluid motion where layers do not mix. Turbulent flow is chaotic, with eddies and mixing. Reynolds number (Re = ρvD/η) predicts flow type: Re < 2000 (laminar), Re > 4000 (turbulent). It depends on density (ρ), velocity (v), characteristic length (D), and viscosity (η).

Card 10

State the equation of continuity. What does it imply about fluid flow in a pipe?

The equation of continuity is A₁v₁ = A₂v₂, where A is cross-sectional area and v is fluid velocity. It implies that for an incompressible fluid, the volume flow rate (Av) is constant along the pipe. Thus, velocity increases where the pipe narrows and decreases where it widens.

Card 11

Derive Bernoulli's equation for an ideal fluid. What are its assumptions?

Bernoulli's equation: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant. Derivation: Apply work-energy theorem to a fluid element moving from point 1 to 2. Work done by pressure forces = change in kinetic + potential energy. Assumptions: fluid is incompressible, non-viscous, flow is steady and streamline, and no energy loss.

Card 12

Explain the Venturi effect using Bernoulli's equation. Give a practical example.

The Venturi effect states that pressure decreases where fluid velocity increases (and vice versa). From Bernoulli's equation: P + ½ρv² = constant. In a constricted pipe, v increases, so P decreases. Example: Venturi meters measure fluid flow rate; atomizers use reduced pressure to draw liquid into a gas stream.

Card 13

A horizontal pipe narrows from 10 cm diameter to 5 cm diameter. If water flows at 2 m/s in the wider section, what is its speed in the narrower section?

Using continuity: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂. A = πr², so (π × 0.05²) × 2 = (π × 0.025²) × v₂. Simplifying, v₂ = (0.05/0.025)² × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8 m/s.

Card 14

Define viscosity. What is its SI unit? How does it affect fluid flow?

Viscosity (η) is a fluid's resistance to flow, caused by internal friction between layers. SI unit: Pascal-second (Pa·s) or N·s/m². Higher viscosity means slower flow (e.g., honey vs. water). It converts kinetic energy to heat, causing pressure drops in pipes (Poiseuille's law).

Card 15

State Stokes' Law. What is its application in NEET-level problems?

Stokes' Law: F = 6πηrv, where F is viscous drag on a spherical object of radius r moving at velocity v in a fluid of viscosity η. Application: Terminal velocity of small spherical particles (e.g., raindrops, dust) falling through fluids. Used to calculate settling velocity or viscosity.

Card 16

A small metal sphere of radius 1 mm falls through glycerin (η = 1.5 Pa·s) at terminal velocity 2 cm/s. Calculate the viscous drag force. (Use Stokes' Law)

F = 6πηrv = 6 × π × 1.5 × 0.001 × 0.02 = 5.65 × 10⁻⁴ N.

Card 17

What is surface tension? Give its SI unit and two examples of its effects.

Surface tension (γ) is the elastic tendency of a fluid surface to minimize area, caused by cohesive forces between molecules. SI unit: N/m. Examples: (1) Water droplets forming spheres (minimum surface area). (2) Insects walking on water without sinking.

Card 18

Derive the excess pressure inside a liquid drop of radius r due to surface tension γ.

Consider a liquid drop of radius r. Surface tension acts along the circumference (2πr), and excess pressure (ΔP) acts over area (πr²). For equilibrium: ΔP × πr² = γ × 2πr. Thus, ΔP = 2γ/r.

Card 19

Why does a soap bubble have higher excess pressure than a liquid drop of the same radius?

A soap bubble has two surfaces (inner and outer), so the total surface tension force is 2 × (2πrγ) = 4πrγ. For equilibrium: ΔP × πr² = 4πrγ → ΔP = 4γ/r. A liquid drop has only one surface, so ΔP = 2γ/r.
