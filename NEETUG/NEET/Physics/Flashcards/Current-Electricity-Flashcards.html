NEET Physics - Current Electricity Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define electric current. State its SI unit and the direction of conventional current.

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. It is defined as I = ΔQ/Δt, where ΔQ is the charge flowing and Δt is the time interval. The SI unit of electric current is the ampere (A). Conventional current flows from the positive terminal to the negative terminal of a battery, opposite to the direction of electron flow.

Card 2

What is Ohm's Law? Under what conditions does it hold true?

Ohm's Law states that the current (I) through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage (V) across the two points, provided the physical conditions (temperature, pressure, etc.) remain constant. Mathematically, V = IR, where R is the resistance. It holds true for ohmic conductors, such as metals at constant temperature.

Card 3

Derive the formula for the resistance of a conductor in terms of its resistivity.

Resistance (R) of a conductor is directly proportional to its length (L) and inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area (A). The proportionality constant is the resistivity (ρ) of the material. Thus, R = ρ(L/A). Resistivity depends on the material and temperature, not on the dimensions of the conductor.

Card 4

Explain the temperature dependence of resistivity for conductors and semiconductors.

For conductors (e.g., metals), resistivity increases with temperature due to increased thermal vibrations of the lattice ions, which scatter electrons more effectively. The relationship is given by ρ = ρ₀[1 + α(T - T₀)], where α is the temperature coefficient of resistivity. For semiconductors, resistivity decreases with temperature as more charge carriers are liberated due to thermal energy.

Card 5

What is the equivalent resistance of two resistors connected in series? Derive the formula.

For resistors in series, the equivalent resistance (R_eq) is the sum of the individual resistances. If R₁ and R₂ are in series, R_eq = R₁ + R₂. Derivation: The same current flows through both resistors, and the total voltage is the sum of the voltages across each resistor (V = V₁ + V₂). Using Ohm's Law, V = IR₁ + IR₂ = I(R₁ + R₂), so R_eq = R₁ + R₂.

Card 6

What is the equivalent resistance of two resistors connected in parallel? Derive the formula.

For resistors in parallel, the reciprocal of the equivalent resistance (1/R_eq) is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances. If R₁ and R₂ are in parallel, 1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂. Derivation: The voltage across each resistor is the same, and the total current is the sum of the currents through each resistor (I = I₁ + I₂). Using Ohm's Law, I = V/R₁ + V/R₂ = V(1/R₁ + 1/R₂), so 1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂.

Card 7

Define electromotive force (emf) and terminal voltage of a cell. How are they related?

Electromotive force (emf, ε) is the maximum potential difference between the terminals of a cell when no current is drawn. Terminal voltage (V) is the potential difference when current is drawn. They are related by V = ε - Ir, where I is the current and r is the internal resistance of the cell. Terminal voltage is always less than emf when current flows.

Card 8

State Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) and Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL).

Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL): The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum of currents leaving the junction (conservation of charge). Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL): The sum of the electromotive forces in any closed loop equals the sum of the potential drops in that loop (conservation of energy).

Card 9

What is the principle of a Wheatstone bridge? When is it considered balanced?

A Wheatstone bridge is used to measure unknown resistances. It consists of four resistors (R₁, R₂, R₃, R₄) arranged in a diamond shape with a galvanometer between two opposite junctions. The bridge is balanced when no current flows through the galvanometer, which occurs when R₁/R₂ = R₃/R₄.

Card 10

Define electrical power. Derive the formula for power dissipated in a resistor.

Electrical power (P) is the rate at which electrical energy is dissipated or consumed. In a resistor, power is given by P = VI, where V is the voltage and I is the current. Using Ohm's Law (V = IR), power can also be expressed as P = I²R or P = V²/R. The SI unit of power is the watt (W).

Card 11

What is the difference between drift velocity and current? Derive the relationship between them.

Drift velocity (v_d) is the average velocity of charge carriers (e.g., electrons) in a conductor under the influence of an electric field. Current (I) is the total charge flowing per unit time. The relationship is I = nAev_d, where n is the number of charge carriers per unit volume, A is the cross-sectional area, e is the charge of an electron, and v_d is the drift velocity.

Card 12

Explain the concept of mobility of charge carriers. How is it related to conductivity?

Mobility (μ) is the drift velocity per unit electric field (μ = v_d/E). Conductivity (σ) of a material is related to mobility by σ = nqμ, where n is the charge carrier density, q is the charge of the carrier, and μ is the mobility. Higher mobility means better conductivity for the same carrier density.

Card 13

What is a potentiometer? How does it measure the emf of a cell?

A potentiometer is a device used to measure the emf of a cell or compare emfs of cells. It consists of a long wire of uniform cross-section and resistivity, with a steady current flowing through it. The emf of the cell is balanced against the potential drop along a segment of the wire. At balance, the emf of the cell equals the potential drop across the segment, and no current flows through the cell.

Card 14

State the principle of superposition of currents in a circuit.

The principle of superposition states that in a linear circuit with multiple sources, the current in any branch is the algebraic sum of the currents produced by each source acting alone, with all other sources replaced by their internal resistances. This principle simplifies the analysis of complex circuits.

Card 15

What is the difference between resistance and resistivity?

Resistance (R) is the opposition to the flow of current in a conductor and depends on its dimensions (length and area) and material. Resistivity (ρ) is a material property that quantifies how strongly a material opposes current flow, independent of dimensions. The relationship is R = ρ(L/A).

Card 16

Explain the color coding of resistors and how to determine their resistance value.

Resistors are color-coded with bands representing digits, multipliers, and tolerance. The first two bands represent the first two digits, the third band is the multiplier (power of 10), and the fourth band (if present) is the tolerance. For example, a resistor with bands brown (1), black (0), red (×10²), and gold (±5%) has a resistance of 10 × 10² Ω = 1000 Ω with 5% tolerance.

Card 17

What is the effect of temperature on the resistance of a conductor?

The resistance of a conductor increases with temperature due to increased thermal agitation of the lattice ions, which scatters electrons more effectively. The relationship is given by R = R₀[1 + α(T - T₀)], where R₀ is the resistance at a reference temperature T₀, and α is the temperature coefficient of resistivity.

Card 18

Define internal resistance of a cell. How does it affect the terminal voltage?

Internal resistance (r) of a cell is the resistance offered by the electrolyte and electrodes within the cell. It causes a voltage drop inside the cell when current flows, reducing the terminal voltage (V) from the emf (ε). The relationship is V = ε - Ir, where I is the current drawn from the cell.

Card 19

What is a meter bridge? How is it used to find unknown resistance?

A meter bridge is a device based on the Wheatstone bridge principle, used to measure unknown resistances. It consists of a wire of uniform cross-section and length 1 meter. The unknown resistance (R) and a known resistance (S) are connected in the gaps, and the balance point is found where the galvanometer shows no deflection. The unknown resistance is calculated using R/S = l₁/l₂, where l₁ and l₂ are the lengths of the wire on either side of the balance point.
