NEET Physics - Electromagnetic Waves Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define electromagnetic waves and state their fundamental nature.

Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation. They are self-sustaining and do not require a medium, propagating through vacuum at the speed of light (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s). Their fundamental nature is that they are produced by accelerated charges and exhibit wave-particle duality.

Card 2

Write the mathematical relationship between the speed of light (c), frequency (ν), and wavelength (λ) of an electromagnetic wave.

The relationship is given by: c = ν × λ, where c is the speed of light in vacuum (3 × 10⁸ m/s), ν is the frequency in hertz (Hz), and λ is the wavelength in meters (m). This equation is derived from the wave equation for electromagnetic radiation.

Card 3

List the components of the electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency.

The electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency (and decreasing wavelength) is: Radio waves → Microwaves → Infrared → Visible light → Ultraviolet → X-rays → Gamma rays. Each region has distinct properties and applications based on its frequency and energy.

Card 4

Explain the significance of Maxwell’s equations in the context of electromagnetic waves.

Maxwell’s equations unify electricity and magnetism, predicting the existence of electromagnetic waves. Specifically, Faraday’s law and Ampere-Maxwell law show that a changing electric field produces a magnetic field and vice versa, leading to self-propagating waves. The wave equation derived from these equations confirms that electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light.

Card 5

What is the energy of a photon associated with an electromagnetic wave of frequency ν? State the formula and its variables.

The energy (E) of a photon is given by: E = h × ν, where h is Planck’s constant (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) and ν is the frequency of the electromagnetic wave in hertz (Hz). This equation highlights the particle nature of electromagnetic waves.

Card 6

Describe the transverse nature of electromagnetic waves with reference to the electric and magnetic fields.

In electromagnetic waves, the electric field (E) and magnetic field (B) oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of wave propagation. This transverse nature is evident in the wave equation solutions, where E and B are in phase but oriented at 90° to each other, ensuring the wave’s self-sustaining propagation.

Card 7

State the range of wavelengths for visible light in the electromagnetic spectrum.

Visible light occupies a narrow range of wavelengths from approximately 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red). This range corresponds to frequencies of about 4.3 × 10¹⁴ Hz to 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz and is the portion of the spectrum detectable by the human eye.

Card 8

How does the intensity of an electromagnetic wave relate to its electric and magnetic field amplitudes?

The intensity (I) of an electromagnetic wave is proportional to the square of the amplitude of its electric field (E₀) or magnetic field (B₀). Mathematically, I = (1/2) × ε₀ × c × E₀², where ε₀ is the permittivity of free space and c is the speed of light. Intensity represents the power per unit area carried by the wave.

Card 9

Explain the concept of polarization in electromagnetic waves.

Polarization refers to the orientation of the electric field vector in an electromagnetic wave. In unpolarized light, the electric field oscillates in all directions perpendicular to propagation. Polarized light has its electric field confined to a single plane, achieved through polarizers or natural processes like reflection or scattering.

Card 10

What is the Poynting vector, and what does it represent in the context of electromagnetic waves?

The Poynting vector (S) represents the directional energy flux (power per unit area) of an electromagnetic wave. It is given by S = (1/μ₀) × (E × B), where E is the electric field, B is the magnetic field, and μ₀ is the permeability of free space. The magnitude of S indicates the intensity of the wave, and its direction is along the wave’s propagation.

Card 11

Describe the production of electromagnetic waves by an oscillating electric dipole.

An oscillating electric dipole consists of two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance, whose separation varies sinusoidally with time. The accelerating charges produce time-varying electric and magnetic fields, which propagate outward as electromagnetic waves. The radiated power is proportional to the square of the dipole’s oscillation frequency and amplitude.

Card 12

State the boundary conditions for electric and magnetic fields at the interface of two media for electromagnetic waves.

At the interface of two media, the boundary conditions for electromagnetic waves are: 1. The tangential component of the electric field (E) is continuous. 2. The tangential component of the magnetic field (B) is continuous if there are no surface currents. 3. The normal component of the electric displacement (D) is continuous if there are no surface charges. 4. The normal component of the magnetic flux density (B) is always continuous.

Card 13

Explain the phenomenon of dispersion in electromagnetic waves.

Dispersion occurs when the phase velocity of an electromagnetic wave depends on its frequency, causing different frequency components to travel at different speeds. This results in the spreading of a wave packet over time. Dispersion is responsible for phenomena like the splitting of white light into its constituent colors by a prism.

Card 14

What is the significance of the refractive index (n) in the context of electromagnetic waves?

The refractive index (n) of a medium is defined as the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum (c) to the speed of light in the medium (v): n = c/v. It determines how much the electromagnetic wave is bent (refracted) when entering the medium from vacuum or another medium. The refractive index is frequency-dependent, leading to dispersion.

Card 15

How does the energy density of an electromagnetic wave relate to its electric and magnetic fields?

The energy density (u) of an electromagnetic wave is the sum of the energy densities of its electric and magnetic fields: u = (1/2) × ε₀ × E² + (1/2μ₀) × B². Since E and B are related by E = cB, the energy density can also be expressed as u = ε₀ × E² or u = B²/μ₀, showing equal contributions from both fields in vacuum.
