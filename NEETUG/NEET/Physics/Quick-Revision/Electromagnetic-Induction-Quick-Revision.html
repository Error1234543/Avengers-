NEET Physics Quick Revision: Electromagnetic Induction

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Electromagnetic Induction: The process of generating an electromotive force (emf) and current in a closed loop due to a changing magnetic flux through the loop. This phenomenon is described by Faraday's Laws of Induction.
  • Magnetic Flux (Φ): The measure of the quantity of magnetic field passing through a given area. Mathematically, Φ = B·A = BA cosθ, where B is the magnetic field, A is the area, and θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal to the area. Unit: Weber (Wb).
  • Induced emf (ε): The electromotive force generated in a circuit due to a change in magnetic flux. It is given by Faraday's Law: ε = -dΦ/dt. The negative sign indicates the direction of the induced emf (Lenz's Law). Unit: Volt (V).
  • Lenz's Law: The direction of the induced emf and current in a circuit is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. This is a consequence of the conservation of energy.
  • Self-Inductance (L): The property of a coil (or circuit) by which it opposes any change in the current flowing through it by inducing an emf in itself. Unit: Henry (H). The induced emf is given by ε = -L(dI/dt).
  • Mutual Inductance (M): The property of two coils where a change in current in one coil induces an emf in the other coil. Unit: Henry (H). The induced emf in the secondary coil is given by ε₂ = -M(dI₁/dt).

Faraday's Second Law quantifies this emf: the magnitude of the induced emf is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux. Mathematically, ε = -dΦ/dt. The negative sign is a consequence of Lenz's Law. For a coil with N turns, the induced emf is ε = -N(dΦ/dt). This law is the foundation for generators, transformers, and many electrical devices."}
  • {'concept': "Lenz's Law and Energy Conservation", 'content': "Lenz's Law ensures that the induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux, thereby conserving energy. For example, if a magnet is moved toward a coil, the induced current in the coil creates a magnetic field that repels the magnet, requiring work to be done to move the magnet closer. This work is converted into electrical energy in the coil. Without this opposition, energy would be created from nothing, violating the conservation of energy."}
  • {'concept': 'Motional emf', 'content': "When a conductor moves through a magnetic field, an emf is induced in it due to the magnetic Lorentz force on the free charges. For a straight conductor of length l moving with velocity v perpendicular to a magnetic field B, the motional emf is ε = Blv. This principle is used in generators and dynamos. The direction of the induced emf can be determined using Fleming's Right-Hand Rule: thumb (motion), index finger (field), middle finger (current)."}
  • {'concept': 'Self-Inductance and Inductors', 'content': 'Self-inductance is the property of a coil to oppose changes in current. When current in a coil changes, it induces an emf in the same coil, given by ε = -L(dI/dt). The self-inductance L depends on the geometry of the coil (number of turns, area, length) and the presence of a magnetic core. Inductors store energy in their magnetic field, with energy given by U = ½LI². In circuits, inductors resist sudden changes in current, making them useful in filters and oscillators.'}
  • {'concept': 'Mutual Inductance and Transformers', 'content': 'Mutual inductance occurs when two coils are placed close to each other, and a change in current in one coil induces an emf in the other. The mutual inductance M depends on the number of turns in both coils, their geometry, and the magnetic coupling between them. Transformers operate on this principle: an alternating current in the primary coil induces an alternating emf in the secondary coil. The voltage ratio is given by V₂/V₁ = N₂/N₁, where N is the number of turns. Transformers are essential for power transmission and voltage regulation.'}
  • {'concept': 'Eddy Currents', 'content': "Eddy currents are loops of electrical current induced within conductors by a changing magnetic field. These currents generate their own magnetic field, which opposes the change in the original magnetic field (Lenz's Law). Eddy currents cause energy loss in the form of heat, which is why transformer cores are laminated to reduce these losses. However, eddy currents are also useful in applications like induction heating and magnetic braking."}

Important facts

  • The direction of induced current in a loop can be determined using Lenz's Law: it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. For example, if the magnetic field through a loop increases, the induced current will create a magnetic field in the opposite direction.
  • Motional emf is generated when a conductor moves through a magnetic field. The magnitude depends on the magnetic field strength, the length of the conductor, and the velocity of the conductor perpendicular to the field.
  • Self-inductance is a measure of a coil's opposition to changes in current. A coil with high self-inductance will resist sudden changes in current more strongly than a coil with low self-inductance.
  • Mutual inductance between two coils depends on their geometry, the number of turns, and the magnetic coupling between them. It is maximized when the coils are closely coupled and aligned.
  • Eddy currents are induced in bulk conductors exposed to changing magnetic fields. They cause energy loss in the form of heat, which is why transformer cores are laminated to reduce these losses.
