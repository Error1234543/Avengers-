NEET Physics Quick Revision: Gravitation

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Gravitation: The natural phenomenon by which all objects with mass or energy are brought toward one another. It is responsible for the motion of planets, stars, and galaxies.
  • Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation: Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers. Mathematically, F = G * (m1 * m2) / r², where G is the gravitational constant (6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²).
  • Gravitational Field: A region of space around a mass in which another mass experiences a gravitational force. The gravitational field strength (g) at a point is defined as the force per unit mass experienced by a small test mass placed at that point.
  • Gravitational Potential Energy (U): The energy an object possesses due to its position in a gravitational field. For two masses m1 and m2 separated by distance r, U = -G * (m1 * m2) / r. The negative sign indicates that the force is attractive.
  • Escape Velocity: The minimum velocity required for an object to escape the gravitational influence of a massive body without further propulsion. For Earth, escape velocity is approximately 11.2 km/s and is given by v = √(2GM/R), where M is the mass of the planet and R is its radius.
  • Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion: Three empirical laws describing the motion of planets around the Sun. (1) Law of Orbits: Planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus. (2) Law of Areas: A line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time. (3) Law of Periods: The square of the orbital period of a planet is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit (T² ∝ r³).

For a point mass M, g = -GM/r² (radially inward). Gravitational potential (V) is a scalar quantity representing the potential energy per unit mass. For a point mass, V = -GM/r. The relationship between field and potential is g = -∇V. Equipotential surfaces are perpendicular to the field lines and represent surfaces of constant potential.'}
  • {'concept': 'Variation of g with Altitude and Depth', 'content': "The acceleration due to gravity (g) varies with altitude (h) above Earth's surface as g' = g * (R/(R+h))², where R is Earth's radius. For small altitudes (h << R), this approximates to g' ≈ g(1 - 2h/R). Below Earth's surface, g decreases linearly with depth (d) as g' = g * (1 - d/R), assuming uniform density. At Earth's center, g = 0. These variations are crucial for understanding satellite motion and geophysical phenomena."}
