NEET Physics: Quick Revision - Thermal Properties of Matter

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Temperature: A measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance, determining the direction of heat flow. SI unit: Kelvin (K).
  • Heat: Energy transferred between two bodies due to a temperature difference. SI unit: Joule (J).
  • Thermal Expansion: The increase in dimensions (length, area, or volume) of a body due to a rise in temperature. Governed by coefficients of linear (α), area (β), and volume (γ) expansion, where β ≈ 2α and γ ≈ 3α for isotropic materials.
  • Specific Heat Capacity (c): The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by 1 K. Formula: Q = mcΔT, where Q is heat, m is mass, and ΔT is temperature change. SI unit: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
  • Latent Heat: The heat absorbed or released during a phase change (e.g., fusion, vaporization) at constant temperature. Latent heat of fusion (L_f) and vaporization (L_v) are key values for substances like water.
  • Thermal Conductivity (k): A measure of a material's ability to conduct heat. Formula: Q/t = kAΔT/d, where Q/t is heat flow rate, A is area, ΔT is temperature difference, and d is thickness. SI unit: W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.
  • Newton’s Law of Cooling: The rate of heat loss of a body is proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings. Formula: dQ/dt = -hA(T - T_s), where h is the heat transfer coefficient, A is area, T is body temperature, and T_s is surroundings temperature.
  • Ideal Gas Law: Relates pressure (P), volume (V), temperature (T), and moles (n) of an ideal gas: PV = nRT, where R is the universal gas constant (8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹).

Important facts

  • Water has its maximum density at 4°C, leading to anomalous expansion between 0°C and 4°C. This is why ice floats on water.
  • The coefficient of linear expansion (α) for most metals ranges from 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁶ K⁻¹. For example, α for steel is ~1.2 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, while for aluminum it is ~2.4 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹.
  • Latent heat of fusion for water is 334 kJ kg⁻¹, and latent heat of vaporization is 2260 kJ kg⁻¹ at 100°C. These values are significantly higher than for most other substances, making water effective for thermal regulation.
  • Thermal conductivity of metals (e.g., copper: 400 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹) is much higher than non-metals (e.g., wood: 0.1–0.2 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹). This is why metals feel colder to touch at room temperature.
  • The triple point of water (273.16 K, 611.657 Pa) is the temperature and pressure at which solid, liquid, and gas phases coexist in equilibrium. It is used to define the Kelvin scale.
  • Black bodies are ideal radiators and absorbers of thermal radiation. The emissivity (e) of a black body is 1, while for real surfaces, e ranges between 0 and 1.
  • The mean free path of gas molecules increases with temperature and decreases with pressure, affecting thermal conductivity in gases.
