NEET Physics Quick Revision: Laws of Motion

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • **Force**: A push or pull acting on an object that changes or tends to change its state of rest or uniform motion. SI unit: Newton (N).
  • **Inertia**: The property of an object to resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion. It is directly proportional to the mass of the object.
  • **Newton’s First Law (Law of Inertia)**: An object remains in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.
  • **Newton’s Second Law**: The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the net external force acting on it and occurs in the direction of the force. Mathematically, F = ma, where F is force, m is mass, and a is acceleration.
  • **Newton’s Third Law**: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. The action and reaction forces act on different bodies.
  • **Momentum (p)**: The product of an object’s mass and velocity (p = mv). It is a vector quantity with the same direction as velocity. SI unit: kg·m/s.
  • **Impulse (J)**: The change in momentum of an object when a force acts on it for a short time interval. J = FΔt = Δp. SI unit: N·s or kg·m/s.
  • **Friction**: A force that opposes the relative motion or tendency of motion between two surfaces in contact. Types: static, kinetic (sliding), and rolling friction.
  • **Tension**: The pulling force transmitted through a string, rope, cable, or wire when it is pulled tight by forces acting from opposite ends.
  • **Normal Force**: The perpendicular force exerted by a surface on an object in contact with it. It balances the component of the object’s weight perpendicular to the surface.

Important facts

  • The coefficient of static friction (μs) is always greater than the coefficient of kinetic friction (μk) for the same pair of surfaces. This is why it takes more force to start an object moving than to keep it moving.
  • Newton’s laws are valid only in inertial reference frames (frames that are not accelerating). In non-inertial frames (e.g., a car accelerating forward), fictitious forces (e.g., centrifugal force) appear to act.
  • The normal force is not always equal to the weight of an object. For example, on an inclined plane, N = mg cosθ, and in an elevator accelerating upward, N = m(g + a).
  • In a system of connected masses, the tension in the string depends on the acceleration of the system and the masses involved. For example, in a two-mass system with a pulley, tension is not simply the weight of one mass.
  • The maximum static friction force is given by fs_max = μsN, but static friction can be less than this value if the applied force is smaller. It adjusts to match the applied force up to its maximum.
  • In circular motion, the centripetal force is always directed toward the center of the circle. It is not a separate force but the net force causing the circular motion (e.g., tension, friction, or gravity).
  • The Law of Conservation of Momentum states that the total momentum of a closed system remains constant unless acted upon by an external force. This law is fundamental in analyzing collisions and explosions.
  • For an object in free fall, the only force acting on it is gravity (ignoring air resistance). Thus, its acceleration is g (9.8 m/s²), and its weight is mg. However, if air resistance is considered, the object reaches terminal velocity when the force of gravity is balanced by air resistance.
