NEET Physics Quick Revision: Ray Optics and Optical Instruments

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Ray Optics: The branch of optics that studies the propagation of light in terms of rays, ignoring wave effects like diffraction and interference. It is valid when the wavelength of light is much smaller than the size of the structures it interacts with.
  • Reflection: The phenomenon where a light ray bounces off a surface, changing direction but remaining in the same medium. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection (Law of Reflection).
  • Refraction: The bending of a light ray as it passes from one medium to another with different refractive indices. Governed by Snell's Law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, where n is the refractive index and θ is the angle with the normal.
  • Refractive Index (n): A dimensionless number that describes how light propagates through a medium. Defined as n = c/v, where c is the speed of light in vacuum and v is the speed in the medium. Higher n means slower light speed in the medium.
  • Lens: A transparent optical device with two refracting surfaces, at least one of which is curved, that converges or diverges light rays to form images. Classified as convex (converging) or concave (diverging).
  • Mirror: A surface that reflects light to form images. Plane mirrors produce virtual, upright images of the same size as the object. Spherical mirrors (concave or convex) form real or virtual images depending on object position.
  • Optical Instrument: A device that processes light waves to enhance an image for viewing or analysis. Examples include microscopes, telescopes, and cameras.
  • Magnification (m): The ratio of the height of the image (hᵢ) to the height of the object (hₒ). For lenses, m = v/u; for mirrors, m = -v/u. Negative magnification indicates an inverted image.

Important facts

  • The focal length of a spherical mirror is half its radius of curvature (f = R/2).
  • For a convex lens, the focal length is positive; for a concave lens, it is negative. The opposite is true for mirrors (concave mirror: negative f; convex mirror: positive f).
  • A real image is formed when light rays converge at a point; it can be projected on a screen. A virtual image is formed when light rays appear to diverge from a point; it cannot be projected.
  • The magnification for a plane mirror is always +1, indicating an upright, virtual image of the same size as the object.
  • The power of a lens is measured in diopters (D), where 1 D = 1 m⁻¹. A lens with f = 50 cm has a power of +2 D (convex) or -2 D (concave).
  • In a compound microscope, the final image is inverted with respect to the object. In a telescope, the final image is also inverted, but this is acceptable for astronomical observations.
  • The resolving power of a microscope or telescope is its ability to distinguish between two closely spaced objects. It depends on the wavelength of light and the numerical aperture of the lens.
  • Optical fibers use total internal reflection to transmit light signals over long distances with minimal loss. The core has a higher refractive index than the cladding.
  • The human eye can be modeled as a convex lens with a variable focal length (accommodation). Common defects include myopia (short-sightedness, corrected with concave lenses) and hypermetropia (long-sightedness, corrected with convex lenses).
  • The angle of deviation in a prism is given by δ = i + e - A, where i is the angle of incidence, e is the angle of emergence, and A is the angle of the prism. The minimum deviation occurs when i = e.
