NEET Physics Quick Revision: Mechanical Properties of Solids

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Elasticity: The property of a material to regain its original shape and size after the removal of deforming forces.
  • Stress: The restoring force per unit area set up inside a deformed body. Mathematically, Stress (σ) = Force (F) / Area (A). SI unit: N/m² or Pascal (Pa).
  • Strain: The ratio of change in dimension to the original dimension. It is a dimensionless quantity.
  • Hooke’s Law: Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain. Mathematically, Stress ∝ Strain or σ = Eε, where E is the modulus of elasticity.
  • Young’s Modulus (Y): The ratio of longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain within the elastic limit. Y = (F/A) / (ΔL/L). SI unit: N/m² or Pa.
  • Bulk Modulus (B): The ratio of volumetric stress to volumetric strain within the elastic limit. B = - (ΔP) / (ΔV/V). SI unit: N/m² or Pa.
  • Shear Modulus (G) or Modulus of Rigidity: The ratio of shear stress to shear strain within the elastic limit. G = (F/A) / θ, where θ is the angle of shear. SI unit: N/m² or Pa.
  • Poisson’s Ratio (σ): The ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain. σ = - (ΔD/D) / (ΔL/L), where ΔD is the change in diameter and ΔL is the change in length. It is a dimensionless quantity.
  • Elastic Limit: The maximum stress a material can withstand without permanent deformation.
  • Breaking Stress: The maximum stress a material can withstand before it breaks.

Important facts

  • The elastic limit is the maximum stress a material can withstand without permanent deformation. Beyond this limit, the material undergoes plastic deformation.
  • Young’s Modulus is a measure of the stiffness of a material. Higher Y indicates a stiffer material (e.g., steel has a higher Y than rubber).
  • Bulk Modulus measures a material’s resistance to uniform compression. Liquids and gases have lower Bulk Moduli compared to solids.
  • Shear Modulus measures a material’s resistance to shape change under shear stress. It is zero for fluids (liquids and gases) as they cannot sustain shear stress.
  • Poisson’s Ratio for most metals lies between 0.25 and 0.35. Cork has a Poisson’s Ratio close to 0, while rubber has a value close to 0.5.
  • The stress-strain curve for brittle materials (e.g., glass, cast iron) shows little to no plastic deformation before fracture.
  • Ductile materials (e.g., copper, aluminum) exhibit significant plastic deformation before breaking, making them suitable for wire drawing and forging.
  • Elastic hysteresis occurs when the loading and unloading curves of a material do not coincide, leading to energy loss in the form of heat. This is common in materials like rubber.
  • The area under the stress-strain curve up to the elastic limit represents the elastic potential energy stored per unit volume of the material.
  • For a given material, Young’s Modulus, Bulk Modulus, and Shear Modulus are related by the equation: 9/Y = 3/G + 1/B.

Memory aids

  • Hooke’s Law: 'Stress is directly proportional to strain' can be remembered as 'Hooke’s Law is Straight' (S ∝ S).
