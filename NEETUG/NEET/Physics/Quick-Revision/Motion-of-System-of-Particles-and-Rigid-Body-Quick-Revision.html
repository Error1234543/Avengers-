NEET Physics Quick Revision: Motion of System of Particles and Rigid Body

Definitions

  • {'term': 'System of Particles', 'definition': 'A collection of particles where the position and motion of each particle are interdependent. The system can be discrete (individual particles) or continuous (rigid bodies).'}
  • {'term': 'Rigid Body', 'definition': 'A body in which the distance between any two given points remains constant over time, regardless of external forces. It does not deform under stress.'}
  • {'term': 'Centre of Mass (COM)', 'definition': 'A unique point in a system of particles or rigid body where the entire mass of the system can be considered to be concentrated for translational motion. For a uniform body, it coincides with the geometric centre.'}
  • {'term': 'Moment of Inertia (I)', 'definition': "A measure of a body's resistance to rotational motion about a particular axis. It depends on the mass distribution relative to the axis of rotation. SI unit: kg·m²."}
  • {'term': 'Torque (τ)', 'definition': 'The rotational equivalent of force. It is the product of force and the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation to the line of action of the force. τ = r × F. SI unit: N·m.'}
  • {'term': 'Angular Momentum (L)', 'definition': 'The rotational equivalent of linear momentum. For a particle, L = r × p, where p is linear momentum. For a rigid body, L = Iω, where ω is angular velocity. SI unit: kg·m²/s.'}
  • {'term': 'Conservation of Angular Momentum', 'definition': 'In the absence of external torque, the total angular momentum of a system remains constant. Mathematically, L_initial = L_final.'}

Key concepts

  • {'concept': 'Centre of Mass Calculation', 'content': 'For a system of n particles, the position vector of the centre of mass (R) is given by R = (Σ m_i r_i) / (Σ m_i), where m_i and r_i are the mass and position vector of the ith particle, respectively. For continuous bodies, integration replaces summation: R = (∫ r dm) / (∫ dm). In uniform gravitational fields, the COM coincides with the centre of gravity.'}
  • {'concept': 'Moment of Inertia and Parallel Axis Theorem', 'content': 'Moment of inertia depends on the axis of rotation. The parallel axis theorem states that the moment of inertia about any axis parallel to an axis through the COM is I = I_com + Md², where M is the total mass and d is the perpendicular distance between the two axes. This is crucial for calculating I for complex bodies.'}
  • {'concept': 'Torque and Rotational Motion', 'content': "Torque causes angular acceleration (α) in a rigid body. The relationship is given by τ = Iα, analogous to Newton's second law (F = ma) for translational motion. The direction of torque is given by the right-hand rule and is perpendicular to both r and F."}
  • {'concept': 'Angular Momentum and Its Conservation', 'content': 'Angular momentum is conserved when no external torque acts on the system. This principle explains phenomena like the increase in angular velocity of a spinning ice skater when they pull their arms inward (reducing I, thus increasing ω to keep L constant). For a system of particles, L = Σ (r_i × p_i).'}
  • {'concept': 'Equations of Rotational Motion', 'content': 'For constant angular acceleration, the following equations apply: ω = ω₀ + αt, θ = ω₀t + 0.5αt², ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ, where ω₀ is initial angular velocity, ω is final angular velocity, α is angular acceleration, θ is angular displacement, and t is time. These are analogous to linear motion equations.'}
  • {'concept': 'Rolling Motion Without Slipping', 'content': 'For a body rolling without slipping, the condition v_com = rω must hold, where v_com is the linear velocity of the centre of mass and r is the radius. The total kinetic energy is the sum of translational and rotational kinetic energies: KE = 0.5 M v_com² + 0.5 I_com ω². For a solid sphere, I_com = (2/5)Mr².'}

Important facts

  • The centre of mass of a uniform symmetric body (e.g., sphere, cube, cylinder) lies at its geometric centre.
  • For a system of particles, the COM may lie outside the physical body (e.g., a hollow ring or a boomerang).
  • Moment of inertia is not a fixed property of a body; it depends on the axis of rotation. For example, I for a rod about its centre is (1/12)ML², but about one end it is (1/3)ML².
  • Torque is maximum when the force is applied perpendicular to the position vector (θ = 90°). No torque is produced if the force is parallel to the position vector (θ = 0° or 180°).
  • Conservation of angular momentum explains why planets speed up as they approach the Sun (Kepler's second law) and why a diver tucks their body to spin faster.
  • In rolling motion without slipping, the point of contact with the ground is instantaneously at rest. This is why static friction (not kinetic) acts on the rolling body.
  • The moment of inertia of a body can be reduced by redistributing mass closer to the axis of rotation, which increases angular velocity for the same angular momentum (e.g., ice skater pulling in arms).
  • For a rigid body in pure rotation, all particles have the same angular velocity but different linear velocities depending on their distance from the axis.
