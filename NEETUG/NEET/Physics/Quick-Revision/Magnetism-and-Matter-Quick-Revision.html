  • {'term': 'Paramagnetism', 'definition': 'Paramagnetism is a form of magnetism whereby certain materials are weakly attracted by an externally applied magnetic field and form internal, induced magnetic fields in the direction of the applied magnetic field. Key concepts

  • {'concept': 'Magnetic Dipole Moment and Torque', 'content': "A magnetic dipole of moment **m** placed in a uniform magnetic field **B** experiences a torque **τ = m × B**, which tends to align the dipole with the field. The potential energy of the dipole is given by U = -m·B. This explains why a compass needle aligns with Earth's magnetic field. In NEET, numerical problems often involve calculating torque or potential energy for a given orientation of the dipole."}
  • {'concept': "Earth's Magnetism", 'content': "Earth behaves like a giant magnetic dipole with its magnetic south pole near the geographic north pole and vice versa. Key parameters include: (1) **Magnetic Declination**: The angle between geographic north and magnetic north at a given location. (2) **Magnetic Inclination (Dip)**: The angle made by Earth's magnetic field with the horizontal at a location. At the magnetic poles, the dip is 90°, and at the magnetic equator, it is 0°. (3) **Horizontal Component (B_H)**: The component of Earth's magnetic field parallel to the surface, given by B_H = B cos θ, where θ is the dip angle."}
  • {'concept': 'Magnetic Materials and Their Properties', 'content': "Materials are classified based on their magnetic properties: (1) **Diamagnetic Materials**: χ < 0 (e.g., copper, water, bismuth). They are weakly repelled by a magnetic field. (2) **Paramagnetic Materials**: χ > 0 (e.g., aluminum, oxygen, platinum). They are weakly attracted by a magnetic field. (3) **Ferromagnetic Materials**: χ >> 0 (e.g., iron, cobalt, nickel). They exhibit strong attraction and can retain magnetization. Curie's Law (for paramagnets): χ ∝ 1/T, where T is the absolute temperature. Ferromagnets lose their properties above the Curie temperature."}
  • {'concept': 'Hysteresis Loop', 'content': 'The hysteresis loop is a graphical representation of the relationship between the magnetic field strength (H) and the magnetic flux density (B) for a ferromagnetic material. Key points: (1) **Retentivity (Br)**: The residual magnetism when H is reduced to zero. (2) **Coercivity (Hc)**: The reverse magnetic field required to reduce B to zero. (3) **Area of the Loop**: Represents the energy lost as heat during one cycle of magnetization. Soft magnetic materials (e.g., soft iron) have narrow loops (low Hc), while hard magnetic materials (e.g., steel) have wide loops (high Hc).'}
  • {'concept': 'Magnetic Field Due to a Bar Magnet', 'content': 'The magnetic field at a point along the axial line of a bar magnet of length 2l and magnetic moment m is given by B_axial = (μ₀/4π) * (2m)/(r² - l²)², where r is the distance from the center of the magnet. Important facts

  • The magnetic dipole moment of a current loop is given by **m = I * A**, where I is the current and A is the area vector of the loop. The direction of A is given by the right-hand thumb rule.
  • The magnetic field inside a solenoid is **B = μ₀nI**, where n is the number of turns per unit length and I is the current. For a toroid, the field is **B = (μ₀NI)/(2πr)**, where N is the total number of turns and r is the radius of the toroid.
  • The relative magnetic permeability (μ_r) of a material is defined as μ_r = μ/μ₀, where μ₀ is the permeability of free space (4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m). For diamagnetic materials, μ_r < 1; for paramagnetic materials, μ_r > 1; and for ferromagnetic materials, μ_r >> 1.
  • The magnetic susceptibility (χ) and relative permeability (μ_r) are related by **μ_r = 1 + χ**. For diamagnetic materials, χ is negative and small (e.g., -10⁻⁵); for paramagnetic materials, χ is positive and small (e.g., 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻³); for ferromagnetic materials, χ is positive and large (e.g., 10³ to 10⁵).
  • The magnetic field of Earth is approximately **3 × 10⁻⁵ T** (0.3 G) at the surface. The horizontal component (B_H) is maximum at the magnetic equator and zero at the magnetic poles, while the vertical component is maximum at the poles and zero at the equator.
  • Ferromagnetic materials exhibit **spontaneous magnetization** below their Curie temperature due to the alignment of magnetic domains. Above the Curie temperature, they become paramagnetic.
  • The **tangent galvanometer** is a device used to measure small currents by balancing the horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field with the magnetic field produced by the current. The current I is given by I = (2rB_H tan θ)/(μ₀n), where r is the radius of the coil, n is the number of turns, and θ is the deflection angle.
