NEET Physics: Quick Revision - Waves

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Wave: A disturbance that transfers energy from one point to another without transferring matter. Waves can be mechanical (requiring a medium) or electromagnetic (not requiring a medium).
  • Transverse Wave: A wave in which the particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation (e.g., electromagnetic waves, waves on a string).
  • Longitudinal Wave: A wave in which the particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation (e.g., sound waves).
  • Amplitude (A): The maximum displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position in a wave. It determines the energy carried by the wave.
  • Wavelength (λ): The distance between two consecutive points in phase (e.g., crest to crest or trough to trough) in a wave.
  • Frequency (f): The number of complete wave cycles passing a given point per unit time, measured in Hertz (Hz).
  • Time Period (T): The time taken for one complete wave cycle to pass a given point. It is the reciprocal of frequency (T = 1/f).
  • Wave Speed (v): The speed at which a wave propagates through a medium. For mechanical waves, it depends on the properties of the medium (v = fλ).
  • Phase: A measure of the position of a point in a wave cycle, often expressed in degrees or radians. Two points are in phase if they have the same displacement and direction of motion.
  • Superposition Principle: When two or more waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the displacements of the individual waves.

Mechanical waves require a medium (e.g., sound waves, water waves) and can be transverse or longitudinal. Electromagnetic waves (e.g., light, radio waves) do not require a medium and are always transverse. In NEET, focus on the properties and differences between these types, especially sound waves (longitudinal) and light waves (transverse).'}
  • {'concept': 'Equation of a Progressive Wave', 'content': 'The general equation for a progressive wave traveling along the positive x-axis is: y = A sin(kx - ωt + φ), where y is the displacement, A is the amplitude, k is the wave number (k = 2π/λ), ω is the angular frequency (ω = 2πf), t is time, x is position, and φ is the phase constant. For a wave traveling along the negative x-axis, the equation becomes y = A sin(kx + ωt + φ). Derive the relationship between wave speed, frequency, and wavelength (v = fλ) from this equation.'}
  • {'concept': 'Sound Waves', 'content': 'Sound waves are longitudinal mechanical waves that propagate through compressions and rarefactions in a medium. Key properties include: (1) Speed of sound depends on the medium (faster in solids > liquids > gases) and temperature (v ∝ √T for gases). (2) Intensity of sound is proportional to the square of the amplitude and decreases with distance (inverse square law for spherical waves). (3) Pitch is determined by frequency, while loudness is determined by intensity. (4) The Doppler effect describes the change in frequency of sound due to relative motion between the source and observer.'}
  • {'concept': 'Doppler Effect', 'content': "The Doppler effect is the apparent change in frequency of a wave due to the relative motion of the source and observer. For sound waves, the observed frequency (f') is given by: f' = f (v ± v₀) / (v ∓ vₛ), where v is the speed of sound, v₀ is the speed of the observer, and vₛ is the speed of the source. Use the upper signs (+ for v₀, - for vₛ) when the observer/source is moving toward the other, and lower signs when moving away. This concept is frequently tested in NEET with numerical problems."}
  • {'concept': 'Superposition and Interference', 'content': "When two or more waves superpose, they interfere constructively or destructively. Constructive interference occurs when waves are in phase (path difference = nλ), resulting in increased amplitude. Destructive interference occurs when waves are out of phase (path difference = (n + 1/2)λ), resulting in decreased amplitude. For two coherent sources (constant phase difference), the condition for maxima and minima can be derived using path difference. Interference is a key concept in wave optics (e.g., Young's double-slit experiment)."}
  • {'concept': 'Standing Waves', 'content': 'Standing waves are formed by the superposition of two waves of the same frequency and amplitude traveling in opposite directions. They are characterized by nodes (points of zero displacement) and antinodes (points of maximum displacement). The distance between two consecutive nodes or antinodes is λ/2. Standing waves are important in musical instruments (e.g., strings, air columns) and are described by the equation: y = 2A sin(kx) cos(ωt). For a string fixed at both ends, the fundamental frequency (first harmonic) is f = v/(2L), where L is the length of the string.'}
  • {'concept': 'Beats', 'content': 'Beats occur when two waves of slightly different frequencies superpose, resulting in a periodic variation in amplitude. The beat frequency is given by f_beat = |f₁ - f₂|. Beats are used to tune musical instruments and are a common application of the superposition principle in NEET.'}
  • {'concept': 'Reflection and Refraction of Waves', 'content': "When a wave encounters a boundary between two media, it can be reflected or refracted. Reflection involves a change in direction of the wave, while refraction involves a change in speed and direction (described by Snell's law for light waves: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂). For sound waves, refraction occurs due to changes in temperature or medium density. Reflection of sound waves is responsible for echoes."}
