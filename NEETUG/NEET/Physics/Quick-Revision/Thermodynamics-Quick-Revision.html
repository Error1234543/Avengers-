Definitions

  • Thermodynamics: The branch of physics that deals with the relationships between heat, work, temperature, and energy. It describes how thermal energy is converted to and from other forms of energy and how it affects matter.
  • System: A specific portion of the universe under study, separated from the surroundings by a boundary. Systems can be open (exchange of energy and matter), closed (exchange of energy only), or isolated (no exchange).
  • Surroundings: Everything outside the system that can exchange energy or matter with it.
  • Thermodynamic Equilibrium: A state where the macroscopic properties of a system (pressure, volume, temperature) do not change with time. It includes thermal, mechanical, and chemical equilibrium.
  • State Variables: Properties that define the state of a system, such as pressure (P), volume (V), temperature (T), internal energy (U), enthalpy (H), and entropy (S).
  • Process: A transition of a system from one equilibrium state to another. Processes can be isothermal (constant temperature), adiabatic (no heat transfer), isobaric (constant pressure), or isochoric (constant volume).
  • Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics: If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This law establishes temperature as a fundamental property.
  • First Law of Thermodynamics: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or converted. Mathematically, ΔU = Q - W, where ΔU is the change in internal energy, Q is heat added to the system, and W is work done by the system.
  • Second Law of Thermodynamics: The total entropy of an isolated system always increases over time. It introduces the concept of irreversibility and defines the direction of spontaneous processes.
  • Third Law of Thermodynamics: As the temperature of a system approaches absolute zero, the entropy approaches a minimum constant value (usually zero for a perfect crystal).

Important facts

  • The first law of thermodynamics is a statement of energy conservation, while the second law introduces the concept of irreversibility and entropy.
  • Work done by a gas during expansion is positive, and work done on the gas during compression is negative (sign convention).
  • For an ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature (U ∝ T).
  • In an isothermal process, the temperature remains constant, so ΔU = 0 for an ideal gas, and Q = W.
  • In an adiabatic process, no heat is exchanged (Q = 0), so ΔU = -W. The relationship PV^γ = constant holds for an ideal gas.
  • The efficiency of a heat engine can never exceed that of a Carnot engine operating between the same two temperatures.
  • Entropy is a state function, but heat and work are path functions.
  • The third law of thermodynamics implies that absolute zero (0 K) is unattainable in a finite number of steps.
  • For a reversible process, the entropy change of the universe is zero. For an irreversible process, it is positive.
  • The coefficient of performance (COP) of a refrigerator or heat pump can be greater than 1, unlike the efficiency of a heat engine (which is always ≤ 1).
