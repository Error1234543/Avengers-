NEET Physics Quick Revision: Moving Charges and Magnetism

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Magnetic Field (B): A vector field around a moving electric charge or a magnet, measured in tesla (T) or gauss (G), where 1 T = 10⁴ G. It exerts a force on moving charges or other magnets.
  • Lorentz Force: The combined electric and magnetic force on a point charge q moving with velocity v in electric field E and magnetic field B: F = q(E + v × B).
  • Cyclotron Frequency: The angular frequency (ω = qB/m) at which a charged particle spirals in a uniform magnetic field, independent of its velocity.
  • Biot-Savart Law: The magnetic field dB at a point due to a small current element Idl is dB = (μ₀/4π) * (Idl × r̂)/r², where r is the distance from the element to the point.
  • Ampere’s Circuital Law: The line integral of magnetic field around any closed loop equals μ₀ times the total current passing through the loop: ∮B·dl = μ₀I_enc.
  • Solenoid: A tightly wound helical coil of wire that produces a uniform magnetic field inside when carrying current. Ideal solenoid field: B = μ₀nI, where n is turns per unit length.
  • Toroid: A doughnut-shaped coil with a uniform magnetic field inside its core: B = (μ₀NI)/(2πr), where N is total turns and r is the radius of the circular path.

Important facts

  • The magnetic force on a moving charge does no work because it is always perpendicular to velocity. Thus, it changes direction but not speed.
  • A charged particle moving parallel to a magnetic field experiences no magnetic force.
  • The magnetic field inside a toroid is uniform along a circular path but varies with radius. It is zero outside the toroid.
  • The magnetic field due to a circular loop is maximum at the center and decreases as you move along the axis.
  • Ampere’s law is most useful for calculating magnetic fields in situations with high symmetry (e.g., straight wires, solenoids, toroids).
  • The right-hand rule for a current-carrying wire: thumb in direction of current, fingers curl in direction of magnetic field.
  • The right-hand rule for Lorentz force: index finger in direction of velocity, middle finger in direction of B, thumb gives force direction for positive charge (reverse for negative).
  • A velocity selector uses perpendicular E and B fields to filter particles by velocity: only particles with v = E/B pass undeflected.

Memory aids

  • F = qvB sinθ: Remember 'FBI' — Force = q * v * B * sinθ. The angle θ is between v and B.
  • Right-hand rule for wire: 'Thumb for current, fingers for field' — like holding a wire with your right hand.
  • Cyclotron frequency: ω = qB/m — 'Quick Bee over Em' for the formula.
  • Solenoid field: B = μ₀nI — 'Moon Night' (μ₀ n I) to remember the variables.
  • Torque on loop: τ = NIAB sinθ — 'NIABS' (like 'knives') for the variables.

Common traps

  • Forgetting that magnetic force is perpendicular to both velocity and magnetic field, not along either. This leads to incorrect direction predictions.
  • Assuming magnetic force does work on a charge. It does not — kinetic energy remains constant in a pure magnetic field.
  • Mixing up the right-hand rules for current-carrying wires and moving charges. Use the correct rule for each scenario.
