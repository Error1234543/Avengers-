NEET Physics Quick Revision: Current Electricity

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Electric Current (I): The rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor, measured in amperes (A). Defined as I = ΔQ/Δt, where ΔQ is the charge flowing through a cross-section in time Δt.
  • Drift Velocity (v_d): The average velocity acquired by charge carriers (electrons) in a conductor under the influence of an electric field. Given by v_d = (I)/(nAe), where n is the number density of charge carriers, A is the cross-sectional area, and e is the charge of an electron.
  • Ohm's Law: For a conductor at constant temperature, the current (I) flowing through it is directly proportional to the potential difference (V) across its ends. Mathematically, V = IR, where R is the resistance of the conductor.
  • Resistance (R): The opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current. Measured in ohms (Ω). Depends on material properties (resistivity, ρ), length (L), and cross-sectional area (A): R = ρL/A.
  • Resistivity (ρ): A material property representing the resistance of a unit length and unit cross-sectional area of the material. Measured in ohm-meter (Ω·m). Inversely related to conductivity (σ): ρ = 1/σ.
  • Electrical Power (P): The rate at which electrical energy is dissipated or consumed in a circuit. Given by P = VI = I²R = V²/R, measured in watts (W).
  • Electromotive Force (emf, ε): The maximum potential difference provided by a cell or battery when no current is drawn from it. Measured in volts (V). Represents the work done per unit charge to move charge through the entire circuit.
  • Internal Resistance (r): The resistance offered by the electrolyte and electrodes inside a cell or battery to the flow of current. Causes the terminal voltage (V) to be less than the emf when current is drawn: V = ε - Ir.

Non-ohmic devices (e.g., diodes, thermistors) do not follow Ohm's Law. For such devices, the V-I graph is non-linear. NEET often tests the distinction between ohmic and non-ohmic behavior, especially in circuit analysis questions."}
  • {'concept': 'Combination of Resistors', 'content': 'Resistors can be combined in series or parallel. In series, equivalent resistance (R_eq) is the sum of individual resistances: R_eq = R₁ + R₂ + ... + Rₙ. Current is the same through all resistors, but voltage divides. In parallel, the reciprocal of equivalent resistance is the sum of reciprocals: 1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ... + 1/Rₙ. Voltage is the same across all resistors, but current divides. For two resistors in parallel, R_eq = (R₁R₂)/(R₁ + R₂).'}
  • {'concept': "Kirchhoff's Laws", 'content': "Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL): The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum of currents leaving it (conservation of charge). Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL): The algebraic sum of all potential differences around any closed loop is zero (conservation of energy). These laws are essential for analyzing complex circuits, especially those with multiple loops or cells. NEET questions often require applying KVL to find unknown currents or voltages in circuits with 2-3 loops."}
  • {'concept': 'Wheatstone Bridge and Meter Bridge', 'content': 'A Wheatstone bridge is a circuit used to measure unknown resistances. It consists of four resistors (R₁, R₂, R₃, R₄) arranged in a diamond shape with a galvanometer between two opposite junctions. The bridge is balanced when R₁/R₂ = R₃/R₄, and no current flows through the galvanometer. The meter bridge is a practical application of the Wheatstone bridge, where the unknown resistance is determined using the null deflection method. NEET frequently tests the condition for balance and calculations involving unknown resistances.'}
  • {'concept': 'Potentiometer', 'content': 'A potentiometer is a device used to measure the emf of a cell or compare emfs of two cells without drawing current from them. It works on the principle that the potential drop across a segment of a wire is directly proportional to its length (V ∝ L). For comparing emfs of two cells (ε₁ and ε₂), ε₁/ε₂ = L₁/L₂, where L₁ and L₂ are the balancing lengths. The potentiometer is more accurate than a voltmeter because it does not draw current from the cell being measured.'}
  • {'concept': 'Heating Effect of Current', 'content': "When current flows through a conductor, electrical energy is dissipated as heat. The heat produced (H) is given by Joule's Law: H = I²Rt, where I is the current, R is the resistance, and t is the time. This principle is used in devices like electric heaters, bulbs, and fuses. NEET questions often involve calculating heat produced, power dissipated, or comparing heating effects in different resistors."}
  • {'concept': 'Cells in Series and Parallel', 'content': 'Cells can be combined to increase the total emf or reduce internal resistance. In series, the total emf is the sum of individual emfs, and the total internal resistance is the sum of individual internal resistances: ε_total = ε₁ + ε₂ + ... + εₙ, r_total = r₁ + r₂ + ... + rₙ. In parallel, the total emf remains the same as that of one cell (if all cells are identical), but the total internal resistance decreases: 1/r_total = 1/r₁ + 1/r₂ + ... + 1/rₙ. For n identical cells in parallel, r_total = r/n.'}
