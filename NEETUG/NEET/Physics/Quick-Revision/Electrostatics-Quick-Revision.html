NEET Physics: Quick Revision - Electrostatics

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Electrostatics: The study of stationary electric charges and the forces, fields, and potentials associated with them.
  • Electric Charge (q): A fundamental property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electromagnetic field. SI unit: Coulomb (C).
  • Coulomb's Law: The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. Formula: F = k * (q₁ * q₂) / r², where k = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻² (Coulomb's constant).
  • Electric Field (E): The force per unit positive charge experienced by a test charge placed in the field. Formula: E = F / q. SI unit: N C⁻¹ or V m⁻¹.
  • Electric Potential (V): The work done per unit charge in bringing a positive test charge from infinity to a point in the field. Formula: V = W / q. SI unit: Volt (V).
  • Electric Potential Energy (U): The energy a charge possesses due to its position in an electric field. Formula: U = q * V. SI unit: Joule (J).
  • Capacitance (C): The ability of a system to store charge per unit potential difference. Formula: C = Q / V. SI unit: Farad (F).
  • Dielectric: An insulating material that, when placed between the plates of a capacitor, increases its capacitance by reducing the electric field strength.

Important facts

  • The electric field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is zero. Any excess charge resides on the surface.
  • The electric field just outside the surface of a charged conductor is perpendicular to the surface and has magnitude E = σ / ε₀, where σ is the surface charge density.
  • The potential at all points inside a charged spherical shell is the same as the potential on its surface.
  • For a point charge, the electric potential at a distance r is V = k * q / r. Note that potential is a scalar, so potentials due to multiple charges add algebraically.
  • The work done in moving a charge between two points in an electric field is independent of the path taken (conservative field).
  • The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor increases by a factor of K when a dielectric of dielectric constant K is inserted between the plates.
  • When a dielectric is inserted into a charged capacitor (disconnected from the battery), the voltage decreases by a factor of K, but the charge remains constant. When connected to a battery, the voltage remains constant, but the charge increases by a factor of K.
  • The energy density (energy per unit volume) in an electric field is given by u = ½ * ε₀ * E².
