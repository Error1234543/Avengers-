NEET Physics Quick Revision: Semiconductor Electronics

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Semiconductor: A material with electrical conductivity between that of conductors and insulators, typically having a resistivity in the range of 10⁻⁴ to 10⁷ Ωm. Its conductivity increases with temperature, unlike metals.
  • Intrinsic Semiconductor: A pure semiconductor (e.g., silicon or germanium) with no impurities, where the number of electrons in the conduction band equals the number of holes in the valence band at thermal equilibrium.
  • Extrinsic Semiconductor: A semiconductor doped with impurities to modify its electrical properties. It can be n-type (doped with donor impurities like phosphorus) or p-type (doped with acceptor impurities like boron).
  • Doping: The intentional introduction of impurities into a semiconductor to change its electrical conductivity. Donor impurities add free electrons, while acceptor impurities create holes.
  • PN Junction: A boundary or interface between p-type and n-type semiconductor materials, forming a depletion region that acts as a potential barrier to current flow.
  • Diode: A two-terminal electronic component with a PN junction that allows current to flow in one direction (forward bias) and blocks it in the opposite direction (reverse bias).
  • Transistor: A three-terminal semiconductor device (BJT or FET) used to amplify or switch electronic signals. Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJTs) operate using both electrons and holes, while Field-Effect Transistors (FETs) use an electric field to control current.
  • Logic Gates: Electronic circuits that perform basic logical functions (AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR) and are the building blocks of digital circuits.
  • Rectifier: A device that converts alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC) using diodes. Half-wave rectifiers use one diode, while full-wave rectifiers use two or four diodes.

Important facts

  • The conductivity of a semiconductor increases with temperature due to the increased generation of electron-hole pairs, unlike metals where conductivity decreases with temperature.
  • The mobility of electrons (μe) is higher than that of holes (μh) in most semiconductors, leading to faster electron movement in n-type materials.
  • The built-in potential (Vbi) of a PN junction at room temperature is approximately 0.7 V for silicon and 0.3 V for germanium.
  • Zener diodes are designed to operate in reverse breakdown mode and are used for voltage regulation. They maintain a constant voltage over a range of currents.
  • The current gain (β) of a BJT is temperature-dependent and decreases with increasing temperature.
  • MOSFETs are the most widely used transistors in digital integrated circuits due to their low power consumption and high input impedance.
  • The fan-out of a logic gate is the maximum number of inputs that can be connected to its output without degrading performance. It depends on the gate's output current capability and the input current requirements of the connected gates.
  • CMOS (Complementary MOS) logic gates use both n-channel and p-channel MOSFETs to achieve low power dissipation and high noise immunity, making them ideal for modern digital circuits.
