Important facts

  • The gravitational force is the weakest of the four fundamental forces but has an infinite range, making it dominant at astronomical scales.
  • The electromagnetic force is about 10³⁶ times stronger than the gravitational force and governs most everyday phenomena, including chemical reactions and light.
  • The strong nuclear force is the strongest of the four fundamental forces but acts only over a very short range (~10⁻¹⁵ m), binding protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
  • The weak nuclear force is responsible for beta decay and neutrino interactions, playing a crucial role in nuclear fusion in stars.
  • The SI unit of length, the meter, is defined as the distance traveled by light in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second.
  • The SI unit of mass, the kilogram, was redefined in 2019 based on the Planck constant (h), a fundamental constant of quantum mechanics.
  • The dimensional formula for force is [M L T⁻²], and for energy, it is [M L² T⁻²].
  • Accuracy and precision are not the same: accuracy refers to closeness to the true value, while precision refers to the consistency of repeated measurements.
  • Systematic errors can be minimized by calibrating instruments and improving experimental techniques, while random errors can be reduced by taking multiple measurements and averaging them.
  • The number of significant figures in a measurement reflects its precision. For example, 0.00450 has three significant figures (4, 5, and the trailing zero).
