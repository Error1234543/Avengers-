NEET Physics Quick Revision: Oscillations

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Oscillations: The repetitive variation, typically in time, of some measure about a central value (often a point of equilibrium) or between two or more different states.
  • Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM): A type of periodic motion where the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement and acts in the direction opposite to that of displacement. Mathematically, F = -kx, where F is the restoring force, k is the force constant, and x is the displacement.
  • Amplitude (A): The maximum displacement from the equilibrium position in SHM.
  • Time Period (T): The time taken to complete one full oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): The number of oscillations per unit time. It is the reciprocal of the time period, f = 1/T.
  • Angular Frequency (ω): The rate of change of phase of a sinusoidal waveform, or the rate of oscillation in radians per second. ω = 2πf = 2π/T.
  • Phase: A measure of the position of a point in time on a waveform cycle, often expressed as an angle in radians.
  • Damping: The gradual decrease in amplitude of an oscillatory system due to dissipative forces like friction or resistance.
  • Resonance: The phenomenon where a system oscillates with greater amplitude at specific frequencies, known as resonant frequencies.

Important facts

  • In SHM, the acceleration is always directed towards the equilibrium position and is proportional to the displacement from it.
  • The time period of a simple pendulum is independent of the mass of the bob and the amplitude (for small angles).
  • The total energy in SHM is constant and is proportional to the square of the amplitude.
  • In damped oscillations, the system loses energy over time, and the amplitude decreases exponentially.
  • Resonance can lead to large amplitude oscillations, which can be destructive in mechanical systems (e.g., bridges, buildings).
  • The phase difference between displacement, velocity, and acceleration in SHM is 90° (π/2 radians). Velocity leads displacement by 90°, and acceleration leads velocity by 90°.
  • For a spring-mass system, the time period increases with the mass and decreases with the spring constant.
  • The restoring force in SHM is conservative, meaning the work done by it over a complete cycle is zero.

Memory aids

  • SHM Formula Trick: Remember the basic SHM equation x = A sin(ωt + φ) as 'A sin of omega t plus phi'.
  • Time Period Mnemonic: For a spring-mass system, T = 2π√(m/k) can be remembered as '2π root m over k'. For a pendulum, T = 2π√(L/g) is '2π root L over g'.
  • Energy in SHM: Total energy E = (1/2)kA². Think of it as 'half k A squared'.
  • Phase Relationship: 'Displacement lags velocity by 90°, velocity lags acceleration by 90°'. Visualize a sine wave where velocity is the cosine (shifted left by 90°) and acceleration is the negative sine (shifted left by another 90°).
  • Damping Effect: The amplitude in damped oscillations decreases as e^(-bt/2m). Remember 'e to the power of negative b t over 2 m'.
