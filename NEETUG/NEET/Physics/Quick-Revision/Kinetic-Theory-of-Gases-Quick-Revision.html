NEET Physics Quick Revision: Kinetic Theory of Gases

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Kinetic Theory of Gases: A microscopic model that explains the macroscopic properties of gases (pressure, temperature, volume) by considering the motion of individual gas molecules.
  • Ideal Gas: A hypothetical gas that perfectly follows the gas laws (Boyle’s, Charles’s, Avogadro’s) under all conditions of temperature and pressure. Real gases approximate ideal behavior at low pressures and high temperatures.
  • Root Mean Square (RMS) Speed: The square root of the average of the squares of the speeds of all molecules in a gas. It is a measure of the typical speed of gas molecules at a given temperature.
  • Mean Free Path: The average distance a gas molecule travels between successive collisions with other molecules.
  • Boltzmann Constant (k): A physical constant relating the average kinetic energy of gas molecules to the absolute temperature of the gas. Value: 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K.
  • Avogadro’s Number (Nₐ): The number of molecules in one mole of a substance. Value: 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

This relationship shows that temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of gas molecules. For one mole of gas, the total kinetic energy is (3/2) * R * T, where R is the universal gas constant (R = Nₐ * k).'}

Important facts

  • The universal gas constant R is related to the Boltzmann constant k by R = Nₐ * k, where Nₐ is Avogadro’s number.
  • At the same temperature, lighter gas molecules have higher RMS speeds than heavier ones (v_rms ∝ 1/√M). This explains why hydrogen diffuses faster than oxygen.
  • The ratio of RMS speed to average speed to most probable speed is √3 : √(8/π) : √2, approximately 1.225 : 1.128 : 1.
  • The mean free path of air molecules at room temperature and atmospheric pressure is approximately 68 nm.
  • For a diatomic gas at room temperature, the molar specific heat at constant volume (C_v) is (5/2)R, and at constant pressure (C_p) is (7/2)R, due to 5 active degrees of freedom.
  • The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution is temperature-dependent but independent of pressure for an ideal gas.
  • The van der Waals constant 'a' is larger for gases with stronger intermolecular forces (e.g., ammonia has a higher 'a' than helium).
  • The critical temperature of a gas is the highest temperature at which the gas can be liquefied by pressure alone. Above this temperature, no amount of pressure can liquefy the gas.

Memory aids

  • To remember the order of molecular speeds: 'RMS is the fastest, most probable is the slowest' (v_rms > v_avg > v_mp).
