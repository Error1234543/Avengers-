NEET Physics Quick Revision: Mechanical Properties of Fluids

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Fluid: A substance that can flow and does not have a fixed shape. Includes liquids and gases.
  • Density (ρ): Mass per unit volume of a substance. Formula: ρ = m/V, where m is mass and V is volume. SI unit: kg/m³.
  • Pressure (P): Force exerted per unit area. Formula: P = F/A, where F is force and A is area. SI unit: Pascal (Pa) or N/m².
  • Pascal’s Law: Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished throughout the fluid in all directions.
  • Viscosity: A measure of a fluid’s resistance to flow. Higher viscosity means thicker fluid. SI unit: Pascal-second (Pa·s).
  • Surface Tension (T): The property of a liquid surface to behave like a stretched elastic membrane. SI unit: N/m.
  • Streamline Flow: Flow in which every particle of the fluid follows the same path as the preceding particle. Also called laminar flow.
  • Turbulent Flow: Irregular flow with random variations in pressure and velocity, characterized by eddies and whirls.
  • Bernoulli’s Principle: For an incompressible, non-viscous fluid in streamline flow, the sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy per unit volume remains constant.
  • Torricelli’s Law: The velocity of efflux of a liquid from an orifice is given by v = √(2gh), where h is the height of the liquid above the orifice.

Important facts

  • Atmospheric pressure at sea level is approximately 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa (760 mm Hg).
  • The density of water is 1000 kg/m³ at 4°C, which is its maximum density.
  • The angle of contact determines whether a liquid wets a surface: acute angle (θ < 90°) means wetting (e.g., water on glass), obtuse angle (θ > 90°) means non-wetting (e.g., mercury on glass).
  • The terminal velocity of a falling object in a viscous fluid is reached when the viscous drag force equals the weight of the object minus the buoyant force.
  • Bernoulli’s equation assumes incompressible, non-viscous, and irrotational flow; real fluids deviate from this due to viscosity and turbulence.
  • The Magnus effect, caused by pressure differences due to rotation, explains the curved path of spinning balls in sports like cricket and tennis.
  • Blood pressure in humans is typically measured in mm Hg, with normal systolic/diastolic values around 120/80 mm Hg.
  • The Venturi meter uses Bernoulli’s principle to measure fluid flow rate by creating a pressure difference in a constricted pipe.
