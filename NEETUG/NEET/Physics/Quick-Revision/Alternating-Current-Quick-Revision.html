NEET Physics Quick Revision: Alternating Current

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Alternating Current (AC): An electric current that reverses its direction periodically, typically sinusoidal in nature, described by the equation I = I₀ sin(ωt), where I₀ is the peak current, ω is the angular frequency, and t is time.
  • Peak Value (I₀ or V₀): The maximum value of current or voltage in an AC circuit, measured from zero to the highest point of the waveform.
  • Root Mean Square (RMS) Value: The effective value of an alternating current or voltage, calculated as I_rms = I₀/√2 or V_rms = V₀/√2. It represents the equivalent DC value that would produce the same power dissipation in a resistive load.
  • Angular Frequency (ω): The rate of change of phase of the AC waveform, related to the frequency (f) by ω = 2πf. Unit: radians per second (rad/s).
  • Phase Difference (φ): The angular difference between the voltage and current waveforms in an AC circuit, determined by the nature of the circuit elements (resistor, inductor, capacitor).
  • Impedance (Z): The total opposition offered by an AC circuit to the flow of current, combining resistance (R), inductive reactance (X_L), and capacitive reactance (X_C). Unit: ohm (Ω).
  • Resonance: A condition in an AC circuit where the inductive reactance (X_L) equals the capacitive reactance (X_C), resulting in maximum current flow and minimum impedance. The resonant frequency is given by f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)).
  • Power Factor (cos φ): The ratio of the real power (P) to the apparent power (S) in an AC circuit, given by cos φ = R/Z. It indicates the efficiency of power usage in the circuit.

Important facts

  • In a purely resistive AC circuit, voltage and current are in phase, meaning they reach their peak values simultaneously.
  • In a purely inductive AC circuit, voltage leads current by 90°, while in a purely capacitive AC circuit, voltage lags current by 90°.
  • The impedance of an AC circuit depends on the frequency of the AC source. For example, inductive reactance increases with frequency, while capacitive reactance decreases with frequency.
  • At resonance in an RLC circuit, the impedance is minimum (equal to R), and the current is maximum. The circuit behaves as purely resistive.
  • The quality factor (Q) of a resonant circuit indicates the sharpness of the resonance peak. Higher Q values correspond to narrower and taller resonance peaks.
  • Transformers are highly efficient (typically >95%) due to the absence of moving parts and minimal energy loss, primarily through copper losses (I²R) and core losses (hysteresis and eddy currents).
  • AC power transmission is preferred over DC for long distances because transformers can easily step up voltages to reduce current, minimizing power loss (I²R losses) during transmission.
