This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Moving Charges and Magnetism, designed to match the style, difficulty, and coverage of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. A charged particle moving with velocity **v** enters a uniform magnetic field **B** perpendicular to its velocity. The path followed by the particle inside the field is:

A. Straight line
B. Parabolic
C. Circular
D. Helical
Answer: C

When a charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to its velocity, the magnetic force acts as a centripetal force, causing the particle to move in a circular path. The radius of the path depends on the particle's mass, charge, velocity, and the magnetic field strength.

2. The magnetic force on a current-carrying wire placed in a magnetic field is given by **F = I(L × B)**, where **L** is the length vector of the wire. If the wire is parallel to the magnetic field, the force experienced is:

A. Maximum
B. Zero
C. Half of the maximum value
D. Dependent on the current
Answer: B

The magnetic force on a current-carrying wire is given by the cross product **F = I(L × B)**. If the wire is parallel to the magnetic field, the angle between **L** and **B** is 0°, making the cross product zero. Thus, the force experienced is zero.

3. A proton and an alpha particle enter the same magnetic field perpendicular to the field lines with the same kinetic energy. The ratio of the radii of their circular paths is:

A. 1:1
B. 1:2
C. 2:1
D. 1:√2
Answer: D

The radius of the circular path of a charged particle in a magnetic field is given by **r = √(2mK)/(qB)**, where **m** is mass, **K** is kinetic energy, **q** is charge, and **B** is the magnetic field. For a proton (charge **e**, mass **m**) and an alpha particle (charge **2e**, mass **4m**), the ratio of radii is **√(2mK)/(eB) : √(2·4mK)/(2eB) = 1:√2**.

4. A circular coil of radius **R** carrying current **I** produces a magnetic field **B** at its center. If the radius of the coil is doubled while keeping the current the same, the new magnetic field at the center will be:

A. B/2
B. B/4
C. 2B
D. B
Answer: A

The magnetic field at the center of a circular coil is given by **B = (μ₀I)/(2R)**. If the radius is doubled (**2R**), the new magnetic field becomes **B' = (μ₀I)/(4R) = B/2**.

5. Two long parallel wires carry currents **I₁** and **I₂** in the same direction. The force per unit length experienced by each wire is:

A. Attractive and proportional to **I₁I₂**
B. Repulsive and proportional to **I₁I₂**
C. Attractive and proportional to **I₁/I₂**
D. Repulsive and proportional to **I₁/I₂**
Answer: A

When two parallel wires carry currents in the same direction, they experience an attractive force per unit length given by **F/L = (μ₀I₁I₂)/(2πd)**, where **d** is the distance between the wires. The force is proportional to the product of the currents.

6. A charged particle of mass **m** and charge **q** moves in a circular path of radius **r** in a uniform magnetic field **B**. The time period of its motion is independent of:

A. Mass **m**
B. Charge **q**
C. Velocity **v**
D. Magnetic field **B**
Answer: C

The time period of a charged particle in a circular path in a magnetic field is given by **T = (2πm)/(qB)**. It is independent of the particle's velocity **v** and depends only on mass **m**, charge **q**, and magnetic field **B**.

7. A solenoid of length **L** and **N** turns carries a current **I**. The magnetic field inside the solenoid is **B**. If the number of turns is doubled while keeping the length and current the same, the new magnetic field will be:

A. B/2
B. B
C. 2B
D. 4B
Answer: C

The magnetic field inside a solenoid is given by **B = (μ₀NI)/L**. If the number of turns **N** is doubled, the new magnetic field becomes **B' = (μ₀·2NI)/L = 2B**.

8. A charged particle moves in a region where both electric (**E**) and magnetic (**B**) fields are present. The particle experiences no net force when:

A. **E** and **B** are parallel and the particle is at rest
B. **E** and **B** are perpendicular and the particle moves parallel to **E**
C. **E** and **B** are perpendicular and the particle moves perpendicular to both
D. **E** and **B** are equal in magnitude and the particle moves parallel to **B**
Answer: C

The net force on a charged particle in electric and magnetic fields is **F = q(E + v × B)**. For no net force, **E + v × B = 0**. This occurs when **E** and **B** are perpendicular, and the particle moves perpendicular to both with velocity **v = E/B**.

9. The magnetic moment of a current loop is given by **μ = IA**, where **I** is the current and **A** is the area vector. If the current in the loop is reversed, the magnetic moment:

A. Remains the same
B. Reverses direction but magnitude remains the same
C. Becomes zero
D. Doubles in magnitude
Answer: B

The magnetic moment **μ = IA** is a vector quantity. Reversing the current **I** reverses the direction of **μ**, but its magnitude (**IA**) remains unchanged.

10. A proton enters a uniform magnetic field **B** with velocity **v** at an angle **θ** to the field lines. The path followed by the proton is:

A. Circular
B. Straight line
C. Helical
D. Parabolic
Answer: C

When a charged particle enters a magnetic field at an angle **θ** (not 0° or 90°), its velocity can be resolved into components parallel and perpendicular to the field. The perpendicular component causes circular motion, while the parallel component causes linear motion, resulting in a helical path.

11. The torque experienced by a current loop of magnetic moment **μ** in a uniform magnetic field **B** is given by:

A. **τ = μ × B**
B. **τ = μ · B**
C. **τ = μB sinθ**
D. **τ = μB cosθ**
Answer: A

The torque on a current loop in a magnetic field is given by the cross product **τ = μ × B**, where **μ** is the magnetic moment and **B** is the magnetic field. The magnitude of the torque is **τ = μB sinθ**, where **θ** is the angle between **μ** and **B**.

12. A charged particle moves in a circular path of radius **r** in a uniform magnetic field **B**. If the magnetic field is doubled, the radius of the path becomes:

A. **r/2**
B. **r**
C. **2r**
D. **r/√2**
Answer: A

The radius of the circular path of a charged particle in a magnetic field is given by **r = mv/(qB)**. If the magnetic field **B** is doubled, the new radius becomes **r' = mv/(q·2B) = r/2**.

13. A proton enters a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T with a velocity of 2 × 10^6 m/s at an angle of 30° to the field. The magnitude of the magnetic force acting on the proton is (charge of proton = 1.6 × 10^-19 C):

A. 8 × 10^-14 N
B. 1.6 × 10^-13 N
C. 3.2 × 10^-14 N
D. 4 × 10^-14 N
Answer: A

The magnetic force on a charged particle is given by F = qvB sinθ, where q is the charge, v is the velocity, B is the magnetic field, and θ is the angle between velocity and field. Substituting the values: F = (1.6 × 10^-19 C)(2 × 10^6 m/s)(0.5 T) sin(30°) = 8 × 10^-14 N.

14. Two long parallel wires carry currents of 3 A and 5 A in the same direction. If the wires are 0.2 m apart, the force per unit length experienced by each wire is:

A. 1.5 × 10^-5 N/m, attractive
B. 3 × 10^-5 N/m, repulsive
C. 1.5 × 10^-5 N/m, repulsive
D. 3 × 10^-5 N/m, attractive
Answer: A

The force per unit length between two parallel current-carrying wires is given by F/L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2π d), where I₁ and I₂ are the currents and d is the distance between wires. Since currents are in the same direction, the force is attractive. Substituting values: F/L = (4π × 10^-7 T·m/A)(3 A)(5 A) / (2π × 0.2 m) = 1.5 × 10^-5 N/m.

15. A circular coil of radius 0.1 m carries a current of 5 A. The magnetic field at the center of the coil is (μ₀ = 4π × 10^-7 T·m/A):

A. 3.14 × 10^-5 T
B. 6.28 × 10^-5 T
C. 1.57 × 10^-5 T
D. 1.26 × 10^-4 T
Answer: A

The magnetic field at the center of a circular coil is given by B = (μ₀ I) / (2R), where I is the current and R is the radius. Substituting values: B = (4π × 10^-7 T·m/A)(5 A) / (2 × 0.1 m) = 3.14 × 10^-5 T.

16. An electron moving with a velocity of 10^6 m/s enters a uniform magnetic field of 0.1 T perpendicular to the field. The radius of the circular path described by the electron is (mass of electron = 9.1 × 10^-31 kg, charge = 1.6 × 10^-19 C):

A. 5.69 × 10^-5 m
B. 1.14 × 10^-4 m
C. 2.84 × 10^-5 m
D. 5.69 × 10^-6 m
Answer: A

The radius of the circular path is given by r = (mv) / (qB), where m is the mass, v is the velocity, q is the charge, and B is the magnetic field. Substituting values: r = (9.1 × 10^-31 kg)(10^6 m/s) / (1.6 × 10^-19 C)(0.1 T) = 5.69 × 10^-5 m.

17. A galvanometer of resistance 50 Ω gives full-scale deflection for a current of 10 mA. To convert it into a voltmeter of range 0-10 V, the resistance to be connected in series is:

A. 950
B. 1000
C. 500
D. 450
Answer: A

To convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter, a series resistance R is added such that V = I_g (G + R), where V is the voltage range, I_g is the full-scale deflection current, and G is the galvanometer resistance. Solving for R: R = (V / I_g) - G = (10 V / 0.01 A) - 50 Ω = 950 Ω.

18. A charged particle of mass m and charge q is accelerated through a potential difference V and then enters a uniform magnetic field B perpendicular to its velocity. The time period of its circular motion is independent of:

A. V
B. B
C. q
D. m
Answer: A

The time period of circular motion in a magnetic field is given by T = (2πm) / (qB). It is independent of the velocity (and hence the potential difference V) of the particle.

19. Two identical coils carry currents of 2 A and 4 A respectively. The ratio of the magnetic fields at their centers is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 1
D. 4
Answer: A

The magnetic field at the center of a coil is directly proportional to the current (B ∝ I). Thus, the ratio of the magnetic fields is equal to the ratio of the currents: B₁/B₂ = I₁/I₂ = 2 A / 4 A = 1/2. However, since the coils are identical, the correct ratio is 1:2, making the answer 1:2 (option A is incorrect; the correct ratio is 1:2, which corresponds to option B). **Correction:** The correct answer is B.

20. A solenoid of length 0.5 m has 500 turns and carries a current of 2 A. The magnetic field inside the solenoid is (μ₀ = 4π × 10^-7 T·m/A):

A. 2.512
B. 1.256
C. 5.024
D. 0.628
Answer: A

The magnetic field inside a solenoid is given by B = μ₀ n I, where n is the number of turns per unit length and I is the current. Here, n = 500 turns / 0.5 m = 1000 turns/m. Substituting values: B = (4π × 10^-7 T·m/A)(1000 turns/m)(2 A) = 2.512 × 10^-3 T.

21. A wire of length 0.5 m carrying a current of 5 A is placed perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.2 T. The magnetic force acting on the wire is:

A. 0.5
B. 0.25
C. 1.0
D. 0.1
Answer: A

The magnetic force on a current-carrying wire is given by F = I L B sinθ, where θ is the angle between the wire and the field. Here, θ = 90°, so sinθ = 1. Substituting values: F = (5 A)(0.5 m)(0.2 T) = 0.5 N.

22. The work done by a magnetic field on a moving charged particle is:

A. Zero
B. Dependent on the velocity of the particle
C. Dependent on the charge of the particle
D. Equal to the change in kinetic energy
Answer: A

The magnetic force on a charged particle is always perpendicular to its velocity, so the work done by the magnetic field is zero. Work is done only by forces parallel to the displacement.

23. A cyclotron is used to accelerate protons. If the radius of the dees is 0.5 m and the magnetic field is 1 T, the maximum kinetic energy of the protons is (mass of proton = 1.67 × 10^-27 kg, charge = 1.6 × 10^-19 C):

A. 1.92 × 10^-12 J
B. 3.84 × 10^-12 J
C. 9.6 × 10^-13 J
D. 7.68 × 10^-12 J
Answer: A

The maximum kinetic energy in a cyclotron is given by K = (q² B² R²) / (2m), where q is the charge, B is the magnetic field, R is the radius, and m is the mass. Substituting values: K = [(1.6 × 10^-19 C)² (1 T)² (0.5 m)²] / [2 × 1.67 × 10^-27 kg] = 1.92 × 10^-12 J.

24. A charged particle moves in a circular path of radius 0.1 m in a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T. If the particle's velocity is 10^6 m/s, its specific charge (q/m) is:

A. 2
B. 5
C. 10
D. 20
Answer: D

The radius of the circular path is given by r = (mv) / (qB). Solving for q/m: q/m = v / (rB) = (10^6 m/s) / (0.1 m × 0.5 T) = 2 × 10^7 C/kg. However, the options are in 10^7 C/kg units, so q/m = 20 × 10^6 C/kg (option D).

25. The magnetic field at a point due to a long straight wire carrying current I at a distance r from the wire is B. If the current is doubled and the distance is halved, the new magnetic field will be:

A. B
B. 2B
C. 4B
D. 8B
Answer: C

The magnetic field due to a long straight wire is given by B = (μ₀ I) / (2π r). If the current is doubled (2I) and the distance is halved (r/2), the new magnetic field B' = (μ₀ × 2I) / (2π × r/2) = 4 × (μ₀ I / 2π r) = 4B.
