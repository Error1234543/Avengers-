This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Electromagnetic Induction, styled after NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks with 1 mark deducted for incorrect answers. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. A circular loop of radius 0.1 m is placed in a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T perpendicular to the plane of the loop. If the magnetic field is reduced to zero in 0.2 s, the magnitude of the induced emf in the loop is:

A. 0.0785 V
B. 0.0393 V
C. 0.157 V
D. 0.0196 V
Answer: A

The induced emf (ε) in a loop is given by Faraday's law: ε = -dΦ/dt, where Φ = BA. Here, B changes from 0.5 T to 0 T in 0.2 s, so dΦ/dt = (0 - 0.5 × π × (0.1)²) / 0.2 = -0.0785 V. The magnitude of induced emf is 0.0785 V.

2. The self-inductance of a solenoid of length 50 cm, area of cross-section 20 cm², and 1000 turns is:

A. 5.03 × 10⁻⁴ H
B. 2.51 × 10⁻⁴ H
C. 1.26 × 10⁻³ H
D. 7.54 × 10⁻⁴ H
Answer: A

Self-inductance (L) of a solenoid is given by L = μ₀N²A/l, where μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m, N = 1000 turns, A = 20 × 10⁻⁴ m², and l = 0.5 m. Substituting values: L = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 1000² × 20 × 10⁻⁴) / 0.5 = 5.03 × 10⁻⁴ H.

3. A conducting rod of length 1 m moves with a velocity of 2 m/s perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T. The emf induced between the ends of the rod is:

A. 0.5 V
B. 1 V
C. 2 V
D. 0.25 V
Answer: B

The induced emf (ε) in a rod moving perpendicular to a magnetic field is given by ε = Blv, where B = 0.5 T, l = 1 m, and v = 2 m/s. Thus, ε = 0.5 × 1 × 2 = 1 V.

4. Two coils have a mutual inductance of 0.5 H. If the current in one coil changes from 2 A to 6 A in 0.2 s, the magnitude of the induced emf in the second coil is:

A. 5 V
B. 10 V
C. 15 V
D. 20 V
Answer: B

The induced emf (ε) in the second coil is given by ε = -M(dI/dt), where M = 0.5 H and dI/dt = (6 - 2)/0.2 = 20 A/s. Thus, ε = 0.5 × 20 = 10 V (magnitude).

5. A coil of resistance 10 Ω and inductance 0.5 H is connected to a 100 V DC supply. The time constant of the circuit is:

A. 0.05 s
B. 0.5 s
C. 5 s
D. 0.1 s
Answer: A

The time constant (τ) of an RL circuit is given by τ = L/R, where L = 0.5 H and R = 10 Ω. Thus, τ = 0.5 / 10 = 0.05 s.

6. The magnetic flux through a coil changes from 0.1 Wb to 0.3 Wb in 0.05 s. The magnitude of the induced emf is:

A. 2 V
B. 4 V
C. 6 V
D. 8 V
Answer: B

The induced emf (ε) is given by ε = -dΦ/dt. Here, dΦ = 0.3 - 0.1 = 0.2 Wb and dt = 0.05 s. Thus, ε = 0.2 / 0.05 = 4 V (magnitude).

7. A transformer has 500 turns in the primary and 100 turns in the secondary. If the primary voltage is 220 V, the secondary voltage is:

A. 44 V
B. 110 V
C. 22 V
D. 55 V
Answer: A

For a transformer, Vₛ/Vₚ = Nₛ/Nₚ, where Vₚ = 220 V, Nₚ = 500, and Nₛ = 100. Thus, Vₛ = (100/500) × 220 = 44 V.

8. The direction of induced current in a coil is given by:

A. Lenz's law
B. Faraday's law
C. Ampere's law
D. Ohm's law
Answer: A

Lenz's law states that the direction of induced current in a coil is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it.

9. A metal ring is placed in a magnetic field that is increasing with time. The induced current in the ring will:

A. Produce a magnetic field opposing the increase
B. Produce a magnetic field aiding the increase
C. Not produce any magnetic field
D. Flow in the direction of the magnetic field
Answer: A

According to Lenz's law, the induced current in the ring will produce a magnetic field that opposes the increasing external magnetic field.

10. The energy stored in an inductor of inductance 0.2 H carrying a current of 5 A is:

A. 2.5 J
B. 5 J
C. 1 J
D. 0.5 J
Answer: A

The energy (U) stored in an inductor is given by U = 0.5LI², where L = 0.2 H and I = 5 A. Thus, U = 0.5 × 0.2 × 5² = 2.5 J.

11. A rectangular loop of area 0.5 m² is placed in a magnetic field of 0.2 T perpendicular to the plane of the loop. If the loop is rotated by 180° in 0.1 s, the magnitude of the induced emf is:

A. 1 V
B. 2 V
C. 4 V
D. 0.5 V
Answer: B

The change in magnetic flux (ΔΦ) when the loop is rotated by 180° is ΔΦ = 2BA = 2 × 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.2 Wb. The induced emf (ε) = ΔΦ/Δt = 0.2 / 0.1 = 2 V.

12. The mutual inductance between two coils is 0.1 H. If the current in one coil changes at the rate of 5 A/s, the induced emf in the second coil is:

A. 0.5 V
B. 1 V
C. 2 V
D. 0.2 V
Answer: A

The induced emf (ε) in the second coil is given by ε = -M(dI/dt), where M = 0.1 H and dI/dt = 5 A/s. Thus, ε = 0.1 × 5 = 0.5 V (magnitude).

13. Two identical coils A and B are placed coaxially. Coil A carries a current that increases uniformly from 0 to 5 A in 0.1 s. If the mutual inductance between the coils is 0.2 H, the magnitude of the induced emf in coil B is:

A. 1 V
B. 5 V
C. 10 V
D. 20 V
Answer: C

The induced emf in coil B due to the changing current in coil A is given by ε = -M * (dI/dt), where M is the mutual inductance. Here, dI/dt = (5 - 0)/0.1 = 50 A/s. Thus, ε = 0.2 * 50 = 10 V.

14. The self-inductance of a solenoid is 0.5 H. If the current through it changes from 2 A to 4 A in 0.1 s, the magnitude of the induced emf in the solenoid is:

A. 5 V
B. 10 V
C. 15 V
D. 20 V
Answer: B

The induced emf in a solenoid due to a changing current is given by ε = -L * (dI/dt), where L is the self-inductance. Here, dI/dt = (4 - 2)/0.1 = 20 A/s. Thus, ε = 0.5 * 20 = 10 V.

15. In an AC generator, the maximum value of the induced emf is 220 V. The rms value of the induced emf is:

A. 110 V
B. 155.6 V
C. 220 V
D. 311 V
Answer: B

The rms value of an AC emf is given by Erms = E₀/√2, where E₀ is the maximum value. Here, Erms = 220/√2 ≈ 155.6 V.

16. A loop of area 0.2 m² is placed in a magnetic field of 0.5 T, with its plane perpendicular to the field. If the loop is rotated by 180° in 0.1 s, the magnitude of the induced emf is:

A. 1 V
B. 2 V
C. 4 V
D. 8 V
Answer: B

When the loop is rotated by 180°, the change in magnetic flux is ΔΦ = 2BA (since the flux reverses direction). Here, ΔΦ = 2 * 0.5 * 0.2 = 0.2 Wb. The induced emf is ε = ΔΦ/Δt = 0.2/0.1 = 2 V.

17. A metal rod of length 1 m is moved with a velocity of 5 m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.2 T. The potential difference between the ends of the rod is:

A. 0.5 V
B. 1 V
C. 1.5 V
D. 2 V
Answer: B

The potential difference (emf) induced in the rod is given by ε = Blv, where B is the magnetic field, l is the length of the rod, and v is the velocity. Here, ε = 0.2 * 1 * 5 = 1 V.

18. In an LR circuit, the current grows to 63.2% of its maximum value in one time constant. If the resistance is 10 Ω and the inductance is 0.5 H, the time constant is:

A. 0.02 s
B. 0.05 s
C. 0.1 s
D. 0.5 s
Answer: B

The time constant (τ) of an LR circuit is given by τ = L/R. Here, τ = 0.5/10 = 0.05 s. This is the time in which the current reaches 63.2% of its maximum value.

19. A conducting rod of length 0.5 m moves with a velocity of 4 m/s perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.2 T. If the rod is part of a circuit with a total resistance of 2 Ω, what is the power dissipated in the circuit due to the induced current?

A. 0.08 W
B. 0.16 W
C. 0.32 W
D. 0.64 W
Answer: C

The induced emf (ε) in the rod is given by ε = B * l * v, where B is the magnetic field, l is the length of the rod, and v is the velocity. Substituting the values: ε = 0.2 T * 0.5 m * 4 m/s = 0.4 V. The induced current (I) is ε / R = 0.4 V / 2 Ω = 0.2 A. Power dissipated (P) is I² * R = (0.2 A)² * 2 Ω = 0.08 W.
