This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Laws of Motion, styled after NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks with 1 mark deducted for an incorrect answer. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. A block of mass 2 kg is placed on a rough horizontal surface with a coefficient of static friction μₛ = 0.4. What is the minimum horizontal force required to just start the block moving? (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 4 N
B. 8 N
C. 12 N
D. 16 N
Answer: B

The minimum force required to overcome static friction is given by fₛ = μₛ × N, where N is the normal force. For a horizontal surface, N = mg. Thus, fₛ = 0.4 × 2 kg × 10 m/s² = 8 N.

2. A force F acts on a body of mass m, producing an acceleration a. If the same force acts on a body of mass 2m, the acceleration produced will be:

A. a/2
B. a
C. 2a
D. 4a
Answer: A

From Newton's second law, F = ma. If mass becomes 2m, the new acceleration a' = F/(2m) = a/2.

3. Two blocks of masses 3 kg and 5 kg are connected by a light inextensible string and pulled by a force of 20 N on a frictionless surface. The tension in the string connecting the two blocks is:

A. 5 N
B. 7.5 N
C. 10 N
D. 12.5 N
Answer: B

Total mass = 3 kg + 5 kg = 8 kg. Acceleration a = F/m = 20 N / 8 kg = 2.5 m/s². Tension in the string connecting the 3 kg block is T = m₁ × a = 3 kg × 2.5 m/s² = 7.5 N.

4. A lift is moving downward with an acceleration of 2 m/s². The apparent weight of a person of mass 60 kg inside the lift is: (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 480 N
B. 600 N
C. 720 N
D. 840 N
Answer: A

Apparent weight = m(g - a) = 60 kg × (10 m/s² - 2 m/s²) = 60 kg × 8 m/s² = 480 N.

5. A car of mass 1000 kg is moving with a velocity of 15 m/s. If the brakes are applied to bring it to rest in 5 seconds, the average force exerted by the brakes is:

A. 2000 N
B. 3000 N
C. 4000 N
D. 5000 N
Answer: B

Using F = m × (Δv/Δt), Δv = 0 - 15 m/s = -15 m/s, Δt = 5 s. Thus, F = 1000 kg × (-15 m/s / 5 s) = -3000 N. The magnitude of the force is 3000 N.

6. Which of the following statements is correct regarding Newton's third law of motion?

A. Action and reaction forces act on the same body.
B. Action and reaction forces are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction and act on different bodies.
C. Action and reaction forces cancel each other out.
D. Action and reaction forces are always perpendicular to each other.
Answer: B

Newton's third law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction, and these forces act on different bodies.

7. A body of mass 5 kg is subjected to two perpendicular forces of 3 N and 4 N. The magnitude of the acceleration produced is:

A. 0.2 m/s²
B. 1 m/s²
C. 1.4 m/s²
D. 2 m/s²
Answer: B

Resultant force F = √(3² + 4²) = 5 N. Acceleration a = F/m = 5 N / 5 kg = 1 m/s².

8. A block slides down an inclined plane of angle 30° with an acceleration of 2 m/s². The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the plane is: (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 0.1
B. 0.2
C. 0.3
D. 0.4
Answer: C

Net acceleration down the plane: a = g sinθ - μₖ g cosθ. Thus, 2 = 10 × sin30° - μₖ × 10 × cos30°. Solving, 2 = 5 - μₖ × 8.66 → μₖ = (5 - 2)/8.66 ≈ 0.3.

9. A rocket of mass 1000 kg ejects mass at a rate of 10 kg/s with a velocity of 2000 m/s relative to the rocket. The thrust force on the rocket is:

A. 10,000 N
B. 20,000 N
C. 30,000 N
D. 40,000 N
Answer: B

Thrust force F = v × (dm/dt), where v is the exhaust velocity and dm/dt is the mass ejection rate. Thus, F = 2000 m/s × 10 kg/s = 20,000 N.

10. A block of mass 1 kg is placed on a horizontal surface. A horizontal force of 5 N is applied, but the block does not move. The frictional force acting on the block is:

A. 0 N
B. 2.5 N
C. 5 N
D. 10 N
Answer: C

Since the block does not move, the frictional force exactly balances the applied force. Thus, frictional force = 5 N.

11. A body of mass 2 kg is moving with a velocity of 4 m/s. If a constant force of 8 N acts on it for 3 seconds, the final velocity of the body is:

A. 10 m/s
B. 12 m/s
C. 16 m/s
D. 20 m/s
Answer: C

Acceleration a = F/m = 8 N / 2 kg = 4 m/s². Change in velocity Δv = a × t = 4 m/s² × 3 s = 12 m/s. Final velocity = initial velocity + Δv = 4 m/s + 12 m/s = 16 m/s.

12. The impulse of a force is equal to the change in:

A. Kinetic energy of the body
B. Momentum of the body
C. Potential energy of the body
D. Acceleration of the body
Answer: B

Impulse is defined as the change in momentum of a body, given by J = Δp = F × Δt.

13. A car of mass 1000 kg is moving with a velocity of 15 m/s. If the brakes are applied to bring it to rest in 5 seconds, what is the average braking force acting on the car?

A. 2000 N
B. 3000 N
C. 4000 N
D. 5000 N
Answer: B

Using Newton's second law, F = ma. The acceleration (deceleration) is a = (v - u)/t = (0 - 15 m/s)/5 s = -3 m/s². Thus, F = 1000 kg * 3 m/s² = 3000 N (magnitude).

14. Two blocks of masses 3 kg and 5 kg are connected by a light string and placed on a frictionless horizontal surface. If a force of 16 N is applied to the 5 kg block, what is the tension in the string?

A. 4 N
B. 6 N
C. 8 N
D. 10 N
Answer: B

The acceleration of the system is a = F/(m₁ + m₂) = 16 N / (3 kg + 5 kg) = 2 m/s². The tension in the string is the force required to accelerate the 3 kg block: T = m₁ * a = 3 kg * 2 m/s² = 6 N.

15. A lift is moving upward with an acceleration of 2 m/s². If a person of mass 60 kg is standing inside the lift, what is the apparent weight of the person? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 480 N
B. 600 N
C. 720 N
D. 840 N
Answer: C

The apparent weight is given by N = m(g + a) = 60 kg * (10 m/s² + 2 m/s²) = 720 N.

16. A particle of mass 2 kg is moving with a velocity of 5 m/s. If a constant force of 4 N acts on it for 3 seconds, what is the final velocity of the particle?

A. 8 m/s
B. 11 m/s
C. 14 m/s
D. 17 m/s
Answer: B

Using F = ma, the acceleration is a = F/m = 4 N / 2 kg = 2 m/s². The change in velocity is Δv = a * t = 2 m/s² * 3 s = 6 m/s. Thus, the final velocity is v = u + Δv = 5 m/s + 6 m/s = 11 m/s.

17. A body of mass 10 kg is moving with a velocity of 10 m/s. If a constant retarding force of 5 N acts on it, how long will it take to come to rest?

A. 10 s
B. 20 s
C. 30 s
D. 40 s
Answer: B

Using F = ma, the deceleration is a = F/m = 5 N / 10 kg = 0.5 m/s². The time to come to rest is t = (v - u)/a = (0 - 10 m/s)/(-0.5 m/s²) = 20 s.

18. Two masses, 3 kg and 7 kg, are connected by a light string passing over a frictionless pulley. What is the acceleration of the system?

A. 2 m/s²
B. 3 m/s²
C. 4 m/s²
D. 5 m/s²
Answer: C

The net force acting on the system is F = (m₂ - m₁)g = (7 kg - 3 kg) * 10 m/s² = 40 N. The total mass is m₁ + m₂ = 10 kg. Thus, the acceleration is a = F/(m₁ + m₂) = 40 N / 10 kg = 4 m/s².

19. A block of mass 5 kg is placed on a rough horizontal surface. If a horizontal force of 30 N is applied and the block moves with an acceleration of 2 m/s², what is the coefficient of kinetic friction?

A. 0.2
B. 0.4
C. 0.6
D. 0.8
Answer: A

The net force is F - f = ma, where f = μₖ * N = μₖ * mg. Thus, 30 N - μₖ * 5 kg * 10 m/s² = 5 kg * 2 m/s². Solving for μₖ: μₖ = (30 - 10)/50 = 0.4.

20. A block of mass 2 kg is placed on a rough horizontal surface with a coefficient of static friction μₛ = 0.4. A horizontal force F is applied to the block. If the block just begins to slide when F = 8 N, what is the maximum static frictional force acting on the block?

A. 2 N
B. 4 N
C. 8 N
D. 16 N
Answer: C

The maximum static frictional force (fₛₘₐₓ) is the force at which the block just begins to slide. It is given by fₛₘₐₓ = μₛ × N, where N is the normal force. For a block on a horizontal surface, N = mg. Here, m = 2 kg and g = 10 m/s², so N = 20 N. Thus, fₛₘₐₓ = 0.4 × 20 = 8 N. The applied force F at the point of sliding equals the maximum static friction, confirming the answer as 8 N.
