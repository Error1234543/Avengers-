1. The half-life of a radioactive isotope is 3 hours. If the initial mass of the isotope is 300 g, what mass remains undecayed after 9 hours?

A. 37.5 g
B. 75 g
C. 150 g
D. 225 g
Answer: A

The half-life of the isotope is 3 hours. After 9 hours, 3 half-lives have passed (9/3 = 3). The remaining mass is calculated as: Initial mass × (1/2)^n, where n is the number of half-lives. Thus, 300 g × (1/2)^3 = 300 g × 1/8 = 37.5 g.

2. Which of the following statements correctly describes the binding energy per nucleon curve?

A. It peaks at iron (Fe) and decreases for both lighter and heavier nuclei.
B. It increases continuously with the mass number of the nucleus.
C. It is constant for all nuclei regardless of their mass number.
D. It decreases continuously with the mass number of the nucleus.
Answer: A

The binding energy per nucleon curve peaks around iron (Fe) with a mass number of approximately 56. It decreases for both lighter nuclei (e.g., hydrogen) and heavier nuclei (e.g., uranium), indicating that iron has the most stable nucleus.

3. In a nuclear reaction, the mass defect is 0.02 amu. What is the energy released in MeV? (1 amu = 931.5 MeV)

A. 18.63 MeV
B. 9.315 MeV
C. 4.6575 MeV
D. 0.02 MeV
Answer: A

Energy released (E) is calculated using Einstein's equation: E = Δm × c², where Δm is the mass defect. Here, Δm = 0.02 amu. Since 1 amu = 931.5 MeV, E = 0.02 × 931.5 = 18.63 MeV.

4. The radius of a nucleus is proportional to which of the following?

A. A^(1/3), where A is the mass number
B. A^(2/3), where A is the mass number
C. A, where A is the mass number
D. A^2, where A is the mass number
Answer: A

The radius (R) of a nucleus is given by R = R₀ × A^(1/3), where R₀ is a constant (~1.2 fm) and A is the mass number. This shows that the radius is proportional to the cube root of the mass number.

5. Which of the following particles is emitted in beta-minus (β⁻) decay?

A. Electron and antineutrino
B. Positron and neutrino
C. Proton and electron
D. Alpha particle
Answer: A

In beta-minus (β⁻) decay, a neutron is converted into a proton, and an electron (β⁻ particle) along with an antineutrino is emitted from the nucleus.

6. The decay constant (λ) of a radioactive substance is 0.0693 per day. What is its half-life?

A. 10 days
B. 5 days
C. 20 days
D. 15 days
Answer: A

The half-life (T₁/₂) is related to the decay constant (λ) by the formula: T₁/₂ = ln(2)/λ. Here, λ = 0.0693 per day, so T₁/₂ = 0.693/0.0693 ≈ 10 days.

7. Which of the following nuclei is most likely to undergo nuclear fission?

A. Uranium-235
B. Carbon-12
C. Helium-4
D. Iron-56
Answer: A

Uranium-235 is a heavy nucleus that is fissile, meaning it can undergo nuclear fission when bombarded with slow neutrons. Lighter nuclei like Carbon-12, Helium-4, and Iron-56 are not fissile under normal conditions.

8. In a nuclear reactor, what is the primary function of the moderator?

A. To slow down fast neutrons to thermal energies
B. To absorb excess neutrons
C. To increase the speed of neutrons
D. To cool the reactor core
Answer: A

The moderator in a nuclear reactor is used to slow down fast neutrons to thermal energies, which are more likely to cause fission in fissile nuclei like Uranium-235. Common moderators include water, heavy water, and graphite.

9. The Q-value of a nuclear reaction is defined as:

A. The energy released or absorbed during the reaction
B. The mass defect of the reaction
C. The binding energy of the products
D. The kinetic energy of the reactants
Answer: A

The Q-value of a nuclear reaction represents the energy released or absorbed during the reaction. It is calculated as the difference in mass between the reactants and products, converted to energy using E = Δm × c².

10. Which of the following is a characteristic of gamma (γ) decay?

A. It involves the emission of high-energy photons from an excited nucleus
B. It changes the atomic number of the nucleus
C. It results in the emission of an alpha particle
D. It converts a proton into a neutron
Answer: A

Gamma (γ) decay involves the emission of high-energy photons (gamma rays) from an excited nucleus. It does not change the atomic number or mass number of the nucleus but helps it reach a lower energy state.

11. If the activity of a radioactive sample decreases from 800 Bq to 100 Bq in 12 hours, what is its half-life?

A. 4 hours
B. 6 hours
C. 3 hours
D. 8 hours
Answer: A

The activity decreases from 800 Bq to 100 Bq, which is a reduction by a factor of 8 (800/100 = 8). This corresponds to 3 half-lives (since 2^3 = 8). Thus, the half-life is 12 hours / 3 = 4 hours.

12. Which of the following statements about nuclear fusion is correct?

A. It is the process of combining lighter nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy
B. It involves the splitting of a heavy nucleus into lighter nuclei
C. It occurs only at very low temperatures
D. It is the primary process in nuclear reactors
Answer: A

Nuclear fusion is the process where lighter nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus, releasing a significant amount of energy. This process occurs at extremely high temperatures, such as those found in the Sun, and is not the primary process in current nuclear reactors (which use fission).

13. In a nuclear reaction, the Q-value is defined as:

A. The energy absorbed during the reaction.
B. The difference in mass between reactants and products converted into energy.
C. The kinetic energy of the incident particle.
D. The binding energy of the daughter nucleus.
Answer: B

The Q-value of a nuclear reaction is the difference in mass-energy between the reactants and products, calculated using E = Δm × c², where Δm is the mass defect.

14. The radius of a nucleus is proportional to:

A. A^(1/3), where A is the mass number
B. A^(2/3), where A is the mass number
C. A, where A is the mass number
D. Z, where Z is the atomic number
Answer: A

The radius of a nucleus is given by R = R₀ × A^(1/3), where R₀ is a constant (~1.2 fm) and A is the mass number.

15. The decay constant (λ) of a radioactive substance is related to its half-life (T₁/₂) by the equation:

A. λ = ln(2) / T₁/₂
B. λ = T₁/₂ / ln(2)
C. λ = 1 / T₁/₂
D. λ = (T₁/₂)²
Answer: A

The decay constant (λ) and half-life (T₁/₂) are related by the equation λ = ln(2) / T₁/₂, where ln(2) is the natural logarithm of 2.

16. In a nuclear fission reaction, the energy released per fission of U-235 is approximately:

A. 2 MeV
B. 20 MeV
C. 200 MeV
D. 2000 MeV
Answer: C

The energy released per fission of U-235 is approximately 200 MeV, which includes the kinetic energy of fission fragments, neutrons, and gamma rays.

17. The mass defect of a nucleus is defined as:

A. The difference between the mass of the nucleus and the sum of the masses of its protons.
B. The difference between the mass of the nucleus and the sum of the masses of its neutrons.
C. The difference between the mass of the nucleus and the sum of the masses of its protons and neutrons.
D. The difference between the mass of the nucleus and the mass of its electrons.
Answer: C

The mass defect is the difference between the mass of a nucleus and the sum of the masses of its individual protons and neutrons, which is converted into binding energy.

18. Which of the following is NOT a property of nuclear forces?

A. They are charge-independent.
B. They are short-range forces.
C. They are stronger than electrostatic forces.
D. They obey the inverse-square law.
Answer: D

Nuclear forces are short-range and do not obey the inverse-square law, unlike electrostatic forces. They are charge-independent and stronger than electrostatic forces within their range.

19. In a nuclear reactor, the function of the moderator is to:

A. Absorb neutrons to control the reaction.
B. Slow down fast neutrons to thermal energies.
C. Increase the number of neutrons in the reaction.
D. Convert neutrons into protons.
Answer: B

The moderator in a nuclear reactor slows down fast neutrons to thermal energies, making them more likely to cause further fission in U-235.

20. The binding energy per nucleon is maximum for nuclei with mass number around:

A. 10
B. 56
C. 100
D. 238
Answer: B

The binding energy per nucleon is maximum for nuclei with mass number around 56 (e.g., iron-56), indicating the highest stability for these nuclei.

21. A radioactive nucleus undergoes a series of decays: α, β⁻, β⁻, α. If the final nucleus has a mass number of 216 and an atomic number of 84, what were the mass number and atomic number of the original nucleus?

A. Mass number: 224, Atomic number: 88
B. Mass number: 220, Atomic number: 86
C. Mass number: 228, Atomic number: 90
D. Mass number: 226, Atomic number: 88
Answer: A

Let the original nucleus have mass number A and atomic number Z. Each α-decay reduces the mass number by 4 and the atomic number by 2. Each β⁻-decay increases the atomic number by 1 without changing the mass number. The decay series is: α (A-4, Z-2) → β⁻ (A-4, Z-1) → β⁻ (A-4, Z) → α (A-8, Z-2). The final nucleus has A-8 = 216 and Z-2 = 84. Solving, A = 224 and Z = 88. Thus, the original nucleus was Radium-224 (²²⁴₈₈Ra).

22. A radioactive nucleus undergoes a series of decays: first an alpha decay, followed by two beta-minus decays. If the original nucleus is represented as \(_{Z}^{A}X\), which of the following correctly represents the final nucleus?

A. \(_{Z-2}^{A-4}Y\)
B. \(_{Z}^{A-4}Y\)
C. \(_{Z+1}^{A-4}Y\)
D. \(_{Z}^{A}Y\)
Answer: B

In an alpha decay, the nucleus loses 2 protons and 4 nucleons, resulting in \(_{Z-2}^{A-4}X'\). Each beta-minus decay converts a neutron into a proton, increasing the atomic number by 1 while leaving the mass number unchanged. After two beta-minus decays, the atomic number becomes \(Z-2 + 2 = Z\), and the mass number remains \(A-4\). Thus, the final nucleus is \(_{Z}^{A-4}Y\).
