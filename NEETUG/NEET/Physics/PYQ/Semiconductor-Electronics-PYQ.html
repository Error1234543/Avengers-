This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) based on the NEET pattern for Semiconductor Electronics. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer from the given options.

1. In an intrinsic semiconductor, the number of free electrons (nₑ) and holes (nₕ) at thermal equilibrium is related by:

A. nₑ = nₕ
B. nₑ > nₕ
C. nₑ < nₕ
D. nₑ + nₕ = 0
Answer: A

In an intrinsic semiconductor, thermal excitation generates electron-hole pairs. At thermal equilibrium, the number of free electrons (nₑ) is equal to the number of holes (nₕ) because each electron that is excited to the conduction band leaves behind a hole in the valence band. Thus, nₑ = nₕ.

2. Which of the following statements is true for a p-type semiconductor?

A. It has a majority of free electrons and a minority of holes.
B. It is formed by doping an intrinsic semiconductor with a pentavalent impurity.
C. It has a majority of holes and a minority of free electrons.
D. Its conductivity decreases with an increase in temperature.
Answer: C

A p-type semiconductor is created by doping an intrinsic semiconductor (e.g., silicon) with a trivalent impurity (e.g., boron). This introduces holes as the majority charge carriers and free electrons as the minority charge carriers. Its conductivity increases with temperature due to the generation of more charge carriers.

3. The forbidden energy gap (E_g) for silicon at room temperature is approximately:

A. 0.7 eV
B. 1.1 eV
C. 1.4 eV
D. 2.0 eV
Answer: B

The forbidden energy gap (E_g) for silicon at room temperature is approximately 1.1 electron volts (eV). This is the energy required to excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band in silicon.

4. In a p-n junction diode under reverse bias, the depletion region:

A. Narrows and allows current to flow easily.
B. Widens and allows current to flow easily.
C. Narrows and prevents current flow.
D. Widens and prevents current flow.
Answer: D

Under reverse bias, the depletion region of a p-n junction diode widens because the applied voltage increases the potential barrier, preventing the majority charge carriers from crossing the junction. This results in negligible current flow (only a small reverse saturation current exists).

5. The dynamic resistance (r_d) of a diode in its forward-biased region is given by:

A. r_d = ΔV / ΔI
B. r_d = ΔI / ΔV
C. r_d = V / I
D. r_d = I / V
Answer: A

The dynamic resistance (r_d) of a diode in its forward-biased region is defined as the ratio of the change in voltage (ΔV) to the change in current (ΔI) across the diode. Mathematically, r_d = ΔV / ΔI.

6. In a common emitter amplifier, the phase difference between the input and output signals is:

A. 0°
B. 90°
C. 180°
D. 270°
Answer: C

In a common emitter amplifier, the output signal is inverted with respect to the input signal. This results in a phase difference of 180° between the input and output signals.

7. The current gain (β) of a transistor in common emitter configuration is defined as:

A. β = I_C / I_B
B. β = I_B / I_C
C. β = I_E / I_B
D. β = I_C / I_E
Answer: A

The current gain (β) of a transistor in common emitter configuration is defined as the ratio of the collector current (I_C) to the base current (I_B). Mathematically, β = I_C / I_B.

8. Which logic gate produces a high output only when all its inputs are high?

A. OR gate
B. AND gate
C. NOT gate
D. NAND gate
Answer: B

An AND gate produces a high output (logic 1) only when all its inputs are high (logic 1). If any input is low (logic 0), the output is low.

9. The truth table for a NAND gate is identical to that of:

A. An AND gate followed by a NOT gate.
B. An OR gate followed by a NOT gate.
C. A NOT gate followed by an AND gate.
D. A NOR gate followed by a NOT gate.
Answer: A

A NAND gate is equivalent to an AND gate followed by a NOT gate. The output of a NAND gate is the negation of the output of an AND gate.

10. In a full-wave rectifier using a center-tapped transformer, the peak inverse voltage (PIV) across each diode is:

A. Equal to the peak secondary voltage (V_m).
B. Twice the peak secondary voltage (2V_m).
C. Half the peak secondary voltage (V_m / 2).
D. Equal to the RMS secondary voltage.
Answer: B

In a full-wave rectifier using a center-tapped transformer, the peak inverse voltage (PIV) across each diode is twice the peak secondary voltage (2V_m). This is because the diode must withstand the maximum voltage when it is reverse-biased.

11. The output of a half-wave rectifier is:

A. A pure DC voltage with no ripple.
B. A pulsating DC voltage with ripple at the input frequency.
C. An AC voltage with reduced amplitude.
D. A DC voltage with ripple at twice the input frequency.
Answer: B

The output of a half-wave rectifier is a pulsating DC voltage with ripple at the same frequency as the input AC voltage. This is because only one half of the input AC cycle is allowed to pass through the diode.

12. In an n-type semiconductor, which of the following statements is correct regarding the majority and minority charge carriers?

A. Majority carriers are electrons, and minority carriers are holes.
B. Majority carriers are holes, and minority carriers are electrons.
C. Both majority and minority carriers are electrons.
D. Both majority and minority carriers are holes.
Answer: A

In an n-type semiconductor, doping with pentavalent impurities (e.g., phosphorus) introduces excess electrons, making electrons the majority charge carriers. Holes, which are the absence of electrons, become the minority charge carriers.

13. The forbidden energy gap in a semiconductor is 1.1 eV. What is the maximum wavelength of radiation that can excite an electron from the valence band to the conduction band?

A. 1127 nm
B. 1127 Å
C. 1127 μm
D. 1127 pm
Answer: A

The energy of a photon is given by E = hc/λ, where E is the energy gap (1.1 eV), h is Planck's constant, and c is the speed of light. Converting 1.1 eV to joules (1.1 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) and solving for λ gives λ = hc/E ≈ 1127 nm.

14. In a common emitter amplifier, the input signal is applied between which two terminals?

A. Base and emitter
B. Base and collector
C. Emitter and collector
D. Collector and ground
Answer: A

In a common emitter amplifier, the input signal is applied between the base and emitter terminals, while the output is taken between the collector and emitter. The emitter is common to both input and output circuits.

15. The current gain (β) of a transistor in common emitter mode is 100. If the base current is 50 μA, what is the collector current?

A. 5 mA
B. 50 mA
C. 0.5 mA
D. 500 mA
Answer: A

The current gain (β) is defined as the ratio of collector current (I_C) to base current (I_B). Given β = 100 and I_B = 50 μA, I_C = β × I_B = 100 × 50 μA = 5000 μA = 5 mA.

16. The depletion region in a p-n junction diode widens when the diode is:

A. Forward biased
B. Reverse biased
C. Unbiased
D. Connected to an AC source
Answer: B

In reverse bias, the applied voltage increases the potential barrier across the depletion region, causing it to widen. This reduces the flow of majority carriers and increases the width of the depletion region.

17. A full-wave rectifier uses how many diodes?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4
Answer: B

A full-wave rectifier typically uses two diodes to rectify both halves of the AC input signal. Each diode conducts during one half-cycle, resulting in a continuous DC output.

18. The output of an AND gate is connected to the input of a NOT gate. The combination is equivalent to which logic gate?

A. NAND gate
B. NOR gate
C. XOR gate
D. OR gate
Answer: A

An AND gate followed by a NOT gate is equivalent to a NAND gate. The NAND gate produces the inverse of the AND gate's output.

19. In an intrinsic semiconductor, the number of electrons in the conduction band is equal to the number of:

A. Holes in the valence band
B. Donor atoms
C. Acceptor atoms
D. Impurity atoms
Answer: A

In an intrinsic semiconductor, thermal excitation creates electron-hole pairs. The number of electrons in the conduction band is equal to the number of holes in the valence band, as each electron leaving the valence band creates a hole.

20. The voltage drop across a forward-biased silicon diode is approximately:

A. 0.2 V
B. 0.7 V
C. 1.1 V
D. 0.3 V
Answer: B

A forward-biased silicon diode typically has a voltage drop of approximately 0.7 V. This is due to the potential barrier that must be overcome for current to flow through the diode.

A. Both the base-emitter and base-collector junctions are reverse biased.
B. The base-emitter junction is forward biased, and the base-collector junction is reverse biased.
C. Both the base-emitter and base-collector junctions are forward biased.
D. The base-emitter junction is reverse biased, and the base-collector junction is forward biased.

22. In a p-n junction diode, the depletion region widens when:

A. the diode is forward-biased and the p-side is connected to the positive terminal of the battery.
B. the diode is reverse-biased and the p-side is connected to the negative terminal of the battery.
C. the diode is forward-biased and the n-side is connected to the positive terminal of the battery.
D. the diode is reverse-biased and the n-side is connected to the negative terminal of the battery.
Answer: B

The depletion region in a p-n junction diode widens under reverse bias conditions. In reverse bias, the p-side is connected to the negative terminal and the n-side to the positive terminal of the battery. This increases the potential barrier, causing the depletion region to expand as majority carriers are pulled away from the junction. Forward bias narrows the depletion region by reducing the potential barrier, allowing current to flow.
