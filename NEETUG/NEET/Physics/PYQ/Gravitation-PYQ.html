This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions modelled on previous NEET Physics papers. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. Two point masses m₁ and m₂ are placed a distance r apart. If the gravitational force between them is F, what will be the new force when the distance is halved and each mass is doubled?

A. 16 F
B. 8 F
C. 4 F
D. 2 F
Answer: A

Gravitational force F = G m₁ m₂ / r². New masses are 2 m₁ and 2 m₂, new distance is r/2. New force = G (2 m₁)(2 m₂) / (r/2)² = G · 4 m₁ m₂ / (r²/4) = 16 G m₁ m₂ / r² = 16 F.

2. The acceleration due to gravity on the surface of a planet is g. If the planet’s radius is doubled while keeping its mass constant, what will be the new acceleration due to gravity on its surface?

A. g/4
B. g/2
C. 2 g
D. 4 g
Answer: A

g = G M / R². New radius = 2 R. New g' = G M / (2 R)² = G M / (4 R²) = g / 4.

3. A satellite orbits Earth in a circular path of radius r with orbital speed v. If the satellite is moved to a new orbit of radius 4 r, what will be its new orbital speed?

A. v/4
B. v/2
C. 2 v
D. 4 v
Answer: B

Orbital speed v = √(G M / r). New radius = 4 r. New speed v' = √(G M / 4 r) = v / 2.

4. The gravitational potential energy of a body of mass m at a height h above Earth’s surface is U. If the body is raised to a height 2 h, what is the new gravitational potential energy? (Assume h is small compared to Earth’s radius.)

A. U/2
B. U
C. 2 U
D. 3 U
Answer: C

For small h, U = m g h. New height = 2 h, so new U' = m g (2 h) = 2 U.

5. Kepler’s third law states that the square of the orbital period T of a planet is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis a of its orbit. Mathematically, this is expressed as

A. T ∝ a
B. T² ∝ a³
C. T ∝ a²
D. T³ ∝ a²
Answer: B

Kepler’s third law is T² ∝ a³, where T is the orbital period and a is the semi-major axis.

6. A geostationary satellite orbits Earth at a height of approximately 36,000 km above the equator. If Earth’s radius is 6,400 km, what is the approximate orbital radius of the satellite?

A. 6,400 km
B. 12,800 km
C. 36,000 km
D. 42,400 km
Answer: D

Orbital radius = Earth’s radius + height = 6,400 km + 36,000 km = 42,400 km.

7. The escape velocity from the surface of a planet depends on which of the following?

A. Mass of the planet only
B. Radius of the planet only
C. Both mass and radius of the planet
D. Neither mass nor radius of the planet
Answer: C

Escape velocity vₑ = √(2 G M / R), so it depends on both the mass M and radius R of the planet.

8. If the mass of Earth were to suddenly become four times its current value while its radius remained unchanged, what would be the new acceleration due to gravity on its surface?

A. g/4
B. g/2
C. 2 g
D. 4 g
Answer: D

g = G M / R². New mass = 4 M. New g' = G (4 M) / R² = 4 g.

9. The gravitational field intensity at a point inside a spherical shell of uniform mass distribution is

A. Zero
B. Proportional to the distance from the centre
C. Inversely proportional to the distance from the centre
D. Constant but non-zero
Answer: A

By the shell theorem, the gravitational field inside a uniform spherical shell is zero.

10. A body is projected vertically upward from Earth’s surface with a velocity equal to half the escape velocity. What is the maximum height reached by the body? (Neglect air resistance and assume Earth’s radius is R.)

A. R/3
B. R/2
C. R
D. 2 R
Answer: A

Using energy conservation: (1/2) m v² - G M m / R = - G M m / (R + h). Given v = vₑ / 2 = √(2 G M / R) / 2, solving gives h = R / 3.

11. The gravitational potential at a point due to a point mass M is V. If the mass is doubled and the distance from the point is halved, what is the new gravitational potential?

A. V/4
B. V/2
C. 2 V
D. 4 V
Answer: D

Gravitational potential V = - G M / r. New mass = 2 M, new distance = r / 2. New V' = - G (2 M) / (r / 2) = - 4 G M / r = 4 V.

12. Which of the following statements about gravitational force is correct?

A. It is always attractive and depends on the medium between the masses.
B. It is always attractive and independent of the medium between the masses.
C. It can be attractive or repulsive depending on the masses.
D. It depends on the velocity of the masses.
Answer: B

Gravitational force is always attractive and does not depend on the medium between the masses.

13. A satellite is revolving around the Earth in a circular orbit of radius R. If the radius of the orbit is increased to 4R, the time period of the satellite will become:

A. 2 times the original period
B. 4 times the original period
C. 8 times the original period
D. 16 times the original period
Answer: C

The time period (T) of a satellite in a circular orbit is given by Kepler's Third Law: T² ∝ R³. If the radius increases from R to 4R, the new time period T' satisfies (T'/T)² = (4R/R)³ = 64. Thus, T' = 8T, meaning the time period becomes 8 times the original period.

14. The gravitational potential energy of a body of mass m at a height h above the Earth's surface is (R = radius of Earth, g = acceleration due to gravity at surface):

A. -mgR / (R + h)
B. -mgh
C. -mgR² / (R + h)
D. mg(R + h)
Answer: A

Gravitational potential energy (U) at a height h is given by U = -GMm / (R + h), where G is the gravitational constant and M is the mass of the Earth. Since g = GM/R², substituting gives U = -mgR / (R + h).

15. Two planets of masses M₁ and M₂ have radii R₁ and R₂, respectively. The ratio of their escape velocities is:

A. √(M₁R₁ / M₂R₂)
B. √(M₁R₂ / M₂R₁)
C. √(M₁ / M₂) × (R₂ / R₁)
D. √(2GM₁ / R₁) / √(2GM₂ / R₂)
Answer: B

Escape velocity (vₑ) is given by vₑ = √(2GM/R). Thus, the ratio of escape velocities is vₑ₁/vₑ₂ = √(M₁/R₁) / √(M₂/R₂) = √(M₁R₂ / M₂R₁).

16. A body is projected vertically upward from the Earth's surface with a velocity equal to half the escape velocity. The maximum height attained by the body is (R = radius of Earth):

A. R/3
B. R/2
C. R
D. 2R/3
Answer: A

Escape velocity vₑ = √(2gR). Initial velocity v = vₑ/2 = √(2gR)/2. Using energy conservation: (1/2)mv² - GMm/R = -GMm/(R + h). Substituting v and simplifying gives h = R/3.

17. The gravitational force between two masses is F when they are separated by a distance r. If the distance is reduced to r/2, the force becomes:

A. F/4
B. F/2
C. 2F
D. 4F
Answer: D

Gravitational force F ∝ 1/r². If the distance is halved (r/2), the new force F' ∝ 1/(r/2)² = 4/r². Thus, F' = 4F.

18. A geostationary satellite orbits the Earth with a time period of 24 hours. If the Earth's rotational period were to become 12 hours, the radius of the geostationary orbit would:

A. Decrease by a factor of 2^(1/3)
B. Increase by a factor of 2^(1/3)
C. Remain unchanged
D. Decrease by a factor of 2
Answer: A

For a geostationary orbit, T² ∝ R³. If the time period T is halved (12 hours), the new radius R' satisfies (T/2)² ∝ R'³ ⇒ R' = R × (1/2)^(2/3) = R / 2^(1/3). Thus, the radius decreases by a factor of 2^(1/3).

19. The acceleration due to gravity at a depth d below the Earth's surface is (g = surface gravity, R = radius of Earth):

A. g(1 - d/R)
B. g(1 + d/R)
C. g(R - d)/R
D. gR/(R - d)
Answer: A

At depth d, the effective mass of the Earth contributing to gravity is proportional to (R - d)³. Thus, g' = g(R - d)/R = g(1 - d/R).

20. A planet has a mass twice that of Earth and a radius three times that of Earth. The escape velocity from this planet compared to Earth's escape velocity is:

A. √(2/3) times
B. √(3/2) times
C. 2/3 times
D. 3/2 times
Answer: A

Escape velocity vₑ = √(2GM/R). For the planet, M' = 2M and R' = 3R. Thus, vₑ' = √(2G×2M / 3R) = √(4GM / 3R) = √(2/3) × √(2GM/R) = √(2/3) vₑ.

21. If the Earth were to shrink suddenly to half its present radius without any change in mass, the duration of the day would:

A. Increase
B. Decrease
C. Remain unchanged
D. Become zero
Answer: B

Angular momentum is conserved: Iω = constant. If the radius halves, the moment of inertia I (∝ R²) becomes I/4. Thus, ω must increase to 4ω, reducing the time period (duration of the day).

22. The gravitational potential at a point due to a uniform spherical shell of mass M and radius R is (r = distance from center, r > R):

A. -GM/r
B. -GM/R
C. -GM/(r - R)
D. -GM/r²
Answer: A

For a uniform spherical shell, the gravitational potential outside the shell (r > R) is the same as that of a point mass: V = -GM/r.

23. A satellite is moving in a circular orbit around the Earth. If its speed is doubled, it will:

A. Continue in the same orbit
B. Move to a higher orbit
C. Escape the Earth's gravitational field
D. Spiral inward toward the Earth
Answer: C

The orbital speed v is given by v = √(GM/R). If the speed is doubled, it exceeds the escape velocity (√2 × orbital speed), causing the satellite to escape the Earth's gravitational field.

24. The ratio of the kinetic energy to the potential energy of a satellite in a circular orbit around the Earth is:

A. 1:2
B. 2:1
C. 1:1
D. 1:-2
Answer: D

For a satellite in circular orbit, kinetic energy (K) = GMm/2R and potential energy (U) = -GMm/R. Thus, K/U = (GMm/2R) / (-GMm/R) = -1/2, or 1:-2.

25. A satellite is revolving around the Earth in a circular orbit of radius 4R, where R is the radius of the Earth. If the satellite is moved to a new circular orbit of radius 9R, the ratio of the new orbital velocity to the original orbital velocity is:

A. 2/3
B. 3/2
C. 4/9
D. 2/√3
Answer: A

The orbital velocity (v) of a satellite in a circular orbit is given by the formula: v = √(GM/r), where G is the gravitational constant, M is the mass of the Earth, and r is the radius of the orbit. For the original orbit (radius 4R), the velocity v₁ = √(GM/4R). For the new orbit (radius 9R), the velocity v₂ = √(GM/9R). The ratio v₂/v₁ = √[(GM/9R) / (GM/4R)] = √(4/9) = 2/3. Thus, the new orbital velocity is 2/3 times the original orbital velocity.
