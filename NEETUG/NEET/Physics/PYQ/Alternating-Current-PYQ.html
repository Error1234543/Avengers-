This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Alternating Current, styled after NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks with 1 mark deducted for incorrect answers. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. An alternating voltage is given by V = 200 sin(100πt). The RMS value of the voltage is

A. 100 V
B. 200 V
C. 100√2 V
D. 200√2 V
Answer: C

The RMS value of an alternating voltage V = V₀ sin(ωt) is given by V_rms = V₀ / √2. Here, V₀ = 200 V, so V_rms = 200 / √2 = 100√2 V.

2. In an AC circuit, the current lags behind the voltage by 90°. The circuit contains

A. only resistance
B. only inductance
C. only capacitance
D. a combination of resistance and capacitance
Answer: B

In a purely inductive circuit, the current lags behind the voltage by 90°. In a resistive circuit, current and voltage are in phase, while in a capacitive circuit, current leads the voltage by 90°.

3. The power factor of an AC circuit is 0.5. If the frequency of the AC supply is 50 Hz, the phase difference between current and voltage is

A. 30°
B. 45°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: C

Power factor (cos φ) = 0.5 implies φ = cos⁻¹(0.5) = 60°. This is the phase difference between current and voltage in the circuit.

4. An inductor of inductance 0.5 H is connected to an AC source of 220 V, 50 Hz. The inductive reactance is

A. 50 Ω
B. 100 Ω
C. 157 Ω
D. 220 Ω
Answer: C

Inductive reactance X_L = 2πfL. Here, f = 50 Hz and L = 0.5 H, so X_L = 2π × 50 × 0.5 = 157 Ω (approximated to 3 significant figures).

5. In an LCR series circuit, the resonance frequency is 1000 Hz. If the inductance is doubled and the capacitance is halved, the new resonance frequency will be

A. 500 Hz
B. 1000 Hz
C. 1414 Hz
D. 2000 Hz
Answer: C

Resonance frequency f₀ = 1 / (2π√(LC)). If L is doubled and C is halved, the new frequency f₀' = 1 / (2π√(2L × C/2)) = 1 / (2π√(LC)) = f₀. However, √(2L × C/2) = √(LC), so f₀' = f₀ / √(1) = 1000 Hz × √2 ≈ 1414 Hz.

6. A capacitor of capacitance 10 μF is connected to an AC source of 200 V, 50 Hz. The capacitive reactance is

A. 318 Ω
B. 400 Ω
C. 500 Ω
D. 628 Ω
Answer: A

Capacitive reactance X_C = 1 / (2πfC). Here, f = 50 Hz and C = 10 μF = 10 × 10⁻⁶ F, so X_C = 1 / (2π × 50 × 10 × 10⁻⁶) ≈ 318 Ω.

7. The quality factor (Q) of an LCR circuit is given by

A. R / (2πfL)
B. 2πfL / R
C. 1 / (2πfRC)
D. 2πfRC
Answer: B

The quality factor Q of an LCR circuit is defined as Q = 2πfL / R, where f is the resonance frequency, L is the inductance, and R is the resistance.

8. An AC circuit has a resistance of 30 Ω and an inductive reactance of 40 Ω. The impedance of the circuit is

A. 30 Ω
B. 40 Ω
C. 50 Ω
D. 70 Ω
Answer: C

Impedance Z of an RL circuit is given by Z = √(R² + X_L²). Here, R = 30 Ω and X_L = 40 Ω, so Z = √(30² + 40²) = √(900 + 1600) = √2500 = 50 Ω.

9. In an AC circuit, the power dissipated is given by P = V_rms I_rms cos φ. The term cos φ represents the

A. resistance of the circuit
B. reactance of the circuit
C. power factor of the circuit
D. impedance of the circuit
Answer: C

The term cos φ in the power formula P = V_rms I_rms cos φ represents the power factor of the circuit, which is the cosine of the phase angle between current and voltage.

10. A series LCR circuit is connected to an AC source. At resonance, the current in the circuit is maximum because

A. the resistance is minimum
B. the inductive reactance equals the capacitive reactance
C. the voltage across the inductor is maximum
D. the voltage across the capacitor is minimum
Answer: B

At resonance in an LCR circuit, the inductive reactance (X_L) equals the capacitive reactance (X_C), so they cancel each other out. This results in the impedance being purely resistive, leading to maximum current.

11. The average power dissipated in an AC circuit is zero if the circuit contains

A. only resistance
B. only inductance
C. only capacitance
D. either inductance or capacitance
Answer: D

In purely inductive or purely capacitive circuits, the phase difference between current and voltage is 90°, so the power factor cos φ = 0. Thus, the average power dissipated P = V_rms I_rms cos φ = 0.

12. An AC voltage is applied across a resistor. The power dissipated in the resistor is 100 W. If the same voltage is applied across a capacitor, the power dissipated will be

A. 0 W
B. 50 W
C. 100 W
D. 200 W
Answer: A

In a purely capacitive circuit, the power dissipated is zero because the phase difference between current and voltage is 90°, making the power factor cos φ = 0. Thus, P = V_rms I_rms cos φ = 0 W.

13. An AC voltage source of peak value 200 V is connected to a resistor of 50 Ω. The average power dissipated in the resistor over one complete cycle is

A. 200 W
B. 400 W
C. 800 W
D. 100 W
Answer: B

The average power P_avg in an AC circuit with a resistor is given by P_avg = (V_rms)^2 / R, where V_rms = V_peak / √2. Here, V_rms = 200 / √2 = 100√2 V. Thus, P_avg = (100√2)^2 / 50 = (20000) / 50 = 400 W.

14. In an LCR series circuit, the voltage across the inductor is 40 V, across the capacitor is 30 V, and across the resistor is 20 V. The supply voltage (rms) is

A. 10 V
B. 50 V
C. 22.36 V
D. 30 V
Answer: C

In an LCR series circuit, the supply voltage V is given by V = √(V_R^2 + (V_L - V_C)^2). Here, V_R = 20 V, V_L = 40 V, V_C = 30 V. Thus, V = √(20^2 + (40 - 30)^2) = √(400 + 100) = √500 = 22.36 V (rms).

15. A coil of inductance 0.5 H and resistance 50 Ω is connected to an AC source of 200 V, 50 Hz. The phase angle between the current and the voltage is

A. tan⁻¹(π)
B. tan⁻¹(2π)
C. tan⁻¹(1/π)
D. tan⁻¹(π/2)
Answer: A

The phase angle φ in an LR circuit is given by tan φ = X_L / R, where X_L = 2πfL. Here, X_L = 2π × 50 × 0.5 = 50π Ω, R = 50 Ω. Thus, tan φ = 50π / 50 = π, so φ = tan⁻¹(π).

16. The resonant frequency of an LCR series circuit is 1000 Hz. If the capacitance is doubled and the inductance is halved, the new resonant frequency will be

A. 500 Hz
B. 1000 Hz
C. 1414 Hz
D. 2000 Hz
Answer: B

Resonant frequency f₀ = 1 / (2π√(LC)). If capacitance C is doubled and inductance L is halved, the new frequency f₀' = 1 / (2π√((L/2)(2C))) = 1 / (2π√(LC)) = f₀. Thus, the frequency remains 1000 Hz.

17. The quality factor (Q) of an LCR series circuit at resonance is given by

A. Q = R√(L/C)
B. Q = (1/R)√(L/C)
C. Q = R√(C/L)
D. Q = (1/R)√(C/L)
Answer: B

The quality factor Q of an LCR series circuit at resonance is defined as Q = (1/R)√(L/C), where R is the resistance, L is the inductance, and C is the capacitance.

18. An AC voltage V = 100 sin(100πt) is applied to a pure inductor of 0.5 H. The current in the circuit is

A. 0.637 sin(100πt - π/2) A
B. 0.637 sin(100πt + π/2) A
C. 0.637 sin(100πt) A
D. 1.274 sin(100πt - π/2) A
Answer: A

For a pure inductor, V = V₀ sin(ωt), and I = (V₀ / X_L) sin(ωt - π/2), where X_L = ωL. Here, V₀ = 100 V, ω = 100π rad/s, L = 0.5 H. Thus, X_L = 100π × 0.5 = 50π Ω, and I₀ = 100 / 50π ≈ 0.637 A. The current lags the voltage by π/2, so I = 0.637 sin(100πt - π/2) A.

19. In an LCR series circuit, the power factor is 0.5. The phase difference between the current and the voltage is

A. 30°
B. 45°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: C

The power factor cos φ = 0.5 implies φ = cos⁻¹(0.5) = 60°. Thus, the phase difference between current and voltage is 60°.

20. A series LCR circuit is connected to an AC source of variable frequency. At resonance, the

A. current is minimum
B. impedance is maximum
C. voltage across the capacitor is maximum
D. current is maximum
Answer: D

At resonance in an LCR series circuit, the impedance is minimum (equal to R), and the current is maximum because the inductive and capacitive reactances cancel each other out.

21. The instantaneous value of an AC current is given by I = 5 sin(314t) A. The rms value of the current is

A. 5 A
B. 3.54 A
C. 7.07 A
D. 2.5 A
Answer: B

The rms value of an AC current is given by I_rms = I₀ / √2, where I₀ is the peak value. Here, I₀ = 5 A, so I_rms = 5 / √2 ≈ 3.54 A.

22. In an AC circuit, the voltage and current are given by V = 200 sin(ωt) and I = 10 sin(ωt - π/3). The average power consumed in the circuit is

A. 500 W
B. 1000 W
C. 500√3 W
D. 250 W
Answer: A

The average power P_avg in an AC circuit is given by P_avg = V_rms × I_rms × cos φ, where φ is the phase difference. Here, V_rms = 200 / √2, I_rms = 10 / √2, and φ = π/3. Thus, P_avg = (200 / √2) × (10 / √2) × cos(π/3) = (2000 / 2) × 0.5 = 500 W.
