This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) based on the NEET exam pattern for the topic 'Mechanical Properties of Fluids'. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the correct option for each question.

1. A liquid of density ρ is flowing through a horizontal pipe of varying cross-sectional area. If the velocity of the liquid at a point where the cross-sectional area is A₁ is v₁, and at another point where the cross-sectional area is A₂ is v₂, which of the following expressions correctly represents the relationship between these quantities according to the equation of continuity?

A. A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
B. ρA₁v₁ = ρA₂v₂
C. A₁/v₁ = A₂/v₂
D. A₁v₁² = A₂v₂²
Answer: A

The equation of continuity states that the mass flow rate of a fluid remains constant through a pipe of varying cross-sectional area. For an incompressible fluid (constant density ρ), this simplifies to A₁v₁ = A₂v₂, where A is the cross-sectional area and v is the velocity of the fluid. This equation ensures the conservation of mass.

2. The pressure at a depth 'h' in a liquid of density 'ρ' is given by P = P₀ + ρgh, where P₀ is the atmospheric pressure. If the atmospheric pressure is 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, the density of water is 1000 kg/m³, and g = 9.8 m/s², what is the pressure at a depth of 10 m in water?

A. 9.8 × 10⁴ Pa
B. 1.99 × 10⁵ Pa
C. 2.01 × 10⁵ Pa
D. 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa
Answer: B

Using the formula P = P₀ + ρgh, substitute the given values: P₀ = 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s², and h = 10 m. Thus, P = 1.01 × 10⁵ + (1000 × 9.8 × 10) = 1.01 × 10⁵ + 0.98 × 10⁵ = 1.99 × 10⁵ Pa.

3. A hydraulic lift is used to lift a car of mass 1500 kg. If the area of the larger piston is 0.5 m² and the area of the smaller piston is 0.05 m², what is the minimum force required on the smaller piston to lift the car? (Assume g = 10 m/s²)

A. 150 N
B. 1500 N
C. 15000 N
D. 15 N
Answer: A

In a hydraulic lift, the pressure on both pistons is the same. Using Pascal's law: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂, where F₁ is the force on the smaller piston, A₁ = 0.05 m², F₂ = weight of the car = 1500 × 10 = 15000 N, and A₂ = 0.5 m². Thus, F₁ = (F₂ × A₁) / A₂ = (15000 × 0.05) / 0.5 = 150 N.

4. Which of the following statements about surface tension is INCORRECT?

A. Surface tension is the property of a liquid due to which it tends to minimize its surface area.
B. Surface tension decreases with an increase in temperature.
C. Surface tension is independent of the area of the liquid surface.
D. Surface tension is caused by the gravitational forces between liquid molecules.
Answer: D

Surface tension is caused by the cohesive forces between liquid molecules, not gravitational forces. It is the property that allows the liquid to resist an external force and minimize its surface area. Surface tension decreases with temperature and is independent of the area of the liquid surface.

5. A capillary tube of radius 'r' is dipped vertically in a liquid of density 'ρ' and surface tension 'T'. If the angle of contact is 'θ', the height 'h' to which the liquid rises in the tube is given by:

A. h = (2T cosθ) / (ρ g r)
B. h = (2T) / (ρ g r)
C. h = (T cosθ) / (ρ g r)
D. h = (2T sinθ) / (ρ g r)
Answer: A

The height to which a liquid rises in a capillary tube is given by Jurin's law: h = (2T cosθ) / (ρ g r), where T is the surface tension, θ is the angle of contact, ρ is the density of the liquid, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and r is the radius of the capillary tube.

6. A block of wood floats in water with 40% of its volume submerged. If the same block is placed in a liquid of density 800 kg/m³, what percentage of its volume will be submerged? (Density of water = 1000 kg/m³)

A. 32%
B. 50%
C. 60%
D. 80%
Answer: B

For floating objects, the weight of the displaced liquid equals the weight of the object. Let V be the volume of the block and ρ_block be its density. In water: ρ_block × V × g = 0.4V × 1000 × g ⇒ ρ_block = 400 kg/m³. In the new liquid: ρ_block × V × g = xV × 800 × g ⇒ 400 = 800x ⇒ x = 0.5 or 50%.

7. The terminal velocity of a spherical ball falling through a viscous fluid is given by Stokes' law. If the radius of the ball is doubled, how does the terminal velocity change?

A. It becomes half.
B. It becomes one-fourth.
C. It becomes four times.
D. It becomes double.
Answer: C

Stokes' law states that the terminal velocity v is proportional to the square of the radius r of the spherical object: v ∝ r². If the radius is doubled, the terminal velocity becomes (2)² = 4 times the original velocity.

8. A liquid is flowing through a horizontal pipe with a constriction. At the constriction, the:

A. pressure increases and velocity decreases.
B. pressure decreases and velocity increases.
C. pressure and velocity both increase.
D. pressure and velocity both decrease.
Answer: B

According to the Bernoulli's principle, for a horizontal pipe, the sum of the pressure and kinetic energy per unit volume remains constant. At the constriction, the cross-sectional area decreases, so the velocity increases (equation of continuity). Consequently, the pressure decreases to maintain the energy balance.

9. The excess pressure inside a soap bubble of radius 'r' and surface tension 'T' is given by:

A. 2T / r
B. 4T / r
C. T / r
D. 8T / r
Answer: B

A soap bubble has two surfaces (inner and outer), so the excess pressure inside it is given by 4T / r, where T is the surface tension and r is the radius of the bubble. This is derived from the Laplace pressure formula for spherical surfaces.

10. A liquid drop of radius 'r' is split into 8 identical smaller drops. If the surface tension of the liquid is 'T', the work done in this process is:

A. 4πr²T
B. 8πr²T
C. 12πr²T
D. 16πr²T
Answer: A

The work done in splitting a liquid drop is equal to the increase in surface energy. The surface area of the original drop is 4πr². The radius of each smaller drop is r/2 (since volume is conserved: (4/3)πr³ = 8 × (4/3)π(r/2)³). The total surface area of 8 smaller drops is 8 × 4π(r/2)² = 8πr². The increase in surface area is 8πr² - 4πr² = 4πr². Thus, the work done is 4πr²T.

11. Which of the following is NOT a unit of viscosity?

A. Pascal-second (Pa·s)
B. Poise
C. Newton per meter (N/m)
D. Centipoise
Answer: C

Viscosity is measured in Pascal-second (Pa·s), Poise, or Centipoise (1 Poise = 0.1 Pa·s). Newton per meter (N/m) is the unit of surface tension, not viscosity.

12. A U-tube contains two immiscible liquids of densities ρ₁ and ρ₂ (ρ₁ > ρ₂). If the height of the liquid columns above the interface are h₁ and h₂ respectively, the relationship between the densities is:

A. ρ₁h₁ = ρ₂h₂
B. ρ₁h₂ = ρ₂h₁
C. ρ₁/h₁ = ρ₂/h₂
D. ρ₁ + ρ₂ = h₁ + h₂
Answer: A

In a U-tube containing two immiscible liquids, the pressures at the interface must be equal. Thus, ρ₁gh₁ = ρ₂gh₂, which simplifies to ρ₁h₁ = ρ₂h₂, where h₁ and h₂ are the heights of the liquid columns above the interface.

13. A liquid of density ρ is flowing through a horizontal pipe of varying cross-sectional area. If the pressure difference between two points A and B is ΔP, and the velocity of the liquid at A is v₁ and at B is v₂, which of the following expressions correctly relates these quantities according to Bernoulli’s principle?

A. ΔP = ρ(v₂² - v₁²)/2
B. ΔP = ρ(v₁² - v₂²)/2
C. ΔP = ρ(v₁² + v₂²)/2
D. ΔP = ρ(v₂ - v₁)²/2
Answer: B

According to Bernoulli’s principle for a horizontal pipe (where height difference is zero), the pressure difference ΔP between two points is given by ΔP = (1/2)ρ(v₁² - v₂²). This accounts for the conservation of energy, where the decrease in pressure corresponds to an increase in kinetic energy.

14. Two identical drops of water are falling through air with a terminal velocity v. If these drops coalesce to form a single drop, the new terminal velocity will be:

A. v/2^(1/3)
B. 2^(1/3)v
C. 2^(2/3)v
D. v/2^(2/3)
Answer: C

When two identical drops coalesce, the volume doubles, so the radius becomes 2^(1/3) times the original radius. Terminal velocity v is proportional to r² (from Stokes' law), so the new terminal velocity becomes (2^(1/3))²v = 2^(2/3)v.

15. A fluid of viscosity η is flowing through a cylindrical pipe of radius R and length L under a pressure difference ΔP. The volume flow rate Q is given by Poiseuille’s law as:

A. Q = (πR⁴ΔP)/(8ηL)
B. Q = (πR²ΔP)/(8ηL)
C. Q = (8πR⁴ΔP)/(ηL)
D. Q = (πR⁴ΔP)/(4ηL)
Answer: A

Poiseuille’s law states that the volume flow rate Q of a viscous fluid through a cylindrical pipe is Q = (πR⁴ΔP)/(8ηL), where R is the radius, ΔP is the pressure difference, η is the viscosity, and L is the length of the pipe. This formula is derived for laminar flow.

16. The Reynolds number for a fluid flowing through a pipe is 2000. This indicates that the flow is:

A. Laminar
B. Turbulent
C. Transitional
D. Unpredictable
Answer: C

The Reynolds number (Re) determines the nature of fluid flow. For Re < 2000, the flow is laminar; for Re > 4000, it is turbulent; and for 2000 < Re < 4000, the flow is transitional. Thus, Re = 2000 indicates transitional flow.

17. A block of wood of density ρ_w is floating in water of density ρ. If the fraction of the volume of the block submerged in water is f, which of the following is true?

A. f = ρ_w/ρ
B. f = ρ/ρ_w
C. f = (ρ - ρ_w)/ρ
D. f = (ρ_w - ρ)/ρ
Answer: A

For a floating object, the fraction of the volume submerged (f) is given by the ratio of the density of the object (ρ_w) to the density of the fluid (ρ), i.e., f = ρ_w/ρ. This is derived from Archimedes' principle, where the weight of the displaced fluid equals the weight of the object.

18. The velocity of efflux of a liquid from an orifice at a depth h below the free surface of the liquid is given by:

A. v = √(gh)
B. v = √(2gh)
C. v = 2√(gh)
D. v = gh
Answer: B

According to Torricelli’s law, the velocity of efflux (v) of a liquid from an orifice at a depth h below the free surface is v = √(2gh), where g is the acceleration due to gravity. This is derived from Bernoulli’s principle.

19. A liquid is flowing through a horizontal pipe with a constriction. If the pressure at the wider section is P₁ and at the narrower section is P₂, which of the following is correct?

A. P₁ > P₂
B. P₁ < P₂
C. P₁ = P₂
D. P₁ = 2P₂
Answer: A

According to the continuity equation and Bernoulli’s principle, the velocity of the fluid increases in the narrower section, leading to a decrease in pressure. Thus, the pressure at the wider section (P₁) is greater than the pressure at the narrower section (P₂).

20. The angle of contact for a liquid-solid interface is 120°. This indicates that the liquid:

A. Wets the solid surface
B. Does not wet the solid surface
C. Forms a convex meniscus
D. Has zero surface tension
Answer: B

An angle of contact greater than 90° indicates that the liquid does not wet the solid surface. The liquid forms a convex meniscus, and the cohesive forces within the liquid are stronger than the adhesive forces between the liquid and the solid.

21. A spherical ball of radius r and density ρ is falling through a viscous fluid of density σ and viscosity η. The terminal velocity v of the ball is given by Stokes’ law as:

A. v = (2/9)(r²g(ρ - σ))/η
B. v = (2/9)(r²g(ρ + σ))/η
C. v = (9/2)(r²g(ρ - σ))/η
D. v = (2/9)(rg(ρ - σ))/η
Answer: A

Stokes’ law states that the terminal velocity v of a spherical object falling through a viscous fluid is v = (2/9)(r²g(ρ - σ))/η, where r is the radius, g is acceleration due to gravity, ρ is the density of the object, σ is the density of the fluid, and η is the viscosity of the fluid.

22. A liquid of density ρ and surface tension T rises to a height h in a capillary tube of radius r. If the angle of contact between the liquid and the tube is θ, the correct expression for the height h is:

A. h = (2T cosθ) / (ρ g r)
B. h = (2T) / (ρ g r cosθ)
C. h = (T cosθ) / (2ρ g r)
D. h = (2T cosθ r) / (ρ g)
Answer: A

The rise of a liquid in a capillary tube is governed by the balance between the force due to surface tension and the weight of the liquid column. The vertical component of the surface tension force is 2πrT cosθ, and the weight of the liquid column is πr²hρg. Equating these forces gives: 2πrT cosθ = πr²hρg. Solving for h yields h = (2T cosθ) / (ρ g r). This formula accounts for the density of the liquid (ρ), surface tension (T), radius of the tube (r), angle of contact (θ), and acceleration due to gravity (g). Option A correctly represents this relationship.

23. A block of wood floats in water with 40% of its volume submerged. When the same block is placed in an unknown liquid, it floats with 50% of its volume submerged. The density of the unknown liquid is: (Density of water = 1000 kg/m³)

A. 800 kg/m³
B. 1000 kg/m³
C. 1250 kg/m³
D. 8000 kg/m³
Answer: A

According to Archimedes' principle, the weight of the floating body is equal to the weight of the displaced fluid. For the block in water: Weight of block = Weight of displaced water → Vρ_wood g = 0.4V × 1000 × g → ρ_wood = 400 kg/m³. For the block in the unknown liquid: Weight of block = Weight of displaced unknown liquid → V × 400 × g = 0.5V × ρ_liquid × g → ρ_liquid = (400 / 0.5) = 800 kg/m³. Thus, the density of the unknown liquid is 800 kg/m³.
