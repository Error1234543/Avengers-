1. An electromagnetic wave propagates in the positive x-direction. At a particular point in space, the electric field vector is along the positive y-direction. The magnetic field vector at this point will be along the:

A. positive x-direction
B. negative x-direction
C. positive z-direction
D. negative z-direction
Answer: C

In an electromagnetic wave, the electric field (E), magnetic field (B), and direction of propagation (k) are mutually perpendicular. Using the right-hand rule, if E is along the positive y-direction and the wave propagates along the positive x-direction, B must be along the positive z-direction.

2. The ratio of the amplitudes of electric field to magnetic field in an electromagnetic wave propagating in vacuum is equal to:

A. speed of light in vacuum (c)
B. permeability of free space (μ₀)
C. permittivity of free space (ε₀)
D. 1/c
Answer: A

For an electromagnetic wave in vacuum, the ratio of the amplitude of the electric field (E₀) to the amplitude of the magnetic field (B₀) is equal to the speed of light in vacuum (c), i.e., E₀/B₀ = c.

3. Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the shortest wavelength?

A. Radio waves
B. Infrared rays
C. Ultraviolet rays
D. Gamma rays
Answer: D

The electromagnetic spectrum is arranged in order of increasing frequency and decreasing wavelength. Gamma rays have the highest frequency and shortest wavelength among the given options.

4. The energy density of an electromagnetic wave in vacuum is given by:

A. (1/2)ε₀E² + (1/2)B²/μ₀
B. (1/2)ε₀E²
C. (1/2)B²/μ₀
D. ε₀E² + B²/μ₀
Answer: A

The energy density (u) of an electromagnetic wave in vacuum is the sum of the energy densities of the electric and magnetic fields: u = (1/2)ε₀E² + (1/2)B²/μ₀.

5. A plane electromagnetic wave of frequency 25 MHz travels in free space along the x-direction. The amplitude of the electric field is 1 V/m. The amplitude of the magnetic field is:

A. 3.33 × 10⁻⁹ T
B. 3.33 × 10⁻⁸ T
C. 3.33 × 10⁻⁷ T
D. 3.33 × 10⁻⁶ T
Answer: A

The amplitude of the magnetic field (B₀) is related to the amplitude of the electric field (E₀) by B₀ = E₀/c, where c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s). Thus, B₀ = 1 / (3 × 10⁸) = 3.33 × 10⁻⁹ T.

6. Which of the following statements about electromagnetic waves is INCORRECT?

A. They are transverse waves.
B. They require a medium to propagate.
C. They travel at the speed of light in vacuum.
D. They can be polarized.
Answer: B

Electromagnetic waves do not require a medium to propagate; they can travel through vacuum. This is a key difference between electromagnetic waves and mechanical waves.

7. The intensity of an electromagnetic wave is 2 W/m². The amplitude of the electric field in this wave is:

A. 27.4 V/m
B. 38.8 V/m
C. 42.4 V/m
D. 54.8 V/m
Answer: B

The intensity (I) of an electromagnetic wave is related to the amplitude of the electric field (E₀) by I = (1/2)ε₀cE₀². Solving for E₀: E₀ = √(2I/ε₀c). Substituting I = 2 W/m², ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/N·m², and c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, we get E₀ ≈ 38.8 V/m.

8. The correct order of increasing frequency of electromagnetic waves is:

A. Radio waves < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible light
B. Microwaves < Radio waves < Infrared < Visible light
C. Visible light < Infrared < Microwaves < Radio waves
D. Infrared < Visible light < Radio waves < Microwaves
Answer: A

The electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency (and decreasing wavelength) is: Radio waves < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible light < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Gamma rays.

9. A radio station transmits electromagnetic waves of wavelength 300 m. The frequency of the transmitted waves is:

A. 1 MHz
B. 10 MHz
C. 100 kHz
D. 1 kHz
Answer: A

The frequency (f) of an electromagnetic wave is related to its wavelength (λ) by f = c/λ, where c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s). Thus, f = 3 × 10⁸ / 300 = 1 × 10⁶ Hz = 1 MHz.

10. The electric field component of an electromagnetic wave in vacuum is given by E = E₀ sin(kx - ωt) î. The magnetic field component of this wave is:

A. B = (E₀/c) sin(kx - ωt) ĵ
B. B = (E₀/c) sin(kx - ωt) k̂
C. B = (E₀/c) sin(kx - ωt) (-ĵ)
D. B = (E₀/c) sin(kx + ωt) ĵ
Answer: A

For an electromagnetic wave propagating in the x-direction with the electric field along the y-direction, the magnetic field must be along the z-direction (using the right-hand rule). The magnetic field is given by B = (E₀/c) sin(kx - ωt) ĵ.

11. The pressure exerted by an electromagnetic wave of intensity I on a perfectly absorbing surface is:

A. I/c
B. 2I/c
C. Ic
D. I/c²
Answer: A

The radiation pressure (P) exerted by an electromagnetic wave on a perfectly absorbing surface is given by P = I/c, where I is the intensity of the wave and c is the speed of light in vacuum.

12. Which of the following electromagnetic waves is used in radar systems?

A. Radio waves
B. Microwaves
C. Infrared rays
D. Ultraviolet rays
Answer: B

Microwaves are used in radar systems due to their ability to travel long distances, penetrate clouds, and reflect off objects, making them ideal for detecting the position and speed of objects.

13. An electromagnetic wave propagates in the positive x-direction. At a particular point in space, the electric field vector points in the positive y-direction. In which direction does the magnetic field vector point at that point?

A. Positive x-direction
B. Negative x-direction
C. Positive z-direction
D. Negative z-direction
Answer: C

For an electromagnetic wave propagating in the positive x-direction, the electric field (E), magnetic field (B), and direction of propagation (k) are mutually perpendicular. If E points in the positive y-direction, B must point in the positive z-direction to satisfy the right-hand rule (E × B = k).

14. The ratio of the amplitudes of the electric field to the magnetic field in an electromagnetic wave in free space is equal to:

A. Speed of light (c)
B. 1/Speed of light (1/c)
C. Permeability of free space (μ₀)
D. Permittivity of free space (ε₀)
Answer: A

In free space, the ratio of the amplitude of the electric field (E₀) to the amplitude of the magnetic field (B₀) is equal to the speed of light (c), i.e., E₀/B₀ = c. This is derived from Maxwell's equations for electromagnetic waves.

15. Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the highest frequency?

A. Microwaves
B. Infrared waves
C. Ultraviolet rays
D. X-rays
Answer: D

The electromagnetic spectrum is ordered by increasing frequency as follows: radio waves < microwaves < infrared < visible light < ultraviolet < X-rays < gamma rays. Thus, X-rays have the highest frequency among the given options.

16. A plane electromagnetic wave of frequency 25 MHz travels in free space. The wavelength of the wave is:

A. 12 m
B. 1.2 m
C. 120 m
D. 0.12 m
Answer: A

The wavelength (λ) of an electromagnetic wave is given by λ = c/f, where c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s) and f is the frequency. For f = 25 MHz = 25 × 10⁶ Hz, λ = (3 × 10⁸)/(25 × 10⁶) = 12 m.

17. The intensity of an electromagnetic wave is proportional to:

A. The square of the amplitude of the electric field
B. The amplitude of the electric field
C. The square root of the amplitude of the electric field
D. The frequency of the wave
Answer: A

The intensity (I) of an electromagnetic wave is proportional to the square of the amplitude of the electric field (E₀²), i.e., I ∝ E₀². This is because intensity is the average power per unit area, which depends on the energy density of the wave.

18. In an electromagnetic wave, the electric field and magnetic field are related by:

A. E = cB
B. B = cE
C. E = B/c
D. E = c²B
Answer: A

In an electromagnetic wave, the electric field (E) and magnetic field (B) are related by E = cB, where c is the speed of light. This relationship is derived from Maxwell's equations.

19. Which of the following is NOT a source of electromagnetic waves?

A. Accelerating charges
B. Oscillating dipoles
C. Stationary charges
D. Alternating current circuits
Answer: C

Electromagnetic waves are produced by accelerating charges, oscillating dipoles, or alternating current circuits. Stationary charges do not produce electromagnetic waves because they do not generate changing electric or magnetic fields.

20. The speed of electromagnetic waves in a medium with permittivity ε and permeability μ is given by:

A. 1/√(εμ)
B. √(ε/μ)
C. √(εμ)
D. 1/(εμ)
Answer: A

The speed (v) of electromagnetic waves in a medium is given by v = 1/√(εμ), where ε is the permittivity and μ is the permeability of the medium. In vacuum, this reduces to c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀).

21. A radio wave of wavelength 300 m is emitted by a transmitter. The frequency of the wave is:

A. 1 MHz
B. 10 MHz
C. 100 kHz
D. 1 kHz
Answer: A

The frequency (f) of an electromagnetic wave is given by f = c/λ, where c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s) and λ is the wavelength. For λ = 300 m, f = (3 × 10⁸)/300 = 1 × 10⁶ Hz = 1 MHz.

22. The electromagnetic spectrum includes all of the following EXCEPT:

A. Gamma rays
B. Sound waves
C. X-rays
D. Ultraviolet rays
Answer: B

The electromagnetic spectrum includes gamma rays, X-rays, ultraviolet rays, visible light, infrared, microwaves, and radio waves. Sound waves are mechanical waves and are not part of the electromagnetic spectrum.

23. An electromagnetic wave propagating in vacuum has an electric field amplitude of 60 V/m. The amplitude of the magnetic field associated with this wave is:

A. 2 × 10⁻⁷ T
B. 1.8 × 10⁻⁷ T
C. 2 × 10⁻⁸ T
D. 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ T
Answer: A

For an electromagnetic wave in vacuum, the relationship between the electric field amplitude (E₀) and the magnetic field amplitude (B₀) is given by E₀ = cB₀, where c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s). Rearranging, B₀ = E₀ / c. Substituting E₀ = 60 V/m, B₀ = 60 / (3 × 10⁸) = 2 × 10⁻⁷ T. This matches option A. The question tests the fundamental relationship between electric and magnetic field amplitudes in electromagnetic waves, a key concept for NEET.

24. An electromagnetic wave propagates in vacuum with an electric field amplitude of 60 V/m. If the wave is linearly polarized in the x-direction and propagates along the z-axis, which of the following correctly represents the magnetic field component of the wave at a point where the electric field is maximum?

A. B = (2 × 10⁻⁷ T) sin(kz - ωt) 𝐣̂
B. B = (2 × 10⁻⁷ T) cos(kz - ωt) 𝐢̂
C. B = (60 V/m) sin(kz - ωt) 𝐤̂
D. B = (2 × 10⁻⁷ T) sin(kz + ωt) 𝐣̂
Answer: A

For an electromagnetic wave in vacuum, the electric field (E) and magnetic field (B) are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation (z-axis). The relationship between their amplitudes is E₀ = cB₀, where c is the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s). Given E₀ = 60 V/m, B₀ = E₀/c = 60 / (3 × 10⁸) = 2 × 10⁻⁷ T. Since E is polarized along the x-axis (𝐢̂), B must be along the y-axis (𝐣̂). The wave propagates in the +z direction, so the phase is (kz - ωt). Thus, B = (2 × 10⁻⁷ T) sin(kz - ωt) 𝐣̂. Option A is correct.
