This set contains 12 PYQ-style multiple-choice questions on the topic of Waves for NEET Physics. Each question carries 4 marks with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Choose the correct option for each question.

1. A transverse wave travels along a string with a speed of 200 m/s. If the frequency of the wave is 50 Hz, what is the wavelength of the wave?

A. 0.25 m
B. 2 m
C. 4 m
D. 8 m
Answer: C

The wavelength (λ) of a wave is given by the formula: λ = v / f, where v is the speed of the wave and f is the frequency. Here, v = 200 m/s and f = 50 Hz. Thus, λ = 200 / 50 = 4 m.

2. Which of the following statements is true for a progressive wave?

A. All particles of the medium vibrate in phase.
B. The amplitude of vibration is the same for all particles.
C. Energy is transferred in the direction of wave propagation.
D. The wave does not transport energy.
Answer: C

A progressive wave transfers energy in the direction of its propagation. Particles of the medium vibrate with different phases, and the amplitude may vary depending on the medium's properties.

3. The equation of a wave is given by y = 0.02 sin(π(2x - 50t)), where x and y are in meters and t in seconds. The speed of the wave is:

A. 25 m/s
B. 50 m/s
C. 100 m/s
D. 200 m/s
Answer: A

The general form of a wave equation is y = A sin(kx - ωt). Comparing with the given equation, k = 2π and ω = 50π. The speed of the wave (v) is given by v = ω / k = 50π / 2π = 25 m/s.

4. In a stationary wave, the distance between two consecutive nodes is:

A. λ/4
B. λ/2
C. λ
D. 2λ
Answer: B

In a stationary wave, the distance between two consecutive nodes is half the wavelength (λ/2). This is because nodes are points of zero displacement, and they occur at intervals of λ/2.

5. A sound wave travels from air into water. Which of the following properties of the wave remains unchanged?

A. Speed
B. Wavelength
C. Frequency
D. Amplitude
Answer: C

When a wave travels from one medium to another, its frequency remains unchanged. The speed and wavelength change due to the change in the medium's properties, and amplitude may also change due to energy loss.

6. The phenomenon of beats is observed when two sound waves of:

A. Same amplitude and same frequency interfere.
B. Different amplitudes and same frequency interfere.
C. Same amplitude and slightly different frequencies interfere.
D. Different amplitudes and different frequencies interfere.
Answer: C

Beats are produced when two sound waves of the same amplitude but slightly different frequencies interfere. The resultant wave has a frequency equal to the average of the two frequencies and an amplitude that varies periodically.

7. The intensity of a sound wave is proportional to:

A. The amplitude of the wave.
B. The square of the amplitude of the wave.
C. The frequency of the wave.
D. The square of the frequency of the wave.
Answer: B

The intensity of a sound wave is proportional to the square of its amplitude. This is because intensity is a measure of the energy carried by the wave, and energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude.

8. A pipe closed at one end resonates at a fundamental frequency of 256 Hz. If the pipe is opened at both ends, the fundamental frequency will be:

A. 128 Hz
B. 256 Hz
C. 512 Hz
D. 768 Hz
Answer: C

For a pipe closed at one end, the fundamental frequency is f = v / (4L). For a pipe open at both ends, the fundamental frequency is f' = v / (2L). Thus, f' = 2f = 2 * 256 = 512 Hz.

9. The Doppler effect is observed when:

A. The source of sound is stationary and the observer is moving.
B. The observer is stationary and the source of sound is moving.
C. Both the source and the observer are moving relative to each other.
D. All of the above.
Answer: D

The Doppler effect is observed whenever there is relative motion between the source of sound and the observer. This includes cases where the source is moving, the observer is moving, or both are moving relative to each other.

10. A wave has a frequency of 500 Hz and a wavelength of 0.6 m. The time period of the wave is:

A. 0.002 s
B. 0.005 s
C. 0.02 s
D. 0.05 s
Answer: A

The time period (T) of a wave is the reciprocal of its frequency (f). Thus, T = 1 / f = 1 / 500 = 0.002 s.

11. In a transverse wave, the particles of the medium vibrate:

A. In the direction of wave propagation.
B. Perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
C. In a circular path.
D. In an elliptical path.
Answer: B

In a transverse wave, the particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. This is in contrast to longitudinal waves, where particles vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation.

12. The speed of sound in air at 0°C is 330 m/s. If the temperature increases to 273°C, the speed of sound becomes:

A. 330 m/s
B. 467 m/s
C. 660 m/s
D. 990 m/s
Answer: B

The speed of sound in air increases with temperature according to the formula: v = v₀ √(1 + T/273), where v₀ is the speed at 0°C and T is the temperature in °C. Here, T = 273°C, so v = 330 √(1 + 273/273) = 330 √2 ≈ 467 m/s.

13. A transverse wave is described by the equation y = 0.02 sin(π(0.5x - 200t)), where x and y are in meters and t is in seconds. The wavelength of the wave is:

A. 2 m
B. 4 m
C. 0.5 m
D. 1 m
Answer: B

The general form of a transverse wave is y = A sin(kx - ωt), where k = 2π/λ. Here, k = 0.5π, so λ = 2π/k = 2π/(0.5π) = 4 m.

14. The speed of sound in air at 25°C is approximately 346 m/s. If the frequency of a sound wave is 500 Hz, the wavelength of the wave is:

A. 0.692 m
B. 0.346 m
C. 1.44 m
D. 0.173 m
Answer: A

The wavelength λ is given by λ = v/f, where v is the speed of sound and f is the frequency. Thus, λ = 346/500 = 0.692 m.

15. Two waves of the same frequency and amplitude interfere constructively. If the amplitude of each wave is A, the amplitude of the resultant wave is:

A. A
B. 2A
C. A/2
D. 0
Answer: B

When two waves of the same frequency and amplitude interfere constructively, their amplitudes add up. Thus, the resultant amplitude is A + A = 2A.

16. A string fixed at both ends vibrates in its third harmonic. If the length of the string is 1.5 m, the wavelength of the standing wave formed is:

A. 1.0 m
B. 1.5 m
C. 2.0 m
D. 3.0 m
Answer: A

For the third harmonic (n = 3), the wavelength λ is given by L = nλ/2, where L is the length of the string. Thus, 1.5 = 3λ/2 ⇒ λ = 1.0 m.

17. The phenomenon of beats is observed when two sound waves of slightly different frequencies interfere. If the frequencies of the two waves are 256 Hz and 260 Hz, the beat frequency is:

A. 4 Hz
B. 2 Hz
C. 8 Hz
D. 6 Hz
Answer: A

The beat frequency is the absolute difference between the two frequencies: |260 - 256| = 4 Hz.

18. The intensity level of a sound wave is increased by 20 dB. The intensity of the sound wave increases by a factor of:

A. 10
B. 100
C. 20
D. 200
Answer: B

An increase in intensity level by 20 dB corresponds to an increase in intensity by a factor of 10^2 = 100, since the decibel scale is logarithmic (β = 10 log(I/I₀)).

19. A pipe closed at one end resonates at a fundamental frequency of 256 Hz. If the speed of sound in air is 340 m/s, the length of the pipe is:

A. 0.33 m
B. 0.66 m
C. 0.165 m
D. 1.32 m
Answer: A

For a pipe closed at one end, the fundamental frequency f is given by f = v/(4L), where v is the speed of sound and L is the length of the pipe. Thus, L = v/(4f) = 340/(4 × 256) ≈ 0.33 m.

20. The Doppler effect is observed when there is relative motion between the source of sound and the observer. If the source moves away from the observer, the observed frequency:

A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Remains the same
D. Becomes zero
Answer: B

When the source moves away from the observer, the observed frequency decreases due to the Doppler effect, as the wavelength of the sound wave appears stretched.

21. A wave pulse on a string is reflected from a fixed end. The reflected pulse:

A. Is inverted and has the same amplitude
B. Is not inverted and has the same amplitude
C. Is inverted and has reduced amplitude
D. Is not inverted and has reduced amplitude
Answer: A

When a wave pulse is reflected from a fixed end, it undergoes a phase change of 180°, resulting in an inverted pulse with the same amplitude.

22. A sound wave of frequency 500 Hz travels through a medium with a wavelength of 0.68 m. The speed of sound in the medium is:

A. 340 m/s
B. 300 m/s
C. 380 m/s
D. 420 m/s
Answer: A

The speed of sound v is given by v = fλ, where f is the frequency and λ is the wavelength. Thus, v = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s.

23. A transverse wave is described by the equation y = 0.02 sin(π(4x - 50t)), where y and x are in meters and t is in seconds. The wavelength and frequency of the wave are respectively:

A. 0.5 m and 25 Hz
B. 0.25 m and 50 Hz
C. 0.5 m and 50 Hz
D. 0.25 m and 25 Hz
Answer: A

The standard form of a transverse wave is y = A sin(kx - ωt), where k = 2π/λ and ω = 2πf. Comparing with the given equation y = 0.02 sin(π(4x - 50t)), we get k = 4π and ω = 50π. Therefore, wavelength λ = 2π/k = 2π/(4π) = 0.5 m, and frequency f = ω/(2π) = 50π/(2π) = 25 Hz. Thus, the correct answer is 0.5 m and 25 Hz.
