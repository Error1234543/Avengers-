This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic of Atoms, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks with 1 mark deducted for an incorrect answer. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. In the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom, the radius of the nth orbit is given by rₙ = a₀n², where a₀ is the Bohr radius. If an electron transitions from the n = 3 to the n = 1 orbit, the ratio of the final to initial orbit radius is:

A. 1:9
B. 1:3
C. 3:1
D. 9:1
Answer: A

The radius of the nth orbit in Bohr’s model is rₙ = a₀n². For n = 3, r₃ = a₀ × 3² = 9a₀. For n = 1, r₁ = a₀ × 1² = a₀. The ratio r₁ : r₃ = a₀ : 9a₀ = 1 : 9.

2. The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by Eₙ = -13.6 eV / n². What is the energy released when an electron jumps from the n = 4 to the n = 2 orbit?

A. 0.85 eV
B. 2.55 eV
C. 3.40 eV
D. 6.80 eV
Answer: B

Energy at n = 4: E₄ = -13.6 / 16 = -0.85 eV. Energy at n = 2: E₂ = -13.6 / 4 = -3.40 eV. Energy released = E₂ - E₄ = (-3.40) - (-0.85) = -2.55 eV (magnitude 2.55 eV).

3. The Rydberg formula for the wavelengths of spectral lines in hydrogen is 1/λ = R (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²). For the Balmer series, n₁ is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4
Answer: B

The Balmer series corresponds to transitions where the electron falls to the n = 2 level from higher levels (n₂ > 2). Thus, n₁ = 2 in the Rydberg formula.

4. An electron in a hydrogen atom is excited to the n = 5 level. The maximum number of spectral lines emitted when it returns to the ground state is:

A. 4
B. 5
C. 10
D. 15
Answer: C

The number of possible transitions (spectral lines) from level n to lower levels is given by n(n-1)/2. For n = 5, this is 5 × 4 / 2 = 10.

5. The ionization energy of a hydrogen atom in its ground state is 13.6 eV. What is the ionization energy for a He⁺ ion in its ground state?

A. 13.6 eV
B. 27.2 eV
C. 54.4 eV
D. 122.4 eV
Answer: C

For a hydrogen-like ion with atomic number Z, the ionization energy is Z² × 13.6 eV. For He⁺ (Z = 2), this is 2² × 13.6 = 54.4 eV.

6. In the Bohr model, the velocity of an electron in the nth orbit is given by vₙ = v₀ / n, where v₀ is the velocity in the first orbit. If the electron in a hydrogen atom moves from n = 2 to n = 1, its velocity:

A. doubles
B. halves
C. remains the same
D. becomes zero
Answer: A

Velocity in the nth orbit is vₙ = v₀ / n. For n = 2, v₂ = v₀ / 2. For n = 1, v₁ = v₀. Thus, the velocity doubles when the electron moves from n = 2 to n = 1.

7. The wavelength of the first line of the Lyman series in hydrogen is approximately 121.6 nm. What is the wavelength of the first line of the Balmer series?

A. 486.4 nm
B. 656.3 nm
C. 121.6 nm
D. 91.2 nm
Answer: B

The first line of the Lyman series corresponds to n = 2 → n = 1 (λ = 121.6 nm). The first line of the Balmer series corresponds to n = 3 → n = 2. Using the Rydberg formula, the wavelength for the Balmer line is longer, approximately 656.3 nm.

8. Which of the following transitions in a hydrogen atom emits a photon of the highest frequency?

A. n = 2 → n = 1
B. n = 3 → n = 2
C. n = 4 → n = 3
D. n = 5 → n = 4
Answer: A

The frequency of the emitted photon is proportional to the energy difference between levels. The transition n = 2 → n = 1 has the largest energy difference among the given options, thus emitting the highest frequency photon.

9. The angular momentum of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is L = nh/2π. If the electron is in the n = 3 orbit, its angular momentum is:

A. h/2π
B. 3h/2π
C. 6h/2π
D. 9h/2π
Answer: B

According to Bohr’s quantization condition, the angular momentum L = nh/2π. For n = 3, L = 3h/2π.

10. A hydrogen atom in its ground state absorbs a photon of energy 12.09 eV. To which energy level is the electron excited?

A. n = 2
B. n = 3
C. n = 4
D. n = 5
Answer: B

The energy of the nth level is Eₙ = -13.6 / n² eV. The energy absorbed is 12.09 eV, so the final energy is E_final = E₁ + 12.09 = -13.6 + 12.09 = -1.51 eV. Solving -13.6 / n² = -1.51 gives n² ≈ 9, so n = 3.

11. The ratio of the kinetic energy to the potential energy of an electron in a hydrogen atom in the nth orbit is:

A. 1:2
B. 2:1
C. 1:-2
D. -1:2
Answer: C

In a hydrogen atom, the total energy E = K + U, where K is kinetic energy and U is potential energy. For a Coulomb potential, U = -2K. Thus, K:U = 1:-2.

12. In the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom, the ratio of the kinetic energy to the total energy of the electron in the nth orbit is:

A. 1 : 1
B. 1 : -1
C. -1 : 1
D. 2 : 1
Answer: C

In the Bohr model, the total energy (E) of the electron in the nth orbit is negative and equal to half the potential energy. The kinetic energy (K) is positive and equal to the negative of the total energy (K = -E). Thus, the ratio of kinetic energy to total energy is -1 : 1.

13. The wavelength of the first line of the Balmer series in the hydrogen spectrum is approximately:

A. 410 nm
B. 434 nm
C. 486 nm
D. 656 nm
Answer: D

The first line of the Balmer series (n=3 to n=2 transition) in the hydrogen spectrum corresponds to a wavelength of approximately 656 nm, which lies in the visible red region.

14. An electron in a hydrogen atom transitions from the n=4 to the n=2 energy level. The energy of the emitted photon is closest to:

A. 2.55 eV
B. 3.40 eV
C. 0.85 eV
D. 1.89 eV
Answer: A

The energy difference between the n=4 and n=2 levels in hydrogen is calculated using the formula ΔE = 13.6 (1/2² - 1/4²) eV = 13.6 (1/4 - 1/16) eV = 13.6 × 3/16 eV = 2.55 eV.

15. The angular momentum of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom, according to Bohr's theory, is quantized as:

A. n²h/2π
B. nh/2π
C. n²h/π
D. nh/π
Answer: B

Bohr's quantization condition states that the angular momentum (L) of an electron in the nth orbit is given by L = nh/2π, where n is the principal quantum number and h is Planck's constant.

16. The ionization energy of a hydrogen atom in its ground state is 13.6 eV. The energy required to excite the electron from the ground state to the n=3 level is:

A. 12.09 eV
B. 10.20 eV
C. 9.07 eV
D. 1.51 eV
Answer: A

The energy required to excite the electron from the ground state (n=1) to the n=3 level is ΔE = 13.6 (1/1² - 1/3²) eV = 13.6 (1 - 1/9) eV = 13.6 × 8/9 eV ≈ 12.09 eV.

17. In the hydrogen spectrum, the series limit for the Lyman series corresponds to a transition from:

A. n=2 to n=1
B. n=∞ to n=1
C. n=∞ to n=2
D. n=3 to n=1
Answer: B

The series limit for the Lyman series corresponds to the transition from n=∞ (ionization) to n=1, which represents the shortest wavelength (highest energy) in the series.

18. The radius of the first Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom is 0.53 Å. The radius of the third Bohr orbit is:

A. 1.59 Å
B. 2.12 Å
C. 4.77 Å
D. 0.177 Å
Answer: C

The radius of the nth Bohr orbit is given by rₙ = n² × r₁, where r₁ is the radius of the first orbit. For n=3, r₃ = 3² × 0.53 Å = 9 × 0.53 Å = 4.77 Å.

19. The ratio of the wavelengths of the first line of the Lyman series to the first line of the Balmer series in the hydrogen spectrum is:

A. 5/27
B. 27/5
C. 4/9
D. 9/4
Answer: A

The first line of the Lyman series corresponds to the transition n=2 to n=1, and its wavelength is given by 1/λ_L = R (1/1² - 1/2²) = 3R/4. The first line of the Balmer series corresponds to n=3 to n=2, with 1/λ_B = R (1/2² - 1/3²) = 5R/36. The ratio λ_L/λ_B = (5R/36) / (3R/4) = 5/27.

20. In the Bohr model, the velocity of the electron in the nth orbit is proportional to:

A. n
B. 1/n
C. n²
D. 1/n²
Answer: B

In the Bohr model, the velocity (v) of the electron in the nth orbit is given by v ∝ 1/n. This is derived from the quantization of angular momentum and the balance between electrostatic and centripetal forces.

21. The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen-like atom with atomic number Z is given by:

A. -13.6 Z²/n² eV
B. -13.6 Z/n² eV
C. -13.6 Z²/n eV
D. -13.6 Z/n eV
Answer: A

The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen-like atom is given by Eₙ = -13.6 Z²/n² eV, where Z is the atomic number and n is the principal quantum number.
