This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on the topic of Oscillations, designed to match the style and difficulty of NEET previous-year questions. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the correct option for each question.

1. A particle executes simple harmonic motion (SHM) with an amplitude of 5 cm and a period of 2 s. What is the maximum velocity of the particle?

A. 5π cm/s
B. 10π cm/s
C. 15π cm/s
D. 20π cm/s
Answer: A

The maximum velocity (v_max) in SHM is given by v_max = Aω, where A is the amplitude and ω is the angular frequency. Here, A = 5 cm and ω = 2π/T = 2π/2 = π rad/s. Thus, v_max = 5π cm/s.

2. The displacement of a particle in SHM is given by x = 10 sin(20t + π/4). What is the phase constant of the motion?

A. π/4
B. π/2
C. π
D. 20
Answer: A

The general equation for SHM is x = A sin(ωt + φ), where φ is the phase constant. Comparing with the given equation x = 10 sin(20t + π/4), the phase constant φ is π/4.

3. A spring-mass system has a spring constant of 100 N/m and a mass of 1 kg. If the system is set into oscillation, what is the time period of the oscillation?

A. 0.2π s
B. 0.4π s
C. π/5 s
D. 2π/10 s
Answer: D

The time period (T) of a spring-mass system is given by T = 2π√(m/k), where m is the mass and k is the spring constant. Here, m = 1 kg and k = 100 N/m. Thus, T = 2π√(1/100) = 2π/10 s.

4. In SHM, the acceleration of a particle is proportional to:

A. Displacement from the mean position
B. Velocity of the particle
C. Amplitude of the motion
D. Time period of the motion
Answer: A

In SHM, the acceleration (a) is given by a = -ω²x, where x is the displacement from the mean position. Thus, acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement.

5. A pendulum clock is taken to the moon where the acceleration due to gravity is 1/6th of that on Earth. If the clock keeps correct time on Earth, how will it behave on the moon?

A. It will run faster
B. It will run slower
C. It will stop
D. It will keep the same time
Answer: B

The time period of a pendulum is given by T = 2π√(L/g). On the moon, g decreases to 1/6th of its Earth value, so the time period increases. Thus, the clock will run slower.

6. The total energy of a particle executing SHM is 50 J. If the amplitude is doubled, what will be the new total energy?

A. 50 J
B. 100 J
C. 200 J
D. 25 J
Answer: C

The total energy (E) in SHM is proportional to the square of the amplitude (A). If the amplitude is doubled, the new energy becomes 4 times the original energy. Thus, E_new = 4 × 50 J = 200 J.

7. A particle in SHM has a velocity of 4 m/s at a displacement of 3 m from the mean position. If the amplitude is 5 m, what is the angular frequency of the motion?

A. 1 rad/s
B. 2 rad/s
C. 4 rad/s
D. 5 rad/s
Answer: A

The velocity (v) in SHM is given by v = ω√(A² - x²). Here, v = 4 m/s, A = 5 m, and x = 3 m. Substituting, 4 = ω√(25 - 9) → 4 = ω × 4 → ω = 1 rad/s.

8. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of simple harmonic motion?

A. The restoring force is proportional to the displacement
B. The motion is periodic
C. The acceleration is constant
D. The total energy remains constant
Answer: C

In SHM, the acceleration is not constant; it varies with displacement (a = -ω²x). The other options are true characteristics of SHM.

9. A particle executes SHM with a frequency of 5 Hz. What is the time period of the motion?

A. 0.1 s
B. 0.2 s
C. 0.5 s
D. 1 s
Answer: B

The time period (T) is the reciprocal of the frequency (f). Thus, T = 1/f = 1/5 s = 0.2 s.

10. In a damped harmonic oscillator, the amplitude of oscillation decreases with time due to:

A. Increase in potential energy
B. Dissipation of energy
C. Increase in kinetic energy
D. Constant external force
Answer: B

In a damped harmonic oscillator, the amplitude decreases over time due to the dissipation of energy, typically as heat or other forms of energy loss.

11. The phase difference between the displacement and velocity of a particle in SHM is:

A. 0
B. π/4
C. π/2
D. π
Answer: C

In SHM, the velocity leads the displacement by a phase difference of π/2. This is because velocity is the time derivative of displacement, introducing a 90-degree phase shift.

12. A particle in SHM has a maximum acceleration of 20 m/s² and an amplitude of 2 m. What is the angular frequency of the motion?

A. 5 rad/s
B. 10 rad/s
C. √10 rad/s
D. 20 rad/s
Answer: B

The maximum acceleration (a_max) in SHM is given by a_max = Aω². Here, a_max = 20 m/s² and A = 2 m. Thus, 20 = 2ω² → ω² = 10 → ω = √10 × √10 = 10 rad/s.

13. The displacement of a particle in SHM is given by x = 10 sin(20t + π/4) cm. The frequency of oscillation is:

A. 10/π Hz
B. 20/π Hz
C. 10 Hz
D. 20 Hz
Answer: A

The general form of displacement in SHM is x = A sin(ωt + φ), where ω = 2πf. Here, ω = 20 rad/s, so f = ω/2π = 20/2π = 10/π Hz.

14. In SHM, the acceleration of a particle is zero when:

A. Displacement is maximum
B. Velocity is maximum
C. Kinetic energy is maximum
D. Potential energy is maximum
Answer: B

In SHM, acceleration is given by a = -ω²x. Acceleration is zero when displacement (x) is zero, which occurs when velocity is maximum (at the mean position).

15. The total energy of a particle executing SHM is proportional to:

A. Amplitude
B. Square of amplitude
C. Frequency
D. Square of frequency
Answer: B

The total energy (E) in SHM is given by E = (1/2)kA², where A is the amplitude. Thus, E is proportional to the square of the amplitude.

16. A particle in SHM has a displacement of A/2 at a certain instant. The ratio of its kinetic energy to potential energy at this instant is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4
Answer: C

At displacement x = A/2, potential energy (PE) = (1/2)kx² = (1/2)k(A/2)² = kA²/8. Total energy (E) = (1/2)kA², so kinetic energy (KE) = E - PE = (1/2)kA² - kA²/8 = 3kA²/8. Thus, KE/PE = (3kA²/8)/(kA²/8) = 3.

17. The phase difference between the velocity and acceleration of a particle in SHM is:

A. 0
B. π/2
C. π
D. 3π/2
Answer: C

In SHM, velocity leads displacement by π/2, and acceleration leads velocity by π/2. Thus, acceleration leads displacement by π, meaning the phase difference between velocity and acceleration is π.

18. A particle executes SHM with a time period of 4 s. The time taken by the particle to go from the mean position to half the amplitude is:

A. 1/3 s
B. 1/6 s
C. 2/3 s
D. 1/12 s
Answer: A

The displacement in SHM is x = A sin(ωt). For x = A/2, A/2 = A sin(ωt) ⇒ sin(ωt) = 1/2 ⇒ ωt = π/6. Since ω = 2π/T = 2π/4 = π/2 rad/s, t = (π/6)/(π/2) = 1/3 s.

19. The equation of SHM is given by x = 5 cos(2πt + π/4). The initial phase angle is:

A. 0
B. π/4
C. π/2
D. π
Answer: B

The general form of SHM is x = A cos(ωt + φ), where φ is the initial phase angle. Here, φ = π/4.

20. A spring-mass system oscillates with a frequency of 5 Hz. If the mass is doubled, the new frequency will be:

A. 5 Hz
B. 10 Hz
C. 5/√2 Hz
D. 5√2 Hz
Answer: C

The frequency (f) of a spring-mass system is given by f = (1/2π)√(k/m). If mass is doubled, f_new = (1/2π)√(k/2m) = f/√2 = 5/√2 Hz.

21. In SHM, the restoring force is proportional to:

A. Displacement
B. Velocity
C. Acceleration
D. Amplitude
Answer: A

In SHM, the restoring force (F) is given by F = -kx, where x is the displacement. Thus, F is proportional to displacement.

22. A particle in SHM has a maximum velocity of 2 m/s and a maximum acceleration of 4 m/s². The amplitude of oscillation is:

A. 0.5 m
B. 1 m
C. 2 m
D. 4 m
Answer: B

Maximum velocity (v_max) = Aω = 2 m/s. Maximum acceleration (a_max) = Aω² = 4 m/s². Dividing, ω = a_max/v_max = 4/2 = 2 rad/s. Thus, A = v_max/ω = 2/2 = 1 m.
