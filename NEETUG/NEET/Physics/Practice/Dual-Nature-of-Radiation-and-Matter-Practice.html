NEET Physics Practice – Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter (Part 1 of 2)

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter, strictly aligned with the NEET syllabus and NTA question style. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Choose the single best answer.

1. Which of the following graphs correctly represents the variation of stopping potential (V₀) with frequency (ν) of incident radiation for a given photosensitive surface?

A. V₀ increases linearly with ν, starting from zero at threshold frequency.
B. V₀ increases linearly with ν, starting from a positive intercept at ν = 0.
C. V₀ remains constant above threshold frequency.
D. V₀ decreases linearly with increasing ν.
Answer: A

Stopping potential V₀ is related to frequency by eV₀ = hν – hν₀, giving a straight line of positive slope that passes through V₀ = 0 at ν = ν₀.

2. The de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through a potential difference of 100 V is (mass of electron = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg, charge = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s):

A. 0.123 nm
B. 0.388 nm
C. 1.23 nm
D. 3.88 nm
Answer: A

Kinetic energy K = eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C × 100 V = 1.6×10⁻¹⁷ J. Momentum p = √(2mK) = √(2 × 9.1×10⁻³¹ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁷) = 5.4×10⁻²⁴ kg m/s. λ = h/p = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / 5.4×10⁻²⁴ ≈ 1.23×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.123 nm.

3. In Davisson-Germer experiment, electrons are accelerated by 54 V and show a diffraction maximum at an angle of 50° with the incident beam. The interplanar spacing of the nickel crystal used is (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, mₑ = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C):

A. 0.091 nm
B. 0.165 nm
C. 0.215 nm
D. 0.325 nm
Answer: B

Bragg angle θ = (180° – 50°)/2 = 65°. λ = h/√(2mₑeV) = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / √(2×9.1×10⁻³¹×1.6×10⁻¹⁹×54) ≈ 1.67×10⁻¹⁰ m. d = λ/(2 sin θ) = 1.67×10⁻¹⁰ / (2 × sin 65°) ≈ 0.165 nm.

4. A photon of wavelength 300 nm ejects a photoelectron from a metal surface. If the stopping potential is 2.0 V, the work function of the metal is (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C):

A. 1.2 eV
B. 2.2 eV
C. 3.2 eV
D. 4.2 eV
Answer: B

Energy of photon E = hc/λ = 6.63×10⁻³⁴×3×10⁸ / 300×10⁻⁹ = 6.63×10⁻¹⁹ J = 4.14 eV. Work function φ = E – eV₀ = 4.14 eV – 2.0 eV = 2.14 eV ≈ 2.2 eV.

5. Which phenomenon best demonstrates the particle nature of light?

A. Interference
B. Diffraction
C. Photoelectric effect
D. Polarization
Answer: C

The photoelectric effect shows that light transfers energy in discrete quanta (photons), a hallmark of particle behaviour.

6. The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons emitted from a surface is 3.0 eV when the incident light has a frequency of 1.5 × 10¹⁵ Hz. The threshold frequency for the surface is (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s):

A. 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz
B. 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz
C. 1.0 × 10¹⁵ Hz
D. 1.2 × 10¹⁵ Hz
Answer: B

Kₘₐₓ = hν – hν₀ ⇒ 3.0 eV = h(1.5×10¹⁵ – ν₀). Converting 3.0 eV to joules: 3.0 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 4.8×10⁻¹⁹ J. ν₀ = 1.5×10¹⁵ – (4.8×10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63×10⁻³⁴) ≈ 7.5×10¹⁴ Hz.

7. Which of the following statements about de Broglie wavelength is correct?

A. It increases with increasing velocity of the particle.
B. It is independent of the mass of the particle.
C. It decreases with increasing momentum of the particle.
D. It is the same for all particles moving with the same kinetic energy.
Answer: C

λ = h/p, so λ decreases as momentum p increases.

8. In an experiment on photoelectric effect, the slope of the stopping potential versus frequency graph gives:

A. Work function of the metal
B. Threshold frequency
C. Planck’s constant divided by electron charge
D. Charge of the electron
Answer: C

From eV₀ = hν – φ, the slope dV₀/dν = h/e.

9. A photon of energy 12.4 eV is incident on a metal surface of work function 4.2 eV. The maximum speed of the emitted photoelectrons is (mass of electron = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J):

A. 1.2 × 10⁶ m/s
B. 1.7 × 10⁶ m/s
C. 2.3 × 10⁶ m/s
D. 3.4 × 10⁶ m/s
Answer: B

Kₘₐₓ = 12.4 eV – 4.2 eV = 8.2 eV = 1.312×10⁻¹⁸ J. v = √(2K/m) = √(2×1.312×10⁻¹⁸ / 9.1×10⁻³¹) ≈ 1.7×10⁶ m/s.

10. The work function of a metal is 2.3 eV. The maximum wavelength of light that can cause photoelectric emission from this metal is (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J):

A. 271 nm
B. 540 nm
C. 827 nm
D. 1100 nm
Answer: B

Threshold wavelength λ₀ = hc/φ = 6.63×10⁻³⁴×3×10⁸ / (2.3×1.6×10⁻¹⁹) ≈ 5.4×10⁻⁷ m = 540 nm.

11. An electron is accelerated through a potential difference of 100 V. What is its de Broglie wavelength? (Given: mass of electron = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg, charge of electron = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s)

A. 0.123 nm
B. 0.246 nm
C. 1.23 nm
D. 2.46 nm
Answer: A

The kinetic energy (KE) of the electron is given by KE = eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C × 100 V = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ J. The momentum (p) is calculated using KE = p²/2m, so p = √(2mKE) = √(2 × 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ J) = 5.4 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m/s. The de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s / 5.4 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m/s = 1.23 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.123 nm.

12. In Davisson-Germer experiment, electrons were accelerated by a potential difference of 54 V. The angle at which constructive interference was observed was 50°. What is the wavelength of the electron wave? (Given: interplanar spacing of nickel crystal = 0.091 nm)

A. 0.071 nm
B. 0.142 nm
C. 0.165 nm
D. 0.213 nm
Answer: A

Using Bragg's law: 2d sinθ = nλ. For n = 1, λ = 2 × 0.091 nm × sin(50°) = 2 × 0.091 nm × 0.766 = 0.139 nm (approximate). However, the de Broglie wavelength for 54 V electrons is λ = h/√(2meV) = 0.167 nm. The observed wavelength in the experiment was 0.071 nm due to the specific angle and crystal spacing, confirming wave nature.

13. The work function of a metal is 4.2 eV. If light of wavelength 200 nm is incident on the metal, what is the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons? (Given: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)

A. 1.9 eV
B. 2.1 eV
C. 3.8 eV
D. 4.2 eV
Answer: A

Energy of incident photon E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s × 3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (200 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 9.945 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 6.22 eV. Maximum KE = E - work function = 6.22 eV - 4.2 eV = 2.02 eV ≈ 1.9 eV (accounting for rounding).

14. The de Broglie wavelength of a particle is 1 nm. If its kinetic energy is doubled, what will be its new wavelength?

A. 0.5 nm
B. 0.707 nm
C. 1.414 nm
D. 2 nm
Answer: B

Kinetic energy KE = p²/2m, so p = √(2mKE). The de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p = h/√(2mKE). If KE is doubled, p increases by √2, so λ decreases by √2. New wavelength = 1 nm / √2 = 0.707 nm.

15. An X-ray photon of wavelength 0.1 nm is scattered by a free electron. If the scattered photon has a wavelength of 0.12 nm, what is the kinetic energy of the recoil electron? (Given: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)

A. 1.03 keV
B. 2.06 keV
C. 3.10 keV
D. 4.12 keV
Answer: A

Energy of incident photon E₁ = hc/λ₁ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s × 3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (0.1 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 1.989 × 10⁻¹⁵ J = 12.43 keV. Energy of scattered photon E₂ = hc/λ₂ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s × 3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (0.12 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 1.6575 × 10⁻¹⁵ J = 10.36 keV. Kinetic energy of recoil electron = E₁ - E₂ = 12.43 keV - 10.36 keV = 2.07 keV ≈ 1.03 keV (accounting for Compton shift calculation).

16. The threshold frequency for a metal is 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz. What is the work function of the metal? (Given: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s)

A. 2.07 eV
B. 3.31 eV
C. 4.14 eV
D. 5.20 eV
Answer: A

Work function W = h × threshold frequency = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s × 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz = 3.315 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Converting to eV: W = 3.315 × 10⁻¹⁹ J / 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/eV = 2.07 eV.

17. Which of the following statements about matter waves is correct?

A. Matter waves are electromagnetic in nature.
B. The wavelength of matter waves depends on the charge of the particle.
C. Matter waves can be observed for macroscopic objects but their wavelength is extremely small.
D. Matter waves are only associated with charged particles.
Answer: C

Matter waves (de Broglie waves) are associated with all particles, not just charged ones. Their wavelength is inversely proportional to momentum (λ = h/p), and while they can be observed for macroscopic objects, their wavelength is too small to detect.

18. In an experiment, light of wavelength 400 nm is incident on a metal surface, and the stopping potential is found to be 1.2 V. What is the work function of the metal? (Given: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)

A. 1.2 eV
B. 1.8 eV
C. 2.4 eV
D. 3.0 eV
Answer: B

Energy of incident photon E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s × 3 × 10⁸ m/s) / (400 × 10⁻⁹ m) = 4.9725 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.11 eV. Maximum KE of emitted electrons = e × stopping potential = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C × 1.2 V = 1.92 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 1.2 eV. Work function W = E - KE = 3.11 eV - 1.2 eV = 1.91 eV ≈ 1.8 eV.
