This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic of Atoms for NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. In Rutherford’s alpha-particle scattering experiment, the number of particles scattered at an angle θ is proportional to:

A. sin⁴(θ/2)
B. cosec⁴(θ/2)
C. cot⁴(θ/2)
D. sec⁴(θ/2)
Answer: B

Rutherford derived that the number of alpha particles scattered at an angle θ is proportional to cosec⁴(θ/2). This relationship arises from the inverse-square law of electrostatic repulsion between the alpha particles and the nucleus.

2. The radius of the nth Bohr orbit for a hydrogen atom is given by rₙ = a₀n², where a₀ is the Bohr radius. If an electron transitions from n = 3 to n = 1, the change in orbital radius is:

A. 8a₀
B. 9a₀
C. 7a₀
D. 6a₀
Answer: A

The radius of the 3rd orbit is r₃ = a₀(3)² = 9a₀, and the radius of the 1st orbit is r₁ = a₀(1)² = a₀. The change in radius is 9a₀ - a₀ = 8a₀.

3. Which of the following statements about the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom is incorrect?

A. The angular momentum of the electron is quantized in integral multiples of h/2π.
B. The electron can exist only in certain discrete orbits without radiating energy.
C. The energy of the electron in the nth orbit is proportional to n².
D. The model successfully explains the spectra of multi-electron atoms.
Answer: D

The Bohr model is limited to hydrogen-like atoms (single-electron systems) and fails to explain the spectra of multi-electron atoms due to electron-electron interactions and other complexities.

4. The energy required to excite an electron in a hydrogen atom from the ground state (n = 1) to the first excited state (n = 2) is:

A. 10.2 eV
B. 13.6 eV
C. 3.4 eV
D. 1.89 eV
Answer: A

The energy of the nth level in hydrogen is Eₙ = -13.6/n² eV. The energy difference between n = 1 and n = 2 is ΔE = E₂ - E₁ = (-13.6/4) - (-13.6) = 10.2 eV.

5. In the hydrogen spectrum, the Lyman series corresponds to transitions where the electron falls to the:

A. n = 1 level from higher levels
B. n = 2 level from higher levels
C. n = 3 level from higher levels
D. n = ∞ level from higher levels
Answer: A

The Lyman series arises from electronic transitions to the n = 1 (ground) level from higher energy levels (n = 2, 3, 4, ...). These transitions emit ultraviolet radiation.

6. An electron in a hydrogen atom makes a transition from an excited state to the ground state, emitting a photon of wavelength 97.2 nm. The initial excited state of the electron was:

A. n = 2
B. n = 3
C. n = 4
D. n = 5
Answer: C

Using the Rydberg formula for hydrogen: 1/λ = R(1/1² - 1/n²). For λ = 97.2 nm, solving gives 1/n² = 1 - (1/λR) = 1 - 0.1875 = 0.8125, so n² = 1/0.8125 = 1.23 ≈ 16/13, leading to n = 4.

7. The ionization energy of a hydrogen-like ion with atomic number Z is 122.4 eV. The value of Z is:

A. 2
B. 3
C. 4
D. 5
Answer: B

The ionization energy of a hydrogen-like ion is given by E = 13.6 Z² eV. Solving 122.4 = 13.6 Z² gives Z² = 9, so Z = 3.

8. Which of the following phenomena cannot be explained by the Bohr model of the atom?

A. The discrete spectral lines of hydrogen
B. The stability of the atom
C. The Zeeman effect
D. The quantization of angular momentum
Answer: C

The Zeeman effect (splitting of spectral lines in a magnetic field) requires the concept of electron spin and quantum mechanics, which are not accounted for in the Bohr model.

9. The angular momentum of an electron in the first Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom is:

A. h/2π
B. h/π
C. 2h/π
D. h
Answer: A

According to Bohr’s quantization condition, the angular momentum L of an electron in the nth orbit is L = n(h/2π). For n = 1, L = h/2π.

10. A hydrogen atom in the ground state absorbs a photon of energy 12.75 eV. The principal quantum number of the final state is:

A. 3
B. 4
C. 5
D. 6
Answer: B

The energy of the nth level is Eₙ = -13.6/n² eV. The energy absorbed is 12.75 eV, so the final energy is E_final = -13.6 + 12.75 = -0.85 eV. Solving -0.85 = -13.6/n² gives n² = 16, so n = 4.

11. In the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom, the radius of the nth orbit is given by rₙ = a₀n², where a₀ is the Bohr radius. If an electron transitions from the 3rd orbit to the 2nd orbit, the change in the radius of the orbit is:

A. 5a₀
B. 3a₀
C. a₀
D. −5a₀
Answer: D

The radius of the 3rd orbit is r₃ = a₀(3)² = 9a₀, and the radius of the 2nd orbit is r₂ = a₀(2)² = 4a₀. The change in radius is r₂ − r₃ = 4a₀ − 9a₀ = −5a₀, indicating a decrease in radius.

12. The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by Eₙ = −13.6/n² eV. What is the energy required to ionize a hydrogen atom from its first excited state?

A. 3.4 eV
B. 10.2 eV
C. 13.6 eV
D. 6.8 eV
Answer: A

The first excited state corresponds to n = 2. The energy of this state is E₂ = −13.6/2² = −3.4 eV. Ionization energy is the energy required to remove the electron from this state to infinity (E = 0), so it is 0 − (−3.4 eV) = 3.4 eV.

13. The wavelength of the first line of the Balmer series in the hydrogen spectrum is approximately 656.3 nm. What is the wavelength of the second line of the Balmer series?

A. 486.1 nm
B. 434.0 nm
C. 410.2 nm
D. 397.0 nm
Answer: A

The Balmer series corresponds to transitions to the n = 2 level. The first line (H-α) is from n = 3 to n = 2, and the second line (H-β) is from n = 4 to n = 2. Using the Rydberg formula, the wavelength for H-β is calculated as 486.1 nm.

14. In a hydrogen-like atom, the energy of the electron in the nth orbit is given by Eₙ = −(13.6 Z²)/n² eV, where Z is the atomic number. For a He⁺ ion (Z = 2), the energy required to excite the electron from the ground state to the first excited state is:

A. 40.8 eV
B. 27.2 eV
C. 13.6 eV
D. 10.2 eV
Answer: A

For He⁺, Z = 2. The ground state energy (n = 1) is E₁ = −13.6(2)²/1² = −54.4 eV. The first excited state (n = 2) energy is E₂ = −13.6(2)²/2² = −13.6 eV. The energy required for excitation is E₂ − E₁ = −13.6 − (−54.4) = 40.8 eV.

15. The de Broglie wavelength of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is related to the circumference of the orbit. Which of the following correctly represents this relationship?

A. 2πrₙ = nλ
B. 2πrₙ = λ/n
C. rₙ = nλ
D. rₙ = λ/2πn
Answer: A

According to the de Broglie hypothesis, the circumference of the nth orbit (2πrₙ) must be an integral multiple of the electron's wavelength (λ), i.e., 2πrₙ = nλ. This is a key postulate of the Bohr model.

16. The Rydberg constant (R) for hydrogen is approximately 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹. The wavelength of the photon emitted during a transition from n = 4 to n = 2 in the hydrogen atom is:

A. 486.1 nm
B. 656.3 nm
C. 434.0 nm
D. 410.2 nm
Answer: A

Using the Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R(1/2² − 1/4²) = R(1/4 − 1/16) = R(3/16). Thus, λ = 16/(3R) = 16/(3 × 1.097 × 10⁷) ≈ 4.861 × 10⁻⁷ m = 486.1 nm.

17. In the Bohr model, the velocity of the electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by vₙ = (e²)/(2ε₀nh). What is the ratio of the velocity of the electron in the first orbit to that in the second orbit?

A. 2:1
B. 1:2
C. 4:1
D. 1:4
Answer: A

The velocity in the nth orbit is inversely proportional to n. Thus, v₁/v₂ = 2/1 = 2:1.

18. The spectral lines of the Lyman series in the hydrogen spectrum are observed in which region of the electromagnetic spectrum?

A. Ultraviolet
B. Visible
C. Infrared
D. Microwave
Answer: A

The Lyman series corresponds to transitions to the n = 1 level, which involve high-energy photons in the ultraviolet region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

19. An electron in a hydrogen atom transitions from a higher energy level to the n = 3 level, emitting a photon of wavelength 1282 nm. To which energy level did the electron transition from?

A. n = 4
B. n = 5
C. n = 6
D. n = 7
Answer: B

Using the Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R(1/3² − 1/n²). Given λ = 1282 nm = 1.282 × 10⁻⁶ m and R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹, solving for n gives n = 5.

20. An electron in a hydrogen atom transitions from the n = 4 energy level to the n = 2 energy level. Given that the Rydberg constant (R) is 1.097 × 10^7 m^-1, the wavelength of the emitted photon is closest to:

A. 486 nm
B. 656 nm
C. 434 nm
D. 122 nm
Answer: A

The wavelength (λ) of the emitted photon during an electronic transition in a hydrogen atom is given by the Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²), where n₁ is the lower energy level and n₂ is the higher energy level. Here, n₁ = 2 and n₂ = 4. Substituting the values: 1/λ = 1.097 × 10^7 (1/2² - 1/4²) = 1.097 × 10^7 (1/4 - 1/16) = 1.097 × 10^7 (3/16). Thus, 1/λ = 2.056875 × 10^6 m^-1, so λ = 1 / (2.056875 × 10^6) ≈ 4.86 × 10^-7 m = 486 nm. This corresponds to the Balmer series, where transitions to n = 2 emit visible light.
