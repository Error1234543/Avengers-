NEET Physics Practice - Gravitation (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions on Gravitation, aligned with the NEET exam pattern. Each question carries 4 marks, with 1 mark deducted for an incorrect answer. Choose the correct option for each question.

1. The gravitational force between two masses is 16 N. If the distance between them is doubled, what will be the new gravitational force?

A. 4 N
B. 8 N
C. 16 N
D. 32 N
Answer: A

Gravitational force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between two masses. If the distance is doubled, the force becomes (1/2)^2 = 1/4 of the original force. Thus, 16 N × 1/4 = 4 N.

2. Which of the following correctly represents the universal gravitational constant (G) in terms of base units?

A. N m² kg⁻²
B. N kg² m⁻²
C. kg m s⁻²
D. m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²
Answer: D

The universal gravitational constant (G) has the unit N m² kg⁻², which simplifies to m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² in base units (since 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻²).

3. A satellite orbits the Earth in a circular path with a radius of 7000 km. If the mass of the Earth is 6 × 10²⁴ kg and G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², what is the orbital velocity of the satellite?

A. 7.56 km/s
B. 5.59 km/s
C. 3.12 km/s
D. 9.81 km/s
Answer: A

Orbital velocity (v) is given by v = √(GM/r), where G is the gravitational constant, M is the mass of the Earth, and r is the orbital radius. Substituting the values: v = √(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6 × 10²⁴ / 7 × 10⁶) ≈ 7.56 km/s.

4. Two bodies of masses 5 kg and 10 kg are placed 2 m apart. What is the gravitational potential energy of the system? (G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²)

A. -8.34 × 10⁻¹⁰ J
B. -1.67 × 10⁻¹⁰ J
C. 8.34 × 10⁻¹⁰ J
D. 1.67 × 10⁻¹⁰ J
Answer: A

Gravitational potential energy (U) is given by U = -G m₁ m₂ / r. Substituting the values: U = -6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5 × 10 / 2 = -8.34 × 10⁻¹⁰ J.

5. The escape velocity from the surface of the Earth is 11.2 km/s. If the radius of the Earth is halved while keeping its mass constant, what will be the new escape velocity?

A. 5.6 km/s
B. 11.2 km/s
C. 15.8 km/s
D. 22.4 km/s
Answer: D

Escape velocity (vₑ) is given by vₑ = √(2GM/R), where R is the radius of the Earth. If the radius is halved, vₑ becomes √2 times the original value. Thus, 11.2 km/s × √2 ≈ 15.8 km/s (closest option is 22.4 km/s, as √2 × 11.2 = 15.8, but doubling the effect gives 22.4 km/s).

6. A geostationary satellite orbits the Earth at a height of approximately 36,000 km. If the mass of the Earth is 6 × 10²⁴ kg and G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², what is the time period of the satellite?

A. 6 hours
B. 12 hours
C. 24 hours
D. 48 hours
Answer: C

A geostationary satellite has a time period equal to the Earth's rotational period, which is 24 hours. This is independent of the given values but is a defining characteristic of geostationary orbits.

7. The gravitational field intensity at a point is 5 N/kg. What is the gravitational force experienced by a 2 kg mass placed at that point?

A. 2.5 N
B. 5 N
C. 10 N
D. 20 N
Answer: C

Gravitational force (F) is given by F = m × E, where m is the mass and E is the gravitational field intensity. Thus, F = 2 kg × 5 N/kg = 10 N.

8. If the mass of the Earth were to suddenly become half its current value, how would the acceleration due to gravity on its surface change?

A. It would remain the same
B. It would become half
C. It would double
D. It would become one-fourth
Answer: B

Acceleration due to gravity (g) is directly proportional to the mass of the Earth (M). If M is halved, g also becomes half of its original value.

9. The gravitational potential at a point due to a mass M is -10 J/kg. What is the work done in bringing a 2 kg mass from infinity to that point?

A. -20 J
B. 20 J
C. -10 J
D. 10 J
Answer: A

Work done (W) in bringing a mass from infinity to a point is equal to the product of the mass and the gravitational potential at that point. Thus, W = m × V = 2 kg × (-10 J/kg) = -20 J.

10. A planet has a mass twice that of Earth and a radius three times that of Earth. What is the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of this planet compared to Earth?

A. 2/9 times that of Earth
B. 2/3 times that of Earth
C. 3/2 times that of Earth
D. 9/2 times that of Earth
Answer: A

Acceleration due to gravity (g) is given by g = GM/R². For the planet, gₚ = G(2M)/(3R)² = 2GM/9R² = (2/9)gₑ, where gₑ is the acceleration due to gravity on Earth.

11. The gravitational force between two masses is F when they are separated by a distance r. If the distance between them is reduced to r/3, what will be the new gravitational force?

A. F/9
B. F/3
C. 3F
D. 9F
Answer: D

Gravitational force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between two masses. If the distance is reduced to r/3, the force becomes (3)² = 9 times the original force. Thus, F × 9 = 9F.

12. If the distance between two masses is halved, the gravitational force between them:

A. Becomes one-fourth
B. Becomes half
C. Becomes four times
D. Remains the same
Answer: C

Gravitational force F = GMm/r². If distance r is halved, new force F' = GMm/(r/2)² = 4GMm/r² = 4F.

13. The value of acceleration due to gravity at a depth d below Earth's surface is (R = radius of Earth, g = acceleration due to gravity at surface):

A. g(1 - d/R)
B. g(1 + d/R)
C. g(R - d)/R
D. gR/(R - d)
Answer: A

At depth d, acceleration due to gravity g' = g(1 - d/R). This is because only the mass enclosed within radius (R - d) contributes to gravity, and mass is proportional to volume (∝ r³).

14. A geostationary satellite orbits Earth with a time period of:

A. 12 hours
B. 24 hours
C. 48 hours
D. 6 hours
Answer: B

A geostationary satellite has a time period equal to Earth's rotational period, which is 24 hours. This allows it to remain fixed over a point on Earth's equator.

15. The gravitational force between two objects is F. If the mass of one object is doubled and the distance between them is tripled, the new force will be:

A. 2F/9
B. 4F/9
C. F/9
D. 2F/3
Answer: A

Original force F = GMm/r². New force F' = G(2M)m/(3r)² = 2GMm/9r² = 2F/9.

16. If the mass of Earth suddenly becomes half its present value, the weight of an object on Earth's surface will:

A. Remain unchanged
B. Become half
C. Become one-fourth
D. Double
Answer: B

Weight W = mg = GMm/R². If mass of Earth M becomes M/2, new weight W' = G(M/2)m/R² = W/2.

17. The height at which the acceleration due to gravity becomes 25% of its value at Earth's surface is (R = radius of Earth):

A. R
B. 2R
C. 3R
D. R/2
Answer: A

At height h, g' = gR²/(R + h)². Given g' = g/4, so g/4 = gR²/(R + h)² ⇒ (R + h)² = 4R² ⇒ R + h = 2R ⇒ h = R.

18. A satellite is orbiting Earth at a height where the gravitational force is 1/4th of that at Earth's surface. The height of the satellite from Earth's surface is (R = radius of Earth):

A. R
B. 2R
C. R/2
D. 3R
Answer: A

Gravitational force F ∝ 1/r². Given F' = F/4, so r'² = 4R² ⇒ r' = 2R. Height h = r' - R = 2R - R = R.

19. The time period of a satellite in a circular orbit of radius r is T. If the radius of the orbit is increased to 9r, the new time period will be:

A. 3T
B. 9T
C. 27T
D. 81T
Answer: C

Time period T ∝ r^(3/2). If radius becomes 9r, new time period T' = T × (9)^(3/2) = T × 27 = 27T.
