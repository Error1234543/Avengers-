NEET Physics Practice: Magnetism and Matter (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Magnetism and Matter for NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. A bar magnet of magnetic moment M is cut into two equal parts along its length. The magnetic moment of each part will be:

A. M/2
B. M
C. 2M
D. M/4
Answer: A

When a bar magnet is cut into two equal parts along its length, the pole strength remains the same, but the length of each part becomes half. Magnetic moment (M) is given by M = m × l, where m is the pole strength and l is the length. Since the length is halved, the magnetic moment of each part becomes M/2.

2. The magnetic susceptibility of a paramagnetic material is:

A. Positive and small
B. Negative and small
C. Positive and large
D. Negative and large
Answer: A

Paramagnetic materials have a small positive magnetic susceptibility because they are weakly attracted by a magnetic field. This is due to the presence of unpaired electrons in the material.

3. A magnetic needle is placed in a uniform magnetic field. The torque experienced by the needle is maximum when the angle between the needle and the field is:

A. 0°
B. 30°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: D

The torque (τ) experienced by a magnetic needle in a magnetic field is given by τ = MB sinθ, where M is the magnetic moment, B is the magnetic field, and θ is the angle between the needle and the field. The torque is maximum when sinθ = 1, which occurs at θ = 90°.

4. The relative permeability of a ferromagnetic material is:

A. Less than 1
B. Equal to 1
C. Greater than 1
D. Much greater than 1
Answer: D

Ferromagnetic materials have a very high relative permeability, much greater than 1, because they are strongly attracted by magnetic fields and can be permanently magnetized.

5. Which of the following materials is NOT ferromagnetic?

A. Iron
B. Nickel
C. Cobalt
D. Aluminium
Answer: D

Aluminium is a paramagnetic material, not ferromagnetic. Ferromagnetic materials include iron, nickel, and cobalt, which exhibit strong magnetic properties.

6. The magnetic field inside a long solenoid carrying current I is given by B = μ₀nI, where n is the number of turns per unit length. If the current is doubled and the number of turns per unit length is halved, the new magnetic field will be:

A. B/2
B. B
C. 2B
D. 4B
Answer: B

The magnetic field inside a solenoid is given by B = μ₀nI. If the current (I) is doubled and the number of turns per unit length (n) is halved, the new magnetic field becomes B' = μ₀(n/2)(2I) = μ₀nI = B. Thus, the magnetic field remains unchanged.

7. The hysteresis loop of a ferromagnetic material represents the relationship between:

A. Magnetic field and magnetic susceptibility
B. Magnetic field and magnetization
C. Magnetic moment and temperature
D. Magnetic field and temperature
Answer: B

The hysteresis loop of a ferromagnetic material shows the relationship between the magnetic field (H) applied to the material and its magnetization (M). It illustrates how the magnetization lags behind the applied field, leading to energy loss in the form of heat.

8. A magnetic dipole of moment M is placed in a magnetic field of strength B. The work done in rotating the dipole from an angle θ₁ to θ₂ is:

A. MB(cosθ₁ - cosθ₂)
B. MB(sinθ₁ - sinθ₂)
C. MB(cosθ₂ - cosθ₁)
D. MB(sinθ₂ - sinθ₁)
Answer: A

The work done (W) in rotating a magnetic dipole from angle θ₁ to θ₂ in a magnetic field is given by W = MB(cosθ₁ - cosθ₂). This is derived from the potential energy of the dipole in the magnetic field, U = -MB cosθ.

9. The magnetic field lines inside a bar magnet:

A. Do not exist
B. Are directed from north to south pole
C. Are directed from south to north pole
D. Form closed loops outside the magnet only
Answer: C

Magnetic field lines inside a bar magnet are directed from the south pole to the north pole. Outside the magnet, they are directed from the north pole to the south pole, forming closed loops.

10. The Curie temperature of a ferromagnetic material is the temperature at which:

A. The material becomes paramagnetic
B. The material loses all its magnetic properties
C. The material becomes diamagnetic
D. The material's magnetization becomes zero
Answer: A

The Curie temperature is the temperature at which a ferromagnetic material loses its ferromagnetic properties and becomes paramagnetic. Above this temperature, the material cannot retain permanent magnetization.

11. A circular loop of radius R carrying current I is placed in a uniform magnetic field B. The torque on the loop is maximum when the plane of the loop is:

A. Parallel to the magnetic field
B. Perpendicular to the magnetic field
C. At 45° to the magnetic field
D. At 60° to the magnetic field
Answer: A

The torque (τ) on a current loop in a magnetic field is given by τ = NIAB sinθ, where N is the number of turns, I is the current, A is the area of the loop, B is the magnetic field, and θ is the angle between the normal to the loop and the magnetic field. The torque is maximum when sinθ = 1, i.e., θ = 90°, which means the plane of the loop is parallel to the magnetic field.

12. The magnetic susceptibility of a paramagnetic substance at 27°C is 2.0 × 10⁻⁵. Its value at 327°C will be approximately:

A. 1.0 × 10⁻⁵
B. 2.0 × 10⁻⁵
C. 4.0 × 10⁻⁵
D. 0.5 × 10⁻⁵
Answer: A

Magnetic susceptibility (χ) of a paramagnetic substance is inversely proportional to absolute temperature (χ ∝ 1/T). Temperature increases from 300 K (27°C) to 600 K (327°C), so χ decreases by half to 1.0 × 10⁻⁵.

13. Which of the following materials is most suitable for making the core of a transformer?

A. Copper
B. Soft iron
C. Steel
D. Aluminium
Answer: B

Soft iron has high magnetic permeability and low hysteresis loss, making it ideal for transformer cores. It can be easily magnetized and demagnetized, reducing energy loss.

14. A magnetic needle suspended freely in a uniform magnetic field experiences:

A. a torque but no net force
B. a net force but no torque
C. both a net force and a torque
D. neither a net force nor a torque
Answer: A

In a uniform magnetic field, equal and opposite forces act on the poles of the needle, resulting in no net force. However, these forces are not collinear, producing a torque that aligns the needle with the field.

15. The angle of dip at a place where the horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field is equal to the vertical component is:

A. 30°
B. 45°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: B

The angle of dip (δ) is given by tan δ = (vertical component / horizontal component). If both components are equal, tan δ = 1, so δ = 45°.

16. A solenoid of length 50 cm and radius 2 cm has 500 turns. If a current of 5 A flows through it, the magnetic field at the center of the solenoid is (μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T m/A):

A. 2π × 10⁻³ T
B. 4π × 10⁻³ T
C. 6π × 10⁻³ T
D. 8π × 10⁻³ T
Answer: A

The magnetic field inside a solenoid is given by B = μ₀nI, where n = number of turns per unit length. Here, n = 500 / 0.5 = 1000 turns/m. Thus, B = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(1000)(5) = 2π × 10⁻³ T.

17. The hysteresis loop of a ferromagnetic material represents:

A. the variation of magnetic field with temperature
B. the variation of magnetization with magnetic field
C. the variation of magnetic susceptibility with temperature
D. the variation of magnetic moment with current
Answer: B

A hysteresis loop plots the magnetization (M) of a ferromagnetic material against the applied magnetic field (H). It shows how the material retains magnetization even after the field is removed, indicating hysteresis.

18. At the magnetic equator, the angle of dip is:

A. 0°
B. 30°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: A

At the magnetic equator, the Earth's magnetic field is parallel to the surface, so the angle of dip (the angle between the field and the horizontal) is 0°.

19. The magnetic field inside a toroidal solenoid of radius R carrying current I is independent of:

A. the number of turns
B. the current I
C. the radius R
D. the permeability of the core
Answer: C

The magnetic field inside a toroidal solenoid is given by B = (μ₀NI)/(2πR), where N is the number of turns. The field depends on the number of turns, current, and permeability but is inversely proportional to the radius R. However, for a given toroid, the field is uniform and does not vary with position inside the toroid, making it independent of the radius in terms of spatial variation.

20. The magnetic field due to a small bar magnet at a point on its axial line at a distance d from its center is B. The magnetic field at a point on its equatorial line at the same distance d will be:

A. B/2
B. B
C. 2B
D. B/4
Answer: A

The magnetic field on the axial line of a bar magnet is B = (μ₀/4π)(2M/d³), while on the equatorial line, it is B' = (μ₀/4π)(M/d³). Thus, B' = B/2.

21. A bar magnet of magnetic moment **M** and length **2l** is bent into a semicircular arc. The new magnetic moment of the system is:

A. M
B. (2M)/π
C. (Mπ)/2
D. (M)/2
Answer: B

When the bar magnet is bent into a semicircular arc, the effective length of the magnet changes. The original magnetic moment **M** is given by **M = m × 2l**, where **m** is the pole strength and **2l** is the length of the magnet. After bending, the distance between the poles becomes the diameter of the semicircle, which is **2r**, where **r** is the radius of the semicircle. The circumference of the semicircle is **πr = 2l**, so **r = (2l)/π**. The new magnetic moment is **M' = m × 2r = m × (4l)/π = (2M)/π**, since **M = m × 2l**. Thus, the new magnetic moment is **(2M)/π**.
