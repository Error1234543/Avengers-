This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Wave Optics for NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. In Young's double-slit experiment, the separation between the slits is doubled while the distance between the slits and the screen remains unchanged. What happens to the fringe width?

A. It becomes half
B. It becomes double
C. It becomes one-fourth
D. It remains unchanged
Answer: A

The fringe width (β) in Young's double-slit experiment is given by β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, D is the distance between the slits and the screen, and d is the separation between the slits. If d is doubled, β becomes half, as β is inversely proportional to d.

2. Which of the following phenomena is NOT explained by the wave theory of light?

A. Interference
B. Diffraction
C. Polarization
D. Photoelectric effect
Answer: D

The photoelectric effect is explained by the particle nature of light (photons), not by the wave theory. Interference, diffraction, and polarization are phenomena that support the wave nature of light.

3. A light wave of wavelength 600 nm is incident on a thin film of refractive index 1.5. What is the minimum thickness of the film for constructive interference in reflected light, assuming normal incidence?

A. 100 nm
B. 150 nm
C. 200 nm
D. 300 nm
Answer: A

For constructive interference in reflected light from a thin film, the condition is 2μt = (m + 1/2)λ, where μ is the refractive index, t is the thickness, λ is the wavelength in vacuum, and m is an integer (m = 0 for minimum thickness). Solving for t: t = (m + 1/2)λ / (2μ) = (0 + 1/2) * 600 nm / (2 * 1.5) = 100 nm.

4. The phenomenon of diffraction is more pronounced when the size of the obstacle or aperture is:

A. Much larger than the wavelength of light
B. Comparable to the wavelength of light
C. Much smaller than the wavelength of light
D. Independent of the wavelength of light
Answer: B

Diffraction is most pronounced when the size of the obstacle or aperture is comparable to the wavelength of light. This is because the wave bends around the edges of the obstacle or aperture, and the effect is significant when their sizes are similar.

5. In a single-slit diffraction experiment, the width of the central maximum is 4 mm on a screen placed 2 m away from the slit. If the wavelength of light used is 500 nm, what is the width of the slit?

A. 0.25 mm
B. 0.5 mm
C. 1 mm
D. 2 mm
Answer: B

The width of the central maximum (W) in single-slit diffraction is given by W = 2λD/a, where λ is the wavelength, D is the distance from the slit to the screen, and a is the width of the slit. Rearranging for a: a = 2λD/W = 2 * 500 nm * 2 m / 4 mm = 0.5 mm.

6. Which of the following statements about polarization is correct?

A. Polarization occurs only in transverse waves
B. Polarization occurs only in longitudinal waves
C. Polarization can occur in both transverse and longitudinal waves
D. Polarization is independent of the nature of the wave
Answer: A

Polarization is a phenomenon unique to transverse waves, where the oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. Longitudinal waves, such as sound waves, cannot be polarized because their oscillations are parallel to the direction of propagation.

7. A beam of light is incident on a polarizer and then passes through an analyzer. If the angle between the polarizer and analyzer is 60°, what fraction of the intensity of the incident unpolarized light is transmitted through the analyzer?

A. 1/2
B. 1/4
C. 1/8
D. 1/16
Answer: C

When unpolarized light passes through a polarizer, its intensity is reduced by half. The intensity of light transmitted through the analyzer is given by Malus' law: I = I₀ cos²θ, where I₀ is the intensity after the polarizer and θ is the angle between the polarizer and analyzer. Here, I₀ = I_incident / 2, and θ = 60°. Thus, I = (I_incident / 2) * cos²(60°) = (I_incident / 2) * (1/4) = I_incident / 8.

8. The resolving power of a microscope is increased by:

A. Increasing the wavelength of light used
B. Decreasing the wavelength of light used
C. Increasing the size of the objective lens
D. Decreasing the numerical aperture of the objective lens
Answer: B

The resolving power of a microscope is given by R = 1.22λ / (2NA), where λ is the wavelength of light and NA is the numerical aperture. Resolving power increases (i.e., the microscope can distinguish finer details) when the wavelength of light is decreased or the numerical aperture is increased.

9. A thin film of oil (refractive index 1.4) floats on water (refractive index 1.33). Light of wavelength 600 nm in air is incident normally on the film. What is the minimum thickness of the oil film for destructive interference in reflected light?

A. 107 nm
B. 214 nm
C. 321 nm
D. 428 nm
Answer: A

For destructive interference in reflected light from a thin film, the condition is 2μt = mλ, where μ is the refractive index of the film, t is the thickness, λ is the wavelength in vacuum, and m is an integer (m = 1 for minimum thickness). Solving for t: t = mλ / (2μ) = 1 * 600 nm / (2 * 1.4) ≈ 214 nm. However, since the film is on water, there is a phase change of π at the oil-water interface, so the condition becomes 2μt = (m + 1/2)λ. For m = 0: t = (0 + 1/2) * 600 nm / (2 * 1.4) ≈ 107 nm.

10. Which of the following is NOT a condition for sustained interference of light?

A. The sources must be coherent
B. The sources must be monochromatic
C. The sources must be of equal intensity
D. The sources must be close to each other
Answer: C

For sustained interference of light, the sources must be coherent (constant phase difference), monochromatic (same wavelength), and close to each other (to produce observable fringes). Equal intensity is not a necessary condition, though it results in better contrast of fringes.

11. In a diffraction grating experiment, the first-order maximum is observed at an angle of 30° for light of wavelength 500 nm. What is the number of lines per cm on the grating?

A. 5000
B. 10000
C. 15000
D. 20000000000
Answer: B

The grating equation is d sinθ = mλ, where d is the grating spacing, θ is the angle of diffraction, m is the order, and λ is the wavelength. For the first-order maximum (m = 1), d sin(30°) = 500 nm. Thus, d = 500 nm / sin(30°) = 500 nm / 0.5 = 1000 nm = 10⁻⁶ m. The number of lines per cm is 1/d = 1 / 10⁻⁶ m = 10⁶ lines/m = 10000 lines/cm.

12. A light wave traveling in air enters a glass slab of refractive index 1.5. If the wavelength of light in air is 600 nm, what is its wavelength in the glass slab?

A. 300
B. 400 nm
C. 600 nm
D. 900 nm
Answer: B

The wavelength of light in a medium is given by λ_medium = λ_air / μ, where μ is the refractive index of the medium. Here, λ_medium = 600 nm / 1.5 = 400 nm.

13. A thin film of refractive index 1.5 and thickness 500 nm is illuminated by white light. For which wavelength in the visible spectrum will the film appear dark due to destructive interference in reflected light, assuming normal incidence?

A. 400 nm
B. 500 nm
C. 600 nm
D. 700 nm
Answer: C

For destructive interference in reflected light, the condition is 2μt = mλ, where μ is the refractive index, t is the thickness, λ is the wavelength, and m is an integer. For m = 1, 2 * 1.5 * 500 nm = λ, so λ = 1500 nm (not visible). For m = 2, λ = 750 nm (not visible). For m = 3, λ = 500 nm (not matching options). For m = 2.5 (half-integer), 2μt = (m + 0.5)λ, so λ = 600 nm, which is in the visible spectrum.

14. The angular width of the central maximum in a single-slit diffraction pattern is 60°. If the wavelength of light used is 600 nm, what is the width of the slit?

A. 600 nm
B. 1200 nm
C. 1800 nm
D. 2400 nm
Answer: B

The angular width of the central maximum in single-slit diffraction is given by 2θ = 2λ/a, where θ is the angle to the first minimum, λ is the wavelength, and a is the slit width. Given 2θ = 60°, θ = 30°. Thus, sin(30°) = λ/a, so a = λ / sin(30°) = 600 nm / 0.5 = 1200 nm.

15. In a Young's double-slit experiment, the slits are separated by 0.5 mm, and the screen is placed 1 m away. If the distance between the second bright fringe and the central bright fringe is 2.4 mm, what is the wavelength of the light used?

A. 400 nm
B. 500 nm
C. 600 nm
D. 700 nm
Answer: C

The position of the nth bright fringe in Young's double-slit experiment is given by y = nλD/d, where n is the fringe order, λ is the wavelength, D is the distance to the screen, and d is the slit separation. For n = 2, y = 2.4 mm = 2.4 × 10⁻³ m, D = 1 m, d = 0.5 × 10⁻³ m. Thus, λ = y d / (n D) = (2.4 × 10⁻³ * 0.5 × 10⁻³) / (2 * 1) = 600 × 10⁻⁹ m = 600 nm.

16. A light wave of wavelength 500 nm is incident on a thin film of refractive index 1.3. If the film appears bright in reflected light, which of the following could be the minimum thickness of the film?

A. 96 nm
B. 192 nm
C. 288 nm
D. 384 nm
Answer: A

For constructive interference in reflected light, the condition is 2μt = (m + 0.5)λ, where μ is the refractive index, t is the thickness, λ is the wavelength, and m is an integer. For minimum thickness, m = 0: 2 * 1.3 * t = 0.5 * 500 nm, so t = (0.5 * 500) / (2 * 1.3) ≈ 96 nm.

17. The resolving power of a microscope is inversely proportional to the wavelength of light used. If the wavelength of light is halved, how does the resolving power change?

A. Doubles
B. Halves
C. Remains the same
D. Quadruples
Answer: A

Resolving power (R) is given by R ∝ 1/λ, where λ is the wavelength. If λ is halved, R becomes 1/(λ/2) = 2/λ, so the resolving power doubles.

18. In a single-slit diffraction experiment, the first minimum is observed at an angle of 30° for light of wavelength 500 nm. What is the width of the slit?

A. 500 nm
B. 1000 nm
C. 1500 nm
D. 2000 nm
Answer: B

For the first minimum in single-slit diffraction, a sinθ = λ, where a is the slit width, θ is the angle, and λ is the wavelength. Given θ = 30° and λ = 500 nm, a = λ / sinθ = 500 nm / 0.5 = 1000 nm.

19. A beam of light of wavelength 600 nm is incident on a grating with 5000 lines/cm. What is the angle of the first-order maximum?

A. 17.46°
B. 30.00°
C. 45.00°
D. 60.00°
Answer: A

The grating equation is d sinθ = nλ, where d is the grating spacing, θ is the angle, n is the order, and λ is the wavelength. Here, d = 1/5000 cm = 2 × 10⁻⁶ m, n = 1, λ = 600 × 10⁻⁹ m. Thus, sinθ = nλ / d = (1 * 600 × 10⁻⁹) / (2 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.3, so θ = sin⁻¹(0.3) ≈ 17.46°.

20. In Young's double-slit experiment, the intensity of the central maximum is I₀. If one of the slits is covered, what is the new intensity at the center of the screen?

A. I₀/4
B. I₀/2
C. I₀
D. 2I₀
Answer: A

When both slits are open, the intensity at the central maximum is I₀ = 4I, where I is the intensity due to one slit. If one slit is covered, the intensity becomes I = I₀/4.

21. A thin film of soap solution (refractive index 1.33) appears dark in reflected light when its thickness is 100 nm. What is the wavelength of light in the visible spectrum that satisfies this condition?

A. 400 nm
B. 532 nm
C. 600 nm
D. 700 nm
Answer: B

For destructive interference in reflected light, 2μt = mλ, where μ is the refractive index, t is the thickness, λ is the wavelength, and m is an integer. For m = 1, 2 * 1.33 * 100 nm = λ, so λ = 266 nm (not visible). For m = 2, λ = 532 nm, which is in the visible spectrum.

22. In a Young's double-slit experiment, the distance between the slits is 0.2 mm, and the screen is placed 1.5 m away. If the wavelength of light used is 500 nm, what is the fringe width?

A. 2.25 mm
B. 3.75 mm
C. 4.50 mm
D. 6.00 mm
Answer: B

The fringe width (β) in Young's double-slit experiment is given by β = λD/d, where λ is the wavelength, D is the distance to the screen, and d is the slit separation. Here, β = (500 × 10⁻⁹ m * 1.5 m) / (0.2 × 10⁻³ m) = 3.75 × 10⁻³ m = 3.75 mm.
