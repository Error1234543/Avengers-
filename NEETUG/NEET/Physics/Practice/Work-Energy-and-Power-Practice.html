NEET Physics Practice - Work, Energy and Power (Part 1)

NEETEnglishPractice

Answer all questions. Each question carries 4 marks. Negative marking: -1 for each incorrect answer. Select the single best answer.

1. A block of mass 2 kg slides down a smooth incline of height 5 m. If the block starts from rest, its kinetic energy at the bottom is (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 50 J
B. 100 J
C. 25 J
D. 75 J
Answer: B

By conservation of mechanical energy, ΔK = −ΔU = m g h = 2×10×5 = 100 J.

2. A spring of force constant k = 200 N/m is compressed by 0.1 m. The potential energy stored in the spring is

A. 1 J
B. 2 J
C. 0.5 J
D. 4 J
Answer: A

U = ½ k x² = ½×200×(0.1)² = 1 J.

3. A car of mass 1000 kg moving at 20 m/s is brought to rest by a constant braking force of 5000 N. The distance travelled during braking is

A. 20 m
B. 40 m
C. 60 m
D. 80 m
Answer: B

Work done by braking force = change in kinetic energy ⇒ 5000×d = ½×1000×20² ⇒ d = 40 m.

4. A body of mass 5 kg is raised vertically through 2 m. If g = 9.8 m/s², the work done against gravity is

A. 49 J
B. 98 J
C. 19.6 J
D. 24.5 J
Answer: B

W = m g h = 5×9.8×2 = 98 J.

5. A machine lifts a 50 kg load to a height of 10 m in 5 s. If the machine is 80% efficient, the power input to the machine is (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 1000 W
B. 1250 W
C. 1500 W
D. 2000 W
Answer: B

Useful power output = (50×10×10)/5 = 1000 W. Input power = 1000/0.8 = 1250 W.

6. A particle moves along the x-axis under a conservative force. If its potential energy at x = 2 m is 10 J and at x = 5 m is 25 J, the work done by the force as the particle moves from x = 2 m to x = 5 m is

A. +15 J
B. −15 J
C. +35 J
D. −35 J
Answer: B

W = −ΔU = U_initial − U_final = 10 J − 25 J = −15 J.

7. Two bodies of masses m and 4m have equal kinetic energies. The ratio of their momenta is

A. 1 : 2
B. 1 : 4
C. 2 : 1
D. 4 : 1
Answer: A

K = p²/(2m) ⇒ p ∝ √m ⇒ p₁/p₂ = √(m/4m) = 1/2.

8. A bullet of mass 0.02 kg moving at 400 m/s penetrates a fixed wooden block to a depth of 0.1 m. The average retarding force exerted by the block is

A. 1600 N
B. 3200 N
C. 800 N
D. 400 N
Answer: A

Work done = change in kinetic energy ⇒ F×0.1 = ½×0.02×400² ⇒ F = 1600 N.

9. A motor pumps water from a well 20 m deep at the rate of 5 kg/s. If the efficiency of the motor is 60%, the electrical power consumed is (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 1.67 kW
B. 2.5 kW
C. 3.33 kW
D. 5 kW
Answer: A

Useful power = 5×10×20 = 1000 W. Input power = 1000/0.6 ≈ 1667 W ≈ 1.67 kW.

10. A block of mass 1 kg is attached to a spring of force constant 100 N/m. If the block is displaced 0.2 m from its equilibrium position and released, its maximum speed is

A. 1 m/s
B. 2 m/s
C. 4 m/s
D. 0.5 m/s
Answer: B

Maximum speed occurs when all energy is kinetic: ½ m v_max² = ½ k A² ⇒ v_max = A√(k/m) = 0.2√(100/1) = 2 m/s.

11. A force of 10 N acts on a body at an angle of 60° to the direction of motion, displacing it by 5 m. The work done by the force is:

A. 25 J
B. 50 J
C. 25√3 J
D. 100 J
Answer: A

Work done (W) is given by W = F · s · cosθ, where F is the force, s is the displacement, and θ is the angle between force and displacement. Here, F = 10 N, s = 5 m, and θ = 60°. Thus, W = 10 × 5 × cos(60°) = 50 × 0.5 = 25 J.

12. A block of mass 2 kg slides down a frictionless inclined plane of height 5 m. The kinetic energy of the block at the bottom of the plane is: (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 50 J
B. 100 J
C. 25 J
D. 200 J
Answer: B

Using the principle of conservation of energy, the potential energy at the top (mgh) is converted to kinetic energy at the bottom (½mv²). Here, m = 2 kg, g = 10 m/s², and h = 5 m. Thus, KE = mgh = 2 × 10 × 5 = 100 J.

13. A machine does 200 J of work in 10 seconds. The power output of the machine is:

A. 20 W
B. 2 W
C. 2000 W
D. 0.2 W
Answer: A

Power (P) is the rate of doing work, given by P = Work / Time. Here, Work = 200 J and Time = 10 s. Thus, P = 200 / 10 = 20 W.

14. A body of mass 5 kg is moving with a velocity of 4 m/s. Its kinetic energy is:

A. 20 J
B. 40 J
C. 80 J
D. 10 J
Answer: B

Kinetic energy (KE) is given by KE = ½mv², where m is the mass and v is the velocity. Here, m = 5 kg and v = 4 m/s. Thus, KE = ½ × 5 × (4)² = 40 J.

15. A body is lifted vertically to a height h. The work done against gravity is:

A. mgh
B. ½mgh
C. mgh²
D. mg/h
Answer: A

Work done against gravity is given by W = mgh, where m is the mass, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and h is the height. This is derived from the change in potential energy.

16. A bullet of mass 0.02 kg is fired with a velocity of 500 m/s. The kinetic energy of the bullet is:

A. 2500 J
B. 5000 J
C. 1000 J
D. 250 J
Answer: A

Kinetic energy (KE) is given by KE = ½mv². Here, m = 0.02 kg and v = 500 m/s. Thus, KE = ½ × 0.02 × (500)² = 2500 J.

17. A force-displacement graph for a body is a straight line passing through the origin. The work done by the force is equal to:

A. Area under the graph
B. Slope of the graph
C. Product of force and displacement
D. Both A and C
Answer: D

For a force-displacement graph, the work done is equal to the area under the graph. Since the graph is a straight line passing through the origin, the area under the graph is also equal to the product of force and displacement (W = F · s).

18. A body of mass 1 kg is dropped from a height of 10 m. Its kinetic energy just before hitting the ground is: (Take g = 10 m/s², ignore air resistance)

A. 100 J
B. 50 J
C. 200 J
D. 10 J
Answer: A

Using the principle of conservation of energy, the potential energy at the height (mgh) is converted to kinetic energy just before hitting the ground. Here, m = 1 kg, g = 10 m/s², and h = 10 m. Thus, KE = mgh = 1 × 10 × 10 = 100 J.

19. The work done by a conservative force on a body moving in a closed loop is:

A. Zero
B. Equal to the change in kinetic energy
C. Equal to the change in potential energy
D. Dependent on the path taken
Answer: A

A conservative force is one where the work done in moving a body in a closed loop is zero. This is because the work done depends only on the initial and final positions, not on the path taken.

20. A block of mass 2 kg is placed on a rough horizontal surface with a coefficient of kinetic friction μₖ = 0.2. A horizontal force of 10 N is applied to the block, causing it to move a distance of 5 m. What is the net work done on the block by all forces acting on it? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 20 J
B. 30 J
C. 40 J
D. 50 J
Answer: B

To find the net work done on the block, we calculate the work done by the applied force and subtract the work done by friction. Work done by the applied force (W₁) = Force × Distance × cos(θ) = 10 N × 5 m × cos(0°) = 50 J. The frictional force (fₖ) = μₖ × Normal force = 0.2 × (2 kg × 10 m/s²) = 4 N. Work done by friction (W₂) = fₖ × Distance × cos(180°) = 4 N × 5 m × (-1) = -20 J. Net work done = W₁ + W₂ = 50 J - 20 J = 30 J.
