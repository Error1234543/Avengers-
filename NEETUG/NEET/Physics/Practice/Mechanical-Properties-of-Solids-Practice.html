NEET Physics Practice – Mechanical Properties of Solids (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Mechanical Properties of Solids, aligned with the NEET Physics syllabus and NTA question style. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. A material has a Poisson’s ratio of 0.5. If a rod of this material is stretched longitudinally, the lateral strain will be:

A. Zero
B. Equal to the longitudinal strain
C. Half the longitudinal strain
D. Negative half the longitudinal strain
Answer: D

Poisson’s ratio (σ) = - (lateral strain / longitudinal strain). If σ = 0.5, lateral strain = -0.5 × longitudinal strain.

2. The work done in stretching a wire of length L and cross-sectional area A through a distance x is given by:

A. (1/2) × (Y A x²) / L
B. (1/2) × (Y A x) / L
C. (Y A x²) / L
D. (Y A x) / L
Answer: A

Work done = (1/2) × stress × strain × volume = (1/2) × (Y × strain) × strain × A L = (1/2) × Y × (x/L)² × A L = (1/2) × (Y A x²) / L.

3. A wire of radius r is stretched by a force F. If the radius is doubled and the force is halved, the longitudinal stress in the wire becomes:

A. One-fourth
B. One-eighth
C. Half
D. Unchanged
Answer: B

Stress = F / A = F / (π r²). New stress = (F/2) / (π (2r)²) = (F/2) / (4 π r²) = (1/8) × (F / π r²) = one-eighth of original stress.

4. The shear modulus of a material is 4 × 10¹⁰ N/m². If a cube of this material of side 10 cm is subjected to a shear force of 10⁴ N, the angle of shear (in radians) is approximately:

A. 2.5 × 10⁻⁶
B. 2.5 × 10⁻⁵
C. 2.5 × 10⁻⁴
D. 2.5 × 10⁻³
Answer: C

Shear strain (θ) = shear stress / shear modulus = (F / A) / η = (10⁴ / 0.01) / (4 × 10¹⁰) = 10⁶ / (4 × 10¹⁰) = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵. However, shear strain is the tangent of the angle, which for small angles equals the angle in radians. Correcting: A = (0.1)² = 0.01 m², so θ = (10⁴ / 0.01) / (4 × 10¹⁰) = 10⁶ / 4 × 10¹⁰ = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ rad. The closest option is 2.5 × 10⁻⁴, but the correct value is 2.5 × 10⁻⁵. Rechecking: 10⁴ / 0.01 = 10⁶, 10⁶ / 4 × 10¹⁰ = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵. Thus, the correct answer is 2.5 × 10⁻⁵, but since it is not listed, the next closest is 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ (likely a typo in options).

5. Which of the following statements about elastic limit is correct?

A. It is the maximum stress a material can withstand without permanent deformation.
B. It is the point where the material breaks.
C. It is the stress at which the material starts to flow plastically.
D. It is the same as the yield point for all materials.
Answer: A

The elastic limit is the maximum stress a material can endure without undergoing permanent deformation. Beyond this limit, the material does not return to its original shape.

6. A steel wire of length 2 m and diameter 1 mm is stretched by 1 mm. If the Young’s modulus of steel is 2 × 10¹¹ N/m², the energy stored in the wire is:

A. 0.039 J
B. 0.39 J
C. 3.9 J
D. 39 J
Answer: A

Energy stored = (1/2) × (Y A x²) / L. A = π (0.0005)² = 7.85 × 10⁻⁷ m². Energy = (1/2) × (2 × 10¹¹ × 7.85 × 10⁻⁷ × (10⁻³)²) / 2 = (1/2) × (2 × 10¹¹ × 7.85 × 10⁻¹³) / 2 = (1/2) × 7.85 × 10⁻² = 0.03925 J ≈ 0.039 J.

7. The ratio of Young’s modulus to the bulk modulus for a material with Poisson’s ratio 0.25 is:

A. 1.5
B. 2
C. 2.5
D. 3
Answer: A

The relationship is Y = 3B (1 - 2σ). For σ = 0.25, Y = 3B (1 - 0.5) = 3B × 0.5 = 1.5 B. Thus, Y / B = 1.5.

8. A wire of length L and cross-sectional area A is stretched by a force F. If the wire is replaced by another wire of the same material but length 2L and area 2A, the elongation for the same force will be:

A. Same as before
B. Half of before
C. Double of before
D. One-fourth of before
Answer: A

Elongation ΔL = (F L) / (A Y). For the new wire, ΔL' = (F × 2L) / (2A × Y) = (F L) / (A Y) = ΔL. Thus, elongation remains the same.

9. A metal rod of length 1 m and radius 5 mm is subjected to a tensile force of 10,000 N. If the Young's modulus of the metal is 2 × 10¹¹ N/m², the stress developed in the rod is:

A. 1.27 × 10⁸ N/m²
B. 2.54 × 10⁸ N/m²
C. 6.37 × 10⁷ N/m²
D. 5.09 × 10⁷ N/m²
Answer: A

Stress = Force / Area. Area = πr² = π × (5 × 10⁻³)² = 7.85 × 10⁻⁵ m². Stress = 10,000 / 7.85 × 10⁻⁵ = 1.27 × 10⁸ N/m².

10. The Poisson's ratio of a material is 0.3. If a wire of this material is stretched longitudinally, the percentage decrease in its diameter is:

A. 15%
B. 30%
C. 60%
D. Cannot be determined without additional data
Answer: D

Poisson's ratio relates lateral strain to longitudinal strain, but without knowing the longitudinal strain or stress, the percentage decrease in diameter cannot be determined.

11. A material has a Young's modulus of 1 × 10¹¹ N/m² and a Poisson's ratio of 0.25. If a cube of this material is subjected to a stress of 1 × 10⁸ N/m² along one axis, the strain in the perpendicular direction is:

A. -2.5 × 10⁻⁴
B. 2.5 × 10⁻⁴
C. -5 × 10⁻⁴
D. 5 × 10⁻⁴
Answer: A

Poisson's ratio (σ) = - (lateral strain) / (longitudinal strain). Longitudinal strain = Stress / Y = 1 × 10⁸ / 1 × 10¹¹ = 1 × 10⁻³. Lateral strain = -σ × longitudinal strain = -0.25 × 1 × 10⁻³ = -2.5 × 10⁻⁴.

12. The work done in stretching a wire of length 1 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm² by 1 mm, if the Young's modulus of the material is 2 × 10¹¹ N/m², is:

A. 0.1 J
B. 0.05 J
C. 0.2 J
D. 0.01 J
Answer: A

Work done = ½ × Stress × Strain × Volume. Stress = Y × Strain = 2 × 10¹¹ × (1 × 10⁻³ / 1) = 2 × 10⁸ N/m². Volume = A × L = 1 × 10⁻⁶ × 1 = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³. Work done = ½ × 2 × 10⁸ × 1 × 10⁻³ × 1 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.1 J.

13. A wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 2 mm² is stretched by 1 mm. If the Young's modulus of the material is 1 × 10¹¹ N/m², the force applied to the wire is:

A. 100 N
B. 200 N
C. 50 N
D. 25 N
Answer: A

Using Y = (F × L) / (A × ΔL). Rearranging for F: F = (Y × A × ΔL) / L = (1 × 10¹¹ × 2 × 10⁻⁶ × 1 × 10⁻³) / 2 = 100 N.

14. A material has a breaking stress of 5 × 10⁸ N/m². The maximum load that can be applied to a wire of radius 1 mm made of this material without breaking it is:

A. 1570 N
B. 785 N
C. 3140 N
D. 157 N
Answer: A

Breaking load = Breaking stress × Area. Area = πr² = π × (1 × 10⁻³)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ m². Breaking load = 5 × 10⁸ × 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ = 1570 N.

15. The elastic potential energy stored in a wire of length 1 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm² when stretched by 1 mm, if the Young's modulus of the material is 2 × 10¹¹ N/m², is:

A. 0.1 J
B. 0.05 J
C. 0.2 J
D. 0.01 J
Answer: A

Elastic potential energy = ½ × Stress × Strain × Volume. Stress = Y × Strain = 2 × 10¹¹ × (1 × 10⁻³ / 1) = 2 × 10⁸ N/m². Volume = A × L = 1 × 10⁻⁶ × 1 = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³. Energy = ½ × 2 × 10⁸ × 1 × 10⁻³ × 1 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.1 J.
