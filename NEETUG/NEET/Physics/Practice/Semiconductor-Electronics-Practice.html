NEET Physics Practice: Semiconductor Electronics (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Semiconductor Electronics, designed to match the difficulty and style of NEET. Each question has four options, a single correct answer, and a detailed explanation. Negative marking applies: +4 for correct, -1 for incorrect. Time yourself as if this were the actual exam.

1. In an intrinsic semiconductor at absolute zero temperature, the material behaves as a:

A. Perfect conductor
B. Perfect insulator
C. Semiconductor with equal electron and hole concentrations
D. Superconductor
Answer: B

At absolute zero temperature (0 K), all electrons in an intrinsic semiconductor are bound to their atoms, and there are no free charge carriers available for conduction. Thus, the material behaves as a perfect insulator. The concept of equal electron and hole concentrations applies at room temperature, not at absolute zero.

2. The conductivity of a semiconductor increases with temperature because:

A. The mobility of charge carriers increases
B. The number of charge carriers increases
C. The band gap decreases
D. The lattice vibrations decrease
Answer: B

In semiconductors, conductivity increases with temperature primarily because the number of thermally generated electron-hole pairs increases. While mobility may decrease slightly due to increased lattice vibrations, the dominant effect is the exponential increase in charge carrier concentration.

3. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of a p-type semiconductor?

A. Majority carriers are holes
B. Doped with trivalent impurities
C. Fermi level lies closer to the conduction band
D. Electrical neutrality is maintained
Answer: C

In a p-type semiconductor, the Fermi level lies closer to the valence band, not the conduction band. This is because the majority carriers are holes, and the material is doped with trivalent impurities (e.g., boron). Electrical neutrality is always maintained in any semiconductor.

4. The depletion region in a p-n junction is formed due to:

A. Diffusion of majority carriers across the junction
B. Drift of minority carriers across the junction
C. Recombination of electrons and holes at the junction
D. Thermal generation of electron-hole pairs
Answer: A

The depletion region is formed when majority carriers (electrons from the n-side and holes from the p-side) diffuse across the junction and recombine, leaving behind immobile ionized donors and acceptors. This creates a region depleted of free charge carriers and establishes an electric field that opposes further diffusion.

5. For a silicon diode, the barrier potential at room temperature is approximately:

A. 0.1 V
B. 0.3 V
C. 0.7 V
D. 1.1 V
Answer: C

At room temperature, the barrier potential (or built-in potential) for a silicon p-n junction diode is approximately 0.7 V. For germanium diodes, it is about 0.3 V. This potential is the voltage required to overcome the depletion region's electric field in forward bias.

6. In a reverse-biased p-n junction, the current is primarily due to:

A. Diffusion of majority carriers
B. Drift of majority carriers
C. Diffusion of minority carriers
D. Drift of minority carriers
Answer: D

In reverse bias, the depletion region widens, and the electric field across the junction increases. The current is primarily due to the drift of minority carriers (electrons from the p-side and holes from the n-side) that are swept across the junction by the electric field. This current is very small and is called the reverse saturation current.

7. The dynamic resistance of a diode in forward bias is given by:

A. The ratio of change in voltage to change in current (ΔV/ΔI)
B. The ratio of change in current to change in voltage (ΔI/ΔV)
C. The product of voltage and current (V × I)
D. The inverse of the barrier potential (1/V₀)
Answer: A

The dynamic resistance (or incremental resistance) of a diode in forward bias is defined as the ratio of the change in voltage (ΔV) to the change in current (ΔI) across the diode. It is a measure of how the diode's resistance changes with small variations in voltage and is given by r_d = ΔV/ΔI.

8. A Zener diode is primarily used for:

A. Rectification of AC signals
B. Voltage regulation in reverse bias
C. Amplification of signals
D. Switching applications in forward bias
Answer: B

A Zener diode is specifically designed to operate in reverse bias and is used for voltage regulation. When the reverse voltage reaches the Zener breakdown voltage, the diode conducts in reverse, maintaining a nearly constant voltage across it despite variations in current. This property makes it ideal for voltage regulation applications.

9. Which of the following logic gates is represented by the Boolean expression Y = A · B?

A. OR gate
B. AND gate
C. NOT gate
D. NAND gate
Answer: B

The Boolean expression Y = A · B represents the AND gate. In an AND gate, the output (Y) is high (1) only when all inputs (A and B) are high (1). The dot (·) symbol denotes the logical AND operation.

10. The truth table for a NAND gate is obtained by:

A. Inverting the output of an AND gate
B. Inverting the output of an OR gate
C. Inverting the inputs of an AND gate
D. Inverting the inputs of an OR gate
Answer: A

A NAND gate is essentially an AND gate followed by a NOT gate. The truth table for a NAND gate is obtained by inverting the output of an AND gate. This means the output is low (0) only when all inputs are high (1), and high (1) otherwise.

11. In an intrinsic semiconductor, the number of electrons in the conduction band is equal to the number of holes in the valence band at:

A. 0 K
B. 300 K
C. Absolute zero temperature
D. All temperatures
Answer: B

In an intrinsic semiconductor, the number of electrons in the conduction band equals the number of holes in the valence band at thermal equilibrium, which is typically around room temperature (300 K). At 0 K or absolute zero, there are no free charge carriers.

12. The forbidden energy gap in a semiconductor is 1.1 eV. The material is most likely:

A. Silicon
B. Germanium
C. Gallium Arsenide
D. Diamond
Answer: A

Silicon has a forbidden energy gap of approximately 1.1 eV at room temperature. Germanium has a smaller gap (~0.7 eV), Gallium Arsenide has a larger gap (~1.4 eV), and Diamond has a much larger gap (~5.5 eV).

13. The current through a p-n junction diode under reverse bias is primarily due to:

A. Majority carriers
B. Minority carriers
C. Both majority and minority carriers
D. Neither majority nor minority carriers
Answer: B

Under reverse bias, the current through a p-n junction diode is primarily due to minority carriers (electrons in the p-region and holes in the n-region) that drift across the junction.

14. The dynamic resistance of a diode is defined as:

A. The ratio of change in voltage to change in current
B. The ratio of change in current to change in voltage
C. The product of voltage and current
D. The ratio of voltage to current
Answer: A

Dynamic resistance (or incremental resistance) of a diode is defined as the ratio of the change in voltage (ΔV) to the change in current (ΔI) across the diode, i.e., r_d = ΔV/ΔI.

15. Which of the following logic gates is universal?

A. AND gate
B. OR gate
C. NAND gate
D. NOT gate
Answer: C

NAND and NOR gates are considered universal gates because any other logic gate (AND, OR, NOT) can be constructed using only NAND gates or only NOR gates.

16. The output of a NOR gate is HIGH only when:

A. All inputs are HIGH
B. All inputs are LOW
C. At least one input is HIGH
D. At least one input is LOW
Answer: B

A NOR gate outputs HIGH only when all its inputs are LOW. If any input is HIGH, the output will be LOW.

17. In a full-wave rectifier, the ripple frequency is:

A. Equal to the input frequency
B. Half the input frequency
C. Twice the input frequency
D. Independent of the input frequency
Answer: C

In a full-wave rectifier, both halves of the input AC waveform are used, resulting in a ripple frequency that is twice the input frequency.

18. The efficiency of a full-wave rectifier is approximately:

A. 40.6%
B. 81.2%
C. 50%
D. 100%
Answer: B

The theoretical maximum efficiency of a full-wave rectifier is approximately 81.2%, which is higher than that of a half-wave rectifier (40.6%) due to better utilization of the input AC waveform.

19. A transistor is operated in common-emitter mode with a current gain (β) of 100. If the base current is 20 μA, the collector current is:

A. 2 mA
B. 0.2 mA
C. 20 mA
D. 1 mA
Answer: A

In a transistor, the current gain (β) in common-emitter mode is defined as the ratio of collector current (I_C) to base current (I_B), i.e., β = I_C / I_B. Given β = 100 and I_B = 20 μA, the collector current I_C = β × I_B = 100 × 20 μA = 2000 μA = 2 mA. This relationship is fundamental for analyzing transistor amplification in electronic circuits.

20. In an n-p-n transistor, the majority charge carriers in the base region are:

A. Electrons
B. Holes
C. Both electrons and holes in equal numbers
D. Neither electrons nor holes
Answer: B

In an n-p-n transistor, the base region is doped with p-type material, where holes are the majority charge carriers. Electrons are the majority carriers in the emitter and collector regions (n-type), but the base is specifically p-type, making holes the dominant carriers there. This distinction is critical for the transistor's amplification mechanism, as the base controls the flow of electrons from emitter to collector.
