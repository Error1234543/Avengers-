This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic of Oscillations for NEET. Each question carries 4 marks with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Choose the correct option for each question.

1. A spring-mass system has a mass of 0.5 kg and a spring constant of 200 N/m. What is the time period of oscillation?

A. π/10 s
B. π/5 s
C. π/20 s
D. π s
Answer: A

The time period T of a spring-mass system is given by T = 2π√(m/k). Here, m = 0.5 kg and k = 200 N/m. Thus, T = 2π√(0.5/200) = 2π√(1/400) = 2π/20 = π/10 s.

2. A simple pendulum has a length of 1 m. What is its time period on the surface of the Earth? (Take g = 9.8 m/s²)

A. 1.0 s
B. 2.0 s
C. π s
D. 2π s
Answer: B

The time period T of a simple pendulum is given by T = 2π√(l/g). Here, l = 1 m and g = 9.8 m/s². Thus, T = 2π√(1/9.8) ≈ 2.0 s.

3. The potential energy of a particle in SHM is maximum at:

A. The mean position
B. The extreme position
C. Halfway between the mean and extreme positions
D. All positions equally
Answer: B

In SHM, the potential energy is maximum at the extreme positions where the displacement is maximum, and the kinetic energy is zero.

4. In simple harmonic motion, the acceleration of the particle is proportional to:

A. Displacement and directed towards the mean position
B. Displacement and directed away from the mean position
C. Velocity and directed towards the mean position
D. Velocity and directed away from the mean position
Answer: A

In simple harmonic motion, the acceleration a is given by a = -ω²x, where x is the displacement. This shows that acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed towards the mean position.

5. A pendulum clock loses 10 seconds per day. What adjustment should be made to the length of the pendulum to correct the time?

A. Increase the length by 0.023%
B. Decrease the length by 0.023%
C. Increase the length by 0.23%
D. Decrease the length by 0.23%
Answer: B

The time period of a pendulum is T = 2π√(L/g). If the clock loses time, the period is longer, so the length must be decreased. The fractional change in time ΔT/T = -0.5 ΔL/L. For ΔT = -10 s/day, ΔL/L ≈ -0.023%, so the length must be decreased by 0.023%.

6. A particle in SHM has a frequency of 5 Hz. What is the angular frequency of the motion?

A. 5π rad/s
B. 10π rad/s
C. 2π rad/s
D. π rad/s
Answer: B

The angular frequency ω is related to the frequency f by ω = 2πf. Here, f = 5 Hz, so ω = 2π × 5 = 10π rad/s.

7. The equation of motion of a particle in SHM is x = 4 cos(3t + π/6) m. What is the amplitude of the motion?

A. 2 m
B. 3 m
C. 4 m
D. 6 m
Answer: C

The general form of displacement in SHM is x = A cos(ωt + φ), where A is the amplitude. Comparing with the given equation x = 4 cos(3t + π/6), the amplitude A is 4 m.

8. A body of mass 0.5 kg is attached to a spring of spring constant 50 N/m. If the body is displaced by 0.1 m from its equilibrium position and released, what is the total energy of the system?

A. 0.25 J
B. 0.5 J
C. 1 J
D. 2 J
Answer: A

The total energy E of a spring-mass system in SHM is given by E = 0.5 kA², where k is the spring constant and A is the amplitude. Here, k = 50 N/m and A = 0.1 m, so E = 0.5 × 50 × (0.1)² = 0.25 J.

9. In damped oscillations, the amplitude of oscillation:

A. Remains constant
B. Increases exponentially with time
C. Decreases exponentially with time
D. Decreases linearly with time
Answer: C

In damped oscillations, the amplitude decreases exponentially with time due to the presence of a damping force, typically proportional to velocity. The amplitude is given by A(t) = A₀ e^(-bt), where b is the damping coefficient.

10. The phase difference between velocity and acceleration in SHM is:

A. 0 rad
B. π/2 rad
C. π rad
D. 3π/2 rad
Answer: C

In SHM, velocity v = ωA cos(ωt + φ) and acceleration a = -ω²A sin(ωt + φ). The phase difference between velocity and acceleration is π rad, as acceleration is π/2 rad ahead of displacement and velocity is π/2 rad ahead of acceleration.

11. A particle executes SHM with a time period of 4 s. If the particle starts from the mean position, what fraction of the total distance covered in one period will it have traveled in 1 s?

A. 1/8
B. 1/4
C. 1/2
D. 3/4
Answer: B

In SHM, the particle covers a distance of 2A in half the period (T/2) and 4A in one full period (T). In 1 s (T/4), the particle will have traveled from the mean position to the extreme position, covering a distance of A. Thus, the fraction of the total distance (4A) covered is A/4A = 1/4.

12. A particle executes simple harmonic motion (SHM) with an amplitude of 0.05 m and a time period of 2 s. If the particle starts from the mean position at t = 0, what is the displacement of the particle at t = 0.5 s?

A. 0.05 m
B. 0.025 m
C. 0.035 m
D. 0.05√2 m
Answer: A

For SHM, the displacement as a function of time is given by x = A sin(ωt), where A is the amplitude and ω is the angular frequency. The angular frequency ω = 2π/T, where T is the time period. Here, T = 2 s, so ω = 2π/2 = π rad/s. At t = 0.5 s, x = 0.05 sin(π × 0.5) = 0.05 sin(π/2) = 0.05 × 1 = 0.05 m. Thus, the displacement at t = 0.5 s is equal to the amplitude, which is 0.05 m.

13. In a simple pendulum, the time period is independent of:

A. Length of the pendulum
B. Mass of the bob
C. Acceleration due to gravity
D. Amplitude (for small angles)
Answer: B

The time period (T) of a simple pendulum is given by T = 2π√(L/g), where L is the length and g is the acceleration due to gravity. The period is independent of the mass of the bob and the amplitude (for small angles).

14. In a damped harmonic oscillator, the amplitude of oscillation decreases exponentially with time as A = A₀ e^(-bt). If the amplitude reduces to half its initial value in 5 seconds, the damping constant b is:

A. (ln 2)/5 s⁻¹
B. (ln 2)/10 s⁻¹
C. 5/ln 2 s⁻¹
D. 10/ln 2 s⁻¹
Answer: A

Given A = A₀ e^(-bt), when A = A₀/2, we have A₀/2 = A₀ e^(-bt). Simplifying, 1/2 = e^(-bt), so ln(1/2) = -bt. Thus, -ln 2 = -bt, and b = (ln 2)/t. Here, t = 5 s, so b = (ln 2)/5 s⁻¹. Option A is correct.

15. Two springs of spring constants k₁ and k₂ are connected in parallel to a mass m. The time period of oscillation of the system is:

A. 2π √(m/(k₁ + k₂))
B. 2π √(m(k₁ + k₂))
C. 2π √(m/k₁) + 2π √(m/k₂)
D. 2π √(m/(k₁k₂))
Answer: A

For springs in parallel, the effective spring constant k_eff = k₁ + k₂. The time period T of a spring-mass system is given by T = 2π √(m/k_eff). Substituting k_eff, we get T = 2π √(m/(k₁ + k₂)). Option A is correct.
