NEET Physics Practice: Laws of Motion (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic of Laws of Motion, designed to match the difficulty and style of NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. A block of mass 2 kg is placed on a frictionless horizontal surface. A horizontal force of 10 N is applied to it. What is the acceleration of the block?

A. 2 m/s²
B. 5 m/s²
C. 10 m/s²
D. 20 m/s²
Answer: B

Using Newton's second law of motion, F = ma. Here, F = 10 N and m = 2 kg. Thus, a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s².

2. Which of the following statements correctly describes Newton's first law of motion?

A. A body accelerates when a net external force acts on it.
B. The rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the applied force.
C. A body remains in its state of rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.
D. For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
Answer: C

Newton's first law, also known as the law of inertia, states that a body remains in its state of rest or uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.

3. A 5 kg object is subjected to two forces: 10 N to the right and 4 N to the left. What is the net force acting on the object?

A. 4 N to the left
B. 6 N to the right
C. 10 N to the right
D. 14 N to the right
Answer: B

The net force is the vector sum of the forces acting on the object. Here, 10 N (right) - 4 N (left) = 6 N to the right.

4. In an elevator moving upward with an acceleration of 2 m/s², a person of mass 60 kg stands on a weighing scale. What is the apparent weight of the person? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 480 N
B. 600 N
C. 720 N
D. 840 N
Answer: C

The apparent weight is given by W = m(g + a). Here, m = 60 kg, g = 10 m/s², and a = 2 m/s². Thus, W = 60(10 + 2) = 720 N.

5. A car of mass 1000 kg is moving with a velocity of 20 m/s. If the brakes apply a force of 5000 N to bring the car to rest, how long does it take for the car to stop?

A. 2 s
B. 4 s
C. 5 s
D. 10 s
Answer: B

Using Newton's second law, F = ma. The acceleration (deceleration) is a = F/m = 5000/1000 = 5 m/s². Using v = u + at, where v = 0, u = 20 m/s, and a = -5 m/s², we get t = (0 - 20)/(-5) = 4 s.

6. A body of mass 10 kg is moving with a constant velocity of 5 m/s. What is the net force acting on the body?

A. 0 N
B. 10 N
C. 50 N
D. 100 N
Answer: A

According to Newton's first law, if a body is moving with constant velocity, the net force acting on it is zero.

7. A 50 kg person is standing in a lift that is accelerating downward at 2 m/s². What is the normal force exerted by the floor of the lift on the person? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 300 N
B. 400 N
C. 500 N
D. 600 N
Answer: B

The normal force is given by N = m(g - a). Here, m = 50 kg, g = 10 m/s², and a = 2 m/s². Thus, N = 50(10 - 2) = 400 N.

8. Which of the following is an example of Newton's third law of motion?

A. A book resting on a table.
B. A car accelerating on a road.
C. The recoil of a gun when a bullet is fired.
D. A ball rolling down an inclined plane.
Answer: C

Newton's third law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. The recoil of a gun when a bullet is fired is an example of this law.

9. A block of mass 4 kg is placed on a rough horizontal surface with a coefficient of kinetic friction of 0.2. If a horizontal force of 20 N is applied, what is the acceleration of the block? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 2 m/s²
B. 3 m/s²
C. 4 m/s²
D. 5 m/s²
Answer: B

The frictional force is f = μ * N = μ * mg = 0.2 * 4 * 10 = 8 N. The net force is F - f = 20 - 8 = 12 N. The acceleration is a = F_net / m = 12 / 4 = 3 m/s².

10. A body of mass 2 kg is subjected to a force that varies with time as F = 4t N, where t is in seconds. What is the velocity of the body at t = 3 s, if it starts from rest?

A. 6 m/s
B. 9 m/s
C. 12 m/s
D. 18 m/s
Answer: B

The acceleration is a = F/m = 4t/2 = 2t m/s². The velocity is the integral of acceleration: v = ∫a dt = ∫2t dt = t² + C. Since the body starts from rest, C = 0. Thus, v = t². At t = 3 s, v = 9 m/s.

11. A car of mass 1000 kg is moving with a velocity of 20 m/s. The brakes are applied, and the car comes to rest in 5 seconds. What is the average force exerted by the brakes?

A. 2000 N
B. 4000 N
C. 6000 N
D. 8000 N
Answer: B

Using Newton's second law, F = ma. First, calculate acceleration: a = (v - u)/t = (0 - 20)/5 = -4 m/s². The magnitude of acceleration is 4 m/s². Thus, F = 1000 kg × 4 m/s² = 4000 N.

12. A block of mass 4 kg is placed on an inclined plane with an angle of 30° to the horizontal. If the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.2, what is the acceleration of the block down the plane? (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 3.2 m/s²
B. 4.0 m/s²
C. 5.0 m/s²
D. 6.0 m/s²
Answer: A

The net force down the plane is mg sinθ - fₖ, where fₖ = μₖ × N = μₖ × mg cosθ. Thus, a = g(sinθ - μₖ cosθ) = 10(sin30° - 0.2 × cos30°) = 10(0.5 - 0.2 × 0.866) = 10(0.5 - 0.1732) = 3.268 ≈ 3.2 m/s².

13. A bullet of mass 0.02 kg is fired horizontally into a block of wood of mass 0.98 kg at rest on a frictionless surface. The bullet embeds itself in the block, and the combined system moves with a velocity of 4 m/s. What was the initial velocity of the bullet?

A. 100 m/s
B. 200 m/s
C. 300 m/s
D. 400 m/s
Answer: B

Using conservation of momentum: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v. Here, u₂ = 0, so 0.02 kg × u₁ = (0.02 kg + 0.98 kg) × 4 m/s. Thus, u₁ = (1 kg × 4 m/s) / 0.02 kg = 200 m/s.

14. A cyclist moves along a circular path of radius 50 m with a constant speed of 10 m/s. What is the centripetal force acting on the cyclist if his mass is 60 kg?

A. 120 N
B. 240 N
C. 360 N
D. 480 N
Answer: A

Centripetal force is given by F = mv²/r. Thus, F = 60 kg × (10 m/s)² / 50 m = 60 × 100 / 50 = 120 N.

15. A block of mass 10 kg is pulled horizontally by a force of 50 N. If the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.3, what is the acceleration of the block? (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 2.0 m/s²
B. 2.5 m/s²
C. 3.0 m/s²
D. 3.5 m/s²
Answer: A

The net force is F - fₖ, where fₖ = μₖ × N = μₖ × mg. Thus, fₖ = 0.3 × 10 kg × 10 m/s² = 30 N. Net force = 50 N - 30 N = 20 N. Acceleration a = F/m = 20 N / 10 kg = 2 m/s².

16. A body of mass 2 kg is moving with a velocity of 5 m/s. It collides with a stationary body of mass 3 kg and sticks to it. What is the velocity of the combined system after the collision?

A. 1 m/s
B. 2 m/s
C. 3 m/s
D. 4 m/s
Answer: B

Using conservation of momentum: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v. Here, u₂ = 0, so 2 kg × 5 m/s = (2 kg + 3 kg) × v. Thus, v = 10 / 5 = 2 m/s.

17. A person of mass 70 kg stands on a weighing machine in a lift. The lift accelerates downward with an acceleration of 2 m/s². What is the reading on the weighing machine? (g = 10 m/s²)

A. 420 N
B. 560 N
C. 700 N
D. 840 N
Answer: B

The apparent weight is given by N = m(g - a). Thus, N = 70 kg × (10 m/s² - 2 m/s²) = 70 kg × 8 m/s² = 560 N.

18. A block of mass 2 kg is placed on a smooth horizontal surface inside a lift that is accelerating upwards with an acceleration of 3 m/s². What is the normal force exerted by the surface of the lift on the block? (Take g = 10 m/s²)

A. 6 N
B. 20 N
C. 26 N
D. 14 N
Answer: C

Since the lift is accelerating upwards, it is a non-inertial frame of reference. We apply Newton's second law in the vertical direction, considering the pseudo-force acting downwards due to the acceleration of the lift. The forces acting on the block are: (1) Gravitational force (mg) acting downwards, (2) Normal force (N) acting upwards, and (3) Pseudo-force (ma) acting downwards due to the lift's acceleration. The net force equation is: N - mg - ma = 0 (since the block is at rest relative to the lift). Substituting the values: N = m(g + a) = 2 kg × (10 m/s² + 3 m/s²) = 2 × 13 = 26 N. Thus, the normal force is 26 N.
