1. The speed of sound in air at 25°C is 346 m/s. If the frequency of a sound wave is 500 Hz, the phase difference between two points separated by a distance of 0.173 m along the direction of propagation is:

A. π/4 rad
B. π/2 rad
C. π rad
D. 2π rad
Answer: B

Phase difference Δφ = (2π/λ) × Δx. Wavelength λ = v/f = 346/500 = 0.692 m. Thus, Δφ = (2π/0.692) × 0.173 = π/2 rad.

2. In a stationary wave, the distance between two consecutive nodes is 20 cm. The wavelength of the wave is:

A. 10 cm
B. 20 cm
C. 40 cm
D. 80 cm
Answer: C

In a stationary wave, the distance between two consecutive nodes is λ/2. Thus, λ/2 = 20 cm ⇒ λ = 40 cm.

3. The fundamental frequency of a closed organ pipe is 256 Hz. The frequency of the third harmonic is:

A. 256 Hz
B. 512 Hz
C. 768 Hz
D. 1024 Hz
Answer: C

For a closed organ pipe, the harmonics are odd multiples of the fundamental frequency. The third harmonic is 3 × 256 Hz = 768 Hz.

4. A string of length 1 m is fixed at both ends. If the speed of the wave in the string is 200 m/s, the frequency of the first overtone is:

A. 100 Hz
B. 200 Hz
C. 300 Hz
D. 400 Hz
Answer: B

For a string fixed at both ends, the first overtone corresponds to the second harmonic. Frequency f = nv/2L, where n = 2. Thus, f = 2 × 200 / (2 × 1) = 200 Hz.

6. The equation of a progressive wave is given by y = 0.02 sin(10πt - 5πx), where x and y are in meters and t is in seconds. What is the speed of the wave?

A. 0.5 m/s
B. 2 m/s
C. 5 m/s
D. 10 m/s
Answer: B

The general form of a progressive wave is y = A sin(ωt - kx). Comparing with the given equation, ω = 10π and k = 5π. The speed of the wave (v) is given by v = ω/k = 10π/5π = 2 m/s.

7. A pipe closed at one end resonates at a fundamental frequency of 256 Hz. What is the frequency of the third harmonic?

A. 256 Hz
B. 512 Hz
C. 768 Hz
D. 1024 Hz
Answer: C

For a pipe closed at one end, the harmonics are odd multiples of the fundamental frequency. The third harmonic is 3 times the fundamental frequency: 3 × 256 Hz = 768 Hz.

8. A wave has a time period of 0.02 s. What is its frequency?

A. 20 Hz
B. 50 Hz
C. 100 Hz
D. 200 Hz
Answer: B

Frequency (f) is the reciprocal of the time period (T): f = 1/T. Here, T = 0.02 s, so f = 1/0.02 = 50 Hz.

9. In the Doppler effect, if the source of sound is moving away from the observer, which of the following is observed?

A. Increase in frequency
B. Decrease in frequency
C. No change in frequency
D. Increase in amplitude
Answer: B

When the source of sound moves away from the observer, the observed frequency decreases due to the Doppler effect. This is because the wavelength of the sound waves appears stretched.

10. A string of length 1 m is fixed at both ends and vibrates in its third harmonic. What is the wavelength of the wave produced?

A. 0.33 m
B. 0.67 m
C. 1 m
D. 2 m
Answer: B

For a string fixed at both ends, the wavelength (λ) of the nth harmonic is given by λ = 2L/n, where L is the length of the string. For the third harmonic (n = 3), λ = 2 × 1/3 = 0.67 m.

11. The speed of sound in air at 25°C is approximately 346 m/s. If the frequency of a sound wave is 173 Hz, what is its wavelength?

A. 0.5 m
B. 1 m
C. 2 m
D. 4 m
Answer: C

The wavelength (λ) of a sound wave is given by λ = v/f, where v is the speed of sound and f is the frequency. Here, v = 346 m/s and f = 173 Hz, so λ = 346/173 = 2 m.

12. Which of the following is true for a transverse wave?

A. The particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation.
B. The particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
C. The wave can only travel through solids.
D. The wave does not require a medium to propagate.
Answer: B

In a transverse wave, the particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. This type of wave can travel through solids and on the surface of liquids but not through gases.

13. Two sound waves of equal amplitude but slightly different frequencies interfere to produce beats. If the beat frequency is 4 Hz and one of the waves has a frequency of 340 Hz, which of the following could be the frequency of the other wave?

A. 332 Hz
B. 336 Hz
C. 344 Hz
D. 348 Hz
Answer: B

Beat frequency is the absolute difference between the frequencies of two interfering waves. Given the beat frequency is 4 Hz and one wave has a frequency of 340 Hz, the other wave's frequency could be either 340 + 4 = 344 Hz or 340 - 4 = 336 Hz. Among the options provided, 336 Hz is the correct choice.
