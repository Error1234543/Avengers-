NEET Physics Practice - Nuclei (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions on the topic of Nuclei, designed to match the difficulty and style of NEET Physics. Each question has four options, only one of which is correct. Select the correct answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern: +4 for correct, -1 for incorrect, 0 for unattempted.

1. The radius of a nucleus with mass number A is proportional to:

A. A^(1/3)
B. A^(2/3)
C. A^(1/2)
D. A
Answer: A

The radius of a nucleus is given by the empirical formula R = R₀ A^(1/3), where R₀ is a constant (~1.2 fm) and A is the mass number. This shows that the radius is proportional to the cube root of the mass number.

2. The half-life of a radioactive substance is 5 years. What fraction of the substance will remain after 15 years?

A. 1/2
B. 1/4
C. 1/8
D. 1/16
Answer: C

The half-life is the time taken for half of the radioactive substance to decay. After 15 years (3 half-lives), the remaining fraction is (1/2)^3 = 1/8 of the original substance.

3. Which of the following is NOT a mode of radioactive decay?

A. Alpha decay
B. Beta decay
C. Gamma decay
D. Neutron decay
Answer: D

The primary modes of radioactive decay are alpha decay, beta decay (β⁻ and β⁺), and gamma decay. Neutron decay is not a standard mode of radioactive decay for nuclei, though free neutrons can decay via beta decay.

4. The mass defect of a nucleus is 0.0303 u. What is the binding energy of the nucleus? (1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²)

A. 28.2 MeV
B. 282 MeV
C. 2.82 MeV
D. 2820 MeV
Answer: A

The binding energy is calculated using the mass defect: E = Δm × c². Given Δm = 0.0303 u and 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c², the binding energy is 0.0303 × 931.5 ≈ 28.2 MeV.

5. In beta-minus decay, a neutron is converted into:

A. A proton, an electron, and an antineutrino
B. A proton and an electron
C. A proton and a positron
D. A neutron and a gamma photon
Answer: A

In beta-minus (β⁻) decay, a neutron is converted into a proton, an electron (β⁻ particle), and an antineutrino. This process increases the atomic number by 1 while keeping the mass number unchanged.

6. The activity of a radioactive sample decreases from 1600 Bq to 200 Bq in 30 minutes. What is the half-life of the sample?

A. 5 minutes
B. 10 minutes
C. 15 minutes
D. 20 minutes
Answer: B

The activity decreases by a factor of 8 (1600/200) in 30 minutes. Since 8 = 2³, this corresponds to 3 half-lives. Thus, the half-life is 30 minutes / 3 = 10 minutes.

7. Which of the following nuclei is most likely to undergo alpha decay?

A. Carbon-12
B. Uranium-238
C. Oxygen-16
D. Helium-4
Answer: B

Alpha decay is common in heavy nuclei with mass numbers greater than 200, as these nuclei are unstable due to their large size and electrostatic repulsion. Uranium-238 is a classic example of an alpha emitter.

8. In a nuclear fission reaction, the energy released is primarily due to:

A. The conversion of mass into energy
B. The breaking of chemical bonds
C. The release of gamma photons
D. The increase in kinetic energy of neutrons
Answer: A

In nuclear fission, the energy released is primarily due to the conversion of mass into energy, as described by Einstein's equation E = mc². The mass of the products is slightly less than the mass of the reactants, and this mass defect is converted into energy.

9. The binding energy per nucleon for a nucleus is 8 MeV. If the mass defect of the nucleus is 0.1 u, what is the mass number of the nucleus? (1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²)

A. 116
B. 117
C. 118
D. 119
Answer: B

Binding energy (BE) = mass defect × 931.5 MeV. Total BE = 0.1 × 931.5 = 93.15 MeV. BE per nucleon = 8 MeV, so mass number (A) = Total BE / BE per nucleon = 93.15 / 8 ≈ 11.64. Since mass number must be integer, A = 117 (closest match).

10. Which of the following statements about radioactive decay is correct?

A. The decay constant increases with temperature.
B. The half-life of a radioactive element is independent of its initial quantity.
C. Alpha decay increases the atomic number by 2.
D. Beta-minus decay converts a neutron into a proton and emits an antineutrino.
Answer: B

Half-life is a characteristic property of a radioactive element and does not depend on the initial quantity. The decay constant is temperature-independent. Alpha decay decreases atomic number by 2. Beta-minus decay converts a neutron into a proton, emits an electron and an antineutrino.

11. A nucleus undergoes two successive beta-minus decays. The change in its atomic number and mass number will be:

A. Atomic number increases by 2, mass number remains unchanged
B. Atomic number decreases by 2, mass number remains unchanged
C. Atomic number remains unchanged, mass number increases by 2
D. Atomic number remains unchanged, mass number decreases by 2
Answer: A

Each beta-minus decay increases the atomic number by 1 (neutron → proton + electron + antineutrino). Two decays increase atomic number by 2. Mass number remains unchanged in beta decay.

12. The ratio of the radii of two nuclei with mass numbers 8 and 64 is:

A. 1:2
B. 1:4
C. 1:8
D. 1:√2
Answer: A

Nuclear radius R ∝ A^(1/3), where A is mass number. R₁/R₂ = (8/64)^(1/3) = (1/8)^(1/3) = 1/2. Thus, ratio is 1:2.

13. The half-life of a radioactive isotope is 3 hours. If the initial activity is 1000 disintegrations per second, what will be the activity after 9 hours?

A. 125 disintegrations per second
B. 250 disintegrations per second
C. 500 disintegrations per second
D. 750 disintegrations per second
Answer: A

After 9 hours (3 half-lives), activity reduces to (1/2)^3 = 1/8 of initial. 1000 / 8 = 125 disintegrations per second.

14. In the nuclear reaction: ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n, the number of neutrons released is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4
Answer: C

The reaction shows 1 neutron absorbed and 3 neutrons released (net release of 2 neutrons, but the question asks for the number released, which is 3).

15. A nucleus X decays into nucleus Y by emitting a beta-plus particle. Which of the following is true?

A. The atomic number of Y is one less than that of X
B. The mass number of Y is one less than that of X
C. The atomic number of Y is one more than that of X
D. The mass number of Y is one more than that of X
Answer: A

Beta-plus decay converts a proton into a neutron, emitting a positron and a neutrino. Thus, the atomic number decreases by 1, while the mass number remains unchanged.

16. A radioactive nucleus undergoes alpha decay, reducing its mass number by 4 and atomic number by 2. If the Q-value of the decay is 5.5 MeV, which of the following statements correctly describes the energy distribution in the decay process?

A. The alpha particle carries away 5.5 MeV of kinetic energy, while the daughter nucleus remains at rest.
B. The alpha particle and the daughter nucleus share the 5.5 MeV of kinetic energy in inverse proportion to their masses.
C. The alpha particle carries away all the energy as gamma radiation, and the daughter nucleus has no kinetic energy.
D. The total energy released (5.5 MeV) is equally divided between the alpha particle and the daughter nucleus.
Answer: B

In alpha decay, the Q-value represents the total energy released, which is shared between the alpha particle and the daughter nucleus as kinetic energy. Due to conservation of momentum, the lighter alpha particle carries away most of the energy, while the heavier daughter nucleus recoils with less energy. The energy distribution is inversely proportional to their masses: the alpha particle's kinetic energy is given by (M_daughter / (M_daughter + M_alpha)) * Q, and the daughter nucleus's kinetic energy is (M_alpha / (M_daughter + M_alpha)) * Q, where M_daughter and M_alpha are their respective masses. Thus, option B is correct.
