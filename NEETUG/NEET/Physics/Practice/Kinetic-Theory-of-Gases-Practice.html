NEET Physics Practice - Kinetic Theory of Gases (Part 1 of 2)

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the Kinetic Theory of Gases for NEET. Each question has four options (A-D), only one of which is correct. Select the correct answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern: +4 for correct, -1 for incorrect, 0 for unattempted. Time yourself for 12 minutes (1 minute per question).

1. The most probable speed (v_mp), average speed (v_avg), and root mean square speed (v_rms) of gas molecules at a given temperature follow the order:

A. v_mp < v_avg < v_rms
B. v_rms < v_avg < v_mp
C. v_avg < v_mp < v_rms
D. v_mp = v_avg = v_rms
Answer: A

For a Maxwellian distribution of molecular speeds, the most probable speed (v_mp) is the speed possessed by the largest number of molecules, followed by the average speed (v_avg), and then the root mean square speed (v_rms). Mathematically, v_mp = √(2RT/M), v_avg = √(8RT/πM), and v_rms = √(3RT/M). Thus, v_mp < v_avg < v_rms.

2. A gas at 300 K has a root mean square speed of 500 m/s. If the temperature is increased to 600 K, the new root mean square speed will be:

A. 500 m/s
B. 707 m/s
C. 1000 m/s
D. 250 m/s
Answer: B

The root mean square speed is proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature: v_rms ∝ √T. If the temperature doubles from 300 K to 600 K, the new rms speed becomes √(600/300) × 500 = √2 × 500 ≈ 707 m/s.

3. The kinetic energy of 1 mole of an ideal gas at 300 K is approximately: (R = 8.314 J/mol·K)

A. 3741 J
B. 2494 J
C. 1247 J
D. 6236 J
Answer: A

The total kinetic energy of 1 mole of an ideal gas is given by KE = (3/2)RT. Substituting R = 8.314 J/mol·K and T = 300 K: KE = (3/2) × 8.314 × 300 ≈ 3741 J.

4. Which of the following is NOT an assumption of the kinetic theory of gases?

A. Gas molecules are in continuous random motion.
B. The volume occupied by gas molecules is negligible compared to the total volume of the gas.
C. Collisions between gas molecules are perfectly elastic.
D. Gas molecules exert attractive forces on each other.
Answer: D

The kinetic theory of gases assumes that gas molecules are in continuous random motion (A), occupy negligible volume (B), and undergo perfectly elastic collisions (C). However, it assumes no attractive or repulsive forces between molecules (ideal gas assumption), so statement D is not an assumption of the theory.

5. A gas has a root mean square speed of 400 m/s at 27°C. If the root mean square speed becomes 800 m/s, the new temperature of the gas is:

A. 1200 K
B. 600 K
C. 900 K
D. 300 K
Answer: A

The root mean square speed is proportional to √T. If the speed doubles (from 400 m/s to 800 m/s), the temperature must quadruple (since (√T_new / √T_initial) = 2 → T_new / T_initial = 4). Initial temperature is 27°C = 300 K, so new temperature is 4 × 300 = 1200 K.

6. At what temperature will the average kinetic energy of a gas molecule be exactly half its value at 27 °C?

A. -123 °C
B. -150 °C
C. 13.5 °C
D. 0 °C
Answer: A

Average kinetic energy per molecule is (3/2)kT. If it is halved, T_new = T_initial / 2 = (273 + 27)/2 = 150 K = –123 °C.

7. A gas at 300 K has a pressure of 1 atm. If the volume is halved and the temperature is raised to 600 K, the new pressure will be:

A. 1 atm
B. 2 atm
C. 4 atm
D. 8 atm
Answer: C

Using the ideal gas law PV/T = constant, P₂ = P₁ × (V₁/V₂) × (T₂/T₁) = 1 atm × 2 × 2 = 4 atm.

8. The ratio of the most probable speed (v_mp) to the root mean square speed (v_rms) for a gas at a given temperature is:

A. √(2/3)
B. √(3/2)
C. 2/3
D. 3/2
Answer: A

v_mp = √(2RT/M) and v_rms = √(3RT/M), so v_mp / v_rms = √(2/3).

9. A container holds 2 moles of an ideal gas at 2 atm pressure and 300 K. If the gas is compressed isothermally to half its initial volume, the work done on the gas is:

A. 3.46 kJ
B. 1.73 kJ
C. 0.86 kJ
D. 6.92 kJ
Answer: A

Work done in isothermal compression is W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁). Here V₂/V₁ = 1/2, so W = 2 × 8.314 × 300 × ln(1/2) = –3458 J (magnitude 3.46 kJ).

10. If the temperature of an ideal gas is doubled while keeping the volume constant, the ratio of the final to initial pressure is:

A. 1:1
B. 1:2
C. 2:1
D. 4:1
Answer: C

At constant volume, P ∝ T. Doubling T doubles P, so ratio is 2:1.

11. Which speed distribution curve for gas molecules is symmetric about its peak?

A. Maxwell speed distribution
B. Maxwell energy distribution
C. Boltzmann distribution
D. None of the above
Answer: D

The Maxwell speed distribution is skewed; none of the standard curves is symmetric about its peak.

12. A gas has a molar mass of 40 g mol⁻¹. At 300 K, the root mean square speed of its molecules is approximately:

A. 300 m s⁻¹
B. 433 m s⁻¹
C. 517 m s⁻¹
D. 612 m s⁻¹
Answer: B

v_rms = √(3RT/M) = √(3 × 8.314 × 300 / 0.040) ≈ 433 m s⁻¹.

13. The kinetic energy of one mole of an ideal gas at 27 °C is:

A. 3.74 kJ
B. 2.49 kJ
C. 1.25 kJ
D. 6.21 kJ
Answer: A

Total kinetic energy for one mole = (3/2)RT = 1.5 × 8.314 × 300 = 3741 J ≈ 3.74 kJ.

14. A gas mixture contains 2 g of H₂ and 8 g of O₂ at STP. The ratio of the number of H₂ molecules to O₂ molecules is:

A. 1:1
B. 2:1
C. 4:1
D. 8:1
Answer: C

Moles of H₂ = 2/2 = 1 mol; moles of O₂ = 8/32 = 0.25 mol. Ratio = 1 : 0.25 = 4 : 1.

15. The root mean square speed of gas molecules is found to be 400 m/s at a certain temperature. If the temperature of the gas is doubled while keeping the pressure constant, what will be the new root mean square speed of the molecules?

A. 200 m/s
B. 400√2 m/s
C. 800 m/s
D. 565.6 m/s
Answer: D

The root mean square (rms) speed of gas molecules is given by the formula: v_rms = √(3RT/M), where R is the universal gas constant, T is the absolute temperature, and M is the molar mass of the gas. Since v_rms is directly proportional to the square root of the temperature (v_rms ∝ √T), doubling the temperature (T' = 2T) will result in the new rms speed being √2 times the original speed. Thus, v_rms' = 400 * √2 = 400 * 1.414 ≈ 565.6 m/s. The correct answer is D.

16. The root mean square speed of gas molecules is found to be 400 m/s at a temperature of 300 K. If the temperature of the gas is increased to 600 K, what will be the new root mean square speed of the molecules? Assume the gas remains ideal and the molecular mass does not change.

A. 400 m/s
B. 566 m/s
C. 800 m/s
D. 283 m/s
Answer: B

The root mean square (rms) speed of gas molecules is given by the formula: v_rms = √(3RT/M), where R is the universal gas constant, T is the absolute temperature, and M is the molar mass of the gas. Since v_rms is directly proportional to the square root of the temperature (v_rms ∝ √T), increasing the temperature from 300 K to 600 K (which is double the original temperature) will increase the rms speed by a factor of √2. Thus, the new rms speed = 400 m/s × √2 = 400 × 1.414 ≈ 566 m/s. This demonstrates the direct relationship between temperature and molecular speed in the kinetic theory of gases.
