NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define 'health' according to the World Health Organization (WHO).

Health, as defined by the WHO, is a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. This definition emphasizes holistic well-being, including psychological and social dimensions, rather than just the absence of illness.

Card 2

What is the difference between infectious and non-infectious diseases? Provide one example of each.

Infectious diseases are caused by pathogenic microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites and can be transmitted from one person to another (e.g., tuberculosis, COVID-19). Non-infectious diseases are not caused by pathogens and cannot be transmitted; they often result from genetic, lifestyle, or environmental factors (e.g., diabetes, hypertension).

Card 3

Name the causative agent of malaria and describe its mode of transmission.

The causative agent of malaria is the protozoan parasite *Plasmodium*, with *Plasmodium falciparum* being the most deadly species. It is transmitted to humans through the bite of an infected female *Anopheles* mosquito. The parasite enters the bloodstream, infects liver cells, and later red blood cells, causing symptoms like fever, chills, and anemia.

Card 4

What are the primary methods of controlling mosquito-borne diseases like dengue and malaria?

Primary methods include: (1) **Vector control**: Eliminating mosquito breeding sites (e.g., stagnant water), using insecticides, and introducing biological controls like larvivorous fish. (2) **Personal protection**: Using mosquito nets, repellents, and wearing protective clothing. (3) **Community awareness**: Educating the public about prevention and early symptoms. (4) **Vaccination and treatment**: Where available, vaccines (e.g., for dengue) and antimalarial drugs (e.g., artemisinin-based combination therapy).

Card 5

Explain the concept of 'immunity' and differentiate between innate and acquired immunity.

Immunity is the body's ability to resist infections or toxins by recognizing and neutralizing pathogens. **Innate immunity** is non-specific, present from birth, and includes physical barriers (e.g., skin), phagocytic cells (e.g., macrophages), and inflammatory responses. **Acquired immunity** is specific, develops after exposure to pathogens, and involves lymphocytes (B-cells and T-cells) that produce antibodies or directly attack infected cells. Acquired immunity can be further divided into active (e.g., vaccination) and passive (e.g., maternal antibodies).

Card 6

What is a vaccine? How does it provide immunity?

A vaccine is a biological preparation containing weakened or killed pathogens, their toxins, or surface proteins. It stimulates the immune system to recognize the pathogen as a threat, produce antibodies, and generate memory cells. Upon future exposure to the actual pathogen, the immune system mounts a faster and stronger response, preventing or reducing the severity of the disease.

Card 7

List the types of vaccines and provide one example for each.

Types of vaccines include: (1) **Live-attenuated vaccines**: Contain weakened pathogens (e.g., MMR vaccine for measles, mumps, and rubella). (2) **Inactivated vaccines**: Contain killed pathogens (e.g., polio vaccine). (3) **Subunit/recombinant vaccines**: Contain specific pathogen proteins (e.g., hepatitis B vaccine). (4) **Toxoid vaccines**: Contain inactivated toxins (e.g., tetanus vaccine). (5) **mRNA vaccines**: Use messenger RNA to instruct cells to produce pathogen proteins (e.g., COVID-19 vaccines like Pfizer-BioNTech).

Card 8

What is the role of antibiotics in treating bacterial infections? Why are they ineffective against viral infections?

Antibiotics are drugs that kill or inhibit the growth of bacteria by targeting specific bacterial structures or processes (e.g., cell wall synthesis, protein synthesis). They are ineffective against viruses because viruses lack these structures and instead hijack host cells to replicate. Antiviral drugs target viral-specific processes (e.g., reverse transcription in HIV). Misuse of antibiotics can lead to antibiotic resistance.

Card 9

Define 'antibiotic resistance' and explain how it develops.

Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria evolve mechanisms to survive exposure to antibiotics that would normally kill them or inhibit their growth. It develops through: (1) **Genetic mutations**: Random mutations conferring resistance. (2) **Horizontal gene transfer**: Bacteria acquiring resistance genes from other bacteria via plasmids. (3) **Selective pressure**: Overuse or misuse of antibiotics kills susceptible bacteria, allowing resistant strains to proliferate. This poses a global health threat, as infections become harder to treat.

Card 10

What are the common methods of cancer detection?

Common methods include: (1) **Biopsy**: Removing and examining tissue samples. (2) **Imaging techniques**: X-rays, CT scans, MRI, and PET scans to visualize tumors. (3) **Blood tests**: Detecting tumor markers (e.g., PSA for prostate cancer). (4) **Endoscopy**: Using a camera to examine internal organs (e.g., colonoscopy). (5) **Self-examination**: For cancers like breast or testicular cancer. Early detection improves treatment outcomes.

Card 11

Explain the term 'metastasis' in the context of cancer.

Metastasis is the process by which cancer cells spread from the primary tumor to other parts of the body via the bloodstream or lymphatic system. These cells invade distant tissues, form secondary tumors, and disrupt normal organ function. Metastasis is a hallmark of malignant (aggressive) cancers and is responsible for the majority of cancer-related deaths.

Card 12

What are the major causes of drug addiction?

Major causes include: (1) **Genetic predisposition**: Family history of addiction increases risk. (2) **Environmental factors**: Peer pressure, stress, trauma, or exposure to drugs. (3) **Neurochemical changes**: Drugs alter brain reward pathways, leading to dependence. (4) **Mental health disorders**: Conditions like depression or anxiety may lead to self-medication. (5) **Social and economic factors**: Poverty, lack of education, or unemployment can contribute to drug abuse.

Card 13

Describe the harmful effects of alcohol consumption on the human body.

Harmful effects include: (1) **Liver damage**: Fatty liver, hepatitis, cirrhosis, or liver cancer. (2) **Cardiovascular issues**: Hypertension, cardiomyopathy, or stroke. (3) **Neurological effects**: Memory loss, blackouts, or Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome. (4) **Digestive problems**: Gastritis, pancreatitis, or malnutrition. (5) **Weakened immunity**: Increased susceptibility to infections. (6) **Mental health**: Depression, anxiety, or addiction. (7) **Social consequences**: Accidents, violence, or family breakdowns.

Card 14

What is the significance of 'World Health Day' and when is it observed?

World Health Day is observed on **April 7th** each year to mark the founding of the World Health Organization (WHO) in 1948. It aims to raise awareness about global health issues, promote healthy lifestyles, and advocate for universal health coverage. Each year focuses on a specific theme (e.g., mental health, climate change, or health equity).

Card 15

Explain the term 'pandemic' and provide an example of a recent pandemic.

A pandemic is an epidemic of an infectious disease that spreads across multiple countries or continents, affecting a large number of people. Unlike epidemics, pandemics have global reach. A recent example is the **COVID-19 pandemic**, caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, which emerged in late 2019 and led to widespread illness, deaths, and disruptions worldwide.

Card 16

What are the key strategies for preventing the spread of HIV/AIDS?

Key strategies include: (1) **Safe sex practices**: Using condoms to prevent transmission. (2) **Needle exchange programs**: Reducing transmission among intravenous drug users. (3) **Blood screening**: Ensuring donated blood is HIV-free. (4) **Antiretroviral therapy (ART)**: Suppressing viral load in infected individuals to reduce transmission. (5) **Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP)**: Medication for high-risk individuals. (6) **Education and awareness**: Promoting testing, reducing stigma, and encouraging early treatment.

Card 17

Define 'immunodeficiency' and name one primary and one secondary immunodeficiency disorder.

Immunodeficiency is a state in which the immune system's ability to fight infections is compromised or absent. **Primary immunodeficiency** is congenital (e.g., **Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID)**). **Secondary immunodeficiency** is acquired due to external factors (e.g., **HIV/AIDS**, chemotherapy, or malnutrition).
