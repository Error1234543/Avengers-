NEET Biology - Diversity in Living World: Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define 'taxonomy' and name the three main branches of taxonomy studied in the Diversity in Living World chapter for NEET.

Taxonomy is the science of identifying, naming, classifying, and describing organisms based on shared characteristics. The three main branches of taxonomy are: (1) **Classification** – grouping organisms into hierarchical categories (taxa) like kingdom, phylum, class, etc.; (2) **Nomenclature** – assigning standardized scientific names to organisms using binomial nomenclature (e.g., *Homo sapiens*); (3) **Identification** – determining the correct taxonomic position of an organism using keys, morphological traits, or molecular data. These branches are fundamental for organizing biodiversity and are frequently tested in NEET.

Card 2

What is binomial nomenclature? State the two rules of binomial nomenclature that are most commonly violated in NEET-style questions.

Binomial nomenclature is the system of naming organisms using two Latinized words: the **genus name** (capitalized) and the **species epithet** (lowercase), both italicized (e.g., *Mangifera indica*). Common violations in NEET questions include: (1) **Incorrect capitalization** – writing the species epithet with a capital letter (e.g., *Mangifera Indica*); (2) **Non-italicized names** – failing to italicize the scientific name (e.g., Mangifera indica). These rules are strictly enforced in taxonomy and are high-yield for NEET.

Card 3

List the five kingdoms of classification proposed by Whittaker. Which kingdom is exclusively prokaryotic, and what are its two major groups?

Whittaker’s five-kingdom classification includes: **Monera**, **Protista**, **Fungi**, **Plantae**, and **Animalia**. The **Monera** kingdom is exclusively prokaryotic and comprises two major groups: (1) **Eubacteria** – true bacteria with peptidoglycan cell walls (e.g., *Escherichia coli*); (2) **Archaebacteria** – extremophiles with unique cell wall compositions (e.g., methanogens, halophiles). NEET often tests distinctions between these groups, such as their habitats or cell wall structures.

Card 4

Differentiate between **autotrophic** and **heterotrophic** nutrition. Provide one example of an organism from each of the five kingdoms that exhibits these modes.

**Autotrophic nutrition**: Organisms synthesize their own food from inorganic sources (e.g., photosynthesis or chemosynthesis). **Heterotrophic nutrition**: Organisms depend on external organic sources for food. Examples from Whittaker’s kingdoms: - **Monera**: Autotrophic – *Cyanobacteria* (photosynthetic); Heterotrophic – *Lactobacillus* (saprophytic). - **Protista**: Autotrophic – *Euglena* (mixotrophic, but primarily photosynthetic); Heterotrophic – *Amoeba* (phagocytosis). - **Fungi**: Heterotrophic – *Agaricus* (saprophytic). - **Plantae**: Autotrophic – *Pinus* (photosynthetic). - **Animalia**: Heterotrophic – *Homo sapiens* (holozoic). NEET questions often ask for kingdom-specific examples.

Card 5

What are the three domains of life proposed by Carl Woese? How do they differ from Whittaker’s five kingdoms?

Carl Woese proposed the **three-domain system**: **Bacteria**, **Archaea**, and **Eukarya**, based on **16S rRNA sequencing** and cellular differences. Key distinctions from Whittaker’s five kingdoms: - **Bacteria**: Prokaryotes with peptidoglycan cell walls (equivalent to most of Monera). - **Archaea**: Prokaryotes with unique membrane lipids and no peptidoglycan (e.g., extremophiles, part of Monera in Whittaker’s system). - **Eukarya**: All eukaryotic organisms (Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia in Whittaker’s system). NEET emphasizes that Woese’s system reflects **evolutionary relationships** (phylogeny), while Whittaker’s is based on **morphology and nutrition**.

Card 6

Explain the term 'species' as defined by the Biological Species Concept (BSC). What are two major limitations of this concept frequently tested in NEET?

The **Biological Species Concept (BSC)**, proposed by Ernst Mayr, defines a species as a group of **interbreeding natural populations** that are **reproductively isolated** from other such groups. **Limitations** commonly tested in NEET: 1. **Asexual organisms**: BSC cannot be applied to bacteria, fungi (e.g., *Penicillium*), or protists (e.g., *Paramecium*), which reproduce asexually. 2. **Fossil species**: Reproductive isolation cannot be tested in extinct organisms. NEET often contrasts BSC with alternative concepts like the **Morphological Species Concept** (based on physical traits) or **Phylogenetic Species Concept** (based on genetic ancestry).

Card 7

What are **taxonomic keys**? Describe the two types of keys used in NEET-relevant identification and provide an example of how a dichotomous key works.

**Taxonomic keys** are tools used to identify organisms based on **contrasting pairs of characteristics**. The two types are: 1. **Dichotomous keys**: Present **two choices (couplets)** at each step, leading to the correct identification. Example: - 1a. Organism has wings → Go to 2 - 1b. Organism lacks wings → *Formica* (ant) - 2a. Wings covered with scales → *Danaus* (butterfly) - 2b. Wings not covered with scales → *Apis* (bee) 2. **Polytomous keys**: Offer **multiple choices** at each step (less common in NEET). Dichotomous keys are prioritized in NEET for their simplicity and direct application in identification questions.

Card 8

Define **herbarium** and **botanical garden**. How do these differ in their roles, and why are they important for NEET aspirants?

**Herbarium**: A **collection of preserved plant specimens** (dried, pressed, and mounted on sheets) with labels containing taxonomic information (e.g., name, location, collector). Used for **reference, research, and verification** of plant identities. **Botanical garden**: A **living collection of plants** cultivated for **education, conservation, and research**, often organized taxonomically (e.g., Lalbagh in Bengaluru). **Importance for NEET**: - Herbariums provide **authentic specimens** for studying morphology and taxonomy. - Botanical gardens offer **practical exposure** to plant diversity, aiding in field identification. NEET may ask about their roles or specific examples (e.g., Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew).

Card 9

What is **endemic species**? Name two endemic species of India and the regions they are restricted to, as relevant for NEET.

**Endemic species** are organisms **restricted to a specific geographic region** and not found naturally elsewhere. Examples from India (high-yield for NEET): 1. **Lion-tailed macaque** (*Macaca silenus*): Endemic to the **Western Ghats** (Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu). 2. **Sangai deer** (*Rucervus eldii eldii*): Endemic to **Keibul Lamjao National Park** in Manipur (floating marshes of Loktak Lake). NEET often tests endemic species in the context of **biodiversity hotspots** (e.g., Western Ghats, Northeast India) or conservation efforts.

Card 10

Explain the term **biodiversity hotspot**. How many biodiversity hotspots are recognized in India, and which two are most frequently tested in NEET?

**Biodiversity hotspots** are regions with **exceptionally high species richness and endemism** that are **under significant threat** from human activities. Criteria: (1) ≥1,500 endemic plant species; (2) ≥70% habitat loss. **India has 4 biodiversity hotspots** (as per Conservation International): 1. **Western Ghats**: Highest priority in NEET; home to endemic species like the **Nilgiri tahr** and **Malabar civet**. 2. **Eastern Himalayas**: Includes Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh; endemic species like the **red panda** and **Himalayan newt**. NEET often links hotspots to **conservation policies** (e.g., Project Tiger) or **climate change impacts**.
