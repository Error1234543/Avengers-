NEET Biology: Structural Organisation in Animals and Plants - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define tissue and classify the four primary types of animal tissues with one example each.

A tissue is a group of cells that are similar in structure and function, working together to perform a specific role. The four primary types of animal tissues are: 1. Epithelial Tissue (e.g., Simple squamous epithelium in alveoli) 2. Connective Tissue (e.g., Bone tissue) 3. Muscular Tissue (e.g., Skeletal muscle) 4. Nervous Tissue (e.g., Neurons in the brain).

Card 2

What is the key structural difference between simple and compound epithelial tissue?

Simple epithelial tissue consists of a single layer of cells, facilitating functions like absorption and filtration (e.g., lining of intestines). Compound epithelial tissue has multiple layers of cells, providing protection against mechanical stress (e.g., skin epidermis).

Card 3

Explain the role of areolar connective tissue in the body with its two main components.

Areolar connective tissue acts as a packing material between organs, providing support and elasticity. Its two main components are: 1. **Fibres**: Collagen (strength), elastin (flexibility), and reticular fibres (support). 2. **Matrix**: Gel-like ground substance containing fibroblasts, macrophages, and mast cells.

Card 4

Differentiate between striated, smooth, and cardiac muscle fibres based on structure and function.

1. **Striated (Skeletal) Muscle**: Long, cylindrical, multinucleated fibres with striations; voluntary control (e.g., biceps). 2. **Smooth Muscle**: Spindle-shaped, uninucleated fibres without striations; involuntary control (e.g., stomach walls). 3. **Cardiac Muscle**: Branched, uninucleated fibres with intercalated discs and striations; involuntary, rhythmic contractions (e.g., heart).

Card 5

Describe the structure and function of a neuron, including its three main parts.

A neuron is the functional unit of the nervous system, consisting of: 1. **Cell Body (Soma)**: Contains the nucleus and organelles; integrates signals. 2. **Dendrites**: Branched extensions that receive signals from other neurons. 3. **Axon**: Long, single fibre transmitting impulses away from the cell body, often myelinated for faster conduction.

Card 6

What are the three types of plant tissues? Provide one example of each.

1. **Meristematic Tissue**: Actively dividing cells (e.g., apical meristem in root tips). 2. **Permanent Tissue**: Non-dividing, differentiated cells: - **Simple Permanent Tissue**: Parenchyma (storage in stems). - **Complex Permanent Tissue**: Xylem (water transport).

Card 7

Explain the function of xylem and phloem in plants, including their key components.

**Xylem**: Transports water and minerals from roots to other parts (unidirectional). Components: Tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres, and parenchyma. **Phloem**: Transports food (sucrose) from leaves to storage organs (bidirectional). Components: Sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem fibres, and parenchyma.

Card 8

What is the role of stomata in plants? Describe their structure and regulation.

Stomata are pores on leaf surfaces that regulate gas exchange (CO₂ in, O₂ out) and transpiration. Structure: - **Guard Cells**: Pair of kidney-shaped cells controlling pore opening. - **Stomatal Pore**: Opening between guard cells. Regulation: Guard cells swell (open) with increased turgor pressure (e.g., in light) and shrink (close) under water stress.

Card 9

Differentiate between collenchyma and sclerenchyma tissues in plants.

**Collenchyma**: Living cells with unevenly thickened primary walls; provide mechanical support to growing regions (e.g., leaf petioles). **Sclerenchyma**: Dead cells with lignified secondary walls; provide rigid support (e.g., fibres in stems, sclereids in seed coats).

Card 10

What is the function of the epidermis in plants? Mention two specialized epidermal cells.

The epidermis is the outermost protective layer of plant organs, preventing water loss and pathogen entry. Specialized cells: 1. **Trichomes**: Hair-like structures reducing transpiration and deterring herbivores. 2. **Root Hairs**: Tubular extensions increasing surface area for water absorption.

Card 11

Explain the concept of secondary growth in dicot stems, naming the tissue responsible.

Secondary growth increases stem thickness in dicots via the **vascular cambium** (a lateral meristem). It produces secondary xylem (wood) inward and secondary phloem outward, while the **cork cambium** (phellogen) forms bark for protection.

Card 12

Describe the structure of a dicot leaf, highlighting its vascular arrangement.

A dicot leaf has: - **Epidermis**: Upper and lower layers with stomata (more on lower surface). - **Mesophyll**: - **Palisade Parenchyma**: Columnar cells with chloroplasts for photosynthesis. - **Spongy Parenchyma**: Loosely arranged cells for gas exchange. - **Vascular Bundles**: Arranged in a network (reticulate venation), with xylem on top and phloem below, surrounded by bundle sheath cells.

Card 13

What are the functions of the following animal organs? 1. Liver 2. Kidney 3. Small Intestine

1. **Liver**: Detoxification, bile production, protein synthesis, and metabolism of carbohydrates/fats. 2. **Kidney**: Filters blood to form urine, regulates electrolyte balance, and maintains pH. 3. **Small Intestine**: Digestion (enzymes) and absorption of nutrients (villi/microvilli increase surface area).

Card 14

Explain the difference between open and closed circulatory systems with examples.

**Open Circulatory System**: Blood flows freely through body cavities (haemocoel) and directly bathes organs (e.g., arthropods like cockroaches). **Closed Circulatory System**: Blood is confined to vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries) for efficient transport (e.g., vertebrates like humans).

Card 15

What is the role of the following plant hormones? 1. Auxin 2. Gibberellin 3. Abscisic Acid

1. **Auxin**: Promotes cell elongation, root initiation, and apical dominance (e.g., IAA). 2. **Gibberellin**: Stimulates stem elongation, seed germination, and fruit growth. 3. **Abscisic Acid**: Inhibits growth, induces dormancy, and closes stomata during water stress.
