Card 1

Define homeostasis and explain its significance in human physiology.

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment within the body despite external changes. It is crucial for survival because it ensures optimal conditions for cellular functions, enzyme activity, and metabolic processes. Key parameters regulated include body temperature, pH, blood glucose levels, and electrolyte balance. Disruption of homeostasis can lead to diseases or even death.

Card 2

Name the three types of muscle tissue in humans and describe their primary functions.

1. **Skeletal Muscle**: Attached to bones, responsible for voluntary movements and locomotion. Striated and multinucleated. 2. **Smooth Muscle**: Found in walls of hollow organs (e.g., stomach, blood vessels), controls involuntary movements like peristalsis. Non-striated and uninucleated. 3. **Cardiac Muscle**: Exclusive to the heart, enables rhythmic contractions to pump blood. Striated, uninucleated, and interconnected via intercalated discs.

Card 3

What is the role of the sinoatrial (SA) node in the cardiac cycle?

The SA node, located in the right atrium, acts as the **natural pacemaker** of the heart. It initiates electrical impulses that trigger atrial contraction, setting the heart's rhythm (typically 60-100 bpm). These impulses travel to the atrioventricular (AV) node, ensuring coordinated contraction of the atria and ventricles. Damage to the SA node can lead to arrhythmias.

Card 4

Explain the mechanism of oxygen transport in blood, including the role of hemoglobin.

Oxygen is primarily transported bound to **hemoglobin (Hb)** in red blood cells (98.5%). Each Hb molecule binds up to 4 O₂ molecules via cooperative binding (affinity increases with each O₂ bound). In lungs (high O₂ partial pressure), Hb binds O₂ (oxyhemoglobin). In tissues (low O₂ partial pressure), O₂ dissociates and diffuses into cells. A small fraction (1.5%) dissolves directly in plasma.

Card 5

Differentiate between inspiration and expiration in terms of muscle activity and pressure changes.

**Inspiration (Active Process)**: Diaphragm contracts (flattens), external intercostal muscles contract, expanding the thoracic cavity. Intrapleural pressure decreases, lung volume increases, and air flows in due to lower intrapulmonary pressure. **Expiration (Passive at Rest)**: Diaphragm relaxes (dome-shaped), intercostal muscles relax, thoracic cavity shrinks. Elastic recoil of lungs increases intrapulmonary pressure, forcing air out. Forced expiration involves abdominal muscles.

Card 6

Describe the process of digestion and absorption of carbohydrates in the human digestive system.

**Digestion**: 1. **Mouth**: Salivary amylase breaks starch into maltose/disaccharides. 2. **Stomach**: Acidic pH inactivates amylase; no further digestion. 3. **Small Intestine**: Pancreatic amylase continues starch digestion. Brush-border enzymes (maltase, lactase, sucrase) hydrolyze disaccharides into monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose). **Absorption**: Monosaccharides are absorbed via **secondary active transport (SGLT1 for glucose/galactose)** or **facilitated diffusion (GLUT5 for fructose)** into intestinal epithelial cells, then into blood via GLUT2.

Card 7

What is the nephron? Outline its key components and their functions.

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney (~1 million per kidney). Components: 1. **Glomerulus**: Capillary network; filters blood (ultrafiltration). 2. **Bowman’s Capsule**: Collects filtrate. 3. **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)**: Reabsorbs 65% of water, glucose, amino acids, and ions (Na⁺, K⁺). 4. **Loop of Henle**: Descending limb (water reabsorption), ascending limb (Na⁺/Cl⁻ reabsorption). 5. **Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)**: Regulates pH and electrolytes (aldosterone acts here). 6. **Collecting Duct**: Final water reabsorption (ADH-regulated); drains urine to renal pelvis.

Card 8

Explain the role of insulin and glucagon in blood glucose regulation.

**Insulin (Beta Cells of Pancreas)**: Lowers blood glucose by: 1. Promoting glucose uptake into cells (muscle/adipose). 2. Stimulating glycogenesis (glucose → glycogen in liver). 3. Inhibiting gluconeogenesis/glycogenolysis. **Glucagon (Alpha Cells of Pancreas)**: Raises blood glucose by: 1. Stimulating glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose in liver). 2. Promoting gluconeogenesis (non-carbs → glucose). 3. Inhibiting glycogenesis. **Balance**: Insulin dominates in fed state; glucagon in fasting state.

Card 9

What are the functions of the hypothalamus in human physiology?

The hypothalamus regulates: 1. **Homeostasis**: Body temperature, thirst, hunger, and circadian rhythms. 2. **Endocrine System**: Secretes releasing/inhibiting hormones (e.g., TRH, GnRH) to control the pituitary gland. 3. **Autonomic Nervous System**: Sympathetic/parasympathetic responses (e.g., heart rate, digestion). 4. **Emotional Behavior**: Links nervous and endocrine systems via limbic system. 5. **Reproduction**: Controls oxytocin (uterine contractions, lactation) and ADH (water balance).

Card 10

Describe the structure and function of a synapse in the nervous system.

**Structure**: A synapse is a junction between two neurons (or neuron-effector cell). Components: 1. **Presynaptic Neuron**: Releases neurotransmitters from synaptic vesicles. 2. **Synaptic Cleft**: 20-40 nm gap. 3. **Postsynaptic Neuron**: Contains receptors for neurotransmitters. **Function**: 1. Action potential arrives at presynaptic terminal → Ca²⁺ influx. 2. Neurotransmitter (e.g., acetylcholine, dopamine) released via exocytosis. 3. Neurotransmitter binds to postsynaptic receptors → excitatory/inhibitory response. 4. Neurotransmitter degraded or reuptaken to terminate signal.

Card 11

What is the role of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) in blood pressure regulation?

RAAS maintains blood pressure via: 1. **Renin Release**: Low blood pressure/Na⁺ triggers juxtaglomerular cells to release renin. 2. **Angiotensinogen → Angiotensin I**: Renin converts liver-produced angiotensinogen to Ang I. 3. **Ang I → Ang II**: ACE (lungs) converts Ang I to Ang II (vasoconstrictor). 4. **Effects of Ang II**: 1. Vasoconstriction (↑ BP). 2. Stimulates aldosterone release (adrenal cortex) → Na⁺/water reabsorption (↑ blood volume). 3. Stimulates ADH release (↑ water retention). 4. Triggers thirst (hypothalamus).

Card 12

Explain the process of erythropoiesis and the role of erythropoietin (EPO).

**Erythropoiesis**: Formation of red blood cells (RBCs) in red bone marrow. Stages: Hemocytoblast → Proerythroblast → Early/Late Erythroblast → Normoblast → Reticulocyte → Erythrocyte. **EPO Role**: 1. Kidneys release EPO in response to **hypoxia** (low O₂). 2. EPO stimulates proliferation/differentiation of erythroid progenitor cells. 3. Increases RBC production rate (3-5 days). **Clinical Relevance**: EPO used to treat anemia (e.g., in chronic kidney disease).

Card 13

Differentiate between humoral and cell-mediated immunity.

**Humoral Immunity (B Cells)**: 1. Targets **extracellular pathogens** (e.g., bacteria, toxins). 2. B cells produce **antibodies** (IgG, IgM, etc.) that neutralize/opsonize pathogens. 3. Memory B cells provide long-term immunity. **Cell-Mediated Immunity (T Cells)**: 1. Targets **intracellular pathogens** (e.g., viruses, cancer cells). 2. **Cytotoxic T Cells (CD8+)** kill infected cells via perforin/granzymes. 3. **Helper T Cells (CD4+)** secrete cytokines to activate B cells/macrophages. 4. No antibodies involved.
