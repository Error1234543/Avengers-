NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define reproduction and classify its two fundamental types with examples.

Reproduction is the biological process by which organisms produce offspring, ensuring species continuity. The two fundamental types are: 1. **Asexual reproduction**: Offspring arise from a single parent without gamete fusion, e.g., binary fission in *Amoeba*, budding in *Hydra*, and vegetative propagation in plants (e.g., potato tubers). 2. **Sexual reproduction**: Offspring result from the fusion of male and female gametes, e.g., humans, flowering plants (angiosperms), and earthworms. Involves meiosis and fertilization, introducing genetic variation.

Card 2

Explain the significance of meiosis in sexual reproduction. Why is it called a 'reductional division'?

Meiosis is critical in sexual reproduction because it: - Reduces chromosome number by half (diploid → haploid), ensuring gametes (sperm/egg) have **n** chromosomes. Fertilization restores the diploid (2n) state in the zygote. - Introduces **genetic variation** via crossing-over (prophase I) and independent assortment (metaphase I). It is called 'reductional division' because the chromosome number is halved during meiosis I (e.g., human cells: 46 → 23). Meiosis II is equational (chromatid separation).

Card 3

Compare spermatogenesis and oogenesis in humans with respect to: (a) Site of occurrence (b) Number of gametes produced per germ cell (c) Duration of process (d) Cytoplasmic division

**Spermatogenesis** (testes): - Produces **4 functional spermatozoa** per primary spermatocyte. - Continuous process (begins at puberty, lifelong). - Equal cytoplasmic division (spermatids → spermatozoa). **Oogenesis** (ovaries): - Produces **1 functional ovum** + 2-3 polar bodies per primary oocyte (polar bodies degenerate). - Discontinuous: begins prenatally (arrested at prophase I), resumes at puberty (meiosis I completes per menstrual cycle), meiosis II completes only if fertilization occurs. - Unequal cytoplasmic division (ovum retains most cytoplasm for zygote nutrition).

Card 4

Describe the structure of a human sperm with a labelled diagram (describe key parts).

A human sperm is a **motile, haploid gamete** with: 1. **Head**: Contains: - **Acrosome**: Cap-like structure with hydrolytic enzymes (e.g., hyaluronidase) to penetrate the ovum’s zona pellucida. - **Nucleus**: Haploid (23 chromosomes), densely packed DNA. 2. **Neck**: Connects head to middle piece; contains centrioles (essential for zygote spindle formation). 3. **Middle piece**: Packed with **mitochondria** (provide ATP for motility via axial filament). 4. **Tail (flagellum)**: 9+2 microtubule arrangement (axoneme) for propulsion; longest part (~50 µm). *Key adaptation*: Streamlined shape for motility; minimal cytoplasm to reduce weight.

Card 5

What is the menstrual cycle? Outline its phases and hormonal regulation.

The **menstrual cycle** (~28 days) is the cyclic preparation of the uterus for pregnancy, regulated by hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian hormones. Phases: 1. **Menstrual phase (Days 1-5)**: - Shedding of endometrium due to **low progesterone/estrogen** (corpus luteum regression). - **FSH** rises, stimulating follicle development. 2. **Follicular/Proliferative phase (Days 6-14)**: - **Estrogen** (from growing follicles) thickens endometrium. - **LH surge** (Day 14) triggers ovulation (release of secondary oocyte). 3. **Luteal/Secretory phase (Days 15-28)**: - **Progesterone** (from corpus luteum) maintains endometrium for implantation. - If no fertilization: corpus luteum degenerates → progesterone drops → menstruation. *Hormonal axis*: Hypothalamus (GnRH) → Anterior pituitary (FSH/LH) → Ovary (estrogen/progesterone).

Card 6

Explain the process of fertilization in humans. Where does it occur, and what are its outcomes?

**Fertilization** (ampulla of fallopian tube) involves: 1. **Acrosomal reaction**: Sperm releases enzymes to penetrate the ovum’s **zona pellucida** and **corona radiata**. 2. **Sperm-egg fusion**: Plasma membranes fuse; sperm nucleus enters ovum. 3. **Cortical reaction**: Ovum releases cortical granules → zona pellucida hardens (blocks polyspermy). 4. **Syngamy**: Haploid sperm (n) and egg (n) nuclei fuse → diploid zygote (2n). **Outcomes**: - Restoration of diploid chromosome number. - Determination of genetic sex (sperm contributes X or Y). - Initiation of cleavage (mitotic divisions).

Card 7

Describe the stages of embryonic development from zygote to gastrula in humans.

1. **Zygote (Day 0)**: Diploid cell formed post-fertilization. 2. **Cleavage**: Rapid mitotic divisions without growth (blastomeres form). - **Morula** (Day 3-4): 16-32 cells; solid ball. - **Blastocyst** (Day 5-6): Fluid-filled cavity (**blastocoel**) forms; two cell types: - **Trophoblast** (outer layer) → placenta. - **Inner cell mass (ICM)** → embryo. 3. **Implantation** (Day 6-7): Blastocyst attaches to endometrium (trophoblast secretes enzymes). 4. **Gastrulation** (Week 3): ICM rearranges into **3 germ layers** via primitive streak: - **Ectoderm**: Nervous system, skin. - **Mesoderm**: Muscles, bones, circulatory system. - **Endoderm**: Digestive/respiratory linings, liver, pancreas. *Key event*: Gastrula formation marks the start of organogenesis.

Card 8

What are the functions of the placenta in humans? List its endocrine secretions.

**Placenta** (formed by trophoblast + maternal endometrium) functions: 1. **Nutrient/gas exchange**: Oxygen/nutrients from mother → fetus; CO₂/waste from fetus → mother (via umbilical cord). 2. **Barrier**: Prevents mixing of maternal/fetal blood (except small molecules like glucose, antibodies). 3. **Endocrine role**: Secretes hormones critical for pregnancy: - **hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin): Maintains corpus luteum (progesterone production) in early pregnancy. - **Progesterone**: Maintains endometrium, suppresses uterine contractions. - **Estrogen**: Stimulates uterine growth, mammary gland development. - **hPL** (human placental lactogen): Regulates maternal metabolism (glucose/fatty acids for fetus). 4. **Immunological protection**: Blocks maternal immune rejection of fetus.

Card 9

Compare asexual and sexual reproduction in plants with examples.

**Asexual reproduction (vegetative propagation)**: Offspring genetically identical to parent. Examples: - **Natural**: Runners (strawberry), tubers (potato), bulbs (onion), rhizomes (ginger). - **Artificial**: Cutting (rose), grafting (mango), layering (jasmine), tissue culture (orchids). **Sexual reproduction**: Involves meiosis and fertilization; introduces genetic variation. Examples: - **Angiosperms**: Pollination → fertilization → seed/fruit formation (e.g., *Mangifera indica*). - **Gymnosperms**: Cones produce pollen (male) and ovules (female) (e.g., *Pinus*). *Key difference*: Asexual reproduction is rapid but lacks variation; sexual reproduction is slower but adaptive.
