NEET Biology: Biotechnology and Its Applications - Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define biotechnology and specify its two main branches as per NEET syllabus.

Biotechnology is the integration of natural sciences and engineering to utilize living organisms, cells, or their components to develop products and technologies for human welfare. The two main branches relevant to NEET are: (1) **Classical Biotechnology**, which involves traditional techniques like fermentation and selective breeding, and (2) **Modern Biotechnology**, which includes genetic engineering, recombinant DNA technology, and molecular diagnostics.

Card 2

What is recombinant DNA technology? Name the key enzymes involved in this process.

Recombinant DNA technology is a technique used to combine DNA molecules from different sources into a single molecule, typically to produce a desired protein or modify an organism's traits. Key enzymes involved are: (1) **Restriction Endonucleases** (e.g., EcoRI, HindIII) – cut DNA at specific sequences, (2) **DNA Ligase** – joins DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds, and (3) **DNA Polymerase** – synthesizes new DNA strands during PCR or cloning.

Card 3

Explain the role of plasmids in genetic engineering. Why are they preferred as vectors?

Plasmids are small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecules found in bacteria, independent of chromosomal DNA. In genetic engineering, they act as **vectors** to transfer foreign DNA into host cells. Plasmids are preferred because: (1) they are **easily isolatable and manipulable**, (2) they **replicate independently** within host cells, (3) they contain **selectable markers** (e.g., antibiotic resistance genes) to identify transformed cells, and (4) they can be **customized** with multiple cloning sites for inserting foreign DNA.

Card 4

What is PCR? Describe its three main steps and its applications in biotechnology.

PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) is a laboratory technique used to amplify a specific DNA segment exponentially. The three main steps are: (1) **Denaturation** (94–98°C) – DNA strands separate, (2) **Annealing** (50–65°C) – primers bind to target sequences, (3) **Extension** (72°C) – DNA polymerase synthesizes new strands. Applications include: DNA fingerprinting, disease diagnosis (e.g., COVID-19), gene cloning, forensic analysis, and studying ancient DNA.

Card 5

Differentiate between **genetically modified organisms (GMOs)** and **transgenic organisms**. Provide one example of each relevant to NEET.

**GMOs** are organisms whose genetic material has been altered using biotechnology, but not necessarily by introducing foreign DNA. Example: **Bt Cotton** – modified to express a bacterial toxin (Cry protein) for pest resistance, but no foreign gene from another species is introduced. **Transgenic organisms** are a subset of GMOs that contain **foreign DNA from another species**. Example: **Golden Rice** – engineered with genes from *Erwinia uredovora* and daffodils to produce beta-carotene (pro-vitamin A).

Card 6

What is **Bt toxin**? Explain its mode of action and its significance in agriculture.

**Bt toxin** is a protein produced by the bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* that acts as a natural insecticide. Its mode of action: (1) Ingested by insect larvae, (2) **alkaline pH of the insect gut** activates the protoxin, (3) activated toxin binds to **receptors on gut epithelial cells**, (4) forms pores in the cell membrane, leading to cell lysis and insect death. Significance: Used in **Bt crops** (e.g., Bt cotton, Bt brinjal) to reduce pesticide use, increase yield, and target specific pests (e.g., bollworms) without harming non-target organisms.

Card 7

Describe the process of **RNA interference (RNAi)** and its biotechnological applications.

RNA interference (RNAi) is a biological process where **small RNA molecules** (siRNA or miRNA) inhibit gene expression by degrading mRNA or blocking translation. Process: (1) **Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA)** is introduced or produced in the cell, (2) **Dicer enzyme** cleaves dsRNA into small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), (3) **RISC complex** incorporates siRNA and guides it to complementary mRNA, (4) mRNA is degraded or translation is inhibited. Applications: (1) **Gene silencing** for functional genomics, (2) **Pest control** (e.g., RNAi-based sprays to target crop pests), (3) **Therapeutics** (e.g., treating viral infections, cancer, or genetic disorders).

Card 8

What are **molecular diagnostics**? Name two techniques used in this field and their applications.

Molecular diagnostics involve techniques to analyze biological markers (DNA, RNA, proteins) for disease diagnosis, prognosis, or monitoring. Two key techniques: 1. **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)**: Used for detecting infectious agents (e.g., HIV, SARS-CoV-2), genetic disorders (e.g., sickle cell anemia), and cancer mutations. 2. **ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)**: Detects antibodies or antigens (e.g., HIV, hepatitis B) by using enzyme-linked antibodies and colorimetric reactions. Applications include early disease detection, personalized medicine, and forensic analysis.

Card 9

Explain the concept of **gene therapy**. Differentiate between somatic and germ-line gene therapy with examples.

Gene therapy is a technique to treat or prevent diseases by correcting defective genes. It involves inserting, altering, or removing genes within a patient's cells. **Somatic Gene Therapy**: Targets non-reproductive cells (e.g., blood, liver). Changes are **not heritable**. Example: **Luxturna** – treats inherited retinal dystrophy by delivering a functional *RPE65* gene to retinal cells. **Germ-line Gene Therapy**: Targets reproductive cells (sperm, egg) or embryos. Changes are **heritable**. Example: **CRISPR-edited embryos** (controversial, not clinically approved) to correct genetic disorders like beta-thalassemia. Note: Germ-line therapy is banned in many countries due to ethical concerns.

Card 10

What are **ethical issues** associated with biotechnology? List three concerns relevant to NEET.

Ethical issues in biotechnology include: 1. **Safety of GMOs**: Potential risks to human health (e.g., allergies) and environment (e.g., gene flow to wild relatives, impact on biodiversity). 2. **Patenting of Life Forms**: Controversy over patenting genetically modified organisms or genes (e.g., *BRCA1* gene patenting). 3. **Germ-line Gene Editing**: Concerns about **designer babies**, unintended mutations, and long-term effects on future generations. Other issues: **privacy** (genetic data misuse), **equity** (access to biotech therapies), and **animal welfare** (use in research).

Card 11

Describe the **Hershey-Chase experiment** and its significance in biotechnology.

The **Hershey-Chase experiment (1952)** demonstrated that DNA, not protein, is the genetic material. Process: (1) **Bacteriophages** (viruses infecting bacteria) were labeled with **radioactive phosphorus (³²P)** (DNA) or **radioactive sulfur (³⁵S)** (protein), (2) Phages infected *E. coli* bacteria, (3) Agitation separated phage coats from bacteria, (4) Only **³²P-labeled DNA** was found inside bacterial cells, proving DNA carries genetic information. Significance: Laid the foundation for **molecular biology** and **recombinant DNA technology**, enabling genetic engineering and biotechnology advancements.

Card 12

What is **CRISPR-Cas9**? Explain its mechanism and two biotechnological applications.

**CRISPR-Cas9** is a genome-editing tool derived from bacterial immune systems. Mechanism: (1) **Guide RNA (gRNA)** binds to a specific DNA sequence, (2) **Cas9 enzyme** cuts the DNA at the target site, (3) **Cellular repair mechanisms** (non-homologous end joining or homology-directed repair) introduce edits. Applications: 1. **Gene Editing in Agriculture**: Developing disease-resistant crops (e.g., wheat resistant to powdery mildew). 2. **Therapeutics**: Correcting genetic disorders (e.g., sickle cell anemia, beta-thalassemia) by editing patient cells *ex vivo* and reintroducing them.

Card 13

Define **biopiracy**. Provide an example relevant to India and discuss its implications.

**Biopiracy** is the unauthorized commercial use of biological resources or traditional knowledge from a country or community without fair compensation. Example: **Neem and Turmeric Cases** - **Neem**: US company WR Grace patented a neem-based pesticide (1995), despite neem's long-standing use in India. The patent was later revoked after legal challenges. - **Turmeric**: US Patent and Trademark Office granted a patent for turmeric's wound-healing properties (1995), which was revoked after India provided evidence of prior art. Implications: Exploits indigenous knowledge, undermines biodiversity conservation, and highlights the need for **Access and Benefit-Sharing (ABS)** mechanisms under the **Nagoya Protocol**.
