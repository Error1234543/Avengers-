NEET Biology: Cell Structure and Function - Exam-Focused Flashcards

NEETEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Define a cell according to the Cell Theory. Which scientists proposed it and in which year?

A cell is the basic structural, functional, and biological unit of all living organisms. The Cell Theory states that: (1) All living organisms are composed of one or more cells, (2) The cell is the basic unit of life, and (3) All cells arise from pre-existing cells. It was proposed by Matthias Schleiden (1838) and Theodor Schwann (1839), with Rudolf Virchow later contributing the third tenet in 1855.

Card 2

Differentiate between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells based on the presence of a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Prokaryotic cells lack a true nucleus (DNA is in the nucleoid region) and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., mitochondria, Golgi apparatus). Eukaryotic cells have a well-defined nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane and contain membrane-bound organelles. Prokaryotes include bacteria and archaea, while eukaryotes include plants, animals, fungi, and protists.

Card 3

Name the three components of the cell envelope in a prokaryotic cell and describe their functions.

1. **Glycocalyx**: Outermost layer (capsule or slime layer) for protection, adhesion, and evading host immune responses. 2. **Cell Wall**: Provides structural support and shape; made of peptidoglycan in bacteria. 3. **Plasma Membrane**: Selectively permeable barrier regulating transport of substances in and out of the cell.

Card 4

What is the function of the nucleolus, and where is it located? Mention its role in ribosome synthesis.

The nucleolus is a dense, spherical structure within the nucleus. It synthesizes ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and assembles ribosomal subunits (large and small) by combining rRNA with proteins imported from the cytoplasm. These subunits are then exported to the cytoplasm to form functional ribosomes.

Card 5

Explain the structure and function of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Differentiate between rough ER and smooth ER.

The ER is a network of membranous tubules and sacs (cisternae) continuous with the nuclear envelope. **Rough ER (RER)**: Studded with ribosomes; synthesizes proteins (e.g., secretory proteins, membrane proteins) and modifies them (e.g., glycosylation). **Smooth ER (SER)**: Lacks ribosomes; synthesizes lipids (e.g., steroids, phospholipids), detoxifies drugs/toxins (liver cells), and stores calcium ions (muscle cells).

Card 6

Describe the structure of the Golgi apparatus and its role in protein modification and sorting.

The Golgi apparatus consists of flattened, stacked membranous sacs (cisternae) with distinct cis (receiving), medial, and trans (shipping) faces. It modifies proteins and lipids received from the ER (e.g., glycosylation, sulfation), sorts them, and packages them into vesicles for transport to their destinations (e.g., lysosomes, plasma membrane, secretion).

Card 7

What are lysosomes? Explain their formation and functions, including their role in autophagy.

Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles containing hydrolytic enzymes (e.g., proteases, lipases) that digest macromolecules. They form by budding from the Golgi apparatus. Functions: (1) **Intracellular digestion**: Break down ingested materials (phagocytosis) or damaged organelles (autophagy). (2) **Apoptosis**: Release enzymes to degrade cellular components during programmed cell death. (3) **Defense**: Destroy pathogens in immune cells (e.g., macrophages).

Card 8

Compare the structure and function of mitochondria and chloroplasts. Mention their evolutionary origin.

**Mitochondria**: Double-membraned (outer smooth, inner folded into cristae); site of aerobic respiration (ATP synthesis via oxidative phosphorylation). Contain their own circular DNA and ribosomes. **Chloroplasts**: Double-membraned with thylakoids stacked into grana (site of photosynthesis); contain chlorophyll and their own DNA/ribosomes. Both are semi-autonomous organelles believed to have originated from endosymbiotic bacteria (endosymbiotic theory).

Card 9

What are peroxisomes? Describe their functions and the enzymes they contain.

Peroxisomes are single-membrane-bound organelles containing oxidative enzymes (e.g., catalase, urate oxidase). Functions: (1) **Detoxification**: Break down hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) into water and oxygen using catalase. (2) **Lipid metabolism**: Oxidize fatty acids (β-oxidation) to produce acetyl-CoA. (3) **Photorespiration**: In plant cells, oxidize glycolate produced during photosynthesis.

Card 10

Explain the structure and function of the cytoskeleton. Name its three main components and their roles.

The cytoskeleton is a dynamic network of protein filaments providing structural support, shape, and motility. Components: 1. **Microfilaments (Actin filaments)**: 7 nm diameter; involved in cell movement (e.g., muscle contraction, cytokinesis), cytoplasmic streaming, and maintaining cell shape. 2. **Intermediate filaments**: 8–12 nm diameter; provide mechanical strength (e.g., keratin in epithelial cells, neurofilaments in neurons). 3. **Microtubules**: 25 nm diameter; hollow tubes of tubulin; form spindle fibers during cell division, maintain cell shape, and serve as tracks for organelle movement (e.g., kinesin/dynein motors).

Card 11

Describe the structure of a plant cell wall. Mention its composition and functions.

The plant cell wall is a rigid, extracellular layer composed primarily of **cellulose** microfibrils embedded in a matrix of **hemicellulose**, **pectin**, and **proteins**. Layers: (1) **Primary wall**: Thin, flexible, laid down during cell growth. (2) **Secondary wall**: Thicker, deposited after cell growth; may contain **lignin** (for strength) or **suberin** (for waterproofing). Functions: Provides structural support, protects against pathogens, prevents excessive water uptake, and determines cell shape.

Card 12

What are plasmodesmata? Explain their structure and role in plant cells.

Plasmodesmata are cytoplasmic channels traversing plant cell walls, connecting adjacent cells. Structure: Lined by the plasma membrane, with a central desmotubule (derived from the ER) and a cytoplasmic sleeve. Function: Enable intercellular transport of water, ions, metabolites, hormones, and signaling molecules (e.g., proteins, RNA), facilitating cell-to-cell communication and coordination in tissues.

Card 13

Differentiate between cilia and flagella in eukaryotic cells. Mention their structural basis and functions.

**Cilia**: Short, hair-like projections (5–10 µm) present in large numbers; beat in coordinated waves (e.g., respiratory tract to move mucus). **Flagella**: Longer (100–200 µm), usually one or few per cell; undulate for locomotion (e.g., sperm tail). Both have a **9+2 arrangement** of microtubules (9 doublets surrounding 2 singlets) anchored by a basal body (9 triplets of microtubules). Dynein arms generate bending motion.

Card 14

What is the fluid mosaic model of the plasma membrane? Name its key components and their functions.

The fluid mosaic model describes the plasma membrane as a fluid lipid bilayer with embedded proteins. Key components: 1. **Phospholipids**: Amphipathic molecules forming the bilayer (hydrophilic heads, hydrophobic tails). 2. **Proteins**: (a) **Integral proteins**: Span the membrane (e.g., transport proteins, receptors). (b) **Peripheral proteins**: Loosely attached to the surface (e.g., enzymes, signaling molecules). 3. **Cholesterol**: Stabilizes membrane fluidity. 4. **Glycolipids/Glycoproteins**: Carbohydrate chains for cell recognition (e.g., blood group antigens). Functions: Selective permeability, signal transduction, cell adhesion, and transport.

Card 15

Explain the process of active transport across the plasma membrane. Give an example.

Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient using energy (ATP). Types: 1. **Primary active transport**: Directly uses ATP (e.g., **Na⁺-K⁺ pump**: 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in per ATP hydrolyzed, maintaining electrochemical gradients). 2. **Secondary active transport**: Uses energy from an electrochemical gradient established by primary transport (e.g., **SGLT1** in intestinal cells co-transports Na⁺ and glucose into cells).
