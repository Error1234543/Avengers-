PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Structural Organisation in Animals and Plants (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) based on the NEET exam pattern. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. Which of the following tissues in plants provides mechanical strength and is primarily composed of dead cells?

A. Parenchyma
B. Collenchyma
C. Sclerenchyma
D. Xylem
Answer: C

Sclerenchyma tissue is composed of dead cells with thick, lignified secondary walls that provide mechanical strength to plants. Parenchyma and collenchyma are living tissues, while xylem primarily conducts water and minerals, though it also provides some mechanical support.

2. In animals, which type of epithelial tissue is found lining the inner surface of the stomach and intestines, and is specialized for secretion and absorption?

A. Simple squamous epithelium
B. Stratified squamous epithelium
C. Simple columnar epithelium
D. Cuboidal epithelium
Answer: C

Simple columnar epithelium is found in the stomach and intestines, where its tall, closely packed cells facilitate secretion and absorption. Simple squamous epithelium is thin and suited for diffusion, while stratified squamous epithelium is protective, and cuboidal epithelium is typically found in glands.

3. The vascular bundle in dicot stems is typically described as:

A. Radial with exarch xylem
B. Conjoint, collateral, and open
C. Concentric with amphivasal arrangement
D. Closed and scattered
Answer: B

In dicot stems, vascular bundles are conjoint (xylem and phloem together), collateral (xylem on the inner side and phloem on the outer side), and open (cambium present between xylem and phloem). Radial bundles are found in roots, and scattered bundles are characteristic of monocot stems.

4. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of meristematic tissues in plants?

A. Thin cell walls with abundant cytoplasm
B. Presence of large vacuoles
C. High mitotic activity
D. Lack of intercellular spaces
Answer: B

Meristematic tissues are characterized by thin cell walls, dense cytoplasm, high mitotic activity, and lack of intercellular spaces. Large vacuoles are typically absent in meristematic cells, as they are found in mature, differentiated cells.

5. In animals, the tissue responsible for the transmission of impulses and coordination of body functions is:

A. Epithelial tissue
B. Connective tissue
C. Muscular tissue
D. Nervous tissue
Answer: D

Nervous tissue is specialized for the transmission of electrical impulses and coordination of body functions. Epithelial tissue covers surfaces, connective tissue supports and connects, and muscular tissue is responsible for movement.

6. The casparian strip in plant roots is primarily composed of:

A. Cellulose
B. Lignin
C. Suberin
D. Pectin
Answer: C

The casparian strip is a band of suberin, a waxy substance, found in the radial and transverse walls of the endodermis in plant roots. It blocks the apoplastic pathway, forcing water and solutes to pass through the symplast, thus regulating their entry into the vascular tissue.

7. Which of the following statements about connective tissue in animals is INCORRECT?

A. It includes bone, cartilage, and blood
B. It is characterized by an abundance of extracellular matrix
C. It is primarily involved in contraction and movement
D. It provides structural support and connects different tissues
Answer: C

Connective tissue is characterized by an abundance of extracellular matrix and includes bone, cartilage, and blood. It provides structural support and connects different tissues. However, contraction and movement are functions of muscular tissue, not connective tissue.

8. In plants, the phloem is primarily responsible for the transport of:

A. Water and minerals from roots to leaves
B. Organic nutrients, particularly sucrose, from leaves to other parts of the plant
C. Oxygen from leaves to roots
D. Carbon dioxide from roots to leaves
Answer: B

Phloem is the vascular tissue in plants responsible for the transport of organic nutrients, particularly sucrose, from the leaves (source) to other parts of the plant (sink). Water and minerals are transported by the xylem, while oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged through stomata.

9. The tissue in animals that stores fat and provides insulation is called:

A. Areolar tissue
B. Adipose tissue
C. Dense regular connective tissue
D. Reticular tissue
Answer: B

Adipose tissue is a type of connective tissue that stores fat and provides insulation, cushioning, and energy reserves. Areolar tissue fills spaces between organs, dense regular connective tissue forms tendons and ligaments, and reticular tissue supports lymphoid organs.

10. In animals, the type of muscle tissue that is striated, multinucleated, and under voluntary control is:

A. Smooth muscle
B. Cardiac muscle
C. Skeletal muscle
D. Visceral muscle
Answer: C

Skeletal muscle tissue is striated, multinucleated, and under voluntary control. Smooth muscle is non-striated and involuntary, cardiac muscle is striated and involuntary, and visceral muscle is another term for smooth muscle.

11. The tissue in plants that is responsible for the lateral transport of water and nutrients across the root is:

A. Epidermis
B. Cortex
C. Endodermis
D. Pericycle
Answer: B

The cortex in plant roots is responsible for the lateral transport of water and nutrients from the epidermis to the vascular tissue. The endodermis regulates the passage of substances into the vascular cylinder, while the pericycle gives rise to lateral roots.

12. In dicot stems, the vascular bundles are typically:

A. Scattered throughout the ground tissue
B. Arranged in a ring with cambium between xylem and phloem
C. Surrounded by a sclerenchymatous bundle sheath
D. Collateral, closed, and endarch
Answer: B

In dicot stems, vascular bundles are arranged in a ring (eustele) and are collateral (xylem and phloem on the same radius) and open (cambium present between xylem and phloem). This arrangement allows for secondary growth. Monocot stems, in contrast, have scattered vascular bundles (atactostele).

13. Which of the following tissues in plants is responsible for the storage of starch and is often found in the cortex of stems and roots?

A. Collenchyma
B. Parenchyma
C. Sclerenchyma
D. Xylem parenchyma
Answer: B

Parenchyma cells are living, thin-walled cells that perform various functions, including storage of starch, proteins, and fats. They are commonly found in the cortex of stems and roots. Collenchyma provides mechanical support, sclerenchyma provides rigidity, and xylem parenchyma is involved in storage but is specific to the xylem tissue.

14. The pericycle in dicot roots is a layer of cells that:

A. Gives rise to lateral roots and secondary growth
B. Forms the outermost layer of the vascular cylinder
C. Is responsible for the absorption of water and minerals
D. Differentiates into the epidermis
Answer: A

The pericycle is a layer of meristematic cells located just inside the endodermis in dicot roots. It gives rise to lateral roots and contributes to secondary growth by forming the vascular cambium and cork cambium. It is not involved in absorption or differentiation into the epidermis.

15. Which of the following statements about the epidermis in plants is INCORRECT?

A. It is usually covered by a waxy cuticle to reduce water loss
B. It contains stomata for gas exchange
C. It is composed of multiple layers of cells in most plants
D. It may bear trichomes for protection and reducing transpiration
Answer: C

The epidermis in plants is typically a single layer of cells (uniseriate) that covers the plant body. It is covered by a waxy cuticle to reduce water loss, contains stomata for gas exchange, and may bear trichomes for protection. The statement that it is composed of multiple layers is incorrect for most plants.

16. In animal tissues, which of the following is a characteristic feature of connective tissue?

A. Presence of tightly packed cells with minimal intercellular matrix
B. Presence of a well-developed intercellular matrix with scattered cells
C. Ability to conduct electrical impulses
D. Presence of contractile proteins for movement
Answer: B

Connective tissue is characterized by a well-developed intercellular matrix (composed of fibers and ground substance) with scattered cells. This matrix provides structural support and connects different tissues. Tightly packed cells are a feature of epithelial tissue, electrical impulse conduction is a feature of nervous tissue, and contractile proteins are found in muscle tissue.

17. The Haversian system (osteon) is a structural unit of:

A. Cartilage
B. Spongy bone
C. Compact bone
D. Blood
Answer: C

The Haversian system, or osteon, is the fundamental structural unit of compact bone. It consists of concentric layers of bone matrix (lamellae) surrounding a central Haversian canal, which contains blood vessels and nerves. Cartilage lacks Haversian systems, spongy bone has trabeculae instead, and blood is a fluid connective tissue.

18. Which of the following cells in animal tissues is responsible for the secretion of antibodies?

A. Mast cells
B. Plasma cells
C. Macrophages
D. Fibroblasts
Answer: B

Plasma cells are differentiated B lymphocytes that secrete large quantities of antibodies during the immune response. Mast cells are involved in allergic reactions, macrophages are phagocytic cells, and fibroblasts synthesize extracellular matrix and collagen.

19. In plant anatomy, the casparian strip is a band of suberin found in the:

A. Epidermis of roots
B. Endodermis of roots
C. Pericycle of stems
D. Cortex of stems
Answer: B

The casparian strip is a band of suberin (a waxy substance) found in the radial and transverse walls of the endodermis in roots. It blocks the apoplastic pathway of water and forces water and dissolved minerals to pass through the symplast, thereby regulating their entry into the vascular cylinder.

20. Which of the following is NOT a function of the epithelial tissue in animals?

A. Protection of underlying tissues
B. Secretion of hormones and enzymes
C. Contraction for movement
D. Absorption of nutrients
Answer: C

Epithelial tissue functions include protection, secretion, absorption, and sensation, but it does not have a role in contraction for movement. Contraction is a function of muscle tissue, which contains contractile proteins like actin and myosin.

21. The type of animal tissue that lines the inner surface of blood vessels and the heart is:

A. Simple squamous epithelium
B. Stratified squamous epithelium
C. Cuboidal epithelium
D. Columnar epithelium
Answer: A

The inner surface of blood vessels and the heart is lined by simple squamous epithelium, also known as endothelium. This thin, single layer of flat cells facilitates the exchange of substances and reduces friction for smooth blood flow. Stratified squamous epithelium is found in areas subject to abrasion, while cuboidal and columnar epithelia are involved in secretion and absorption.

22. In dicot leaves, the mesophyll is differentiated into:

A. Palisade parenchyma and spongy parenchyma
B. Upper epidermis and lower epidermis
C. Xylem and phloem
D. Collenchyma and sclerenchyma
Answer: A

In dicot leaves, the mesophyll (the ground tissue between the upper and lower epidermis) is differentiated into palisade parenchyma (elongated cells rich in chloroplasts for photosynthesis) and spongy parenchyma (loosely arranged cells with air spaces for gas exchange). The other options refer to different tissues or structures.

23. Which of the following statements about sclerenchyma in plants is correct?

A. It is composed of living cells with thickened primary walls
B. It provides mechanical support due to lignified secondary walls
C. It is commonly found in the epidermis of leaves
D. It is involved in the storage of starch
Answer: B

Sclerenchyma is a type of plant tissue composed of dead cells with thick, lignified secondary walls. It provides mechanical support and rigidity to plant parts. It is not involved in storage (parenchyma performs this function) and is not found in the epidermis. Collenchyma, not sclerenchyma, has thickened primary walls and is composed of living cells.

24. In dicotyledonous plants, the vascular cambium is typically:

A. A primary meristem located between the xylem and phloem in vascular bundles.
B. A secondary meristem derived from the procambium and responsible for secondary growth.
C. A type of lateral meristem that produces only secondary xylem toward the inside.
D. A temporary meristematic tissue that degenerates after primary growth is complete.
Answer: B

The vascular cambium in dicotyledonous plants is a secondary meristem that originates from the procambium (a primary meristem) and is responsible for secondary growth. It produces secondary xylem toward the inside and secondary phloem toward the outside, increasing the girth of the plant. Option A is incorrect because the vascular cambium is not a primary meristem. Option C is incorrect because the cambium produces both secondary xylem and phloem. Option D is incorrect because the vascular cambium is a permanent meristematic tissue that remains active throughout the plant's life.
