PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Plant Physiology (Part 1 of 25)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Plant Physiology, designed to match the difficulty and style of NEET Biology. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following pigments is primarily responsible for the phototropic response in plants?

A. Chlorophyll a
B. Chlorophyll b
C. Carotenoids
D. Phototropin
Answer: D

Phototropin is the blue-light photoreceptor protein that mediates phototropism, the growth of plants toward light. While chlorophylls and carotenoids are involved in photosynthesis, they do not directly regulate phototropic responses.

2. A plant shows wilting despite adequate water supply. Which hormone deficiency is most likely responsible for this condition?

A. Auxin
B. Gibberellin
C. Cytokinin
D. Abscisic acid
Answer: C

Cytokinins promote cell division and delay senescence, playing a key role in maintaining turgor pressure in plant cells. A deficiency can lead to wilting even when water is available. Auxin regulates growth, gibberellin promotes stem elongation, and abscisic acid induces stomatal closure during water stress.

3. In C4 plants, the enzyme PEP carboxylase fixes CO2 into which of the following compounds in the mesophyll cells?

A. Oxaloacetate
B. Malate
C. Pyruvate
D. 3-Phosphoglycerate
Answer: A

PEP carboxylase catalyzes the fixation of CO2 to phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) to form oxaloacetate in the mesophyll cells of C4 plants. Oxaloacetate is then converted to malate or aspartate for transport to bundle-sheath cells.

4. A farmer observes that his crop plants exhibit excessive stem elongation, thin stems, and pale leaves. Which hormone is most likely being overproduced in these plants?

A. Auxin
B. Gibberellin
C. Ethylene
D. Abscisic acid
Answer: B

Gibberellins promote stem elongation, and their overproduction leads to excessive growth, thin stems, and pale leaves due to rapid cell division and elongation. Auxin regulates growth direction, ethylene promotes fruit ripening, and abscisic acid induces dormancy.

5. Which of the following is NOT a function of stomata in plants?

A. Regulation of gas exchange
B. Transpiration
C. Absorption of mineral nutrients
D. Prevention of water loss
Answer: C

Stomata regulate gas exchange (CO2 uptake and O2 release), facilitate transpiration (water loss), and help prevent excessive water loss by closing. However, they do not absorb mineral nutrients, which occurs primarily through root hairs.

6. In CAM plants, malate is stored in which cellular compartment during the night?

A. Cytosol
B. Chloroplast
C. Vacuole
D. Mitochondria
Answer: C

CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) plants fix CO2 at night into malate, which is stored in the vacuole. During the day, malate is decarboxylated to release CO2 for the Calvin cycle, minimizing water loss.

7. Which of the following processes is directly inhibited by the presence of abscisic acid (ABA) in plants?

A. Seed germination
B. Fruit ripening
C. Leaf senescence
D. Root elongation
Answer: A

Abscisic acid (ABA) inhibits seed germination and promotes dormancy. It also induces stomatal closure during water stress but does not directly inhibit fruit ripening (ethylene does) or root elongation (auxin regulates).

8. The movement of sucrose from the source (e.g., leaves) to the sink (e.g., roots) in plants occurs through which of the following pathways?

A. Xylem
B. Phloem
C. Apoplast
D. Symplast
Answer: B

Sucrose is transported from source to sink tissues via the phloem, specifically through sieve tubes. The xylem transports water and minerals, while apoplast and symplast are pathways for water movement within tissues.

9. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of the C4 photosynthetic pathway?

A. Photorespiration is completely absent.
B. CO2 fixation occurs only in bundle-sheath cells.
C. PEP carboxylase has a low affinity for CO2.
D. It is more efficient than C3 plants in hot and dry conditions.
Answer: D

C4 plants are more efficient than C3 plants in hot and dry conditions due to their ability to concentrate CO2 in bundle-sheath cells, reducing photorespiration. However, photorespiration is not completely absent. PEP carboxylase has a high affinity for CO2, and CO2 fixation initially occurs in mesophyll cells.

10. Which of the following hormones is synthesized in the roots and transported to other parts of the plant to promote cell division?

A. Auxin
B. Gibberellin
C. Cytokinin
D. Ethylene
Answer: C

Cytokinins are primarily synthesized in root meristems and transported via the xylem to promote cell division and delay senescence. Auxin is synthesized in shoot tips, gibberellins in young tissues, and ethylene is a gaseous hormone produced in various tissues.

11. A plant exhibits a condition where its leaves turn yellow due to the breakdown of chlorophyll, but the veins remain green. This symptom is most likely caused by a deficiency of which nutrient?

A. Nitrogen
B. Magnesium
C. Iron
D. Potassium
Answer: B

Magnesium is a central component of the chlorophyll molecule. Its deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis (yellowing of leaf tissue between veins while veins remain green). Nitrogen deficiency causes uniform yellowing, iron deficiency causes chlorosis in young leaves, and potassium deficiency causes marginal scorching.

12. Which of the following statements correctly describes the role of abscisic acid (ABA) in plants?

A. ABA promotes seed germination by breaking dormancy.
B. ABA is synthesized in response to water stress and induces stomatal closure.
C. ABA enhances cell elongation and stem growth under optimal conditions.
D. ABA is primarily involved in phototropism and gravitropism.
Answer: B

Abscisic acid (ABA) is a plant hormone that plays a crucial role in responding to environmental stresses, particularly water stress. It is synthesized in roots and leaves and induces stomatal closure to reduce water loss through transpiration. ABA also promotes seed dormancy and inhibits germination under unfavorable conditions, contrary to option A. It does not enhance cell elongation (option C) or directly influence phototropism/gravitropism (option D).

13. The process of photophosphorylation in photosynthesis involves:

A. The reduction of NADP+ to NADPH in the stroma.
B. The synthesis of ATP using light energy in the thylakoid membrane.
C. The fixation of carbon dioxide into glucose in the Calvin cycle.
D. The breakdown of water molecules to release oxygen in the lumen.
Answer: B

Photophosphorylation is the process by which light energy is used to synthesize ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate during the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis. This occurs in the thylakoid membrane of chloroplasts. Option A describes the formation of NADPH, not photophosphorylation. Option C refers to the Calvin cycle, and option D describes photolysis of water, both of which are distinct processes.

14. In C4 plants, the enzyme PEP carboxylase is localized in the:

A. Mesophyll cells, where it fixes CO2 into oxaloacetate.
B. Bundle-sheath cells, where it participates in the Calvin cycle.
C. Stroma of chloroplasts, where it catalyzes the formation of 3-PGA.
D. Thylakoid membrane, where it aids in photophosphorylation.
Answer: A

In C4 plants, PEP carboxylase is located in the mesophyll cells, where it fixes carbon dioxide into a four-carbon compound, oxaloacetate, which is then converted to malate or aspartate. This is the first step of the C4 pathway, which helps concentrate CO2 in bundle-sheath cells for the Calvin cycle. PEP carboxylase is not involved in the Calvin cycle (option B), stroma reactions (option C), or photophosphorylation (option D).

15. Which of the following is NOT a function of the plant hormone ethylene?

A. Promoting fruit ripening.
B. Inducing leaf abscission.
C. Stimulating seed germination.
D. Inhibiting root elongation.
Answer: C

Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone known for its roles in promoting fruit ripening (option A), inducing leaf abscission (option B), and inhibiting root elongation (option D). However, ethylene does not stimulate seed germination; in fact, it often inhibits germination and is involved in seed dormancy. Seed germination is primarily promoted by gibberellins and inhibited by abscisic acid.

16. The apoplastic pathway of water movement in plants involves:

A. Movement of water through the cytoplasm of cells via plasmodesmata.
B. Movement of water through the cell walls and intercellular spaces.
C. Active transport of water across cell membranes using aquaporins.
D. Movement of water through the xylem vessels under negative pressure.
Answer: B

The apoplastic pathway refers to the movement of water through the non-living parts of plant tissue, specifically the cell walls and intercellular spaces. This pathway does not involve crossing cell membranes. Option A describes the symplastic pathway, option C refers to transmembrane transport, and option D describes water movement in the xylem, which is a separate process.

17. During the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis, the primary electron donor is:

A. Carbon dioxide (CO2).
B. Oxygen (O2).
C. Water (H2O).
D. NADPH.
Answer: C

In the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis, water (H2O) acts as the primary electron donor. It is split during photolysis, releasing electrons, protons, and oxygen. The electrons are then transferred through the electron transport chain in the thylakoid membrane. Carbon dioxide (option A) is used in the Calvin cycle, oxygen (option B) is a byproduct, and NADPH (option D) is an electron carrier produced during the light-dependent reactions.

18. Which of the following statements about the Calvin cycle is INCORRECT?

A. The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma of chloroplasts.
B. The enzyme RuBisCO catalyzes the carboxylation of RuBP.
C. The cycle requires ATP and NADPH produced in the light-dependent reactions.
D. The primary product of the Calvin cycle is glucose, which is directly synthesized in the cycle.
Answer: D

The primary product of the Calvin cycle is glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), a three-carbon sugar, not glucose. Glucose is synthesized later through additional metabolic pathways. The other options are correct: the Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma (option A), RuBisCO catalyzes the carboxylation of RuBP (option B), and the cycle requires ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions (option C).

19. In plants, the phytochrome system is primarily involved in:

A. Regulating stomatal opening in response to light intensity.
B. Mediating photoperiodic responses such as flowering.
C. Facilitating the absorption of blue light for photosynthesis.
D. Controlling the synthesis of chlorophyll in leaves.
Answer: B

The phytochrome system is a photoreceptor system in plants that primarily mediates photoperiodic responses, such as flowering, seed germination, and stem elongation. It detects red and far-red light and plays a key role in regulating developmental processes based on day length. Option A describes the role of blue light receptors like phototropins, option C is incorrect as phytochromes do not absorb blue light, and option D is unrelated to phytochrome function.

20. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) plants?

A. They fix CO2 into oxaloacetate in mesophyll cells during the day.
B. They open their stomata during the day to maximize CO2 uptake.
C. They store malic acid in vacuoles at night and release CO2 during the day.
D. They lack the enzyme PEP carboxylase and rely solely on RuBisCO for CO2 fixation.
Answer: C

CAM plants are adapted to arid environments and open their stomata at night to minimize water loss. They fix CO2 into malic acid using PEP carboxylase at night and store it in vacuoles. During the day, malic acid is decarboxylated to release CO2, which is then fixed by RuBisCO in the Calvin cycle. Option A describes C4 plants, option B is incorrect as CAM plants open stomata at night, and option D is false as CAM plants do possess PEP carboxylase.

21. The process of vernalization in plants refers to:

A. The induction of flowering by exposure to a period of low temperature.
B. The breaking of seed dormancy by exposure to light.
C. The acceleration of fruit ripening by ethylene treatment.
D. The promotion of root growth by auxin application.
Answer: A

Vernalization is the process by which the flowering process of a plant is accelerated by exposing it to a prolonged period of low temperatures, typically during winter. This is common in biennial and some perennial plants. Option B describes photoblasty, option C refers to ethylene's role in fruit ripening, and option D describes the effect of auxins on root growth.

22. Which of the following is NOT a direct product of the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis?

A. Oxygen (O2).
B. ATP.
C. NADPH.
D. Glucose.
Answer: D

The light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis produce oxygen (O2) as a byproduct of water splitting, ATP through photophosphorylation, and NADPH as an electron carrier. Glucose is not a direct product of the light-dependent reactions; it is synthesized later in the Calvin cycle using ATP and NADPH. Thus, option D is correct.

23. In plants, the hormone cytokinin is primarily responsible for:

A. Promoting cell elongation and stem growth.
B. Stimulating cell division and delaying senescence.
C. Inducing stomatal closure during water stress.
D. Inhibiting seed germination and promoting dormancy.
Answer: B

Cytokinins are plant hormones that primarily promote cell division (cytokinesis) and delay senescence (aging) in plant tissues. They also play roles in shoot initiation and growth. Option A describes the function of auxins, option C is the role of abscisic acid, and option D is incorrect as cytokinins do not inhibit germination or promote dormancy.

24. In the process of photorespiration, which of the following organelles is NOT directly involved?

A. Chloroplast
B. Peroxisome
C. Mitochondrion
D. Golgi apparatus
Answer: D

Photorespiration is a metabolic pathway that occurs in plants under high oxygen and low carbon dioxide conditions. It involves a coordinated interplay between three organelles: the chloroplast (where oxygenation of RuBP occurs via Rubisco), the peroxisome (where glycolate is oxidized and converted to glyoxylate), and the mitochondrion (where glycine is converted to serine, releasing CO2 and NH3). The Golgi apparatus, which is primarily involved in protein modification and sorting, does not participate in photorespiration. Thus, the correct answer is D.
