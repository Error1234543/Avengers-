This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed to mimic the style, difficulty, and coverage of NEET previous-year questions (PYQs) for the topic 'Biology and Human Welfare'. Each question carries 4 marks with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. Which of the following is a CORRECT match of a disease with its causative organism and primary mode of transmission?

A. Filariasis – Wuchereria bancrofti – Mosquito bite
B. Dengue – Plasmodium vivax – Contaminated water
C. Cholera – Vibrio cholerae – Air droplets
D. Tuberculosis – Mycobacterium tuberculosis – Sexual contact
Answer: A

Filariasis is caused by the nematode Wuchereria bancrofti and is transmitted through the bite of infected Culex mosquitoes. Dengue is caused by the dengue virus and transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes, not contaminated water. Cholera is caused by Vibrio cholerae and spreads through contaminated water, not air droplets. Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and primarily spreads through air droplets, not sexual contact.

2. The process of 'vaccination' primarily works by:

A. Directly killing the pathogen in the body
B. Stimulating the immune system to produce memory cells against the pathogen
C. Providing antibiotics to fight the infection
D. Increasing the body's temperature to kill the pathogen
Answer: B

Vaccination introduces antigens (weakened or dead pathogens or their components) into the body to stimulate the immune system to produce memory B-cells and T-cells. These memory cells enable a faster and stronger immune response upon subsequent exposure to the actual pathogen, providing immunity without causing the disease.

3. A farmer notices that his crops are being destroyed by a large number of insects. Which of the following biological control methods would be most appropriate to manage this pest population without harming the environment?

A. Spraying broad-spectrum chemical pesticides
B. Introducing a natural predator of the insect pest
C. Using genetically modified crops resistant to all insects
D. Burning the entire crop field to eliminate the insects
Answer: B

Biological control involves the use of natural predators, parasites, or pathogens to regulate pest populations. Introducing a natural predator of the insect pest is an environmentally friendly method that avoids the harmful effects of chemical pesticides or extreme measures like burning crops. Broad-spectrum pesticides can harm non-target species, and genetically modified crops may have unintended ecological consequences.

4. Which of the following is NOT a function of the World Health Organization (WHO)?

A. Coordinating international responses to global health emergencies
B. Setting global health standards and guidelines
C. Manufacturing and distributing vaccines to all countries
D. Monitoring and reporting on global health trends
Answer: C

The WHO coordinates international health efforts, sets standards, and monitors global health trends, but it does not manufacture or distribute vaccines directly to all countries. Vaccine production and distribution are typically handled by national governments, pharmaceutical companies, or partnerships like GAVI (the Vaccine Alliance).

5. The term 'nosocomial infection' refers to:

A. An infection acquired from contaminated food
B. An infection acquired during a hospital stay
C. An infection transmitted by vectors like mosquitoes
D. An infection caused by a virus
Answer: B

Nosocomial infections, also known as hospital-acquired infections, are infections that patients acquire during the course of receiving treatment for other conditions within a healthcare setting. These infections are not present or incubating at the time of admission and typically manifest 48 hours or more after admission.

6. Which of the following is a CORRECT statement about 'transgenic animals'?

A. They are produced by introducing foreign DNA into their genome
B. They are always resistant to all diseases
C. They are created exclusively for agricultural purposes
D. They cannot pass the introduced gene to their offspring
Answer: A

Transgenic animals are produced by introducing foreign DNA (a transgene) into their genome using techniques like microinjection or embryonic stem cell manipulation. This foreign DNA can be passed to their offspring. Transgenic animals are used in research, medicine (e.g., producing therapeutic proteins), and agriculture, but they are not inherently resistant to all diseases.

7. A patient is diagnosed with a disease caused by a deficiency of insulin. Which of the following technologies would be most directly relevant for treating this condition?

A. Gene therapy to correct the insulin gene
B. Recombinant DNA technology to produce insulin
C. CRISPR-Cas9 to edit the patient's DNA
D. Stem cell therapy to regenerate pancreatic cells
Answer: B

The disease described is diabetes mellitus, caused by a deficiency of insulin. Recombinant DNA technology is used to produce human insulin in bacteria or yeast, which is then purified and used to treat diabetic patients. While gene therapy, CRISPR, and stem cell therapy are promising, recombinant insulin is the most direct and widely used treatment currently.

8. Which of the following is an example of an 'ex-situ' conservation method?

A. National parks
B. Biosphere reserves
C. Zoological gardens
D. Wildlife sanctuaries
Answer: C

Ex-situ conservation involves the protection and maintenance of species outside their natural habitats. Zoological gardens (zoos) are a classic example of ex-situ conservation, where animals are bred and maintained in controlled environments. National parks, biosphere reserves, and wildlife sanctuaries are examples of in-situ conservation, where species are protected within their natural habitats.

9. The 'Green Revolution' in India primarily focused on increasing the production of:

A. Cash crops like cotton and jute
B. Food grains like wheat and rice
C. Horticultural crops like fruits and vegetables
D. Plantation crops like tea and coffee
Answer: B

The Green Revolution in India, initiated in the 1960s, primarily aimed to increase the production of food grains, particularly wheat and rice. This was achieved through the adoption of high-yielding variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and improved irrigation techniques. While it significantly boosted food grain production, it had less impact on cash crops or horticultural crops.

10. Which of the following statements about 'biofortification' is CORRECT?

A. It involves the use of chemical fertilizers to increase crop yield
B. It is a process of breeding crops to increase their nutritional value
C. It refers to the genetic modification of crops to make them resistant to pests
D. It is a method of preserving food using biological agents
Answer: B

Biofortification is the process of increasing the nutritional value of crops through conventional breeding, agronomic practices, or genetic engineering. Unlike traditional fortification, which adds nutrients to food during processing, biofortification enhances the nutrient content of crops themselves. Examples include iron-rich rice, vitamin A-enriched sweet potatoes, and zinc-fortified wheat.

11. A scientist is studying a disease that spreads rapidly through a population and causes high mortality. The disease is later identified as being caused by a virus. Which of the following terms BEST describes this type of disease outbreak?

A. Endemic
B. Epidemic
C. Pandemic
D. Sporadic
Answer: B

An epidemic refers to the rapid spread of a disease to a large number of people in a given population within a short period. If the disease spreads across multiple countries or continents, it is termed a pandemic. Endemic diseases are constantly present in a population at a baseline level, while sporadic diseases occur infrequently and irregularly.

12. Which of the following is a biological control method used to manage insect pests in agriculture?

A. Use of DDT
B. Introduction of ladybird beetles to control aphids
C. Application of Bordeaux mixture
D. Crop rotation with non-host plants
Answer: B

Biological control involves the use of living organisms to manage pest populations. Ladybird beetles (ladybugs) are natural predators of aphids and are introduced to control aphid infestations in crops. DDT is a chemical pesticide, Bordeaux mixture is a fungicide, and crop rotation is a cultural method, not a biological control.

13. Which of the following diseases is caused by a protozoan parasite?

A. Tuberculosis
B. Malaria
C. Cholera
D. Typhoid
Answer: B

Malaria is caused by the protozoan parasite *Plasmodium*, transmitted through the bite of an infected *Anopheles* mosquito. Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, cholera by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae*, and typhoid by the bacterium *Salmonella Typhi*.

14. Which of the following is NOT a method of integrated pest management (IPM)?

A. Use of chemical pesticides as the first line of defense
B. Crop rotation and intercropping
C. Introduction of natural predators
D. Use of pheromone traps
Answer: A

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) emphasizes the use of multiple strategies to control pests, including biological, cultural, and mechanical methods, while minimizing the use of chemical pesticides. Chemical pesticides are used only as a last resort in IPM, not as the first line of defense.

15. The primary goal of the 'Swachh Bharat Abhiyan' in the context of human health is to:

A. Promote the use of renewable energy
B. Reduce the incidence of waterborne diseases through improved sanitation
C. Increase the production of organic fertilizers
D. Encourage the use of electric vehicles
Answer: B

The 'Swachh Bharat Abhiyan' (Clean India Mission) aims to improve sanitation and hygiene practices to reduce the incidence of waterborne diseases like cholera, diarrhea, and typhoid, which are linked to poor sanitation and open defecation.

16. Which of the following is a vector-borne disease?

A. Tuberculosis
B. Dengue
C. Tetanus
D. Leprosy
Answer: B

Dengue is a vector-borne disease transmitted by the *Aedes aegypti* mosquito. Tuberculosis, tetanus, and leprosy are not vector-borne; they are caused by bacteria and transmitted through other means.

17. The process of adding beneficial microorganisms to soil to enhance plant growth is called:

A. Bioaccumulation
B. Bioaugmentation
C. Bioremediation
D. Biomagnification
Answer: B

Bioaugmentation is the process of adding beneficial microorganisms, such as nitrogen-fixing bacteria or mycorrhizal fungi, to soil to improve soil health and enhance plant growth. Bioaccumulation and biomagnification refer to the accumulation of substances in organisms, while bioremediation is the use of microorganisms to clean up pollutants.

18. Which of the following is a chronic respiratory disease caused by prolonged exposure to air pollutants?

A. Asthma
B. Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
C. Tuberculosis
D. Pneumonia
Answer: B

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is a chronic respiratory disease often caused by prolonged exposure to air pollutants, including tobacco smoke and industrial pollutants. Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways, tuberculosis is caused by a bacterial infection, and pneumonia is an acute infection of the lungs.

19. The term 'biopiracy' refers to:

A. The illegal trade of endangered species
B. The unauthorized commercial use of biological resources and traditional knowledge
C. The destruction of habitats for urban development
D. The overharvesting of medicinal plants
Answer: B

Biopiracy refers to the unauthorized commercial use of biological resources (e.g., plants, animals, or genetic material) and traditional knowledge, often without compensation to the indigenous communities or countries of origin. It is a significant ethical and legal issue in biodiversity conservation.

20. Which of the following statements best describes the role of Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) in integrated pest management (IPM)?

A. Bt produces a toxin that specifically targets and kills a wide range of insect pests, including lepidopteran, coleopteran, and dipteran larvae, without harming beneficial insects or humans.
B. Bt is a broad-spectrum chemical pesticide that eradicates all insect pests in a crop field, including pollinators and natural predators, leading to ecological imbalance.
C. Bt is a genetically modified crop that resists herbicides, allowing farmers to spray chemicals to kill weeds without damaging the crop, but it does not target insect pests.
D. Bt is a biological control agent used exclusively in organic farming to enhance soil fertility by fixing atmospheric nitrogen, with no direct role in pest management.
Answer: A

Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) is a soil-dwelling bacterium that produces crystalline proteins (Cry toxins) during sporulation. These toxins are highly specific to certain groups of insects, particularly larvae of lepidopterans (moths and butterflies), coleopterans (beetles), and dipterans (flies and mosquitoes). When ingested by susceptible larvae, the toxin binds to receptors in the gut, causing cell lysis and death. Bt is widely used in integrated pest management (IPM) because it targets pests while sparing beneficial insects like pollinators and natural predators. It is also safe for humans and other vertebrates, as the toxin requires specific gut conditions (alkaline pH) to activate, which are absent in mammals. Option B is incorrect because Bt is not a chemical pesticide and does not harm all insects indiscriminately. Option C is incorrect because Bt does not confer herbicide resistance; that trait is associated with other genetically modified crops like Roundup Ready soybeans. Option D is incorrect because Bt does not fix nitrogen or enhance soil fertility; its primary role is pest control.

21. Which of the following statements correctly describes the role of Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) in agriculture?

A. It is a biofertilizer that enhances nitrogen fixation in leguminous plants.
B. It produces a toxin that specifically targets and kills certain insect larvae, reducing the need for chemical pesticides.
C. It is used to degrade agricultural waste and improve soil fertility through composting.
D. It acts as a biocontrol agent against fungal pathogens by producing antifungal compounds.
Answer: B

Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) is a soil-dwelling bacterium that produces proteins toxic to specific insect larvae, particularly those of lepidopterans (moths and butterflies), coleopterans (beetles), and dipterans (flies and mosquitoes). These proteins, known as Cry toxins, bind to receptors in the gut of the larvae, leading to cell lysis and death. This property makes Bt a widely used biopesticide in genetically modified crops (e.g., Bt cotton) to reduce reliance on chemical pesticides. The other options describe roles of different microorganisms: nitrogen fixation (e.g., Rhizobium), composting (e.g., decomposers), and antifungal activity (e.g., Trichoderma).
