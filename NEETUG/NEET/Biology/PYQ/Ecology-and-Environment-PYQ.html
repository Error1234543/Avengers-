This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) modeled on previous-year NEET patterns for the topic 'Ecology and Environment'. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. Which of the following best defines an ecological niche?

A. The physical location where an organism lives
B. The role and position a species has in its environment, including all its interactions with biotic and abiotic factors
C. A group of organisms of the same species living in a specific area
D. The total number of individuals of a species in a given area
Answer: B

An ecological niche encompasses not just the habitat of an organism but also its functional role in the ecosystem, including its interactions with other species and the environment. This distinguishes it from a habitat, which is merely the physical location where an organism lives.

2. In a food chain consisting of grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk, which organism is the tertiary consumer?

A. Grasshopper
B. Frog
C. Snake
D. Hawk
Answer: C

The grasshopper is the primary consumer (herbivore), the frog is the secondary consumer (carnivore eating the grasshopper), the snake is the tertiary consumer (carnivore eating the frog), and the hawk is the quaternary consumer (apex predator).

3. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of a pioneer community in ecological succession?

A. High species diversity
B. Low biomass
C. Simple food chains
D. High rate of reproduction
Answer: A

Pioneer communities are the first to colonize barren areas and are typically characterized by low species diversity, low biomass, simple food chains, and high reproductive rates to quickly establish populations. High species diversity is a feature of climax communities, not pioneer communities.

4. The phenomenon where two species compete for the same limited resource and one species is driven to local extinction is known as:

A. Mutualism
B. Commensalism
C. Competitive exclusion
D. Parasitism
Answer: C

Competitive exclusion, also known as Gause's principle, states that two species competing for the same limiting resources cannot coexist in the same ecological niche indefinitely. One species will outcompete and exclude the other.

5. Which of the following gases is primarily responsible for the greenhouse effect?

A. Oxygen (O₂)
B. Nitrogen (N₂)
C. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
D. Hydrogen (H₂)
Answer: C

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the primary greenhouse gas responsible for trapping heat in the Earth's atmosphere, leading to the greenhouse effect. While other gases like methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O) also contribute, CO₂ is the most significant due to its abundance and long atmospheric lifetime.

6. The process by which atmospheric nitrogen is converted into ammonia by certain bacteria is called:

A. Nitrification
B. Denitrification
C. Ammonification
D. Nitrogen fixation
Answer: D

Nitrogen fixation is the process where atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) is converted into ammonia (NH₃) or related nitrogenous compounds by nitrogen-fixing bacteria, such as those in the genus *Rhizobium* or free-living bacteria like *Azotobacter*.

7. The term 'biomagnification' refers to:

A. The increase in the concentration of a substance in the tissues of organisms at successively higher levels in a food chain
B. The process by which plants absorb minerals from the soil
C. The growth of biomass in an ecosystem over time
D. The reduction of biodiversity due to habitat destruction
Answer: A

Biomagnification is the process where the concentration of a substance (often a pollutant or toxin) increases as it moves up the food chain. This occurs because the substance is not easily metabolized or excreted and accumulates in the tissues of organisms at higher trophic levels.

8. Which of the following statements about the carbon cycle is correct?

A. Carbon is primarily removed from the atmosphere by the process of respiration
B. Photosynthesis is the primary process by which carbon is released into the atmosphere
C. Fossil fuel combustion is a major source of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere
D. Decomposition of organic matter does not contribute to the carbon cycle
Answer: C

Fossil fuel combustion is a significant anthropogenic source of carbon dioxide (CO₂) in the atmosphere, contributing to the greenhouse effect and climate change. Photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the atmosphere, while respiration and decomposition release it back.

9. The relationship between clownfish and sea anemones, where the clownfish receives protection and the sea anemone is unaffected, is an example of:

A. Mutualism
B. Commensalism
C. Parasitism
D. Predation
Answer: B

Commensalism is a symbiotic relationship where one organism benefits while the other is neither helped nor harmed. In this case, the clownfish benefits by gaining protection from predators, while the sea anemone is unaffected.

10. Which of the following is a density-independent factor affecting population growth?

A. Competition for food
B. Predation
C. Disease
D. Natural disasters
Answer: D

Density-independent factors affect population growth regardless of the population's density. Natural disasters, such as floods, earthquakes, or fires, are examples of density-independent factors. Competition, predation, and disease are density-dependent factors, as their impact increases with population density.

11. The term 'keystone species' refers to:

A. A species that is the most abundant in an ecosystem
B. A species whose removal has a disproportionately large impact on the ecosystem
C. A species that is at the top of the food chain
D. A species that is introduced into a new ecosystem
Answer: B

A keystone species is one whose presence or absence has a disproportionately large impact on the structure and function of an ecosystem. Its removal can lead to significant changes in the ecosystem, often resulting in a loss of biodiversity. Keystone species are not necessarily the most abundant or at the top of the food chain.

12. Which of the following best describes the phenomenon where a species becomes locally extinct in a particular area but continues to exist elsewhere?

A. Global extinction
B. Ecological extinction
C. Extirpation
D. Mass extinction
Answer: C

Extirpation refers to the local extinction of a species in a specific geographic area while the species still exists in other regions. This is different from global extinction, where the species ceases to exist entirely.

13. The relationship between clownfish and sea anemones, where both species benefit, is an example of:

A. Parasitism
B. Commensalism
C. Mutualism
D. Amensalism
Answer: C

The relationship between clownfish and sea anemones is a classic example of mutualism, where both species benefit. The clownfish receives protection from predators by living among the anemone's tentacles, while the anemone benefits from the clownfish's waste, which provides nutrients.

14. In a food chain, if the primary producers have 10,000 units of energy, how much energy is typically available to the tertiary consumers?

A. 1000 units
B. 100 units
C. 10 units
D. 1 unit
Answer: C

According to the 10% law of energy transfer in food chains, only about 10% of the energy from one trophic level is transferred to the next. Thus, if primary producers have 10,000 units of energy, primary consumers receive 1,000 units, secondary consumers receive 100 units, and tertiary consumers receive 10 units.

15. The term 'biodiversity hotspot' refers to regions that:

A. Have high species richness and are under threat from human activities
B. Are exclusively found in tropical rainforests
C. Have low species diversity but high endemism
D. Are protected by international laws and cannot be developed
Answer: A

Biodiversity hotspots are regions with high species richness and a significant number of endemic species that are also under threat from human activities. These areas are prioritized for conservation efforts due to their ecological importance.

16. The process of accumulation of non-biodegradable substances like DDT in higher trophic levels of a food chain is known as:

A. Biomagnification
B. Bioaccumulation
C. Eutrophication
D. Biodegradation
Answer: A

Biomagnification refers to the increase in concentration of non-biodegradable substances (e.g., pesticides like DDT, heavy metals) as they move up the food chain. This occurs because these substances are not metabolized or excreted and accumulate in the tissues of organisms at higher trophic levels.

17. Which of the following statements about the ozone layer is correct?

A. It is found in the troposphere and protects against UV radiation
B. It is depleted by chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and is located in the stratosphere
C. It is responsible for the greenhouse effect
D. It is thickest over the equator and thinnest over the poles
Answer: B

The ozone layer is located in the stratosphere and plays a crucial role in absorbing harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun. It is depleted by chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and other ozone-depleting substances, leading to the formation of the ozone hole, particularly over the polar regions.

18. A species that has a disproportionately large impact on its ecosystem relative to its abundance is called a:

A. Keystone species
B. Dominant species
C. Indicator species
D. Endemic species
Answer: A

A keystone species is one that has a disproportionately large effect on its environment relative to its abundance. The removal of a keystone species can lead to significant changes in the structure and function of an ecosystem. Examples include sea otters in kelp forests and wolves in Yellowstone National Park.

19. Which of the following best describes the phenomenon where a species, due to its critical role in maintaining the structure of an ecological community, has a disproportionately large impact on its environment relative to its abundance?

A. Keystone species
B. Dominant species
C. Indicator species
D. Pioneer species
Answer: A

A keystone species is defined as a species that plays a critical role in maintaining the structure of an ecological community. Its impact on the environment is disproportionately large compared to its abundance. For example, sea otters in kelp forests control the population of sea urchins, which if left unchecked, would overgraze the kelp and destroy the habitat. Dominant species are those that are most abundant or have the highest biomass in a community, while indicator species signal changes in environmental conditions. Pioneer species are the first to colonize barren environments during ecological succession.

20. Which of the following best describes the phenomenon of 'edge effect' in ecology?

A. Increased species diversity at the boundary of two distinct ecosystems due to overlapping habitats
B. Reduction in biodiversity within a single ecosystem due to human interference
C. The dominance of a single species at the center of an ecosystem, reducing competition
D. The gradual transition of one ecosystem into another without any change in species composition
Answer: A

The edge effect refers to the changes in population or community structures that occur at the boundary of two or more habitats. Areas with small habitat fragments exhibit especially pronounced edge effects, often leading to increased biodiversity due to the overlap of species from adjacent ecosystems. This phenomenon is critical in conservation biology, particularly in fragmented landscapes.

21. A researcher observes that a certain species of fish in a lake has developed resistance to a pesticide over a decade. This is an example of:

A. Stabilizing selection
B. Directional selection
C. Disruptive selection
D. Genetic drift
Answer: B

Directional selection occurs when individuals at one end of a phenotypic spectrum have a survival or reproductive advantage, leading to a shift in the population's traits over time. In this case, the fish population shifted toward pesticide resistance due to the selective pressure exerted by the pesticide, favoring individuals with resistant traits.

22. Which of the following statements about the 'Montreal Protocol' is INCORRECT?

A. It aims to phase out the production and consumption of ozone-depleting substances like CFCs
B. It was signed in 1987 and has been ratified by nearly all countries
C. It primarily focuses on reducing carbon dioxide emissions to combat global warming
D. It has successfully led to a reduction in the size of the ozone hole over Antarctica
Answer: C

The Montreal Protocol is an international treaty designed to protect the ozone layer by phasing out the production of ozone-depleting substances such as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs). While it has had positive effects on climate change by reducing greenhouse gases, its primary focus is not on carbon dioxide emissions, which are addressed by other agreements like the Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement.

23. In a food chain consisting of grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk, which trophic level would accumulate the highest concentration of a non-biodegradable pesticide like DDT?

A. Grass
B. Grasshopper
C. Frog
D. Hawk
Answer: D

Biological magnification (or biomagnification) is the process by which the concentration of non-biodegradable substances like DDT increases at higher trophic levels in a food chain. Since hawks occupy the highest trophic level in this chain, they would accumulate the highest concentration of DDT due to the cumulative effect of consuming contaminated prey at lower levels.

24. In a given ecosystem, the primary productivity is 1000 g C/m²/year. If the respiratory loss by producers is 250 g C/m²/year, what is the net primary productivity (NPP) of this ecosystem?

A. 250 g C/m²/year
B. 500 g C/m²/year
C. 750 g C/m²/year
D. 1250 g C/m²/year
Answer: C

Net Primary Productivity (NPP) is calculated by subtracting the respiratory losses of producers from the Gross Primary Productivity (GPP). Here, GPP is given as 1000 g C/m²/year, and respiratory loss is 250 g C/m²/year. Thus, NPP = GPP - Respiratory Loss = 1000 - 250 = 750 g C/m²/year. This concept is critical for understanding energy flow in ecosystems and is frequently tested in NEET.
