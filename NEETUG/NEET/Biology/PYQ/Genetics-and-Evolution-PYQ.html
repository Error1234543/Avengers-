This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions modeled on NEET's Genetics and Evolution topic. Each question carries 4 marks with a -1 penalty for incorrect answers. Choose the single best answer for each question.

1. A cross between two heterozygous tall pea plants (Tt) results in 3 tall and 1 dwarf offspring. Which of the following explains this phenotypic ratio?

A. Law of Dominance
B. Law of Segregation
C. Law of Independent Assortment
D. Incomplete Dominance
Answer: B

The 3:1 phenotypic ratio in the F2 generation of a monohybrid cross is a direct consequence of the Law of Segregation (Mendel's First Law), which states that alleles for a trait separate during gamete formation and reunite randomly at fertilization.

2. In Drosophila, the gene for white eyes (w) is located on the X chromosome. If a white-eyed female is crossed with a red-eyed male, what percentage of male offspring will have white eyes?

A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50%
D. 100%
Answer: D

The white-eyed female (X^w X^w) produces only X^w gametes, while the red-eyed male (X^+ Y) produces X^+ and Y gametes. All male offspring inherit the X^w from the mother and Y from the father, resulting in 100% white-eyed males (X^w Y).

3. Which of the following best describes the genetic basis of sickle cell anemia?

A. Autosomal dominant mutation in the beta-globin gene
B. Autosomal recessive mutation causing substitution of glutamic acid with valine in beta-globin
C. X-linked recessive mutation affecting hemoglobin structure
D. Chromosomal deletion in the alpha-globin gene cluster
Answer: B

Sickle cell anemia is caused by an autosomal recessive mutation where a single nucleotide change (GAG → GTG) results in the substitution of glutamic acid with valine at the 6th position of the beta-globin chain, leading to abnormal hemoglobin (HbS).

4. A DNA sequence reads 5'-ATGCGT-3'. What would be the sequence of its complementary RNA strand?

A. 5'-UACGCA-3'
B. 5'-ACGCAU-3'
C. 3'-UACGCA-5'
D. 3'-TACGCA-5'
Answer: C

During transcription, RNA polymerase synthesizes RNA in the 5' → 3' direction using the DNA template strand (3' → 5'). The complementary RNA sequence for 5'-ATGCGT-3' is 3'-UACGCA-5' (U replaces T in RNA).

5. Which evolutionary mechanism is primarily responsible for the high frequency of the sickle cell allele in malaria-endemic regions?

A. Genetic drift
B. Gene flow
C. Natural selection
D. Mutation pressure
Answer: C

Natural selection favors the sickle cell allele (HbS) in malaria-endemic regions because heterozygous individuals (HbA HbS) exhibit increased resistance to severe malaria while avoiding the full symptoms of sickle cell anemia, leading to a balanced polymorphism.

6. In a dihybrid cross between two pea plants heterozygous for seed shape (Rr) and seed color (Yy), what is the probability of obtaining an offspring with wrinkled seeds and green cotyledons?

A. 1/16
B. 3/16
C. 1/4
D. 9/16
Answer: A

The probability of obtaining wrinkled seeds (rr) is 1/4, and green cotyledons (yy) is 1/4. Since the genes assort independently (Law of Independent Assortment), the combined probability is (1/4) × (1/4) = 1/16.

7. The Hardy-Weinberg principle states that allele frequencies in a population remain constant if certain conditions are met. Which of the following is NOT one of these conditions?

A. No mutations
B. Random mating
C. Small population size
D. No gene flow
Answer: C

The Hardy-Weinberg principle requires a large population size to prevent genetic drift, which can alter allele frequencies due to random sampling effects. Small populations violate this condition.

8. Which of the following is an example of adaptive radiation?

A. Development of antibiotic resistance in bacteria
B. Darwin's finches evolving different beak shapes on the Galápagos Islands
C. Industrial melanism in peppered moths
D. Speciation of Anopheles mosquitoes due to geographic isolation
Answer: B

Adaptive radiation refers to the diversification of a single ancestral species into multiple species, each adapted to a different ecological niche. Darwin's finches exemplify this, as they evolved distinct beak shapes to exploit various food sources on the Galápagos Islands.

9. A man with blood group A (heterozygous) marries a woman with blood group B (heterozygous). What is the probability that their child will have blood group O?

A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50%
D. 75%
Answer: B

The man's genotype is I^A i, and the woman's genotype is I^B i. The probability of both parents passing the recessive i allele to the child is (1/2) × (1/2) = 1/4 or 25%, resulting in blood group O (ii).

10. Which of the following provides the strongest evidence for the common ancestry of all life forms?

A. Universal genetic code
B. Presence of mitochondria in eukaryotic cells
C. Similarity in limb structure among vertebrates
D. Convergent evolution of wings in birds and bats
Answer: A

The universal genetic code, where the same codons specify the same amino acids across nearly all organisms, provides the strongest evidence for the common ancestry of all life forms. This near-universality suggests a single origin of life.

11. In a population of 1000 individuals, 160 exhibit a recessive trait. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what is the frequency of the dominant allele?

A. 0.4
B. 0.6
C. 0.8
D. 0.92
Answer: B

The frequency of the recessive phenotype (q²) is 160/1000 = 0.16. Thus, q = √0.16 = 0.4. The frequency of the dominant allele (p) is 1 - q = 1 - 0.4 = 0.6.

12. Which of the following is NOT a post-zygotic reproductive isolating mechanism?

A. Hybrid sterility
B. Hybrid inviability
C. Temporal isolation
D. Hybrid breakdown
Answer: C

Temporal isolation is a pre-zygotic mechanism where species breed at different times, preventing mating. Hybrid sterility (A), inviability (B), and breakdown (D) are post-zygotic mechanisms that occur after fertilization, leading to non-viable or infertile offspring.

13. A population of moths is in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. The frequency of the recessive allele (q) for dark coloration is 0.3. What is the frequency of heterozygous individuals in the population?

A. 0.21
B. 0.42
C. 0.49
D. 0.09
Answer: B

In Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of heterozygous individuals (2pq) is calculated as 2 × p × q, where p = 1 - q. Here, p = 1 - 0.3 = 0.7, so 2pq = 2 × 0.7 × 0.3 = 0.42.

14. The phenomenon where two or more species reciprocally affect each other's evolution is termed:

A. Convergent evolution
B. Divergent evolution
C. Co-evolution
D. Adaptive radiation
Answer: C

Co-evolution occurs when species exert selective pressures on each other, leading to reciprocal evolutionary changes (e.g., predator-prey or plant-pollinator relationships). Convergent evolution (A) involves unrelated species developing similar traits, divergent evolution (B) involves species branching from a common ancestor, and adaptive radiation (D) is the diversification of a species into multiple forms.

15. Which of the following statements about DNA fingerprinting is INCORRECT?

A. It is based on the variability of short tandem repeats (STRs).
B. It can be used to establish paternity.
C. It requires large amounts of high-quality DNA.
D. It is highly individual-specific.
Answer: C

DNA fingerprinting uses PCR to amplify small quantities of DNA, including degraded samples, so it does not require large amounts of high-quality DNA. The other options are correct: it relies on STRs (A), is used in paternity testing (B), and is highly specific to individuals (D).

16. In a test cross involving a dihybrid (AaBb), the phenotypic ratio of the offspring is 1:1:1:1. This indicates that:

A. The genes are linked.
B. The genes assort independently.
C. The genes show incomplete dominance.
D. The cross is between two homozygous parents.
Answer: B

A 1:1:1:1 phenotypic ratio in a dihybrid test cross (AaBb × aabb) indicates that the genes assort independently, as predicted by Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment. Linked genes (A) would produce a non-Mendelian ratio, incomplete dominance (C) would alter the ratio, and homozygous parents (D) would not produce such a ratio.

17. The evolutionary theory proposing that species remain unchanged for long periods and then undergo rapid change is called:

A. Gradualism
B. Punctuated equilibrium
C. Neutral theory
D. Lamarckism
Answer: B

Punctuated equilibrium, proposed by Stephen Jay Gould and Niles Eldredge, suggests that evolution occurs in rapid bursts followed by long periods of stasis. Gradualism (A) proposes slow, continuous change, neutral theory (C) focuses on genetic drift, and Lamarckism (D) involves the inheritance of acquired traits.

18. Which of the following is an example of a vestigial organ in humans?

A. Appendix
B. Liver
C. Pancreas
D. Thyroid gland
Answer: A

The appendix is a vestigial organ in humans, remnants of a larger cecum used for digesting cellulose in herbivorous ancestors. The liver (B), pancreas (C), and thyroid gland (D) are functional organs with no vestigial role.

19. A mutation that changes a codon for an amino acid into a stop codon is called a:

A. Missense mutation
B. Nonsense mutation
C. Silent mutation
D. Frameshift mutation
Answer: B

A nonsense mutation converts a codon for an amino acid into a premature stop codon, leading to truncated and often non-functional proteins. Missense mutations (A) change one amino acid to another, silent mutations (C) do not alter the amino acid, and frameshift mutations (D) shift the reading frame of the genetic code.

20. Which of the following is NOT a mechanism of genetic recombination in bacteria?

A. Transformation
B. Transduction
C. Conjugation
D. Binary fission
Answer: D

Binary fission is a method of asexual reproduction in bacteria, not a mechanism of genetic recombination. Transformation (A), transduction (B), and conjugation (C) are processes by which bacteria exchange genetic material.

21. The concept of 'survival of the fittest' was proposed by:

A. Charles Darwin
B. Jean-Baptiste Lamarck
C. Alfred Russel Wallace
D. Herbert Spencer
Answer: D

While Charles Darwin (A) and Alfred Russel Wallace (C) developed the theory of natural selection, the phrase 'survival of the fittest' was coined by Herbert Spencer. Lamarck (B) proposed the inheritance of acquired traits, not this concept.

22. In a population of 1000 individuals, 160 exhibit a recessive trait. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, what is the frequency of the heterozygous carriers in this population?

A. 0.24
B. 0.36
C. 0.48
D. 0.60
Answer: C

Given that 160 out of 1000 individuals exhibit the recessive trait, the frequency of the recessive phenotype (q²) is 160/1000 = 0.16. The frequency of the recessive allele (q) is the square root of 0.16, which is 0.4. According to the Hardy-Weinberg principle, p + q = 1, so the frequency of the dominant allele (p) is 1 - 0.4 = 0.6. The frequency of heterozygous carriers (2pq) is 2 * 0.6 * 0.4 = 0.48.
