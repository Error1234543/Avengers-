This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on Human Physiology, designed to match the difficulty and style of NEET. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. Which of the following statements correctly describes the role of surfactant in the human respiratory system?

A. Surfactant increases the surface tension of alveolar fluid, preventing alveolar collapse.
B. Surfactant decreases the surface tension of alveolar fluid, preventing alveolar collapse.
C. Surfactant is produced by type I alveolar cells to facilitate gas exchange.
D. Surfactant directly transports oxygen from alveoli to pulmonary capillaries.
Answer: B

Surfactant is a lipoprotein complex secreted by type II alveolar cells. It reduces the surface tension of the alveolar fluid, preventing alveolar collapse during expiration and reducing the effort required for breathing. It does not transport gases or increase surface tension.

2. A patient presents with symptoms of muscle weakness and fatigue. Laboratory tests reveal elevated levels of acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft. Which of the following enzymes is most likely deficient in this patient?

A. Acetylcholinesterase
B. Choline acetyltransferase
C. Monoamine oxidase
D. Catechol-O-methyltransferase
Answer: A

Acetylcholinesterase is responsible for breaking down acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft. Its deficiency leads to accumulation of acetylcholine, causing prolonged muscle stimulation, weakness, and fatigue. The other enzymes listed are not involved in acetylcholine metabolism.

3. During the cardiac cycle, the 'dup' sound is heard due to the closure of which valves?

A. Tricuspid and mitral valves
B. Aortic and pulmonary valves
C. Mitral and aortic valves
D. Tricuspid and pulmonary valves
Answer: B

The 'dup' sound (second heart sound) occurs during the closure of the semilunar valves, specifically the aortic and pulmonary valves, marking the end of systole and the beginning of diastole.

4. Which segment of the nephron is impermeable to water but actively reabsorbs sodium and chloride ions?

A. Proximal convoluted tubule
B. Descending limb of the loop of Henle
C. Ascending limb of the loop of Henle
D. Distal convoluted tubule
Answer: C

The ascending limb of the loop of Henle is impermeable to water but actively reabsorbs sodium and chloride ions via the Na+-K+-2Cl- cotransporter. This process is crucial for establishing the medullary osmotic gradient necessary for urine concentration.

5. A deficiency of intrinsic factor leads to which of the following conditions?

A. Pernicious anemia
B. Iron-deficiency anemia
C. Aplastic anemia
D. Hemolytic anemia
Answer: A

Intrinsic factor, produced by parietal cells in the stomach, is essential for the absorption of vitamin B12 in the ileum. Its deficiency leads to pernicious anemia, characterized by megaloblastic anemia and neurological symptoms due to impaired DNA synthesis.

6. Which of the following hormones is primarily responsible for stimulating the secretion of pancreatic bicarbonate-rich juice?

A. Gastrin
B. Secretin
C. Cholecystokinin
D. Insulin
Answer: B

Secretin is released by the duodenal mucosa in response to acidic chyme entering the small intestine. It stimulates the pancreas to secrete bicarbonate-rich juice, which neutralizes the acid and provides an optimal pH for enzymatic activity.

7. In the sliding filament model of muscle contraction, which of the following events occurs first?

A. Release of calcium ions from the sarcoplasmic reticulum
B. Binding of myosin heads to actin filaments
C. Hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and inorganic phosphate
D. Power stroke of the myosin head
Answer: A

The sequence of events in the sliding filament model begins with the release of calcium ions from the sarcoplasmic reticulum following neural stimulation. Calcium binds to troponin, exposing binding sites on actin, which then allows myosin heads to bind and initiate contraction.

8. Which of the following is NOT a function of the liver?

A. Synthesis of plasma proteins like albumin and clotting factors
B. Detoxification of drugs and metabolic waste products
C. Production of digestive enzymes like amylase and lipase
D. Storage of vitamins A, D, E, K, and B12
Answer: C

The liver synthesizes bile, which aids in fat digestion, but it does not produce digestive enzymes like amylase and lipase. These enzymes are produced by the pancreas and salivary glands. The other options are well-established functions of the liver.

9. A person with blood group AB negative can receive blood from which of the following blood groups?

A. A positive, B positive, AB positive, O positive
B. A negative, B negative, AB negative, O negative
C. O positive and O negative only
D. AB negative only
Answer: B

A person with blood group AB negative is a universal recipient for plasma but can only receive red blood cells from donors who are Rh-negative and lack A or B antibodies. Thus, they can receive blood from A negative, B negative, AB negative, and O negative donors.

10. Which of the following statements about the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) is correct?

A. Renin is secreted by the adrenal cortex in response to low blood pressure.
B. Angiotensin II stimulates vasodilation and decreases blood pressure.
C. Aldosterone promotes sodium and water reabsorption in the kidneys.
D. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) converts angiotensin I to angiotensinogen.
Answer: C

Aldosterone, secreted by the adrenal cortex, acts on the kidneys to promote sodium and water reabsorption, thereby increasing blood volume and pressure. Renin is secreted by the juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney, angiotensin II is a potent vasoconstrictor, and ACE converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II.

11. Which of the following is the primary site for the absorption of digested fats in the gastrointestinal tract?

A. Stomach
B. Duodenum
C. Jejunum
D. Ileum
Answer: C

The jejunum is the primary site for the absorption of digested fats, which are emulsified by bile salts and broken down into fatty acids and monoglycerides. These products are absorbed into the intestinal epithelial cells and reassembled into triglycerides before being transported as chylomicrons.

12. A patient exhibits symptoms of polyuria, polydipsia, and hyperglycemia. Which of the following hormones is most likely deficient in this patient?

A. Glucagon
B. Insulin
C. Cortisol
D. Thyroxine
Answer: B

The symptoms described—polyuria (excessive urination), polydipsia (excessive thirst), and hyperglycemia (elevated blood glucose)—are classic signs of diabetes mellitus, which results from a deficiency of insulin or insulin resistance. Insulin is essential for glucose uptake by cells and regulation of blood glucose levels.

13. Which of the following statements about the human digestive system is INCORRECT?

A. Pepsinogen is converted to pepsin in the stomach by hydrochloric acid.
B. Bile salts emulsify fats but do not contain any digestive enzymes.
C. The pancreas secretes trypsinogen, which is activated by enterokinase in the small intestine.
D. Salivary amylase continues to digest carbohydrates in the stomach until the pH drops below 3.
Answer: D

Salivary amylase is inactivated by the acidic pH of the stomach (pH < 3). It does not continue carbohydrate digestion in the stomach. The other statements are correct: pepsinogen is activated by HCl, bile salts emulsify fats without enzymes, and trypsinogen is activated by enterokinase.

14. A patient presents with symptoms of muscle cramps, weakness, and irregular heartbeat. Blood tests reveal hypokalemia. Which of the following is the MOST likely cause of these symptoms?

A. Excessive aldosterone secretion leading to increased potassium excretion
B. Reduced parathyroid hormone secretion causing decreased calcium absorption
C. Increased antidiuretic hormone secretion leading to water retention
D. Deficiency of insulin resulting in hyperglycemia
Answer: A

Hypokalemia (low potassium levels) is commonly caused by excessive aldosterone secretion, which increases potassium excretion in the kidneys. This leads to muscle cramps, weakness, and cardiac arrhythmias. The other options do not directly cause hypokalemia.

15. During the process of urine formation, which part of the nephron is primarily responsible for the reabsorption of glucose and amino acids?

A. Bowman's capsule
B. Proximal convoluted tubule
C. Loop of Henle
D. Distal convoluted tubule
Answer: B

The proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) is the primary site for the reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, and other essential nutrients from the filtrate back into the blood. Bowman's capsule filters the blood, while the Loop of Henle and distal convoluted tubule are involved in water and ion balance.

16. Which of the following hormones is secreted by the posterior pituitary gland and plays a key role in uterine contractions during childbirth?

A. Prolactin
B. Oxytocin
C. Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
D. Luteinizing hormone (LH)
Answer: B

Oxytocin is secreted by the posterior pituitary gland and stimulates uterine contractions during childbirth. Prolactin is involved in milk production, while FSH and LH regulate reproductive processes but are not directly involved in uterine contractions.

17. In the human eye, the condition of hypermetropia (farsightedness) is corrected by using which type of lens?

A. Concave lens
B. Convex lens
C. Cylindrical lens
D. Bifocal lens
Answer: B

Hypermetropia (farsightedness) occurs when the eyeball is too short or the lens is too flat, causing light to focus behind the retina. A convex lens corrects this by converging light rays before they enter the eye.

18. Which of the following statements about the human cardiac cycle is TRUE?

A. The tricuspid valve opens during ventricular systole to allow blood flow into the pulmonary artery.
B. The semilunar valves close at the beginning of ventricular diastole to prevent backflow of blood into the ventricles.
C. Atrial systole occurs simultaneously with ventricular systole to maximize cardiac output.
D. The bicuspid valve closes during atrial systole to prevent backflow of blood into the left atrium.
Answer: B

The semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonary) close at the beginning of ventricular diastole to prevent the backflow of blood into the ventricles. The tricuspid valve does not open during ventricular systole, atrial and ventricular systole do not occur simultaneously, and the bicuspid valve closes during ventricular systole, not atrial systole.

19. A deficiency of which vitamin is most likely to cause night blindness and xerophthalmia?

A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin B12
C. Vitamin C
D. Vitamin D
Answer: A

Vitamin A deficiency is associated with night blindness and xerophthalmia (dryness of the conjunctiva and cornea). Vitamin B12 deficiency causes anemia, Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, and Vitamin D deficiency causes rickets or osteomalacia.

20. In the human nervous system, which of the following is responsible for transmitting nerve impulses from the central nervous system to skeletal muscles?

A. Sensory neurons
B. Motor neurons
C. Interneurons
D. Autonomic neurons
Answer: B

Motor neurons transmit nerve impulses from the central nervous system (CNS) to skeletal muscles, enabling voluntary movement. Sensory neurons carry impulses to the CNS, interneurons connect neurons within the CNS, and autonomic neurons regulate involuntary functions.

21. Which of the following processes occurs during the follicular phase of the menstrual cycle?

A. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone to prepare the endometrium for implantation.
B. Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) stimulates the development of ovarian follicles.
C. Luteinizing hormone (LH) triggers ovulation at the end of the phase.
D. The endometrium sheds due to a drop in estrogen and progesterone levels.
Answer: B

During the follicular phase, FSH stimulates the development of ovarian follicles. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone during the luteal phase, LH triggers ovulation at the end of the follicular phase, and the endometrium sheds during menstruation due to a drop in hormone levels.

22. A patient presents with symptoms of muscle cramps, weakness, and irregular heartbeats. Laboratory tests reveal hypokalemia (low potassium levels). Which of the following physiological processes is most directly impaired due to this electrolyte imbalance?

A. Generation and propagation of action potentials in neurons and muscle cells
B. Binding of oxygen to hemoglobin in red blood cells
C. Synthesis of digestive enzymes in the pancreas
D. Reabsorption of glucose in the proximal convoluted tubule of the nephron
Answer: A

Potassium ions (K+) play a critical role in establishing the resting membrane potential and the repolarization phase of action potentials in neurons and muscle cells, including cardiac muscle. Hypokalemia disrupts these processes, leading to symptoms such as muscle cramps, weakness, and arrhythmias. While potassium is also involved in other cellular functions, its role in action potential generation is the most directly impaired in this scenario. Options B, C, and D are unrelated to potassium's primary physiological functions in excitable tissues.
