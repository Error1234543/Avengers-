NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) based on the NEET Biology syllabus topic 'Diversity in Living World.' Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of living organisms?

A. Growth by cell division
B. Ability to respond to external stimuli
C. Presence of a nucleus in all cells
D. Reproduction to produce offspring
Answer: C

While eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus, prokaryotic cells (e.g., bacteria) lack a defined nucleus. Thus, the presence of a nucleus is not a universal characteristic of all living organisms. Growth, response to stimuli, and reproduction are fundamental features of life.

2. The binomial nomenclature system was introduced by:

A. Charles Darwin
B. Carolus Linnaeus
C. Gregor Mendel
D. Robert Whittaker
Answer: B

Carolus Linnaeus, a Swedish botanist, introduced the binomial nomenclature system in the 18th century. This system assigns a two-part scientific name (genus and species) to each organism, standardizing their classification.

3. Which taxonomic category is immediately below 'Order' in the hierarchical classification system?

A. Class
B. Family
C. Genus
D. Phylum
Answer: B

The hierarchical classification system follows the order: Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Thus, 'Family' is the category immediately below 'Order.'

4. Which of the following is a correct example of a taxon?

A. Mammalia
B. Canis lupus
C. Vertebrata
D. All of the above
Answer: D

A taxon refers to any level in the taxonomic hierarchy. 'Mammalia' is a class, 'Canis lupus' is a species, and 'Vertebrata' is a subphylum. All are valid taxa.

5. The five-kingdom classification system proposed by Whittaker is based on all of the following criteria EXCEPT:

A. Cell structure
B. Mode of nutrition
C. Thallus organization
D. Genetic material composition
Answer: D

Robert Whittaker's five-kingdom system (Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia) is based on cell structure (prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic), mode of nutrition (autotrophic vs. heterotrophic), and thallus organization (unicellular vs. multicellular). Genetic material composition (e.g., DNA vs. RNA) was not a primary criterion.

6. Which of the following kingdoms includes organisms that are exclusively heterotrophic?

A. Monera
B. Protista
C. Fungi
D. Plantae
Answer: C

Fungi are exclusively heterotrophic, obtaining nutrients by decomposing organic matter or parasitizing other organisms. Monera and Protista include both autotrophic and heterotrophic members, while Plantae are primarily autotrophic.

7. The term 'species' refers to a group of organisms that:

A. Share a common habitat
B. Can interbreed to produce fertile offspring
C. Have identical physical characteristics
D. Belong to the same genus
Answer: B

Biological species are defined as groups of organisms that can interbreed under natural conditions to produce fertile offspring. While they often share habitats or physical traits, reproductive compatibility is the key criterion.

8. In the five-kingdom classification, blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) are placed under:

A. Monera
B. Protista
C. Plantae
D. Fungi
Answer: A

Blue-green algae, or cyanobacteria, are prokaryotic organisms and are classified under Kingdom Monera. They lack a defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

9. Which of the following pairs of organisms are correctly matched with their taxonomic kingdoms?

A. Amoeba – Protista; Mushroom – Fungi
B. Bacteria – Protista; Yeast – Plantae
C. Paramecium – Monera; Fern – Animalia
D. Euglena – Fungi; Moss – Protista
Answer: A

Amoeba is a unicellular eukaryote (Protista), and mushrooms are multicellular heterotrophic organisms (Fungi). Bacteria belong to Monera, yeast to Fungi, Paramecium to Protista, ferns to Plantae, and Euglena to Protista.

10. The study of the evolutionary history and relationships among organisms is called:

A. Taxonomy
B. Systematics
C. Phylogeny
D. Morphology
Answer: C

Phylogeny is the study of the evolutionary history and relationships among organisms, often represented as phylogenetic trees. Taxonomy involves classification, systematics is the broader study of diversity, and morphology focuses on physical form.

11. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of the kingdom Monera?

A. Presence of peptidoglycan in the cell wall
B. Membrane-bound organelles are absent
C. Genetic material is enclosed within a nuclear membrane
D. Reproduction is primarily asexual
Answer: C

Monera includes prokaryotic organisms like bacteria and blue-green algae, which lack a true nucleus. Their genetic material is not enclosed within a nuclear membrane, making option C incorrect. The other options are characteristic features of Monera.

12. Which of the following pairs of organisms are correctly matched with their respective classes under the phylum Arthropoda?

A. Scorpion - Arachnida; Prawn - Crustacea
B. Housefly - Crustacea; Crab - Insecta
C. Spider - Insecta; Lobster - Arachnida
D. Centipede - Diplopoda; Butterfly - Crustacea
Answer: A

Scorpions belong to the class Arachnida, and prawns belong to the class Crustacea. The other options contain incorrect pairings, such as houseflies (Insecta) and crabs (Crustacea).

13. In the binomial nomenclature system, the scientific name of an organism consists of:

A. Genus and species
B. Family and genus
C. Order and family
D. Class and order
Answer: A

Binomial nomenclature, introduced by Carl Linnaeus, consists of two parts: the genus name (capitalized) and the species name (lowercase), both italicized. For example, *Homo sapiens*.

14. The term 'taxon' refers to:

A. A group of organisms at any level of classification
B. The process of classifying organisms
C. The study of evolutionary relationships
D. A specific type of mutation in genetic studies
Answer: A

A taxon (plural: taxa) is a group of organisms at any level of the hierarchical classification system, such as species, genus, family, or phylum.

15. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of the phylum Cnidaria?

A. Presence of a notochord
B. Radial symmetry and cnidocytes
C. Bilateral symmetry and a coelom
D. Exoskeleton made of chitin
Answer: B

Cnidaria (e.g., jellyfish, hydra, corals) exhibit radial symmetry and possess specialized cells called cnidocytes, which contain stinging structures called nematocysts. The other options describe features of other phyla.

16. In the plant kingdom, bryophytes are distinguished from pteridophytes by:

A. Presence of vascular tissue
B. Dominance of the gametophyte generation
C. Production of seeds
D. Absence of chlorophyll
Answer: B

Bryophytes (e.g., mosses, liverworts) have a dominant gametophyte generation, while pteridophytes (e.g., ferns) have a dominant sporophyte generation. Bryophytes also lack vascular tissue, but this is not the distinguishing feature compared to pteridophytes, which possess it.

17. Which of the following is NOT a feature of gymnosperms?

A. Presence of naked seeds
B. Well-developed vascular tissue
C. Flowers as reproductive structures
D. Heterosporous life cycle
Answer: C

Gymnosperms (e.g., pine, cycad) produce naked seeds and lack flowers. Flowers are a characteristic feature of angiosperms. Gymnosperms have well-developed vascular tissue and exhibit a heterosporous life cycle.

18. The phenomenon where two species resemble each other due to similar environmental pressures, despite not being closely related, is called:

A. Convergent evolution
B. Divergent evolution
C. Adaptive radiation
D. Parallel evolution
Answer: A

Convergent evolution occurs when unrelated species develop similar traits due to similar environmental pressures or ecological niches. Examples include the wings of birds and bats.

19. Which of the following is a correct sequence of taxonomic categories from higher to lower?

A. Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
B. Kingdom → Class → Phylum → Order → Family → Genus → Species
C. Phylum → Kingdom → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
D. Kingdom → Phylum → Order → Class → Family → Genus → Species
Answer: A

The correct hierarchical sequence of taxonomic categories from higher to lower is: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. This sequence is universally accepted in biological classification.

20. Which of the following organisms is an example of a chemoautotroph?

A. *Nostoc*
B. *Nitrosomonas*
C. *Agaricus*
D. *Spirogyra*
Answer: B

*Nitrosomonas* is a chemoautotrophic bacterium that obtains energy by oxidizing ammonia to nitrite. *Nostoc* and *Spirogyra* are photoautotrophs, while *Agaricus* (mushroom) is a heterotroph.

21. Which of the following statements correctly describes the basis of the five-kingdom classification system proposed by R.H. Whittaker?

A. Mode of nutrition, thallus organisation, and reproduction
B. Cell structure, mode of nutrition, and phylogenetic relationships
C. Presence or absence of a nucleus, cell wall composition, and habitat
D. Level of cellular organisation, mode of nutrition, and reproduction
Answer: B

R.H. Whittaker's five-kingdom classification system is based on three primary criteria: cell structure (prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic), mode of nutrition (autotrophic vs. heterotrophic), and phylogenetic relationships (evolutionary history). This system categorises organisms into Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. While options A and D mention some relevant factors, they do not fully capture the core criteria, particularly phylogenetic relationships. Option C incorrectly focuses on the presence of a nucleus and cell wall composition, which are not the primary bases of this classification.
