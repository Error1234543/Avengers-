PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Reproduction (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This section contains 12 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) based on the topic 'Reproduction' from the NEET Biology syllabus. Each question carries 4 marks, with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. Which of the following structures is responsible for the production of testosterone in human males?

A. Seminiferous tubules
B. Leydig cells
C. Sertoli cells
D. Epididymis
Answer: B

Leydig cells, also known as interstitial cells, are located in the testes and are responsible for the production of testosterone, the primary male sex hormone. Seminiferous tubules produce sperm, Sertoli cells support spermatogenesis, and the epididymis stores and matures sperm.

2. In human females, the process of oogenesis is initiated during:

A. Puberty
B. Embryonic development
C. Menstrual cycle
D. Fertilization
Answer: B

Oogenesis begins during embryonic development when primordial germ cells differentiate into oogonia. These oogonia enter meiosis I and arrest at the prophase I stage as primary oocytes, which remain dormant until puberty.

3. Which hormone is primarily responsible for triggering ovulation in the human menstrual cycle?

A. Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
B. Luteinizing hormone (LH)
C. Estrogen
D. Progesterone
Answer: B

A surge in luteinizing hormone (LH) triggers ovulation, the release of the secondary oocyte from the mature Graafian follicle. FSH stimulates follicle development, estrogen prepares the uterine lining, and progesterone maintains it post-ovulation.

4. The placenta in humans is derived from:

A. Only maternal tissue
B. Only fetal tissue
C. Both maternal and fetal tissues
D. Neither maternal nor fetal tissue
Answer: C

The placenta is a composite structure formed from both maternal tissue (decidua basalis) and fetal tissue (chorionic villi). It facilitates nutrient, gas, and waste exchange between the mother and fetus.

5. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of asexual reproduction?

A. Production of genetically identical offspring
B. Involvement of gametes
C. Rapid multiplication of organisms
D. Common in unicellular organisms
Answer: B

Asexual reproduction does not involve the formation or fusion of gametes. It produces genetically identical offspring (clones) and is common in unicellular organisms and some multicellular organisms, enabling rapid population growth.

6. In flowering plants, the process of double fertilization involves the fusion of:

A. One male gamete with the egg and the other with the synergid
B. One male gamete with the egg and the other with the central cell
C. Two male gametes with the egg
D. One male gamete with the antipodal cell and the other with the central cell
Answer: B

Double fertilization in angiosperms involves the fusion of one male gamete with the egg cell to form the zygote (2n) and the other male gamete with the two polar nuclei (or secondary nucleus) of the central cell to form the triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus.

7. Which of the following contraceptive methods acts by preventing ovulation?

A. Condoms
B. Intrauterine devices (IUDs)
C. Oral contraceptive pills
D. Vasectomy
Answer: C

Oral contraceptive pills contain synthetic hormones (estrogen and/or progesterone) that inhibit the release of FSH and LH from the pituitary gland, thereby preventing ovulation. Condoms and IUDs act as barriers or prevent implantation, while vasectomy blocks sperm transport.

8. The correct sequence of stages in the embryonic development of humans is:

A. Cleavage → Morula → Blastula → Gastrula
B. Morula → Cleavage → Blastula → Gastrula
C. Blastula → Morula → Cleavage → Gastrula
D. Cleavage → Blastula → Morula → Gastrula
Answer: A

The sequence of early embryonic development in humans is: Cleavage (rapid mitotic divisions) → Morula (solid ball of cells) → Blastula (hollow ball of cells) → Gastrula (formation of germ layers).

9. In humans, the sperm acrosome contains enzymes that help in:

A. Motility of the sperm
B. Penetration of the ovum
C. Energy production for the sperm
D. Storage of genetic material
Answer: B

The acrosome is a cap-like structure at the tip of the sperm head that contains hydrolytic enzymes (e.g., hyaluronidase and acrosin). These enzymes help the sperm penetrate the zona pellucida and corona radiata of the ovum during fertilization.

10. The term 'menarche' refers to:

A. The cessation of menstrual cycles in women
B. The first menstrual cycle in a woman's life
C. The monthly shedding of the uterine lining
D. The release of the ovum from the ovary
Answer: B

Menarche is the first occurrence of menstruation in a female, marking the beginning of her reproductive years. The cessation of menstrual cycles is called menopause, while ovulation refers to the release of the ovum.

11. In which part of the human female reproductive system does fertilization typically occur?

A. Ovary
B. Uterus
C. Fallopian tube
D. Cervix
Answer: C

Fertilization in humans typically occurs in the ampullary region of the Fallopian tube (oviduct). The ovum is released from the ovary, travels through the Fallopian tube, and if sperm are present, fertilization occurs there before the zygote moves to the uterus for implantation.

12. During the menstrual cycle, the luteal phase is characterized by the secretion of which hormone?

A. Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
B. Luteinizing hormone (LH)
C. Progesterone
D. Estrogen
Answer: C

The luteal phase of the menstrual cycle is dominated by the secretion of progesterone from the corpus luteum. Progesterone prepares the endometrium for potential implantation of a fertilized ovum. FSH and LH peak during the follicular phase, while estrogen is secreted throughout but peaks before ovulation.

13. Identify the correct sequence of events during spermatogenesis in humans:

A. Spermatogonia → Primary spermatocyte → Secondary spermatocyte → Spermatid → Spermatozoa
B. Primary spermatocyte → Spermatogonia → Secondary spermatocyte → Spermatid → Spermatozoa
C. Spermatid → Primary spermatocyte → Secondary spermatocyte → Spermatogonia → Spermatozoa
D. Spermatogonia → Secondary spermatocyte → Primary spermatocyte → Spermatid → Spermatozoa
Answer: A

Spermatogenesis begins with spermatogonia (diploid cells), which undergo mitosis to form primary spermatocytes. These undergo meiosis I to form secondary spermatocytes (haploid), which then undergo meiosis II to form spermatids. Spermatids finally differentiate into spermatozoa (mature sperm).

14. Which of the following is NOT a function of the placenta in humans?

A. Secretion of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)
B. Exchange of gases and nutrients between mother and fetus
C. Production of estrogen and progesterone
D. Production of sperm and ova
Answer: D

The placenta is responsible for secreting hCG, facilitating the exchange of gases and nutrients, and producing estrogen and progesterone to maintain pregnancy. However, it does not produce sperm or ova, which are produced in the gonads (testes and ovaries, respectively).

15. Which of the following statements about the acrosomal reaction is correct?

A. It occurs in the uterus after fertilization.
B. It involves the release of enzymes that help the sperm penetrate the zona pellucida.
C. It is triggered by the binding of the sperm to the endometrium.
D. It results in the formation of the second polar body.
Answer: B

The acrosomal reaction involves the release of hydrolytic enzymes from the acrosome of the sperm, which help it penetrate the zona pellucida of the ovum. This reaction is triggered by the binding of the sperm to the zona pellucida, not the endometrium, and occurs before fertilization.

16. The process of cleavage in human embryonic development results in the formation of:

A. Morula
B. Blastocyst
C. Gastrula
D. Both A and B
Answer: D

Cleavage is a series of rapid mitotic divisions of the zygote, leading to the formation of a morula (a solid ball of cells) and subsequently a blastocyst (a hollow ball of cells with an inner cell mass). Gastrulation occurs later, forming the gastrula.

17. Which hormone is primarily responsible for stimulating uterine contractions during parturition?

A. Estrogen
B. Progesterone
C. Oxytocin
D. Prolactin
Answer: C

Oxytocin, secreted by the posterior pituitary gland, is the primary hormone responsible for stimulating uterine contractions during parturition (childbirth). Estrogen and progesterone play roles in maintaining pregnancy, while prolactin is involved in lactation.

18. In humans, the germinal epithelium of the ovary gives rise to:

A. Primary oocytes
B. Secondary oocytes
C. Oogonia
D. Both A and C
Answer: D

The germinal epithelium of the ovary gives rise to oogonia, which are diploid cells that develop into primary oocytes. Secondary oocytes are formed later during oogenesis after the completion of meiosis I.

19. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of parthenogenesis?

A. Fertilization of an ovum by a sperm
B. Development of an organism from an unfertilized egg
C. Formation of identical twins
D. Development of an organism from a somatic cell
Answer: B

Parthenogenesis is a form of asexual reproduction in which an organism develops from an unfertilized egg. It does not involve fertilization by sperm or the formation of identical twins, nor does it involve somatic cells.

20. The correct sequence of stages in the development of the human embryo is:

A. Zygote → Morula → Blastocyst → Gastrula → Neurula
B. Zygote → Blastocyst → Morula → Gastrula → Neurula
C. Zygote → Morula → Gastrula → Blastocyst → Neurula
D. Zygote → Gastrula → Morula → Blastocyst → Neurula
Answer: A

The correct sequence of embryonic development in humans is: Zygote (fertilized egg) → Morula (solid ball of cells) → Blastocyst (hollow ball of cells) → Gastrula (formation of germ layers) → Neurula (formation of the neural tube).

21. Which of the following is NOT a method of assisted reproductive technology (ART)?

A. In vitro fertilization (IVF)
B. Zygote intrafallopian transfer (ZIFT)
C. Gamete intrafallopian transfer (GIFT)
D. Binary fission
Answer: D

Binary fission is a method of asexual reproduction observed in prokaryotes and some single-celled eukaryotes, not a method of assisted reproductive technology (ART). IVF, ZIFT, and GIFT are all ART techniques used to assist in human reproduction.

22. In humans, the process of spermatogenesis and oogenesis share which of the following features?

A. Both occur continuously from puberty to death in males and females, respectively.
B. Both produce haploid gametes through meiotic divisions, but oogenesis results in one functional ovum while spermatogenesis produces four functional spermatozoa.
C. Both processes are completed entirely within the gonads without any hormonal regulation.
D. Both involve the formation of polar bodies, but only in oogenesis do these polar bodies degenerate.
Answer: B

Spermatogenesis and oogenesis both involve meiotic divisions to produce haploid gametes. However, spermatogenesis results in four functional spermatozoa from one primary spermatocyte, while oogenesis produces one functional ovum and three polar bodies that degenerate. Option A is incorrect because oogenesis is not continuous; it is a cyclic process that ceases at menopause. Option C is incorrect as both processes are hormonally regulated. Option D is incorrect because polar bodies are formed only in oogenesis, not in spermatogenesis.

23. Which of the following statements correctly describes the role of the acrosome in human fertilization?

A. It contains enzymes that help the sperm penetrate the zona pellucida of the ovum.
B. It provides energy for the motility of the sperm tail.
C. It releases calcium ions to trigger the cortical reaction in the ovum.
D. It fuses with the plasma membrane of the ovum to form the zygote nucleus.
Answer: A

The acrosome is a cap-like structure at the anterior end of the sperm head, containing hydrolytic enzymes such as hyaluronidase and acrosin. These enzymes facilitate the sperm's penetration through the zona pellucida, the outer glycoprotein layer of the ovum, enabling fertilization. Option B is incorrect because energy for sperm motility is provided by mitochondria in the midpiece, not the acrosome. Option C is incorrect as the cortical reaction is triggered by the fusion of sperm and ovum membranes, not by calcium ions from the acrosome. Option D is incorrect because the acrosome does not fuse with the ovum membrane; instead, the sperm head fuses with the ovum to form the zygote nucleus.
