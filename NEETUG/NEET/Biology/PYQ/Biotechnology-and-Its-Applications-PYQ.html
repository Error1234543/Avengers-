PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE — NEET PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE: Biotechnology and Its Applications (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPYQ-Style Practice

This set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) modeled on NEET's Biotechnology and Its Applications chapter. Each question carries 4 marks with a -1 negative mark for incorrect answers. Select the single best answer for each question.

1. Which of the following enzymes is used to cut DNA at specific recognition sequences during recombinant DNA technology?

A. DNA polymerase
B. Restriction endonuclease
C. Ligase
D. Helicase
Answer: B

Restriction endonucleases are bacterial enzymes that recognize and cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences, creating sticky or blunt ends essential for recombinant DNA technology. DNA polymerase synthesizes DNA, ligase joins DNA fragments, and helicase unwinds DNA.

2. Golden Rice is genetically engineered to produce beta-carotene, a precursor of which vitamin?

A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin C
C. Vitamin D
D. Vitamin K
Answer: A

Golden Rice is biofortified to produce beta-carotene, which the human body converts into Vitamin A. This addresses Vitamin A deficiency, a major cause of childhood blindness in developing countries.

3. The Ti plasmid used in the creation of transgenic plants is naturally found in which bacterium?

A. Escherichia coli
B. Agrobacterium tumefaciens
C. Bacillus thuringiensis
D. Streptomyces hygroscopicus
Answer: B

Agrobacterium tumefaciens contains the Ti (tumor-inducing) plasmid, which naturally transfers a segment of its DNA (T-DNA) into plant genomes, causing crown gall disease. This property is exploited to introduce foreign genes into plants.

4. Which of the following is NOT a step in the polymerase chain reaction (PCR)?

A. Denaturation
B. Annealing
C. Elongation
D. Transcription
Answer: D

PCR involves three main steps: denaturation (separation of DNA strands), annealing (binding of primers), and elongation (DNA synthesis by Taq polymerase). Transcription is the process of synthesizing RNA from DNA and is not part of PCR.

5. Bt cotton is genetically modified to resist which of the following pests?

A. Aphids
B. Bollworms
C. Whiteflies
D. Locusts
Answer: B

Bt cotton contains genes from Bacillus thuringiensis that produce Cry proteins toxic to bollworms, a major pest of cotton crops. This reduces the need for chemical pesticides.

6. The process of introducing foreign DNA into a host cell is called:

A. Transcription
B. Translation
C. Transformation
D. Transduction
Answer: C

Transformation refers to the uptake and incorporation of foreign DNA into a host cell, a key step in genetic engineering. Transcription and translation are steps in protein synthesis, while transduction involves viral transfer of DNA.

7. Which of the following is a therapeutic product of biotechnology used to treat diabetes?

A. Insulin
B. Interferon
C. Erythropoietin
D. Growth hormone
Answer: A

Human insulin produced via recombinant DNA technology (e.g., Humulin) is used to treat diabetes. It was the first FDA-approved genetically engineered therapeutic product.

8. The technique used to amplify a specific DNA sequence in vitro is:

A. Gel electrophoresis
B. Polymerase chain reaction
C. Southern blotting
D. DNA fingerprinting
Answer: B

Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is an in vitro technique that amplifies a specific DNA sequence exponentially using primers, Taq polymerase, and thermal cycling. Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size, Southern blotting detects specific DNA sequences, and DNA fingerprinting is an application of these techniques.

9. Which of the following statements about genetically modified organisms (GMOs) is correct?

A. GMOs are always harmful to the environment.
B. GMOs can reduce the use of chemical pesticides.
C. GMOs are created by mutating organisms with radiation.
D. GMOs cannot be used in medicine.
Answer: B

GMOs like Bt crops can reduce the need for chemical pesticides by incorporating pest-resistant genes. While GMOs have potential risks, they are not inherently harmful, and many (e.g., insulin-producing bacteria) are used in medicine. Mutation breeding uses radiation, but it is distinct from genetic engineering.

10. The enzyme used to join two DNA fragments in recombinant DNA technology is:

A. DNA polymerase
B. Restriction endonuclease
C. DNA ligase
D. Reverse transcriptase
Answer: C

DNA ligase catalyzes the formation of phosphodiester bonds between DNA fragments, joining them together. Restriction endonucleases cut DNA, DNA polymerase synthesizes DNA, and reverse transcriptase synthesizes DNA from RNA templates.

11. Which of the following is an example of a biotechnological application in environmental cleanup?

A. Production of biofuels
B. Bioremediation using oil-eating bacteria
C. Cloning of endangered species
D. Gene therapy for genetic disorders
Answer: B

Bioremediation involves using microorganisms (e.g., oil-eating bacteria) to degrade environmental pollutants like oil spills. While biofuels are a biotechnological product, they are not primarily used for cleanup. Cloning and gene therapy are medical applications.

12. The first human protein produced using recombinant DNA technology was:

A. Growth hormone
B. Insulin
C. Interferon
D. Erythropoietin
Answer: B

Human insulin (Humulin) was the first recombinant DNA product approved for therapeutic use in 1982. It revolutionized diabetes treatment by providing a reliable, large-scale source of insulin.

13. Golden Rice is genetically engineered to produce beta-carotene, which is a precursor of:

A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin C
C. Vitamin D
D. Vitamin K
Answer: A

Golden Rice is genetically modified to produce beta-carotene, a precursor of Vitamin A. This biofortification aims to address Vitamin A deficiency, which is a major health concern in many developing countries.

14. The enzyme used to cut DNA at specific sequences during recombinant DNA technology is:

A. DNA polymerase
B. Restriction endonuclease
C. DNA ligase
D. RNA polymerase
Answer: B

Restriction endonucleases are enzymes that recognize and cut DNA at specific sequences, typically palindromic sequences. These enzymes are essential tools in recombinant DNA technology for creating DNA fragments with sticky or blunt ends.

15. Which of the following is a commonly used vector in gene cloning for plants?

A. pBR322
B. Ti plasmid
C. Lambda phage
D. BAC (Bacterial Artificial Chromosome)
Answer: B

The Ti (tumor-inducing) plasmid from *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* is commonly used as a vector for gene cloning in plants. It naturally transfers a segment of its DNA (T-DNA) into the plant genome, making it a useful tool for genetic engineering in plants.

16. Bt cotton is genetically modified to resist:

A. Viral infections
B. Bacterial infections
C. Insect pests
D. Herbicides
Answer: C

Bt cotton is genetically modified to express the *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) toxin, which provides resistance against certain insect pests, particularly lepidopteran larvae like the cotton bollworm.

17. Which of the following is an example of a genetically engineered human protein produced using biotechnology?

A. Insulin
B. Hemoglobin
C. Collagen
D. Myoglobin
Answer: A

Insulin is a genetically engineered human protein produced using recombinant DNA technology. It is widely used to treat diabetes and was one of the first therapeutic proteins produced through biotechnology.

18. The technique used to amplify a specific segment of DNA in vitro is:

A. Gel electrophoresis
B. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)
C. Southern blotting
D. DNA sequencing
Answer: B

The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is a technique used to amplify a specific segment of DNA in vitro. It involves repeated cycles of denaturation, annealing, and extension to produce millions of copies of the target DNA sequence.

19. Which of the following is a potential risk associated with genetically modified (GM) crops?

A. Increased nutritional value
B. Development of resistance in pests
C. Reduced use of pesticides
D. Improved crop yield
Answer: B

One potential risk associated with genetically modified (GM) crops is the development of resistance in pests. For example, prolonged use of Bt crops can lead to the evolution of Bt-resistant insect populations, which may reduce the effectiveness of the technology over time.

20. The process of transferring genes from one organism to another using a vector is called:

A. Gene therapy
B. Gene cloning
C. Gene expression
D. Gene mutation
Answer: B

Gene cloning is the process of transferring genes from one organism to another using a vector, such as a plasmid or viral DNA. This technique is fundamental to recombinant DNA technology and is used to produce genetically modified organisms (GMOs).

21. Which of the following techniques is used to identify specific DNA sequences in a sample?

A. PCR
B. Gel electrophoresis
C. Southern blotting
D. DNA sequencing
Answer: C

Southern blotting is a technique used to identify specific DNA sequences in a sample. It involves separating DNA fragments by gel electrophoresis, transferring them to a membrane, and hybridizing them with a labeled probe that binds to the target sequence.

22. The first genetically engineered vaccine approved for human use was for:

A. Polio
B. Hepatitis B
C. Influenza
D. Measles
Answer: B

The first genetically engineered vaccine approved for human use was the hepatitis B vaccine. It was developed using recombinant DNA technology to produce the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) in yeast cells.

23. A genetically engineered crop, 'Golden Rice', was developed to address the deficiency of which vitamin in populations relying heavily on rice as a staple food?

A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin B12
C. Vitamin C
D. Vitamin D
Answer: A

Golden Rice is a genetically modified crop engineered to produce beta-carotene, a precursor of Vitamin A, in the endosperm of rice grains. This was developed to combat Vitamin A deficiency, which is prevalent in populations where rice is a dietary staple and leads to severe health issues like childhood blindness and increased mortality. The gene for beta-carotene synthesis was introduced from daffodils and a soil bacterium, enabling the rice to biosynthesize this essential nutrient.

24. A genetically engineered crop variety, 'Golden Rice', was developed to address which of the following nutritional deficiencies?

A. Vitamin A deficiency
B. Vitamin C deficiency
C. Iron deficiency
D. Protein-energy malnutrition
Answer: A

Golden Rice is a genetically modified crop designed to produce beta-carotene, a precursor of Vitamin A, in the endosperm of rice grains. This biofortification aims to combat Vitamin A deficiency, which is a major public health issue in regions where rice is a dietary staple. The genes inserted into Golden Rice (primarily *psy* and *crtI* from daffodils and bacteria) enable the biosynthesis of beta-carotene, giving the rice its characteristic golden color.

25. In the context of gene therapy, which of the following statements correctly describes the use of adenoviral vectors?

A. They integrate into the host genome, ensuring long-term gene expression.
B. They are non-pathogenic and do not trigger immune responses in humans.
C. They can deliver therapeutic genes to non-dividing cells but may elicit immune reactions.
D. They are primarily used for correcting genetic disorders in germ-line cells.
Answer: C

Adenoviral vectors are commonly used in gene therapy because they can efficiently deliver therapeutic genes to both dividing and non-dividing cells. However, they do not integrate into the host genome, leading to transient gene expression. Additionally, adenoviruses can trigger immune responses, which may limit their repeated use. They are typically employed for somatic cell gene therapy, not germ-line modifications, due to ethical and safety concerns.
