NEET Quick Revision: Plant Physiology

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Plant Physiology: The study of functions and processes occurring in plants, including growth, development, metabolism, and responses to environmental stimuli.
  • Photosynthesis: The process by which green plants synthesize organic compounds (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight, releasing oxygen as a byproduct. Occurs in chloroplasts and involves light-dependent and light-independent (Calvin cycle) reactions.
  • Transpiration: The loss of water vapor from aerial parts of plants, primarily through stomata. It drives water and mineral absorption from roots and helps in cooling the plant.
  • Photoperiodism: The physiological response of plants to the relative lengths of daylight and darkness, influencing flowering, seed germination, and other developmental processes.
  • Phytohormones: Chemical substances produced in plants that regulate growth, development, and responses to environmental stimuli. Major classes include auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, and ethylene.
  • Nitrogen Fixation: The process of converting atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) into ammonia (NH₃) or related compounds, primarily carried out by symbiotic bacteria (e.g., *Rhizobium*) in root nodules of leguminous plants.
  • Respiration in Plants: The biochemical process of oxidizing organic compounds (e.g., glucose) to release energy (ATP), carbon dioxide, and water. Occurs in mitochondria and includes glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and electron transport chain.

Important facts

  • RuBisCO is the most abundant enzyme on Earth and catalyzes the fixation of CO₂ in the Calvin cycle, but it also exhibits oxygenase activity (photorespiration), which reduces photosynthetic efficiency.
  • C4 plants (e.g., maize, sugarcane) minimize photorespiration by spatially separating initial CO₂ fixation (mesophyll cells) and the Calvin cycle (bundle-sheath cells), using PEP carboxylase for the first step.
  • CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) plants (e.g., cacti, pineapples) open stomata at night to fix CO₂ into malate, storing it in vacuoles, and close stomata during the day to reduce water loss while performing the Calvin cycle.
  • The symbiotic relationship between legumes and *Rhizobium* bacteria involves nitrogen fixation in root nodules, where bacteria convert atmospheric N₂ into ammonia, which plants use to synthesize amino acids.
  • Stomatal opening is regulated by guard cells: uptake of K⁺ ions and water causes guard cells to swell, opening the stomatal pore. ABA triggers stomatal closure during water stress by promoting K⁺ efflux.
  • The pressure-flow hypothesis explains phloem transport: sugars are actively loaded into phloem sieve tubes at sources (e.g., leaves), creating high osmotic pressure that drives sap flow to sinks (e.g., roots, fruits).
  • Phototropism (growth toward light) is mediated by auxin, which accumulates on the shaded side of the stem, causing cell elongation and bending toward light. Gravitropism (growth in response to gravity) involves auxin redistribution in roots and shoots.
  • Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that promotes fruit ripening, leaf abscission, and senescence. It is used commercially to ripen fruits like bananas and tomatoes post-harvest.
  • The pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) generates NADPH and ribose-5-phosphate for biosynthetic reactions (e.g., fatty acid synthesis) and nucleotide production, operating parallel to glycolysis.

Memory aids

  • For photosynthesis stages: "Light makes ATP and NADPH, Dark fixes CO₂" (Light-dependent reactions produce ATP/NADPH; Calvin cycle fixes CO₂).
  • For phytohormones: "A G C A E" (Auxin, Gibberellin, Cytokinin, Abscisic acid, Ethylene) — remember as "A Great Chef Adds Eggs."
  • For mineral deficiency symptoms: "N-P-K-Ca-Mg-S" (Nitrogen: chlorosis; Phosphorus: purple leaves; Potassium: scorched edges; Calcium: stunted roots; Magnesium: interveinal chlorosis; Sulfur: yellowing of young leaves).
  • For photoperiodism: "Short-day plants need long nights, long-day plants need short nights" — focus on the critical night length, not day length.
  • For water potential: "Water flows from high to low potential" — think of it like a water slide (high Ψ at the top, low Ψ at the bottom).

Common traps

  • Confusing photorespiration with respiration: Photorespiration is a wasteful process in C3 plants where RuBisCO fixes O₂ instead of CO₂, reducing photosynthetic efficiency. Respiration is the energy-releasing process in all living cells.
  • Assuming all plants use the C3 pathway: C4 and CAM plants have evolved alternative pathways to minimize photorespiration and water loss in hot, dry climates.
