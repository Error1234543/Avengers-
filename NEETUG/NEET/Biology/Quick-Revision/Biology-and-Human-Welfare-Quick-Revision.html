Key concepts

  • {'concept': 'Types of Diseases', 'content': "Diseases can be classified into two broad categories: **infectious (communicable)** and **non-infectious (non-communicable)**. Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens and can spread from person to person (e.g., tuberculosis, malaria, AIDS). Non-infectious diseases are not caused by pathogens and include genetic disorders (e.g., sickle cell anemia), lifestyle diseases (e.g., diabetes, hypertension), and degenerative diseases (e.g., Alzheimer's). NEET often tests the distinction between these categories and the specific pathogens involved in infectious diseases."}
  • {'concept': 'Modes of Transmission of Infectious Diseases', 'content': 'Pathogens spread through various modes: **direct contact** (e.g., skin-to-skin, sexual contact), **indirect contact** (e.g., contaminated objects or surfaces), **droplet transmission** (e.g., coughing, sneezing), **airborne transmission** (e.g., tuberculosis, measles), **vector-borne transmission** (e.g., malaria via mosquitoes, dengue via Aedes aegypti), **fecal-oral transmission** (e.g., cholera, hepatitis A), and **vertical transmission** (e.g., mother to child during pregnancy or childbirth, such as HIV). Understanding these modes is critical for answering application-based questions on disease prevention.'}
  • {'concept': 'Immune System and Immunity', 'content': 'The immune system consists of **innate immunity** (non-specific, present from birth, includes physical barriers like skin, phagocytes, and natural killer cells) and **adaptive immunity** (specific, acquired after exposure to pathogens, involves B-cells, T-cells, and antibodies). Adaptive immunity is further divided into **active immunity** (developed after infection or vaccination, long-lasting) and **passive immunity** (acquired from external sources like maternal antibodies or antiserum, short-lived). NEET questions often focus on the differences between these types and their mechanisms.'}
  • {'concept': 'Vaccines and Immunization', 'content': 'Vaccines work by introducing a harmless version of a pathogen (e.g., killed, attenuated, or subunit) to stimulate the immune system to produce antibodies and memory cells. Examples include the **BCG vaccine** (for tuberculosis), **OPV/IPV** (for polio), **MMR vaccine** (for measles, mumps, rubella), and **DPT vaccine** (for diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus). Vaccination programs like the **Universal Immunization Programme (UIP)** in India aim to control or eradicate diseases (e.g., smallpox eradication, polio elimination). NEET may ask about specific vaccines, their types, or the diseases they prevent.'}
  • {'concept': 'Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Resistance', 'content': 'Antibiotics are classified based on their mechanism of action: **bactericidal** (kill bacteria, e.g., penicillin) or **bacteriostatic** (inhibit bacterial growth, e.g., tetracycline). Overuse or misuse of antibiotics leads to **antimicrobial resistance (AMR)**, where bacteria evolve to resist the effects of antibiotics. AMR is a global health threat, and NEET questions may test knowledge of antibiotic mechanisms, examples, or the consequences of resistance.'}
  • {'concept': 'Common Human Diseases', 'content': 'Key diseases frequently tested in NEET include: **Malaria** (caused by Plasmodium, transmitted by Anopheles mosquito), **Dengue** (caused by dengue virus, transmitted by Aedes aegypti), **Chikungunya** (viral, transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes), **Filariasis** (caused by Wuchereria bancrofti, transmitted by Culex mosquito), **AIDS** (caused by HIV, transmitted via body fluids), **Tuberculosis** (caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, airborne transmission), **Typhoid** (caused by Salmonella typhi, fecal-oral transmission), and **Hepatitis** (viral, types A-E with different modes of transmission). For each disease, NEET expects knowledge of the pathogen, vector (if applicable), symptoms, and prevention.'}
  • {'concept': 'Cancer: Causes and Prevention', 'content': 'Cancer is characterized by uncontrolled cell division due to mutations in genes regulating cell growth (e.g., oncogenes, tumor suppressor genes). Causes include **genetic factors**, **carcinogens** (e.g., tobacco smoke, asbestos, UV radiation), **viruses** (e.g., HPV causing cervical cancer, HBV causing liver cancer), and **lifestyle factors** (e.g., diet, alcohol, lack of exercise). Prevention strategies include avoiding carcinogens, vaccination (e.g., HPV vaccine), regular screening (e.g., Pap smear for cervical cancer), and early detection. NEET questions may focus on specific carcinogens or the role of viruses in cancer.'}
  • {'concept': 'Drugs and Addiction', 'content': 'Drugs of abuse include **opioids** (e.g., heroin, morphine), **cannabinoids** (e.g., marijuana), **cocaine**, **amphetamines**, **alcohol**, and **nicotine**. These substances alter brain chemistry, leading to addiction, dependence, and withdrawal symptoms. **Alcoholism** and **smoking** are major public health concerns, linked to diseases like liver cirrhosis, lung cancer, and cardiovascular disorders. NEET may test the effects of these drugs, their mechanisms, or the diseases they cause.'}
  • {'concept': 'Human Population and Family Planning', 'content': 'Overpopulation leads to resource depletion, environmental degradation, and public health challenges. **Family planning** methods include **natural methods** (e.g., rhythm method, withdrawal), **barrier methods** (e.g., condoms, diaphragms), **hormonal methods** (e.g., oral pills, injectables), **intrauterine devices (IUDs)**, and **sterilization** (e.g., vasectomy, tubectomy). NEET questions may focus on the effectiveness, mechanisms, or advantages/disadvantages of these methods.'}
  • {'concept': 'Global Health Initiatives', 'content': 'Key global and national health programs include: **WHO (World Health Organization)** for international health coordination, **National Health Mission (NHM)** in India (includes **Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK)** for child health and **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)** for maternal health), **Pulse Polio Programme** for polio eradication, and **National AIDS Control Programme (NACP)**. NEET may ask about the objectives or achievements of these programs.'}

Important facts

  • The **Anopheles mosquito** is the vector for **malaria**, while **Aedes aegypti** transmits **dengue** and **chikungunya**.
  • The **BCG vaccine** is used for **tuberculosis**, and the **MMR vaccine** protects against **measles, mumps, and rubella**.
  • HIV attacks **CD4+ T-cells**, weakening the immune system and leading to **AIDS**.
  • The **Pulse Polio Programme** in India has successfully eliminated **polio** (wild poliovirus) from the country.
  • The **Universal Immunization Programme (UIP)** in India covers vaccines for **tuberculosis, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, polio, measles, hepatitis B, and rotavirus**.
  • Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria develop mechanisms to survive exposure to antibiotics, often due to **overuse or incomplete courses of antibiotics**.
  • Cancer-causing agents include **tobacco smoke (lung cancer)**, **HPV (cervical cancer)**, and **HBV (liver cancer)**.
