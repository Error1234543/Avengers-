NEET Biology: Quick Revision - Reproduction

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Reproduction: The biological process by which new individual organisms (offspring) are produced from their parents, ensuring the continuity of species.
  • Asexual Reproduction: A mode of reproduction involving a single parent, resulting in genetically identical offspring (clones). Common in lower organisms like bacteria, fungi, and some plants.
  • Sexual Reproduction: A mode of reproduction involving two parents (male and female), resulting in genetically diverse offspring due to the fusion of gametes (sperm and ovum).
  • Gametogenesis: The process of formation of gametes (sperm and ovum) from germ cells through meiosis, reducing the chromosome number by half (haploid, n).
  • Fertilization: The fusion of male (sperm) and female (ovum) gametes to form a diploid zygote, restoring the chromosome number (2n).
  • Menstrual Cycle: A cyclic physiological process in human females (approximately 28 days) involving hormonal changes, ovulation, and preparation of the uterus for potential pregnancy. If fertilization does not occur, the endometrial lining is shed (menstruation).
  • Placenta: A temporary, disc-shaped organ formed during pregnancy in mammals, facilitating nutrient, gas, and waste exchange between the mother and fetus. It also secretes hormones like human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), estrogen, and progesterone.

Important facts

  • The smallest human cell is the sperm (head ~5 µm), while the largest is the ovum (~120 µm).
  • Human gestation period: ~280 days (40 weeks) from the last menstrual period (LMP).
  • The placenta is a fetomaternal organ, meaning it has both fetal (chorion) and maternal (endometrium) components.
  • Colostrum, the first milk produced post-childbirth, is rich in antibodies (IgA) and provides passive immunity to the newborn.
  • In flowering plants, the endosperm (3n) provides nutrition to the developing embryo, while the seed coat (2n) protects it.
  • Parthenocarpy: Development of fruit without fertilization (e.g., seedless grapes, bananas), often induced artificially using hormones like auxins.
  • Menarche: The first menstrual cycle in females (typically ages 12-15), marking the onset of puberty. Menopause: Cessation of menstrual cycles (~ages 45-55).
  • Ectopic pregnancy: Implantation of the embryo outside the uterus (e.g., fallopian tube), which is life-threatening and requires medical intervention.
  • Testosterone (male) and estrogen/progesterone (female) are steroid hormones regulating reproductive functions and secondary sexual characteristics.
  • In vitro fertilization (IVF): Assisted reproductive technology (ART) where fertilization occurs outside the body, followed by embryo transfer into the uterus.

Memory aids

  • Spermatogenesis vs. Oogenesis: 'Sperm is Simple (4 functional gametes), Ova is One (1 functional gamete + 3 polar bodies).'
  • Menstrual Cycle Hormones: 'FOL(d) M(a)P': FSH (Follicular phase), Ovulation (LH surge), Luteal phase (Progesterone).
  • Parts of the Male Reproductive System: 'SEVEN UP': Seminiferous tubules, Epididymis, Vas deferens, Ejaculatory duct, (Nothing), Urethra, Penis.
  • Double Fertilization in Plants: 'One for Baby (zygote), One for Food (endosperm).'
  • Phases of Embryonic Development: 'ZoMBie GaME': Zygote, Morula, Blastocyst, Gastrula, Embryo.

Common traps

  • Confusing spermatogenesis (4 functional sperm) with oogenesis (1 functional ovum + 3 polar bodies).
  • Assuming all plants reproduce sexually; some (e.g., potatoes, strawberries) reproduce asexually via vegetative propagation.
  • Misidentifying the site of fertilization in humans (fallopian tube, not uterus).
  • Overlooking the role of the corpus luteum in maintaining pregnancy via progesterone secretion (degenerates if no fertilization).
