  • {'term': 'Homeostasis', 'definition': 'The maintenance of a stable internal environment within the body, despite external changes, through regulatory mechanisms involving feedback loops (e.g., temperature, pH, blood glucose levels).'}
  • {'term': 'Neuron', 'definition': 'The basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system, specialized for transmitting electrical and chemical signals (nerve impulses).'}

  • {'term': 'Hormone', 'definition': 'A chemical messenger secreted by endocrine glands into the bloodstream to regulate distant target organs or tissues by binding to specific receptors.'}
  • {'term': 'Peristalsis', 'definition': 'A series of wave-like muscle contractions that move food through the digestive tract, ensuring unidirectional flow.'}
  • {'term': 'Hemoglobin', 'definition': 'An iron-containing protein in red blood cells that binds oxygen reversibly, enabling its transport from the lungs to body tissues and carbon dioxide from tissues to the lungs.'}
  • {'term': 'Nephron', 'definition': 'The functional unit of the kidney, responsible for filtering blood, reabsorbing essential substances, and excreting waste products to form urine.'}
  • {'term': 'Systole', 'definition': 'The phase of the cardiac cycle during which the heart muscle contracts and pumps blood from the chambers into the arteries (e.g., ventricular systole).'}
  • {'term': 'Diastole', 'definition': 'The phase of the cardiac cycle during which the heart muscle relaxes and allows the chambers to fill with blood.'}

Key concepts

  • {'concept': 'Nervous System', 'content': 'Divided into the Central Nervous System (CNS: brain and spinal cord) and Peripheral Nervous System (PNS: cranial and spinal nerves). The CNS integrates sensory input and coordinates motor output, while the PNS transmits signals between the CNS and the rest of the body. Neurons transmit impulses via action potentials, with myelinated fibers (e.g., in the spinal cord) conducting signals faster due to saltatory conduction. Neurotransmitters like acetylcholine, dopamine, and serotonin mediate synaptic transmission, with imbalances linked to disorders (e.g., Parkinson’s disease).'}
  • {'concept': 'Endocrine System', 'content': 'Comprises ductless glands (e.g., pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas) that secrete hormones regulating metabolism, growth, reproduction, and homeostasis. Hormones act via feedback loops (e.g., insulin lowers blood glucose, glucagon raises it). Key hormones include: (1) Thyroxine (T4) from the thyroid gland, regulating basal metabolic rate; (2) Adrenaline (epinephrine) from the adrenal medulla, triggering the ‘fight-or-flight’ response; (3) Growth Hormone (GH) from the anterior pituitary, stimulating growth and cell reproduction. Disorders include diabetes mellitus (insulin deficiency/resistance) and goiter (iodine deficiency).'}
  • {'concept': 'Digestive System', 'content': 'Breaks down food mechanically (e.g., chewing, peristalsis) and chemically (enzymes) into absorbable nutrients. Key organs: (1) Mouth: salivary amylase initiates starch digestion; (2) Stomach: gastric juice (HCl and pepsin) digests proteins; (3) Small Intestine: pancreatic enzymes (trypsin, lipase) and bile (from liver) complete digestion, with villi and microvilli increasing absorption surface area; (4) Large Intestine: absorbs water and electrolytes, forming feces. Disorders include peptic ulcers (H. pylori infection) and lactose intolerance (lactase deficiency).'}
  • {'concept': 'Respiratory System', 'content': 'Facilitates gas exchange (O2 in, CO2 out) via the lungs. Air passes through the nasal cavity → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli (site of gas exchange). Oxygen diffuses into pulmonary capillaries and binds to hemoglobin, while CO2 diffuses from blood into alveoli. The diaphragm and intercostal muscles drive ventilation (inhalation: diaphragm contracts, thoracic volume increases; exhalation: diaphragm relaxes, thoracic volume decreases). Disorders include asthma (bronchospasm) and emphysema (alveolar damage).'}
  • {'concept': 'Circulatory System', 'content': 'A closed system comprising the heart, blood vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries), and blood. The heart pumps oxygenated blood from the left ventricle to the body (systemic circulation) and deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs (pulmonary circulation). Blood components: (1) Plasma (55%): transports nutrients, hormones, and waste; (2) RBCs (erythrocytes): contain hemoglobin for O2 transport; (3) WBCs (leukocytes): immune defense; (4) Platelets: clotting. Blood pressure is regulated by cardiac output and peripheral resistance. Disorders include hypertension (high BP) and atherosclerosis (plaque buildup).'}
  • {'concept': 'Excretory System', 'content': 'Removes metabolic waste (e.g., urea, excess salts) via the kidneys, ureters, bladder, and urethra. Nephrons filter blood in three steps: (1) Glomerular Filtration: blood enters the glomerulus, and filtrate (water, glucose, urea) passes into Bowman’s capsule; (2) Tubular Reabsorption: essential substances (e.g., glucose, amino acids) are reabsorbed into capillaries; (3) Tubular Secretion: additional waste (e.g., H+, K+) is secreted into the filtrate. The hormone ADH (antidiuretic hormone) regulates water reabsorption in the collecting duct. Disorders include kidney stones (mineral deposits) and renal failure (loss of kidney function).'}
  • {'concept': 'Muscular System', 'content': 'Enables movement via skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscle. Skeletal muscle (voluntary) attaches to bones via tendons and contracts via sliding filament mechanism (actin and myosin). Smooth muscle (involuntary) lines organs (e.g., stomach, blood vessels), while cardiac muscle (involuntary) forms the heart wall. Muscle contraction requires ATP and calcium ions (released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum). Disorders include muscular dystrophy (genetic muscle degeneration) and myasthenia gravis (autoimmune neuromuscular disorder).'}
  • {'concept': 'Immune System', 'content': 'Defends against pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi) via innate (non-specific) and adaptive (specific) immunity. Innate immunity includes physical barriers (skin, mucus), phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages), and inflammation. Adaptive immunity involves lymphocytes: (1) B-cells: produce antibodies (humoral immunity); (2) T-cells: destroy infected cells (cell-mediated immunity). Memory cells provide long-term immunity (e.g., vaccines). Disorders include HIV/AIDS (T-cell destruction) and allergies (hypersensitive immune response).'}

Important facts

  • The human brain consumes ~20% of the body’s oxygen and glucose, despite accounting for only 2% of body weight.
  • The stomach’s pH is ~1.5–3.5 due to HCl, which activates pepsinogen into pepsin for protein digestion.
  • The small intestine is ~6–7 meters long, with villi increasing its surface area to ~200 m² for absorption.
  • Hemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen decreases in acidic conditions (Bohr effect), facilitating O2 release in tissues.
  • The kidneys filter ~180 liters of blood daily but excrete only ~1–2 liters of urine.
  • The SA node (sinoatrial node) in the right atrium acts as the heart’s natural pacemaker, initiating ~70–80 beats/min.
  • Insulin (from pancreatic β-cells) lowers blood glucose, while glucagon (from α-cells) raises it.
  • The liver produces bile, which emulsifies fats, and detoxifies blood (e.g., converting ammonia to urea).
  • Skeletal muscle contraction is triggered by acetylcholine release at neuromuscular junctions.
  • The lymphatic system returns interstitial fluid to the bloodstream and filters pathogens via lymph nodes.
