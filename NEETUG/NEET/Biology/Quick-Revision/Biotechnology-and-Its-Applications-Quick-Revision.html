NEET Quick Revision: Biotechnology and Its Applications

NEETEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Biotechnology: The integration of natural sciences and engineering sciences to achieve the application of organisms, cells, parts thereof, and molecular analogues for products and services.
  • Genetic Engineering: The deliberate modification of the characteristics of an organism by manipulating its genetic material.
  • Recombinant DNA Technology: Techniques used to join together DNA molecules from different sources and insert them into a host organism to produce new genetic combinations.
  • Transgenic Organisms: Organisms that have had foreign DNA introduced into their genome through genetic engineering techniques.
  • Bioreactor: A vessel or system designed to support biologically active environments, used for growing cells or tissues in biotechnology processes.
  • PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction): A laboratory technique used to amplify a single or few copies of a segment of DNA across several orders of magnitude, generating thousands to millions of copies.
  • Gel Electrophoresis: A technique used to separate DNA fragments based on their size and charge by applying an electric field to a gel matrix.
  • Cloning Vector: A small piece of DNA that can replicate independently within a host cell and is used to carry foreign DNA into a host cell.
  • Gene Therapy: The therapeutic delivery of nucleic acid into a patient's cells as a drug to treat disease by correcting genetic defects.
  • Bt Cotton: A genetically modified crop that produces an insecticidal protein derived from the bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* to resist pests.

Important facts

  • The first recombinant DNA molecule was created by Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer in 1973, marking the birth of genetic engineering.
  • Insulin was the first recombinant protein approved for human use (1982), produced using *E. coli*.
  • Bt cotton, developed by Monsanto, was the first GM crop approved for commercial cultivation in India (2002).
  • Golden Rice is engineered to produce beta-carotene (a precursor of vitamin A) to combat vitamin A deficiency in developing countries.
  • The Human Genome Project (completed in 2003) mapped all the genes in the human genome, enabling advances in personalized medicine.
  • CRISPR-Cas9 is a revolutionary gene-editing tool that allows precise modification of DNA sequences, with applications in medicine, agriculture, and research.
  • The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2003) is an international agreement to ensure the safe handling, transport, and use of GMOs.
  • Gene therapy for SCID (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency) was the first successful gene therapy trial (1990), though later trials faced setbacks due to leukemia development.
  • Monoclonal antibodies like Trastuzumab (Herceptin) are used to treat HER2-positive breast cancer by targeting specific proteins on cancer cells.
  • Biotechnology is used in the production of biofertilizers (e.g., nitrogen-fixing bacteria like *Rhizobium*) and biopesticides (e.g., *Bacillus thuringiensis*).

Memory aids

  • Remember the steps of genetic engineering with the mnemonic **I-C-A-I-I-S-E**: **I**solation, **C**utting, **A**mplification, **I**nsertion, **I**ntroduction, **S**election, **E**xpression.
  • For PCR steps: **D**enaturation (94°C), **A**nnealing (50-65°C), **E**xtension (72°C) — **DAE** like 'day' in Hindi.
  • Bt crops: **B**acillus **t**huringiensis produces **B**t toxin, which kills **b**oring insects (e.g., bollworms).
  • Golden Rice: **Golden** because it’s rich in **golden** beta-carotene (vitamin A precursor).
  • CRISPR-Cas9: **C**lustered **R**egularly **I**nterspaced **S**hort **P**alindromic **R**epeats — think of it as a **CRISPR** knife cutting DNA precisely.

Exam tips

  • Focus on **applications** rather than just theory: NEET often asks about real-world uses of biotechnology (e.g., insulin production, Bt cotton, gene therapy).
  • Memorize **key examples**: Know specific examples of GM crops (Bt cotton, Golden Rice), recombinant proteins (insulin, growth hormone), and gene therapies (SCID, cystic fibrosis).
  • Understand **PCR steps and temperatures**: Questions may ask about the purpose of each step (denaturation, annealing, extension) or the role of Taq polymerase.
  • Diagrams are crucial: Practice drawing and labeling diagrams of recombinant DNA technology, PCR, gel electrophoresis, and bioreactors.

Common traps

  • Confusing **restriction enzymes** (which cut DNA) with **DNA ligase** (which joins DNA fragments). Restriction enzymes create sticky ends, while ligase seals the nicks.
  • Assuming all GM crops are harmful: While ethical and environmental concerns exist, GM crops like Bt cotton have reduced pesticide use and increased yields in many regions.
  • Misidentifying **plasmids** as the only cloning vectors: While plasmids are common, bacteriophages, cosmids, and artificial chromosomes (YACs, BACs) are also used.
  • Overlooking the difference between **gene therapy** (correcting genetic defects) and **genetic engineering** (modifying genes for desired traits).
  • Forgetting that **PCR requires primers**: Primers are short DNA sequences that flank the target DNA and are essential for amplification.
  • Assuming **stem cells** are only derived from embryos: Adult stem cells (e.g., from bone marrow) are also used in therapies and research.
  • Confusing **DNA fingerprinting** with **DNA sequencing**: Fingerprinting analyzes repetitive sequences for identification, while sequencing determines the exact order of nucleotides.
  • Ignoring **biosafety levels**: Laboratories working with pathogens or GMOs are classified into BSL-1 to BSL-4 based on risk, with BSL-4 being the highest containment level.
