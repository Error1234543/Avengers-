NEET Biology Practice: Diversity in Living World (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 multiple-choice questions focused on the topic 'Diversity in Living World' for NEET. Each question has four options, and only one is correct. Read each question carefully and select the most appropriate answer. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which taxonomic category is immediately below 'Order' and above 'Genus'?

A. Class
B. Phylum
C. Family
D. Species
Answer: C

The hierarchical taxonomic categories are: Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Thus, 'Family' is the category immediately below 'Order' and above 'Genus'.

2. In Whittaker’s five-kingdom classification, which kingdom includes unicellular eukaryotic organisms?

A. Monera
B. Protista
C. Fungi
D. Plantae
Answer: B

Kingdom Protista includes unicellular eukaryotic organisms such as amoeba, Paramecium, and Euglena. Monera includes prokaryotes, while Fungi and Plantae are primarily multicellular.

3. Which of the following is a correct example of a binomial scientific name?

A. Homo
B. Sapiens
C. Homo sapiens
D. Homo Sapiens
Answer: C

In binomial nomenclature, the scientific name consists of the genus (capitalized) followed by the species (lowercase), both italicized or underlined. 'Homo sapiens' is the correct format for humans.

4. Which of the following kingdoms includes organisms that are exclusively heterotrophic and absorb nutrients from dead organic matter?

A. Monera
B. Protista
C. Fungi
D. Animalia
Answer: C

Fungi are heterotrophic organisms that obtain nutrients by decomposing dead organic matter (saprophytic nutrition) or by living as parasites. Monera and Protista include autotrophic and heterotrophic forms, while Animalia ingests food.

5. The three-domain system of classification, proposed by Carl Woese, divides organisms into:

A. Prokaryota, Eukaryota, and Archaea
B. Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya
C. Monera, Protista, and Fungi
D. Plantae, Animalia, and Fungi
Answer: B

Carl Woese proposed the three-domain system, classifying organisms into Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya based on ribosomal RNA sequences. This system reflects fundamental genetic differences.

6. Which of the following is NOT a criterion for classifying organisms in the five-kingdom system?

A. Cell structure (prokaryotic or eukaryotic)
B. Mode of nutrition (autotrophic or heterotrophic)
C. Presence of a cell wall
D. Geographical distribution
Answer: D

The five-kingdom classification system (Whittaker) is based on cell structure, mode of nutrition, thallus organization, and reproduction. Geographical distribution is not a criterion for classification.

7. In the hierarchical classification, which of the following is the broadest category?

A. Class
B. Phylum
C. Kingdom
D. Family
Answer: C

The hierarchical classification ranks from broadest to most specific: Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Thus, 'Kingdom' is the broadest category.

8. The process of arranging organisms into groups based on similarities and differences is called:

A. Identification
B. Nomenclature
C. Classification
D. Taxonomy
Answer: C

Classification is the process of arranging organisms into groups (taxa) based on shared characteristics. Taxonomy is the broader science of classification, nomenclature, and identification.

9. Which of the following pairs of organisms belong to the same kingdom but different phyla?

A. Amoeba and Paramecium
B. Penicillium and Agaricus
C. Spirogyra and Chlamydomonas
D. Moss and Fern
Answer: B

Penicillium (a fungus) and Agaricus (a mushroom) both belong to the kingdom Fungi but are classified under different phyla: Ascomycota and Basidiomycota, respectively. The other pairs belong to the same phylum.

10. Which of the following is a correct statement about lichens?

A. They are a symbiotic association between algae and bacteria.
B. They are classified under the kingdom Plantae.
C. They can survive in extreme environmental conditions.
D. They reproduce sexually by producing spores.
Answer: C

Lichens are a symbiotic association between fungi (usually Ascomycota) and algae or cyanobacteria. They are not classified under Plantae and can survive in extreme conditions like deserts and polar regions. While fungi in lichens can reproduce sexually, lichens themselves primarily reproduce asexually.

11. Which of the following is an example of a gymnosperm?

A. Pinus
B. Mangifera
C. Solanum
D. Triticum
Answer: A

Pinus (pine tree) is a gymnosperm, characterized by naked seeds not enclosed in an ovary. Mangifera (mango), Solanum (potato), and Triticum (wheat) are angiosperms, which produce seeds enclosed in fruits.

12. In the hierarchy of biological classification, which of the following is the correct sequence from highest to lowest rank?

A. Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
B. Kingdom → Class → Phylum → Order → Family → Genus → Species
C. Phylum → Kingdom → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species
D. Kingdom → Phylum → Order → Class → Family → Genus → Species
Answer: A

The correct hierarchical sequence in biological classification is: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, and Species. This sequence reflects the increasing specificity of classification.

13. The red algae (Rhodophyta) are characterized by the presence of:

A. Chlorophyll a and b
B. Phycobilins
C. Fucoxanthin
D. Starch as the storage product
Answer: B

Red algae (Rhodophyta) contain phycobilins (phycoerythrin and phycocyanin) as accessory pigments, which give them their characteristic red color. They primarily contain chlorophyll a and store floridean starch.

14. Which of the following is a distinguishing feature of bryophytes?

A. Presence of vascular tissue
B. Dominant sporophyte generation
C. Absence of roots
D. Seeds enclosed in fruits
Answer: C

Bryophytes (e.g., mosses, liverworts) lack true roots, stems, and leaves. They are non-vascular plants with a dominant gametophyte generation. They do not produce seeds or fruits.

15. A student observed a unicellular organism under a microscope that moves using pseudopodia. This organism most likely belongs to which of the following groups?

A. Ciliophora
B. Sarcodina
C. Mastigophora
D. Sporozoa
Answer: B

Organisms that move using pseudopodia belong to the group Sarcodina (e.g., *Amoeba*). Ciliophora move using cilia, Mastigophora use flagella, and Sporozoa are non-motile parasites.

16. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes between the terms 'species' and 'population' as used in biological classification and ecology?

A. A species is a group of organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring, while a population refers to all the organisms of the same species living in a specific geographic area at a given time.
B. A species includes all organisms that share similar morphological traits, whereas a population refers to organisms that occupy the same ecological niche.
C. A species is a taxonomic rank below genus, while a population is a group of organisms that share the same genetic mutations.
D. A species is defined by its ability to survive in a particular habitat, while a population is defined by its evolutionary history.
Answer: A

The correct distinction is that a **species** is a group of organisms capable of interbreeding and producing fertile offspring (biological species concept), while a **population** refers to all individuals of the same species living in a specific geographic area at a particular time. This definition aligns with standard biological and ecological principles tested in NEET. Option B incorrectly conflates species with morphological similarity, which is not the primary criterion. Option C misrepresents population as a genetic concept rather than a spatial-temporal one. Option D confuses habitat and evolutionary history with the definitions of species and population.

17. Which of the following statements correctly differentiates between the terms 'species' and 'population' as used in the study of biodiversity?

A. A species refers to a group of organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring, while a population refers to all the organisms of the same species living in a specific geographic area at a given time.
B. A species is a broader taxonomic rank than a population, which is a subset of a species confined to a particular habitat.
C. A population consists of multiple species interacting in a common environment, whereas a species is a single type of organism.
D. A species is defined by morphological similarities, while a population is defined by genetic diversity within a species.
Answer: A

The correct answer is A. In biological classification, a 'species' is defined as a group of organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring under natural conditions. A 'population,' on the other hand, refers to all the individuals of a particular species living in a specific geographic area at a given time. This distinction is fundamental in the study of biodiversity and ecology. Option B incorrectly suggests that a population is a taxonomic rank, which it is not. Option C misrepresents the definition of a population, as it consists of individuals of the same species, not multiple species. Option D confuses the definitions by incorrectly associating species with morphology and population with genetics, though genetics and morphology are relevant to both concepts.
