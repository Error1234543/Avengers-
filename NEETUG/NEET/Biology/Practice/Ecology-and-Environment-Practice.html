NEET Biology Practice - Ecology and Environment (Part 1)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Ecology and Environment for NEET. Each question has four options, only one of which is correct. Select the best answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following best defines an ecosystem?

A. A group of organisms of the same species living in a specific area
B. A community of living organisms interacting with their physical environment
C. The study of relationships between organisms and their environment
D. A large community of plants and animals occupying a major habitat
Answer: B

An ecosystem is defined as a community of living organisms (biotic components) interacting with their physical environment (abiotic components). It includes both living and non-living elements and their interactions.

2. In a food chain, which trophic level typically has the highest biomass?

A. Primary consumers
B. Secondary consumers
C. Tertiary consumers
D. Producers
Answer: D

Producers, such as plants and algae, form the base of the food chain and typically have the highest biomass. They convert solar energy into chemical energy through photosynthesis, supporting higher trophic levels.

3. Which of the following is an example of an in-situ conservation method?

A. Botanical gardens
B. Zoological parks
C. National parks
D. Seed banks
Answer: C

In-situ conservation involves protecting species in their natural habitats. National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and biosphere reserves are examples of in-situ conservation methods. Botanical gardens, zoological parks, and seed banks are ex-situ methods.

4. The phenomenon where pollutants become increasingly concentrated in higher trophic levels is known as:

A. Eutrophication
B. Biomagnification
C. Bioaccumulation
D. Desertification
Answer: B

Biomagnification refers to the increasing concentration of pollutants, such as pesticides or heavy metals, in the tissues of organisms at higher trophic levels in a food chain. This occurs because these pollutants are not easily metabolized or excreted.

5. The process by which nitrogen gas from the atmosphere is converted into ammonia by certain bacteria is called:

A. Nitrification
B. Denitrification
C. Ammonification
D. Nitrogen fixation
Answer: D

Nitrogen fixation is the process by which atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) is converted into ammonia (NH₃) or related nitrogenous compounds by certain bacteria, such as Rhizobium in leguminous plants or free-living bacteria like Azotobacter.

6. Which of the following is a consequence of ozone layer depletion?

A. Increased global temperatures
B. Increased incidence of skin cancer
C. Acid rain formation
D. Eutrophication of water bodies
Answer: B

Ozone layer depletion leads to increased ultraviolet (UV) radiation reaching the Earth's surface, which can cause skin cancer, cataracts, and other health issues in humans, as well as harm to plants and animals.

7. The relationship between clownfish and sea anemones is an example of:

A. Parasitism
B. Commensalism
C. Mutualism
D. Competition
Answer: C

The relationship between clownfish and sea anemones is an example of mutualism. The clownfish receives protection from predators by living among the anemone's tentacles, while the anemone benefits from the clownfish's waste, which provides nutrients.

8. Which of the following best describes the process of eutrophication?

A. The accumulation of pollutants in higher trophic levels
B. The enrichment of water bodies with nutrients, leading to excessive plant growth
C. The conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia
D. The depletion of oxygen in aquatic ecosystems due to pollution
Answer: B

Eutrophication is the process by which water bodies receive excessive nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, leading to dense plant growth (e.g., algae blooms). This can deplete oxygen levels and harm aquatic life.

9. Which of the following best describes the term 'ecological succession'?

A. The process of evolution of a species in response to environmental changes
B. The gradual and predictable change in species composition of a given area over time
C. The migration of species from one ecosystem to another due to climate change
D. The seasonal variation in population density of a species within an ecosystem
Answer: B

Ecological succession refers to the gradual and predictable process by which the species composition of a community changes over time in a given area. It can be primary (starting from bare rock) or secondary (starting from soil after a disturbance).

10. A species that has a disproportionately large impact on its environment relative to its abundance is known as a:

A. Keystone species
B. Dominant species
C. Pioneer species
D. Indicator species
Answer: A

A keystone species plays a critical role in maintaining the structure of an ecological community. Its impact is much larger than its abundance would suggest. Examples include sea otters in kelp forests or wolves in Yellowstone National Park.

11. The phenomenon where pollutants become increasingly concentrated in the tissues of organisms at higher trophic levels is called:

A. Eutrophication
B. Bioaccumulation
C. Biomagnification
D. Bioremediation
Answer: C

Biomagnification is the process by which the concentration of toxic substances increases in each successive link in the food chain. For example, DDT concentrations are higher in predatory birds than in the fish they eat.

12. Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?

A. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
B. Methane (CH₄)
C. Nitrogen (N₂)
D. Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
Answer: C

Nitrogen (N₂) is not a greenhouse gas. Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere and include carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and fluorinated gases.

13. The 'Red Data Book' is maintained by which international organization?

A. World Health Organization (WHO)
B. International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)
C. United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP)
D. World Wildlife Fund (WWF)
Answer: B

The Red Data Book is maintained by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN). It documents the conservation status of species and highlights those threatened with extinction.

14. Which of the following is a major cause of soil erosion?

A. Afforestation
B. Deforestation
C. Crop rotation
D. Terracing
Answer: B

Deforestation is a major cause of soil erosion. The removal of trees and vegetation reduces the soil's ability to hold together, making it more susceptible to erosion by wind and water.

15. The term 'biodiversity hotspot' refers to:

A. A region with high levels of species richness and endemism that is under threat from human activities
B. A region with extreme climatic conditions that supports only a few specialized species
C. A protected area where hunting and logging are strictly prohibited
D. A region with high levels of genetic diversity within a single species
Answer: A

A biodiversity hotspot is a region with high levels of species richness and a significant number of endemic species (species found nowhere else) that is also under threat from human activities. Examples include the Western Ghats and Sri Lanka.

16. Which of the following gases is primarily responsible for acid rain?

A. Carbon monoxide (CO)
B. Sulfur dioxide (SO₂)
C. Methane (CH₄)
D. Ozone (O₃)
Answer: B

Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) are the primary gases responsible for acid rain. These gases react with water, oxygen, and other chemicals in the atmosphere to form sulfuric and nitric acids, which fall to the ground as acid rain.

17. In a food chain consisting of phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → large fish → shark, which of the following statements correctly describes the energy flow and ecological efficiency?

A. The energy transferred from zooplankton to small fish is approximately 90% of the energy received by zooplankton from phytoplankton, as energy loss is minimal in aquatic ecosystems.
B. The ecological efficiency between trophic levels in this food chain is typically around 10%, meaning only about 10% of the energy from one trophic level is transferred to the next.
C. Sharks, being apex predators, receive nearly 50% of the energy originally captured by phytoplankton due to their high metabolic efficiency.
D. Energy loss in this food chain is primarily due to the production of biomass at each trophic level, with no significant loss as heat or through respiration.
Answer: B

Ecological efficiency refers to the percentage of energy transferred from one trophic level to the next in a food chain. In most ecosystems, including aquatic ones, this efficiency is typically around 10%. This means that only about 10% of the energy available at one trophic level is passed on to the next level. The remaining 90% is lost primarily as heat due to metabolic processes (respiration) and as waste. This principle explains why food chains rarely exceed 4-5 trophic levels, as energy becomes insufficient to support higher levels. The other options incorrectly suggest higher efficiencies or misrepresent the causes of energy loss.

18. In a food chain consisting of phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → large fish → shark, which of the following organisms is most likely to exhibit biomagnification of a non-biodegradable pesticide like DDT?

A. Phytoplankton
B. Zooplankton
C. Small fish
D. Shark
Answer: D

Biomagnification refers to the increasing concentration of non-biodegradable substances, such as pesticides like DDT, as they move up the food chain. Since the shark occupies the highest trophic level in this food chain, it will accumulate the highest concentration of DDT due to the consumption of large fish, which have already accumulated the pesticide from lower trophic levels. Phytoplankton, being at the base of the food chain, will have the lowest concentration of DDT.
