NEET Biology Practice: Genetics and Evolution (Part 1 of 2)

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Genetics and Evolution, aligned with the NEET Biology syllabus. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate option for each question.

1. Which of the following best describes the phenomenon where a single gene influences multiple phenotypic traits?

A. Polygenic inheritance
B. Pleiotropy
C. Epistasis
D. Incomplete dominance
Answer: B

Pleiotropy occurs when one gene affects multiple, seemingly unrelated phenotypic traits. For example, the gene responsible for sickle cell anemia also provides resistance to malaria. Polygenic inheritance involves multiple genes affecting a single trait, epistasis is the interaction between genes where one gene masks the expression of another, and incomplete dominance is a blending of traits where neither allele is fully dominant.

2. In a cross between two heterozygous tall pea plants (Tt), what is the probability of obtaining a homozygous recessive offspring?

A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50%
D. 75%
Answer: B

A cross between two heterozygous individuals (Tt × Tt) follows a 1:2:1 genotypic ratio. The probability of obtaining a homozygous recessive (tt) offspring is 25%, as one out of four possible combinations results in tt.

3. Which of the following is NOT a postulate of Darwin’s theory of natural selection?

A. Variations exist within populations
B. Acquired characteristics are inherited
C. Organisms compete for limited resources
D. Survival of the fittest
Answer: B

Darwin’s theory of natural selection is based on variations within populations, competition for resources, and survival of the fittest. The inheritance of acquired characteristics is a concept proposed by Lamarck, not Darwin.

4. In a dihybrid cross between two heterozygous individuals (YyRr × YyRr), what is the phenotypic ratio of the offspring?

A. 1:1:1:1
B. 3:1
C. 9:3:3:1
D. 1:2:1
Answer: C

A dihybrid cross between two heterozygous individuals (YyRr × YyRr) results in a phenotypic ratio of 9:3:3:1. This ratio represents the four possible combinations of dominant and recessive traits for two genes.

5. Which of the following mechanisms is responsible for the evolution of antibiotic resistance in bacteria?

A. Genetic drift
B. Natural selection
C. Gene flow
D. Mutation
Answer: B

Natural selection is the primary mechanism driving the evolution of antibiotic resistance in bacteria. Bacteria with mutations that confer resistance survive and reproduce in the presence of antibiotics, leading to the spread of resistant strains.

6. A man with hemophilia (X-linked recessive disorder) marries a woman who is a carrier for the disorder. What is the probability that their son will inherit hemophilia?

A. 0%
B. 25%
C. 50%
D. 100%
Answer: C

Hemophilia is an X-linked recessive disorder. The man’s genotype is XhY, and the woman’s genotype is XHXh. Their sons inherit the Y chromosome from the father and an X chromosome from the mother. The probability of inheriting the Xh chromosome (and thus hemophilia) is 50%.

7. Which of the following is a key difference between allopatric and sympatric speciation?

A. Allopatric speciation involves geographic isolation, while sympatric speciation does not
B. Sympatric speciation involves geographic isolation, while allopatric speciation does not
C. Allopatric speciation occurs only in plants, while sympatric speciation occurs only in animals
D. Sympatric speciation is driven by natural selection, while allopatric speciation is not
Answer: A

Allopatric speciation occurs when populations are geographically isolated, leading to reproductive isolation. Sympatric speciation occurs without geographic isolation, often due to polyploidy or ecological niche differentiation.

8. Which of the following statements about genetic linkage is correct?

A. Linked genes are always inherited together
B. Linked genes are located on different chromosomes
C. The closer two genes are on a chromosome, the higher the probability of crossing over between them
D. Linked genes do not assort independently during meiosis
Answer: D

Genetic linkage refers to genes located close to each other on the same chromosome, which do not assort independently during meiosis. While linked genes are often inherited together, crossing over can separate them. The closer two genes are, the lower the probability of crossing over between them.

9. Which of the following is an example of convergent evolution?

A. Wings of bats and birds
B. Forelimbs of humans and whales
C. Beaks of Darwin’s finches
D. Mimicry in butterflies
Answer: A

Convergent evolution occurs when unrelated species evolve similar traits due to similar environmental pressures. The wings of bats (mammals) and birds (avians) are an example of convergent evolution, as they evolved independently for flight. The other options represent divergent evolution or adaptive radiation.

10. A cross between a homozygous tall pea plant (TT) and a homozygous dwarf pea plant (tt) produces F1 progeny that are all tall. When these F1 plants are self-pollinated, what is the phenotypic ratio observed in the F2 generation?

A. 1:1
B. 3:1
C. 1:2:1
D. 9:3:3:1
Answer: B

This is a classic monohybrid cross demonstrating Mendel's Law of Segregation. The F1 generation (Tt) is heterozygous and exhibits the dominant tall phenotype. Self-pollination of F1 produces F2 progeny in a 3:1 phenotypic ratio (3 tall:1 dwarf), as the recessive trait reappears in one-fourth of the offspring.

11. Which of the following statements correctly describes the phenomenon of incomplete dominance?

A. The heterozygous phenotype is an exact blend of the two homozygous phenotypes.
B. The dominant allele completely masks the effect of the recessive allele in heterozygotes.
C. The heterozygous phenotype exhibits a distinct intermediate trait not seen in either homozygote.
D. Both alleles are expressed equally and independently in the heterozygote.
Answer: C

Incomplete dominance occurs when the heterozygous phenotype is an intermediate between the two homozygous phenotypes, not a blend. For example, in snapdragons, a cross between red (RR) and white (rr) flowers produces pink (Rr) flowers, demonstrating an intermediate trait.

12. The Hardy-Weinberg principle states that allele frequencies in a population remain constant from generation to generation in the absence of evolutionary influences. Which of the following is NOT an assumption of this principle?

A. No mutations occur
B. The population is infinitely large
C. Mating is non-random
D. No gene flow occurs
Answer: C

The Hardy-Weinberg principle assumes random mating, not non-random mating. Other assumptions include no mutations, no gene flow, no genetic drift (infinitely large population), and no natural selection. Non-random mating disrupts equilibrium by altering genotype frequencies.

13. Which of the following best describes the concept of genetic drift?

A. The movement of alleles between populations due to migration
B. Random changes in allele frequencies due to chance events in small populations
C. Differential survival and reproduction of individuals based on heritable traits
D. The introduction of new alleles into a population through mutation
Answer: B

Genetic drift refers to random fluctuations in allele frequencies, particularly in small populations, due to chance events like the founder effect or bottleneck effect. It is a non-selective mechanism of evolution and can lead to the loss or fixation of alleles.

14. The evolution of Darwin's finches on the Galápagos Islands is an example of which of the following?

A. Convergent evolution
B. Adaptive radiation
C. Parallel evolution
D. Coevolution
Answer: B

Adaptive radiation occurs when a single ancestral species diversifies into multiple species, each adapted to a different ecological niche. Darwin's finches evolved from a common ancestor into species with distinct beak shapes suited to different food sources on the Galápagos Islands.

15. The theory of punctuated equilibrium proposes that:

A. Evolution occurs gradually over long periods of time
B. Evolutionary changes occur in rapid bursts followed by long periods of stasis
C. Species remain unchanged for millions of years and then suddenly go extinct
D. Natural selection acts uniformly across all populations
Answer: B

Punctuated equilibrium, proposed by Stephen Jay Gould and Niles Eldredge, suggests that species experience long periods of little or no change (stasis), interrupted by brief periods of rapid evolutionary change. This contrasts with the gradualism model of evolution.

16. Which of the following statements about homologous structures is correct?

A. They have similar functions but different evolutionary origins
B. They are evidence of convergent evolution
C. They share a common evolutionary origin but may have different functions
D. They are found only in closely related species
Answer: C

Homologous structures are anatomical features that share a common evolutionary origin but may have different functions in different organisms. For example, the forelimbs of humans, bats, and whales have the same basic bone structure but serve different purposes (grasping, flying, swimming).

17. A population of moths exhibits industrial melanism, where dark-colored moths become more common in polluted areas. This is an example of:

A. Stabilizing selection
B. Directional selection
C. Disruptive selection
D. Sexual selection
Answer: B

Directional selection occurs when one extreme phenotype is favored over others, shifting the population's trait distribution in one direction. Industrial melanism in moths is a classic example, where dark-colored moths became more common in polluted areas due to better camouflage on soot-covered trees.
