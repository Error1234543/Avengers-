NEET Biology Practice - Human Physiology (Part 1 of 25)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Human Physiology for NEET. Each question has four options, only one of which is correct. Select the best answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following structures is responsible for the initiation of the heartbeat in a healthy human?

A. Atrioventricular node (AV node)
B. Sinoatrial node (SA node)
C. Bundle of His
D. Purkinje fibres
Answer: B

The sinoatrial node (SA node), located in the right atrium, is the natural pacemaker of the heart. It initiates the electrical impulses that regulate the heartbeat, causing the atria to contract. The AV node, Bundle of His, and Purkinje fibres conduct the impulse but do not initiate it.

2. A patient presents with symptoms of fatigue, pale skin, and shortness of breath. Blood tests reveal low haemoglobin levels. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Polycythaemia
B. Leukaemia
C. Anaemia
D. Haemophilia
Answer: C

Anaemia is characterised by a deficiency of haemoglobin or red blood cells, leading to reduced oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood. Symptoms include fatigue, pale skin, and shortness of breath. Polycythaemia is an excess of red blood cells, leukaemia is a cancer of white blood cells, and haemophilia is a bleeding disorder.

3. During the process of inspiration in humans, which of the following events occurs first?

A. Contraction of the external intercostal muscles
B. Relaxation of the diaphragm
C. Decrease in thoracic cavity volume
D. Increase in intra-pulmonary pressure
Answer: A

Inspiration begins with the contraction of the external intercostal muscles and the diaphragm. This contraction increases the volume of the thoracic cavity, leading to a decrease in intra-pulmonary pressure, which allows air to flow into the lungs. Relaxation of the diaphragm and a decrease in thoracic cavity volume occur during expiration.

4. Which hormone is primarily responsible for stimulating the secretion of hydrochloric acid (HCl) in the stomach?

A. Secretin
B. Gastrin
C. Cholecystokinin (CCK)
D. Somatostatin
Answer: B

Gastrin, secreted by G cells in the stomach lining, stimulates parietal cells to secrete hydrochloric acid (HCl). Secretin and cholecystokinin (CCK) are involved in pancreatic and biliary secretions, while somatostatin inhibits gastric secretion.

5. A person with blood group AB can receive blood from which of the following blood groups during a transfusion?

A. Only AB
B. A, B, AB, and O
C. Only A and B
D. Only O
Answer: B

Individuals with blood group AB are universal recipients because their red blood cells possess both A and B antigens and lack anti-A and anti-B antibodies. Thus, they can receive blood from A, B, AB, and O blood groups.

6. Which part of the nephron is primarily responsible for the reabsorption of glucose and amino acids from the filtrate?

A. Glomerulus
B. Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)
C. Loop of Henle
D. Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)
Answer: B

The proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) is the primary site for the reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, water, and ions from the glomerular filtrate. The glomerulus filters the blood, while the Loop of Henle and DCT are involved in concentration and selective reabsorption of ions and water.

7. In the human digestive system, bile is stored and concentrated in which organ before being released into the duodenum?

A. Liver
B. Pancreas
C. Gallbladder
D. Stomach
Answer: C

Bile, produced by the liver, is stored and concentrated in the gallbladder before being released into the duodenum to aid in the digestion and absorption of fats. The pancreas produces digestive enzymes but does not store bile.

8. Which of the following is a function of the hormone insulin?

A. Increasing blood glucose levels
B. Stimulating glycogenolysis in the liver
C. Promoting the uptake of glucose by cells
D. Stimulating gluconeogenesis
Answer: C

Insulin, secreted by the beta cells of the pancreas, promotes the uptake of glucose by cells, thereby lowering blood glucose levels. It also stimulates glycogenesis (formation of glycogen) and inhibits glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.

9. A defect in the production of which clotting factor is responsible for haemophilia A?

A. Factor VIII
B. Factor IX
C. Factor X
D. Factor XI
Answer: A

Haemophilia A is caused by a deficiency or defect in clotting Factor VIII. Haemophilia B is caused by a deficiency in Factor IX. Factors X and XI are involved in other stages of the clotting cascade but are not associated with haemophilia A.

10. Which of the following best describes the role of the juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA) in the kidney?

A. Filtration of blood plasma
B. Reabsorption of water and ions
C. Regulation of blood pressure and glomerular filtration rate
D. Secretion of hydrogen ions
Answer: C

The juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA) plays a key role in regulating blood pressure and the glomerular filtration rate (GFR) by secreting renin in response to low blood pressure or low sodium levels. Filtration occurs in the glomerulus, reabsorption in the tubules, and hydrogen ion secretion in the distal tubule.

11. A patient presents with symptoms of muscle cramps, weakness, and irregular heartbeat. Blood tests reveal low calcium levels. Which hormone is most likely deficient in this patient?

A. Parathyroid hormone (PTH)
B. Calcitonin
C. Thyroxine (T4)
D. Aldosterone
Answer: A

Parathyroid hormone (PTH) regulates calcium levels in the blood. Its deficiency leads to hypocalcemia, causing muscle cramps, weakness, and irregular heartbeat. Calcitonin lowers blood calcium, while thyroxine and aldosterone do not primarily regulate calcium.

12. Which of the following correctly describes the role of the sinoatrial (SA) node in the human heart?

A. It delays the electrical impulse to allow ventricular filling.
B. It initiates the heartbeat and sets the pace for the entire heart.
C. It conducts impulses from the atria to the ventricles via the bundle of His.
D. It is responsible for the contraction of the ventricles.
Answer: B

The sinoatrial (SA) node is the natural pacemaker of the heart, initiating the heartbeat and determining the heart rate. The atrioventricular (AV) node delays the impulse, while the bundle of His and Purkinje fibers conduct impulses to the ventricles.

13. In the human digestive system, which enzyme is responsible for the breakdown of proteins into peptides in the stomach?

A. Amylase
B. Lipase
C. Pepsin
D. Trypsin
Answer: C

Pepsin is the enzyme secreted in the stomach that breaks down proteins into smaller peptides. Amylase digests carbohydrates, lipase digests fats, and trypsin acts in the small intestine.

14. Which of the following hormones is secreted by the posterior pituitary gland and plays a key role in water reabsorption in the kidneys?

A. Oxytocin
B. Antidiuretic hormone (ADH)
C. Growth hormone (GH)
D. Prolactin
Answer: B

Antidiuretic hormone (ADH), also known as vasopressin, is secreted by the posterior pituitary gland and increases water reabsorption in the kidneys, reducing urine output. Oxytocin is involved in childbirth and lactation, while GH and prolactin have other functions.

15. A deficiency of vitamin D in the human body primarily affects which of the following processes?

A. Blood clotting
B. Calcium absorption in the intestines
C. Collagen synthesis
D. Night vision
Answer: B

Vitamin D is essential for calcium absorption in the intestines. Its deficiency leads to impaired calcium absorption, causing bone disorders like rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults. Blood clotting is affected by vitamin K, collagen synthesis by vitamin C, and night vision by vitamin A.

16. During strenuous exercise, which of the following changes occurs in the human body to meet increased oxygen demand?

A. Decreased heart rate to conserve energy
B. Vasoconstriction of blood vessels in skeletal muscles
C. Increased cardiac output and blood flow to muscles
D. Reduced breathing rate to prevent hyperventilation
Answer: C

During strenuous exercise, the body increases cardiac output (heart rate and stroke volume) and redirects blood flow to skeletal muscles to meet the increased oxygen demand. Vasodilation, not vasoconstriction, occurs in muscles, and breathing rate increases to enhance oxygen intake.

17. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of the human small intestine that enhances nutrient absorption?

A. Presence of rugae to increase surface area
B. Villi and microvilli to increase surface area
C. Thick muscular walls to aid peristalsis
D. Secretion of hydrochloric acid for digestion
Answer: B

The small intestine contains villi and microvilli, which significantly increase the surface area for nutrient absorption. Rugae are found in the stomach, thick muscular walls aid peristalsis but do not enhance absorption, and hydrochloric acid is secreted in the stomach, not the small intestine.

18. A patient presents with symptoms of muscle cramps, weakness, and irregular heartbeat. Laboratory tests reveal hypokalemia (low potassium levels). Which of the following physiological processes is most likely to be directly affected by this condition?

A. Depolarization of the neuronal resting membrane potential
B. Repolarization phase of the cardiac action potential
C. Release of neurotransmitters at the neuromuscular junction
D. Contraction of smooth muscle in blood vessels
Answer: B

Potassium ions (K+) play a critical role in the repolarization phase of the cardiac action potential. During repolarization, K+ efflux from cardiac muscle cells restores the negative resting membrane potential. Hypokalemia reduces the availability of K+ for this efflux, leading to prolonged repolarization, which can cause irregular heartbeats (arrhythmias) and muscle weakness. While K+ is also important for neuronal and neuromuscular function, the most direct and life-threatening effect of hypokalemia is on the heart's electrical activity, particularly repolarization.
