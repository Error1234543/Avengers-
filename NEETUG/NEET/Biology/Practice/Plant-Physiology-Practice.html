NEET Biology Practice - Plant Physiology (Part 1)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Plant Physiology for NEET. Each question has four options, only one of which is correct. Select the best answer for each question. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following is the primary site of photolysis of water during the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis?

A. Stroma of chloroplast
B. Thylakoid lumen
C. Outer membrane of chloroplast
D. Granum
Answer: B

Photolysis of water occurs in the thylakoid lumen, where the oxygen-evolving complex (OEC) of Photosystem II splits water molecules into oxygen, protons, and electrons. The protons contribute to the proton gradient, while electrons enter the electron transport chain.

2. A plant shows wilting even when the soil has adequate water. Which hormone is most likely deficient in this plant?

A. Auxin
B. Gibberellin
C. Abscisic acid
D. Cytokinin
Answer: C

Abscisic acid (ABA) regulates stomatal closure, preventing water loss during drought stress. A deficiency in ABA would impair the plant's ability to close stomata, leading to excessive transpiration and wilting despite sufficient soil water.

3. In C4 plants, the enzyme PEP carboxylase fixes CO2 into oxaloacetate in which of the following cells?

A. Bundle sheath cells
B. Mesophyll cells
C. Epidermal cells
D. Guard cells
Answer: B

In C4 plants, the initial fixation of CO2 occurs in mesophyll cells, where PEP carboxylase catalyzes the reaction of CO2 with phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) to form oxaloacetate, which is later converted to malate or aspartate.

4. The phytochrome system in plants is primarily involved in which of the following processes?

A. Phototropism
B. Photoperiodism
C. Gravitropism
D. Thigmotropism
Answer: B

Phytochromes are photoreceptors that regulate photoperiodism, the plant's response to day length, which controls processes such as flowering, seed germination, and dormancy. They exist in two interconvertible forms: Pr (red-absorbing) and Pfr (far-red-absorbing).

5. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of CAM plants?

A. They fix CO2 during the day using PEP carboxylase.
B. They perform the Calvin cycle in bundle sheath cells.
C. They open their stomata at night to minimize water loss.
D. They lack the enzyme RuBisCO.
Answer: C

Crassulacean Acid Metabolism (CAM) plants open their stomata at night to fix CO2 into malate, which is stored in vacuoles. During the day, stomata close to conserve water, and malate is decarboxylated to release CO2 for the Calvin cycle.

6. A researcher observes that a plant exhibits positive phototropism. Which of the following best explains this phenomenon?

A. Auxin accumulates on the illuminated side of the stem, promoting cell elongation.
B. Auxin accumulates on the shaded side of the stem, promoting cell elongation.
C. Cytokinins are transported to the illuminated side, inhibiting growth.
D. Abscisic acid accumulates on the shaded side, promoting growth.
Answer: B

Positive phototropism occurs when auxin accumulates on the shaded side of the stem, causing cell elongation and bending toward the light source. This redistribution of auxin is mediated by phototropins, which are blue-light receptors.

7. In the process of nitrogen fixation, atmospheric nitrogen (N2) is converted into ammonia (NH3) by which of the following enzymes?

A. Nitrate reductase
B. Nitrogenase
C. Glutamine synthetase
D. Nitrite reductase
Answer: B

Nitrogenase is the enzyme complex responsible for nitrogen fixation, catalyzing the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen (N2) into ammonia (NH3). This process is carried out by nitrogen-fixing bacteria, such as Rhizobium in legume root nodules.

8. Which of the following statements about the apoplastic pathway of water movement in plants is correct?

A. Water moves through the cytoplasm of cells via plasmodesmata.
B. Water moves through the cell walls and intercellular spaces.
C. Water is actively transported across cell membranes.
D. Water movement is regulated by aquaporins in the symplast.
Answer: B

The apoplastic pathway involves the movement of water through the cell walls and intercellular spaces, bypassing the cell membranes. This pathway is passive and does not require energy, but it is blocked at the Casparian strip in the endodermis, forcing water into the symplast.

9. A plant exhibits chlorosis, a condition characterized by yellowing of leaves. Which of the following nutrient deficiencies is most likely responsible?

A. Nitrogen
B. Phosphorus
C. Potassium
D. Calcium
Answer: A

Chlorosis, or yellowing of leaves, is commonly caused by nitrogen deficiency. Nitrogen is a key component of chlorophyll, and its deficiency leads to reduced chlorophyll synthesis, resulting in yellowing of leaves, particularly in older leaves first.

10. Which of the following processes is directly responsible for the generation of the proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane during photosynthesis?

A. Photolysis of water
B. Reduction of NADP+ to NADPH
C. ATP synthesis by ATP synthase
D. Fixation of CO2 in the Calvin cycle
Answer: A

The photolysis of water in the thylakoid lumen releases protons (H+), electrons, and oxygen. The protons contribute to the proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane, which drives ATP synthesis via ATP synthase during the light-dependent reactions.

11. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of the Calvin cycle?

A. It occurs in the stroma of chloroplasts
B. It requires ATP and NADPH produced in the light-dependent reactions
C. It directly produces glucose as its end product
D. It involves the enzyme RuBisCO for carbon fixation
Answer: C

The Calvin cycle does not directly produce glucose. Instead, it produces glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), a 3-carbon sugar, which is later used to synthesize glucose and other carbohydrates. The cycle occurs in the stroma, requires ATP and NADPH, and uses RuBisCO for carbon fixation.

12. During photorespiration, which organelle is primarily responsible for the oxygenation of RuBP?

A. Mitochondrion
B. Chloroplast
C. Peroxisome
D. Glyoxysome
Answer: B

Photorespiration begins in the chloroplast when RuBisCO oxygenates RuBP instead of carboxylating it. This produces phosphoglycolate, which is then metabolized in peroxisomes and mitochondria, but the initial oxygenation occurs in the chloroplast.

13. Which of the following statements about C4 plants is correct?

A. They perform the Calvin cycle in mesophyll cells
B. They use PEP carboxylase for initial carbon fixation in bundle-sheath cells
C. They are typically found in temperate climates
D. They exhibit a spatial separation of initial carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle
Answer: D

C4 plants exhibit spatial separation of initial carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle. Initial carbon fixation occurs in mesophyll cells using PEP carboxylase, while the Calvin cycle occurs in bundle-sheath cells. They are typically found in hot, dry climates.

14. The movement of sucrose from leaves to other parts of the plant occurs through:

A. Xylem vessels
B. Phloem sieve tubes
C. Tracheids
D. Companion cells
Answer: B

Sucrose is transported from leaves (source) to other parts of the plant (sink) through phloem sieve tubes via the process of translocation. Xylem vessels and tracheids transport water and minerals, while companion cells assist sieve tubes but do not directly transport sucrose.

15. Which of the following is a direct effect of ethylene in plants?

A. Promotion of seed germination
B. Inhibition of fruit ripening
C. Induction of leaf abscission
D. Stimulation of root elongation
Answer: C

Ethylene is a plant hormone that promotes fruit ripening, induces leaf abscission (shedding), and inhibits root elongation. It does not promote seed germination; gibberellins and abscisic acid are more directly involved in that process.

16. In the process of nitrogen fixation, atmospheric nitrogen (N2) is converted into:

A. Nitrate (NO3-) by nitrifying bacteria
B. Ammonia (NH3) by nitrogen-fixing bacteria
C. Nitrite (NO2-) by denitrifying bacteria
D. Amino acids by plants directly
Answer: B

Nitrogen fixation is the process by which atmospheric nitrogen (N2) is converted into ammonia (NH3) or related nitrogenous compounds by nitrogen-fixing bacteria, such as Rhizobium in root nodules or free-living bacteria like Azotobacter.

17. Which of the following is a symptom of magnesium deficiency in plants?

A. Interveinal chlorosis in older leaves
B. Necrosis at leaf tips and margins
C. Stunted root growth
D. Purple coloration on the underside of leaves
Answer: A

Magnesium is a central component of the chlorophyll molecule. Its deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between veins) in older leaves first, as magnesium is mobile and is translocated to younger leaves.

18. The process by which water is lost from the aerial parts of plants in the form of vapor is called:

A. Guttation
B. Transpiration
C. Respiration
D. Evaporation
Answer: B

Transpiration is the process by which water is lost from the aerial parts of plants, primarily through stomata, in the form of water vapor. Guttation is the loss of liquid water, while evaporation is a general physical process not specific to plants.

19. A plant hormone that promotes cell division and delays senescence is:

A. Auxin
B. Gibberellin
C. Cytokinin
D. Abscisic acid
Answer: C

Cytokinins are plant hormones that promote cell division (cytokinesis), delay senescence (aging), and are involved in shoot initiation and growth. Auxins promote cell elongation, gibberellins promote stem elongation and seed germination, and abscisic acid promotes senescence and stress responses.

20. In CAM plants, malic acid is stored in which organelle during the night?

A. Chloroplast
B. Mitochondrion
C. Vacuole
D. Peroxisome
Answer: C

In CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) plants, CO2 is fixed into malic acid during the night and stored in the vacuole. During the day, malic acid is decarboxylated to release CO2 for the Calvin cycle, allowing stomata to remain closed to conserve water.

21. In a plant cell, the process of photophosphorylation occurs in which of the following structures and involves the synthesis of which molecule?

A. Thylakoid membrane; ATP
B. Stroma; NADPH
C. Mitochondrial inner membrane; ATP
D. Cytosol; Glucose
Answer: A

Photophosphorylation is the process of synthesizing ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate using light energy during the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis. This process occurs in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplasts, where the electron transport chain and ATP synthase are located. The energy from light excites electrons in chlorophyll, driving the formation of a proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane, which is then used by ATP synthase to produce ATP. NADPH is also produced during the light-dependent reactions but is not the direct product of photophosphorylation. The other options incorrectly associate the process with structures or molecules not involved in photophosphorylation.
