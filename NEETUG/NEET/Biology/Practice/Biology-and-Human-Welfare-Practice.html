NEET Biology Practice - Biology and Human Welfare (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Biology and Human Welfare for NEET. Each question has four options, only one of which is correct. Read carefully and select the best answer. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern.

1. Which of the following diseases is caused by a protozoan parasite and transmitted by the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito?

A. Dengue
B. Filariasis
C. Malaria
D. Chikungunya
Answer: C

Malaria is caused by the protozoan parasite Plasmodium and is transmitted through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito. Dengue and Chikungunya are viral diseases transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes, while Filariasis is caused by nematode worms transmitted by Culex mosquitoes.

2. The BCG vaccine is used for the prevention of which of the following diseases?

A. Tuberculosis
B. Diphtheria
C. Tetanus
D. Pertussis
Answer: A

The BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin) vaccine is specifically used for the prevention of tuberculosis (TB), a bacterial disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. The other options are prevented by the DPT vaccine (Diphtheria, Pertussis, and Tetanus).

3. Which of the following is NOT a method of biological control of pests?

A. Use of ladybird beetles to control aphids
B. Introduction of Bacillus thuringiensis to kill caterpillars
C. Spraying DDT to eliminate mosquitoes
D. Release of Trichogramma wasps to destroy insect eggs
Answer: C

Spraying DDT is a chemical method of pest control, not biological. Biological control involves the use of living organisms (e.g., predators, parasites, or pathogens) to control pests, such as ladybird beetles for aphids, Bacillus thuringiensis for caterpillars, and Trichogramma wasps for insect eggs.

4. A patient presents with symptoms of jaundice, fatigue, and abdominal pain. Blood tests reveal the presence of HBsAg. Which of the following diseases is the patient most likely suffering from?

A. Hepatitis A
B. Hepatitis B
C. Hepatitis C
D. Hepatitis E
Answer: B

The presence of HBsAg (Hepatitis B surface antigen) in the blood is a diagnostic marker for Hepatitis B. Hepatitis A and E are typically transmitted through contaminated food/water, while Hepatitis C is primarily bloodborne but does not involve HBsAg.

5. Which of the following statements about antibiotics is INCORRECT?

A. Antibiotics are effective against bacterial infections.
B. Overuse of antibiotics can lead to the development of resistant strains of bacteria.
C. Antibiotics can be used to treat viral infections like the common cold.
D. Penicillin was the first antibiotic discovered by Alexander Fleming.
Answer: C

Antibiotics are ineffective against viral infections, including the common cold. They specifically target bacterial pathogens. The other statements are correct: antibiotics work against bacteria, overuse leads to resistance, and penicillin was the first antibiotic discovered.

6. The process of converting atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia by certain bacteria is called:

A. Nitrification
B. Denitrification
C. Nitrogen fixation
D. Ammonification
Answer: C

Nitrogen fixation is the process by which atmospheric nitrogen (N2) is converted into ammonia (NH3) or related nitrogenous compounds by certain bacteria, such as Rhizobium in leguminous plants. Nitrification is the oxidation of ammonia to nitrites/nitrates, denitrification is the reduction of nitrates to nitrogen gas, and ammonification is the conversion of organic nitrogen to ammonia.

7. Which of the following is a sexually transmitted disease (STD) caused by a bacterium?

A. Genital herpes
B. Gonorrhea
C. HIV/AIDS
D. Hepatitis B
Answer: B

Gonorrhea is a sexually transmitted disease caused by the bacterium Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Genital herpes is caused by the herpes simplex virus, HIV/AIDS by the human immunodeficiency virus, and Hepatitis B by the hepatitis B virus.

8. The vector responsible for transmitting the dengue virus is:

A. Anopheles mosquito
B. Culex mosquito
C. Aedes mosquito
D. Sandfly
Answer: C

The Aedes mosquito (specifically Aedes aegypti) is the primary vector responsible for transmitting the dengue virus. Anopheles mosquitoes transmit malaria, Culex mosquitoes transmit filariasis, and sandflies transmit leishmaniasis.

9. Which of the following is a chronic respiratory disease characterized by the inflammation and narrowing of the airways, leading to symptoms like wheezing, coughing, and shortness of breath?

A. Tuberculosis
B. Asthma
C. Pneumonia
D. Emphysema
Answer: B

Asthma is a chronic respiratory disease characterized by inflammation and narrowing of the airways, resulting in symptoms such as wheezing, coughing, and shortness of breath. Tuberculosis is a bacterial infection, pneumonia is an acute lung infection, and emphysema is a type of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) caused by damage to the alveoli.

10. The term 'vaccination' was coined by Edward Jenner in relation to the prevention of which disease?

A. Smallpox
B. Rabies
C. Polio
D. Measles
Answer: A

Edward Jenner coined the term 'vaccination' after developing the smallpox vaccine using cowpox virus. This was the first successful vaccine and laid the foundation for modern immunology.

11. Which of the following is a waterborne disease caused by a protozoan parasite?

A. Cholera
B. Amoebiasis
C. Typhoid
D. Hepatitis A
Answer: B

Amoebiasis is a waterborne disease caused by the protozoan parasite Entamoeba histolytica. Cholera and typhoid are caused by bacteria (Vibrio cholerae and Salmonella Typhi, respectively), while Hepatitis A is caused by a virus.

12. The use of genetically modified Bt cotton has significantly reduced the need for chemical pesticides because:

A. It produces a toxin that kills all insect pests indiscriminately.
B. It produces a toxin specifically harmful to bollworm larvae.
C. It repels all insects due to its modified genetic structure.
D. It increases the plant's natural resistance to all diseases.
Answer: B

Bt cotton is genetically modified to produce a toxin derived from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), which is specifically harmful to bollworm larvae (a major pest of cotton). This reduces the need for chemical pesticides while targeting only specific pests.

13. Which of the following statements about the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) is correct?

A. HIV can be transmitted through casual contact like shaking hands.
B. HIV primarily attacks red blood cells, leading to anemia.
C. HIV weakens the immune system by destroying CD4+ T cells.
D. HIV can be cured with a course of antibiotics.
Answer: C

HIV weakens the immune system by specifically targeting and destroying CD4+ T cells (a type of white blood cell). It is not transmitted through casual contact, does not primarily attack red blood cells, and cannot be cured with antibiotics (antiretroviral therapy is used to manage the infection).

14. Which of the following is a biological method of pest control that involves the use of natural predators?

A. Herbicides
B. Biomagnification
C. Biological control
D. Bioaccumulation
Answer: C

Biological control is a method of pest management that uses natural predators, parasites, or pathogens to regulate pest populations. For example, ladybird beetles are used to control aphid populations in agriculture. Herbicides are chemical agents, while biomagnification and bioaccumulation refer to the accumulation of substances in organisms.

15. The process of converting organic waste into manure by the action of microorganisms is known as:

A. Composting
B. Incineration
C. Sanitation
D. Sterilization
Answer: A

Composting is the biological decomposition of organic waste by microorganisms under controlled conditions, resulting in nutrient-rich manure. Incineration involves burning waste, sanitation refers to maintaining cleanliness, and sterilization is the elimination of microorganisms.

16. The term 'vaccination' was coined by Edward Jenner based on his work with which disease?

A. Smallpox
B. Cholera
C. Tuberculosis
D. Rabies
Answer: A

Edward Jenner developed the smallpox vaccine using cowpox virus, and the term 'vaccination' is derived from the Latin word *vacca*, meaning cow. This was the first successful vaccine and laid the foundation for immunology.

17. Which of the following is a genetically engineered human insulin used to treat diabetes?

A. Humulin
B. Aspartame
C. Streptokinase
D. Interferon
Answer: A

Humulin is a genetically engineered human insulin produced using *E. coli* bacteria. It is used to manage diabetes by regulating blood glucose levels. Aspartame is an artificial sweetener, streptokinase is a thrombolytic agent, and interferon is used in viral infections and cancer treatment.

18. The phenomenon where a pesticide becomes more concentrated as it moves up the food chain is called:

A. Biofortification
B. Bioremediation
C. Biomagnification
D. Biodegradation
Answer: C

Biomagnification refers to the increasing concentration of a substance, such as a pesticide, in the tissues of organisms at higher levels of the food chain. Biofortification is the process of increasing the nutritional content of crops, bioremediation involves using organisms to clean up pollutants, and biodegradation is the breakdown of substances by microorganisms.

19. Which of the following is a bacterial disease transmitted through contaminated water?

A. Tuberculosis
B. Cholera
C. Influenza
D. Hepatitis B
Answer: B

Cholera is caused by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae* and is transmitted through contaminated water or food. Tuberculosis is airborne, influenza is viral and airborne, and hepatitis B is transmitted through bodily fluids.

20. The practice of growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same field to improve soil fertility and reduce pests is called:

A. Monoculture
B. Crop rotation
C. Mixed cropping
D. Intercropping
Answer: D

Intercropping involves growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same field to optimize resource use, improve soil fertility, and reduce pest and disease incidence. Monoculture refers to growing a single crop, crop rotation involves alternating crops over seasons, and mixed cropping is a broader term that may not involve simultaneous planting.

21. Which of the following is a viral disease that affects the nervous system and is transmitted through the bite of an infected animal?

A. Tetanus
B. Rabies
C. Leprosy
D. Typhoid
Answer: B

Rabies is a viral disease that affects the central nervous system and is transmitted through the bite of an infected animal, such as dogs or bats. Tetanus is bacterial, leprosy is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*, and typhoid is a bacterial infection transmitted through contaminated food or water.

22. The process of introducing desirable traits into plants through genetic engineering is known as:

A. Hybridization
B. Mutation breeding
C. Transgenesis
D. Polyploidy
Answer: C

Transgenesis involves the introduction of foreign genes into plants to confer desirable traits, such as pest resistance or improved nutritional content. Hybridization is the crossing of two genetically different plants, mutation breeding involves inducing mutations, and polyploidy refers to having multiple sets of chromosomes.

23. Which of the following is a fungal disease that affects humans and is commonly known as 'ringworm'?

A. Candidiasis
B. Dermatophytosis
C. Aspergillosis
D. Histoplasmosis
Answer: B

Dermatophytosis, commonly known as ringworm, is a fungal infection of the skin caused by dermatophytes. Candidiasis is caused by *Candida* species, aspergillosis by *Aspergillus*, and histoplasmosis by *Histoplasma capsulatum*.

24. The use of microorganisms to clean up environmental pollutants is called:

A. Biofortification
B. Bioremediation
C. Biomagnification
D. Biodegradation
Answer: B

Bioremediation is the use of microorganisms to degrade or remove environmental pollutants, such as oil spills or heavy metals. Biofortification enhances nutritional content, biomagnification refers to the accumulation of substances in the food chain, and biodegradation is the breakdown of substances by microorganisms.
