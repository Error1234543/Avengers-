NEET Biology Practice: Cell Structure and Function (Part 1 of 2)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on Cell Structure and Function, aligned with the NEET Biology syllabus. Each question carries +4 marks for a correct answer and -1 mark for an incorrect answer. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. Which of the following structures is responsible for the synthesis of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) in eukaryotic cells?

A. Nucleolus
B. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
C. Golgi apparatus
D. Lysosome
Answer: A

The nucleolus is a dense, spherical structure within the nucleus of eukaryotic cells. It is the site of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) synthesis and ribosome assembly. The rRNA combines with proteins to form the subunits of ribosomes, which are then exported to the cytoplasm.

2. A student observes a cell under a microscope and notes the presence of a large central vacuole, chloroplasts, and a cell wall. This cell is most likely derived from which of the following?

A. Animal
B. Bacterium
C. Plant
D. Fungus
Answer: C

The presence of a large central vacuole, chloroplasts, and a cell wall are characteristic features of plant cells. Animal cells lack cell walls and chloroplasts, bacteria lack chloroplasts and a true nucleus, and fungal cells lack chloroplasts.

3. Which organelle is primarily involved in the detoxification of drugs and poisons in liver cells?

A. Mitochondrion
B. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum
C. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
D. Peroxisome
Answer: B

The smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER) is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification of drugs and poisons, particularly in liver cells. It contains enzymes that metabolize toxic substances, making them easier to excrete.

4. Which of the following statements about lysosomes is INCORRECT?

A. Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes active at acidic pH.
B. Lysosomes are involved in the digestion of intracellular waste.
C. Lysosomes are formed by the budding of the Golgi apparatus.
D. Lysosomes are present in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
Answer: D

Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles found only in eukaryotic cells. They contain hydrolytic enzymes that digest intracellular waste and are formed by the budding of the Golgi apparatus. Prokaryotic cells lack membrane-bound organelles, including lysosomes.

5. In which phase of the cell cycle does the replication of DNA occur?

A. G1 phase
B. S phase
C. G2 phase
D. M phase
Answer: B

DNA replication occurs during the S phase (synthesis phase) of the cell cycle. This phase follows the G1 phase and precedes the G2 phase, ensuring that each daughter cell receives an identical copy of the genome during cell division.

6. A cell is placed in a hypertonic solution. What is the most likely outcome?

A. The cell will swell and burst.
B. The cell will shrink due to water loss.
C. The cell will remain unchanged.
D. The cell will actively pump out solutes.
Answer: B

In a hypertonic solution, the concentration of solutes outside the cell is higher than inside. Water moves out of the cell by osmosis, causing the cell to shrink. This process is known as plasmolysis in plant cells.

7. The primary function of microvilli in epithelial cells is to:

A. Increase the surface area for absorption.
B. Facilitate cell movement.
C. Synthesize proteins.
D. Store cellular waste.
Answer: A

Microvilli are finger-like projections of the plasma membrane found in epithelial cells, particularly in the small intestine. They increase the surface area for absorption of nutrients, enhancing the cell's absorptive capacity.

8. Which of the following is a key difference between plant and animal cells?

A. Presence of a nucleus
B. Presence of mitochondria
C. Presence of chloroplasts
D. Presence of ribosomes
Answer: C

Chloroplasts are unique to plant cells and some algae. They are the site of photosynthesis, allowing plants to convert light energy into chemical energy. Animal cells lack chloroplasts but share other organelles like the nucleus, mitochondria, and ribosomes with plant cells.

9. Assertion: The rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) is involved in protein synthesis. Reason: The RER is studded with ribosomes. Which of the following is correct?

A. Both assertion and reason are true, and the reason is the correct explanation of the assertion.
B. Both assertion and reason are true, but the reason is not the correct explanation of the assertion.
C. The assertion is true, but the reason is false.
D. The assertion is false, but the reason is true.
Answer: A

The rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) is involved in protein synthesis because it is studded with ribosomes, which are the sites of protein synthesis. The presence of ribosomes on the RER directly explains its role in protein synthesis.

10. In plant cells, the cell wall is primarily composed of which polysaccharide?

A. Starch
B. Glycogen
C. Cellulose
D. Chitin
Answer: C

The primary component of the plant cell wall is cellulose, a polysaccharide made up of glucose units. Starch is a storage polysaccharide in plants, glycogen is a storage polysaccharide in animals, and chitin is found in the exoskeletons of arthropods and fungal cell walls.

11. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Golgi apparatus?

A. Modification of proteins
B. Sorting and packaging of proteins
C. Synthesis of ribosomal RNA
D. Formation of lysosomes
Answer: C

The synthesis of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) occurs in the nucleolus, not the Golgi apparatus. The Golgi apparatus is responsible for modifying, sorting, and packaging proteins and lipids, as well as forming lysosomes.

12. The process by which cells engulf solid particles from their surroundings is called:

A. Pinocytosis
B. Phagocytosis
C. Exocytosis
D. Osmosis
Answer: B

Phagocytosis is the process by which cells engulf solid particles, such as bacteria or debris, from their surroundings. Pinocytosis involves the ingestion of liquid, exocytosis is the process of expelling materials from the cell, and osmosis is the movement of water across a semi-permeable membrane.

13. Which of the following structures is found in animal cells but NOT in plant cells?

A. Centrioles
B. Mitochondria
C. Endoplasmic reticulum
D. Ribosomes
Answer: A

Centrioles are cylindrical structures found in animal cells that play a key role in cell division. They are absent in most plant cells. Mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and ribosomes are present in both animal and plant cells.

14. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of prokaryotic cells?

A. Presence of a well-defined nucleus
B. Membrane-bound organelles
C. Circular DNA not associated with histones
D. Presence of mitochondria
Answer: C

Prokaryotic cells lack a well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Their DNA is circular and not associated with histone proteins. Mitochondria are absent in prokaryotes.

15. The movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration is called:

A. Diffusion
B. Active transport
C. Osmosis
D. Facilitated diffusion
Answer: C

Osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration (hypotonic) to a region of higher solute concentration (hypertonic). Diffusion is the movement of solutes, active transport requires energy, and facilitated diffusion involves the movement of solutes via transport proteins.

16. Which of the following organelles is involved in the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide in cells?

A. Lysosomes
B. Peroxisomes
C. Mitochondria
D. Endoplasmic reticulum
Answer: B

Peroxisomes contain enzymes like catalase that break down hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen. Lysosomes are involved in digesting cellular waste, mitochondria are the powerhouse of the cell, and the endoplasmic reticulum is involved in protein and lipid synthesis.

17. A student observes a cell under an electron microscope and notes the presence of a well-defined nucleus, numerous membrane-bound organelles, and a complex cytoskeleton. However, the cell lacks a cell wall and plastids. Which of the following cell types is the student most likely observing?

A. Prokaryotic cell of a cyanobacterium
B. Eukaryotic cell of a plant root
C. Eukaryotic cell of a human liver
D. Prokaryotic cell of a bacterial pathogen
Answer: C

The presence of a well-defined nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum) indicates a eukaryotic cell. The absence of a cell wall and plastids rules out plant cells (Option B) and prokaryotic cells (Options A and D). Human liver cells are eukaryotic, lack a cell wall and plastids, and possess a complex cytoskeleton, making Option C the correct choice. This question tests the ability to differentiate between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, as well as plant and animal cells, based on structural features.
