NEET Biology: Reproduction - Practice Set (Part 1 of 2)

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions on the topic of Reproduction for NEET. Each question carries 4 marks with a negative marking of -1 for incorrect answers. Select the most appropriate answer for each question.

1. What is the primary role of the hormone relaxin during pregnancy?

A. Stimulating uterine contractions during labor
B. Inhibiting ovulation during pregnancy
C. Relaxing the pelvic ligaments and cervix to facilitate childbirth
D. Promoting milk production in mammary glands
Answer: C

Relaxin is a hormone produced during pregnancy that relaxes the pelvic ligaments and softens the cervix, facilitating childbirth. It does not stimulate uterine contractions (oxytocin does), inhibit ovulation (progesterone does), or promote milk production (prolactin does).

2. In humans, the process of cleavage results in the formation of a structure called the:

A. Blastocyst
B. Gastrula
C. Morula
D. Neurula
Answer: C

Cleavage is the series of rapid mitotic divisions of the zygote that results in the formation of a solid ball of cells called the morula. The morula later develops into a blastocyst, which implants in the uterus. Gastrulation and neurulation occur later in development.

3. Which of the following is a correct statement about the menstrual cycle?

A. The proliferative phase is dominated by progesterone.
B. Ovulation is triggered by a surge in luteinizing hormone (LH).
C. The secretory phase occurs before ovulation.
D. Menstruation is caused by a rise in estrogen levels.
Answer: B

Ovulation is triggered by a surge in luteinizing hormone (LH) around day 14 of the menstrual cycle. The proliferative phase is dominated by estrogen, the secretory phase occurs after ovulation and is dominated by progesterone, and menstruation is caused by a drop in progesterone and estrogen levels.

4. Which of the following structures is derived from the ectoderm during embryonic development?

A. Liver
B. Spinal cord
C. Muscles
D. Kidneys
Answer: B

The spinal cord is derived from the ectoderm, specifically the neural tube, during embryonic development. The liver is derived from the endoderm, muscles from the mesoderm, and kidneys from the intermediate mesoderm.

5. What is the primary function of the corpus luteum in the absence of fertilization?

A. Secretion of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
B. Production of estrogen and progesterone to maintain the endometrium
C. Degeneration and formation of the corpus albicans
D. Stimulation of ovulation
Answer: C

In the absence of fertilization, the corpus luteum degenerates and forms the corpus albicans, a fibrous scar tissue. It initially produces estrogen and progesterone to maintain the endometrium, but this production ceases as it degenerates. FSH is secreted by the anterior pituitary, and ovulation is stimulated by LH.

6. In human females, the process of oogenesis begins:

A. At puberty
B. During fetal development
C. At menarche
D. After fertilization
Answer: B

Oogenesis in human females begins during fetal development, where oogonia are formed and enter meiosis I to become primary oocytes. These primary oocytes remain arrested in prophase I until puberty, when meiosis resumes.

7. In humans, the sex of the offspring is determined by:

A. The mother's genetic contribution
B. The father's genetic contribution
C. Both parents equally
D. Environmental factors
Answer: B

In humans, the sex of the offspring is determined by the father's genetic contribution. Males have XY sex chromosomes, and females have XX. The sperm carrying an X or Y chromosome determines whether the offspring will be female (XX) or male (XY).

8. Which of the following is a method of vegetative propagation in plants?

A. Pollination
B. Grafting
C. Fertilization
D. Seed dispersal
Answer: B

Grafting is a method of vegetative propagation where a part of one plant (scion) is attached to the root system of another plant (stock). Pollination and fertilization are part of sexual reproduction, while seed dispersal aids in the spread of seeds.

9. The structure that connects the fetus to the placenta and facilitates the exchange of substances is called the:

A. Amnion
B. Umbilical cord
C. Chorion
D. Yolk sac
Answer: B

The umbilical cord connects the fetus to the placenta and contains blood vessels that facilitate the exchange of nutrients, oxygen, and waste products between the mother and fetus. The amnion is a membrane that encloses the embryo, the chorion forms part of the placenta, and the yolk sac provides early nourishment.

10. In the process of spermatogenesis, which of the following cells is the first to become haploid?

A. Primary spermatocyte
B. Secondary spermatocyte
C. Spermatogonium
D. Spermatid
Answer: B

Spermatogenesis is the process of sperm formation. It begins with diploid spermatogonia, which divide mitotically to form primary spermatocytes (also diploid). The primary spermatocyte undergoes meiosis I to form two haploid secondary spermatocytes. Thus, the secondary spermatocyte is the first haploid cell in the sequence. Spermatids are formed after meiosis II of secondary spermatocytes and are also haploid, but they appear later in the process.
