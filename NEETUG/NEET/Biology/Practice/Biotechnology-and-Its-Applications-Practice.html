NEET Biology: Biotechnology and Its Applications - Practice Set (Part 1)

NEETEnglishPractice

This practice set contains 12 exam-focused multiple-choice questions (MCQs) on 'Biotechnology and Its Applications' for NEET. Each question has four options, and only one is correct. Select the most appropriate answer. Negative marking applies as per NEET pattern (+4 for correct, -1 for incorrect).

1. Which of the following is NOT a product of recombinant DNA technology used in medicine?

A. Insulin
B. Human growth hormone
C. Penicillin
D. Hepatitis B vaccine
Answer: C

Penicillin is an antibiotic produced naturally by the fungus *Penicillium* and is not a product of recombinant DNA technology. Insulin, human growth hormone, and the hepatitis B vaccine are all produced using recombinant DNA techniques.

2. The Ti plasmid used in the creation of transgenic plants is derived from which bacterium?

A. *Escherichia coli*
B. *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*
C. *Bacillus thuringiensis*
D. *Streptomyces*
Answer: B

*Agrobacterium tumefaciens* is a soil bacterium that naturally transfers a segment of its Ti (tumor-inducing) plasmid into plant cells, causing crown gall disease. This plasmid is modified in biotechnology to introduce desirable genes into plants.

3. Which of the following is a concern associated with the use of genetically modified (GM) crops?

A. Increased nutritional value of crops
B. Potential for horizontal gene transfer to wild relatives
C. Reduced use of chemical pesticides
D. Higher crop yields
Answer: B

One of the major concerns with GM crops is the potential for horizontal gene transfer, where genes from GM crops could spread to wild relatives or non-target organisms, leading to unintended ecological consequences.

4. PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) is used to:

A. Cut DNA at specific sites
B. Amplify small amounts of DNA
C. Translate DNA into proteins
D. Separate DNA fragments by size
Answer: B

PCR is a technique used to amplify small amounts of DNA exponentially. It involves repeated cycles of denaturation, annealing, and extension, allowing for the production of millions of copies of a specific DNA sequence.

5. Which of the following is an example of a bioreactor used in biotechnology?

A. Centrifuge
B. Fermenter
C. Electrophoresis apparatus
D. Microscope
Answer: B

A fermenter (or bioreactor) is a vessel used to grow microorganisms or cells under controlled conditions for the production of biological products, such as recombinant proteins, antibiotics, or vaccines.

6. The technique used to identify individuals based on their unique DNA sequences is called:

A. Gel electrophoresis
B. DNA fingerprinting
C. Southern blotting
D. Northern blotting
Answer: B

DNA fingerprinting is a technique used to identify individuals based on their unique DNA sequences, particularly in forensic science, paternity testing, and wildlife conservation. It relies on the analysis of variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs) or short tandem repeats (STRs).

7. The process of inserting a normal gene into the cells of a patient to correct a genetic disorder is called:

A. Gene therapy
B. Gene cloning
C. Gene amplification
D. Gene mutation
Answer: A

Gene therapy is a technique used to treat genetic disorders by inserting a normal or functional gene into the cells of a patient to replace or compensate for a defective gene. This can be done using viral vectors or other delivery methods.

8. Which of the following is a limitation of using *E. coli* as a host for recombinant protein production?

A. It cannot produce eukaryotic proteins
B. It lacks the machinery for post-translational modifications
C. It grows too slowly for industrial use
D. It is pathogenic to humans
Answer: B

*E. coli* is a prokaryotic organism and lacks the machinery for post-translational modifications, such as glycosylation, which are often required for the proper function of eukaryotic proteins. This limits its use for producing complex human proteins.

9. Which of the following is the primary advantage of using genetically modified Bt cotton over conventional cotton?

A. Increased resistance to viral infections
B. Enhanced resistance to bollworm pests without chemical pesticides
C. Improved tolerance to drought conditions
D. Higher nutritional content in cottonseeds
Answer: B

Bt cotton is genetically modified to express the Cry protein from *Bacillus thuringiensis*, which provides resistance to bollworm pests. This reduces the need for chemical pesticides, making it a primary advantage over conventional cotton.

10. A patient suffering from adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency is treated using gene therapy. Which of the following steps is NOT part of the standard gene therapy protocol for ADA deficiency?

A. Isolation of lymphocytes from the patient’s blood
B. Introduction of a functional ADA gene into the lymphocytes using a retroviral vector
C. Direct injection of the ADA enzyme into the patient’s bloodstream
D. Reinfusion of the genetically modified lymphocytes back into the patient
Answer: C

Gene therapy for ADA deficiency involves modifying the patient’s lymphocytes to express the functional ADA gene, not directly injecting the enzyme. The corrected cells are then reinfused into the patient.

11. Golden Rice is a genetically modified crop designed to address which nutritional deficiency?

A. Iron deficiency
B. Vitamin A deficiency
C. Protein deficiency
D. Iodine deficiency
Answer: B

Golden Rice is engineered to produce beta-carotene, a precursor of Vitamin A, to combat Vitamin A deficiency, particularly in regions where rice is a dietary staple.

12. Which of the following techniques is used to amplify DNA fragments for applications like DNA fingerprinting or gene cloning?

A. Southern blotting
B. Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)
C. Gel electrophoresis
D. Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (RFLP)
Answer: B

Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) is the technique used to amplify specific DNA fragments in vitro, making it essential for applications like DNA fingerprinting and gene cloning.

13. In the context of biopiracy, which of the following scenarios best illustrates the unethical exploitation of biological resources?

A. A pharmaceutical company patents a traditional medicinal plant used by indigenous communities without their consent or compensation
B. A research institution collaborates with local farmers to develop drought-resistant crop varieties
C. A government regulates the export of endangered plant species to prevent overharvesting
D. A biotech firm shares profits with a country for using its genetic resources in drug development
Answer: A

Biopiracy involves the unauthorized commercial use of biological resources or traditional knowledge from indigenous communities, often without compensation or consent, as illustrated in option A.

14. The process of RNA interference (RNAi) is used in biotechnology primarily for which purpose?

A. Amplifying RNA sequences for cloning
B. Silencing specific genes to study their function or develop pest-resistant crops
C. Synthesizing proteins in bacterial cells
D. Sequencing RNA molecules
Answer: B

RNA interference (RNAi) is a biological process used to silence or knock down the expression of specific genes, making it valuable for functional genomics and developing pest-resistant crops.

15. Which of the following statements about transgenic animals is INCORRECT?

A. Transgenic animals can be used to produce human proteins in their milk
B. Transgenic animals are always larger than their non-transgenic counterparts
C. Transgenic animals can serve as models for studying human diseases
D. Transgenic animals may be used to improve livestock productivity
Answer: B

Transgenic animals are not necessarily larger than their non-transgenic counterparts. Their size depends on the specific genetic modification introduced, not the fact of being transgenic itself.

16. A researcher wants to introduce a foreign gene into a plant cell. Which of the following methods is most commonly used for this purpose?

A. Microinjection
B. *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*-mediated transformation
C. Electroporation of protoplasts
D. Gene gun bombardment
Answer: B

*Agrobacterium tumefaciens*-mediated transformation is the most commonly used method for introducing foreign genes into plant cells due to its efficiency and simplicity.

17. Which of the following is a potential risk associated with the release of genetically modified organisms (GMOs) into the environment?

A. Increased biodiversity due to new species introduction
B. Reduced need for agricultural land
C. Unintended harm to non-target organisms, such as beneficial insects
D. Decreased use of water resources in farming
Answer: C

A potential risk of releasing GMOs into the environment is unintended harm to non-target organisms, such as beneficial insects (e.g., pollinators) that may be affected by toxins produced by GM crops.

18. The term 'molecular farming' refers to which of the following biotechnological applications?

A. Using plants or animals to produce pharmaceutical proteins or vaccines
B. Cultivating crops in controlled indoor environments
C. Developing new farming techniques using molecular biology tools
D. Sequencing the genomes of farm animals
Answer: A

Molecular farming involves using genetically modified plants or animals to produce pharmaceutical proteins, vaccines, or other valuable biomolecules.

19. Which of the following enzymes is essential for the process of creating recombinant DNA?

A. DNA polymerase
B. Restriction endonuclease
C. RNA polymerase
D. Ligase for RNA molecules
Answer: B

Restriction endonucleases are essential for cutting DNA at specific sequences, enabling the creation of recombinant DNA by joining fragments from different sources.

20. A farmer cultivates Bt cotton to protect the crop from bollworm infestation. Which of the following statements best explains the mechanism of action of the Bt toxin produced by this genetically modified crop?

A. The Bt toxin binds to the midgut epithelial cells of the bollworm, creating pores that disrupt osmotic balance, leading to cell lysis and death of the insect.
B. The Bt toxin inhibits the synthesis of chitin in the bollworm's exoskeleton, weakening its structural integrity and causing death during molting.
C. The Bt toxin acts as a neurotoxin, blocking acetylcholine receptors in the bollworm's nervous system, leading to paralysis and death.
D. The Bt toxin interferes with the bollworm's digestive enzymes, preventing nutrient absorption and causing starvation.
Answer: A

The Bt toxin, produced by *Bacillus thuringiensis* and expressed in Bt cotton, is a crystalline protein (Cry protein) that specifically targets certain insects like bollworms. When ingested by the insect, the alkaline pH of the midgut solubilizes the toxin, which then binds to specific receptors on the epithelial cells of the midgut. This binding creates pores in the cell membrane, disrupting the osmotic balance and causing the cells to swell and lyse. The resulting damage to the midgut leads to the death of the insect. This mechanism is highly specific to certain insect orders (e.g., Lepidoptera) and does not affect humans or other non-target organisms.
